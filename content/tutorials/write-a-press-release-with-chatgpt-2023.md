Press releases follow one of the most rigid formats in business writing. There's a headline, a dateline, a lead that answers who/what/when/where/why, a quote or two, supporting detail, a company boilerplate, and media contact info. That rigidity makes them a great fit for ChatGPT. The structure is predictable, and the hard part, deciding what's actually newsworthy, stays with you.

This guide walks through a workflow that takes you from raw facts to a polished release in under an hour. It works with the free version of ChatGPT (GPT-3.5), though GPT-4 on ChatGPT Plus handles the detailed instructions better. Our [GPT-4 vs GPT-3.5 comparison](/compare/gpt-4-vs-gpt-3-5-2023/) explains the difference if you're deciding whether to upgrade.

## Step 1: Build a fact sheet before you open ChatGPT

The biggest mistake people make is asking ChatGPT to "write a press release about our new product" with a one-line description. The model fills the gaps with invented details and generic praise.

Instead, write a short fact sheet in plain bullet points:

- **The news**: what is happening, in one sentence
- **Who**: company name, location, what you do
- **When**: launch or effective date
- **Key details**: features, prices, availability, locations, partners
- **Why it matters**: the problem it solves and who benefits
- **Proof points**: real numbers you're allowed to share (customers, growth, results)
- **Spokesperson**: name, title, and two or three things they actually believe about the news
- **Boilerplate**: your standard "About [Company]" paragraph
- **Media contact**: name, email, phone

This takes 15 minutes and does more for quality than any prompt trick. ChatGPT can only be as accurate as what you give it.

## Step 2: Pressure-test the news angle

Before drafting, use ChatGPT as a skeptical editor. Paste your fact sheet and ask:

```
You are a business journalist who receives 200 press releases a week.
Here is a fact sheet for an announcement. Tell me honestly:
1. Is this newsworthy, and to which kinds of publications?
2. What is the strongest angle?
3. What questions would you ask that the fact sheet doesn't answer?

[paste fact sheet]
```

This step often changes the release. A "new feature launch" might be more interesting framed around a customer problem, a local angle, or a trend. If ChatGPT's questions expose gaps, go back and add the answers to your fact sheet.

## Step 3: Generate headline options

Headlines decide whether a release gets opened. Ask for options in different styles:

```
Write 10 press release headlines for this announcement. Mix styles:
straightforward news, benefit-led, and number-led. Each under 15 words.
No hype words like "revolutionary," "game-changing," or "cutting-edge."
```

Pick the two or three strongest and keep them for later. You'll often end up combining parts of several options. A good headline states the actual news. "Acme Launches Same-Day Repair Service in Portland" beats "Acme Revolutionizes the Repair Experience."

## Step 4: Draft the release with a structured prompt

Now ask for the full draft with explicit constraints:

```
Write a press release using ONLY the facts below. Do not invent
statistics, customers, or quotes beyond what I provide.

Format:
- Headline: [paste your chosen headline]
- Subheadline: one sentence
- Dateline: [CITY, State] — [Month Day, Year]
- Lead paragraph: who, what, when, where, why in under 50 words
- 2-3 body paragraphs with supporting detail
- One quote from [spokesperson name, title]
- Boilerplate (use exactly as written)
- Media contact

Tone: clear, factual, AP style. Total length: 400-500 words.
Mark anything you're unsure about with [CHECK].

[paste fact sheet]
```

The "use ONLY the facts below" line and the [CHECK] instruction matter a lot. They don't eliminate made-up details, but they reduce them and make the risky spots easier to find.

## Step 5: Rewrite the quote so it sounds human

AI-generated quotes are the most obvious tell in a release. They tend to sound like this: "We are thrilled to announce this innovative solution that will empower our customers." No real person talks that way, and journalists skip over quotes like it.

Ask for alternatives with more texture:

```
Rewrite the quote three ways. Make each sound like a real founder
talking to a reporter: specific, a little informal, focused on a
customer problem. Under 40 words each. Avoid "thrilled," "excited,"
and "innovative."
```

Then send the best option to the actual spokesperson. They should edit it into their own words and approve it. Never publish a quote attributed to a real person without their sign-off.

## Step 6: Edit for tightness and style

Paste the full draft back and run an editing pass:

```
Edit this press release. Cut filler words and jargon, keep sentences
under 25 words where possible, use AP style for dates, numbers, and
titles, and flag any sentence that makes a claim not supported by
the fact sheet.
```

Then read it yourself, out loud. Check that the lead paragraph alone tells the whole story. Busy editors often read only the headline and first paragraph.

## Step 7: Fact-check everything

This step is not optional. Go line by line and confirm:

- Names, titles, and spellings
- Dates, days of the week, and times (ChatGPT often gets day-of-week wrong)
- Prices, figures, and percentages
- Product names and capitalization
- Links and contact details

Remove every [CHECK] marker only after you've verified it. If ChatGPT added a statistic you didn't supply, delete it. Don't try to find a source to justify it afterward.

## Step 8: Create the supporting pieces

Once the release is final, ChatGPT can quickly spin off the extras you'll need for distribution:

- **A pitch email**: "Write a 100-word pitch email to a [beat] reporter based on this release. Lead with why their readers would care."
- **Social posts**: "Write a LinkedIn post and a short tweet announcing this news."
- **A website blurb**: "Write a 60-word summary for our newsroom page."

This is where ChatGPT saves the most time. Turning one finished document into several formats is easy, low-risk work for it. The same approach works for other business writing too. See our guide to [writing blog posts with ChatGPT](/tutorials/02-write-blog-posts-chatgpt/).

## Tips for better results

- **Keep one conversation per release.** ChatGPT remembers context within a chat, so your fact sheet and edits stay in play.
- **Give it a sample.** If you have a past release you liked, paste it and say "match this structure and tone."
- **Ask for shorter, not longer.** Most releases are better at 400 words than 800.
- **Use placeholders for sensitive data.** Write "[REVENUE FIGURE]" instead of the real number, then fill it in during your edit.
- **Turn off chat history** in ChatGPT's settings if your company is cautious about launch details being used for training.

## Common pitfalls to avoid

**Inventing news.** If the announcement isn't newsworthy, no prompt will fix it. ChatGPT will happily dress up a minor update as a major launch, and journalists will notice.

**Hype vocabulary.** Search your draft for "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "seamless," "leverage," and "empower," and cut most of them.

**Fabricated quotes.** Every quote must come from or be approved by the named person.

**Wrong dates and details.** The model doesn't know today's date unless you tell it, and it can mix up details from your fact sheet. Verify everything.

**Skipping the human edit.** A ChatGPT draft is a strong starting point, not a finished product. Budget at least 15 minutes of human editing per release.
