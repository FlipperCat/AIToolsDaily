If you use Claude a lot, you've probably noticed the tax: every new conversation starts from zero. You paste the same brand guidelines, re-explain your role, re-upload the same reference doc. Claude Projects, available on Claude Pro and Team plans, exists to kill that repetition. A Project is a persistent workspace with its own custom instructions and knowledge that apply to every chat inside it.

This guide walks through setting one up properly and using it well. It takes about ten minutes and pays off every day after.

## Why Projects Are Worth the Effort

A Project bundles three things:

- **Custom instructions** that tell Claude who you are, how you want it to respond, and what to prioritize — applied automatically to every conversation in the Project.
- **Project knowledge** — documents, notes, transcripts, or code you upload once and Claude can reference in every chat.
- **A collection of related chats** grouped in one place instead of scattered across your history.

The result is that Claude stops feeling like a stranger you brief from scratch each morning and starts feeling like a colleague who already knows the account. If you're new to Claude generally, our [Claude review](/reviews/claude-review/) covers the basics before you dive in here.

## Step 1: Create the Project

From the Claude sidebar, choose **Projects** and create a new one. Give it a clear, specific name — "Q3 Marketing — Acme" beats "Marketing." The name is how you'll find it later, and specificity keeps you from dumping unrelated work into one bloated Project.

Resist the urge to make one giant "Work" Project. The whole value comes from focus. One Project per client, product, or recurring workflow keeps the instructions and knowledge tight and relevant.

## Step 2: Write Custom Instructions

This is where most of the value lives, and where most people underinvest. In the Project's custom instructions, tell Claude:

- **Who you are and your role** — "I'm a content marketer at a B2B SaaS company."
- **What this Project is for** — "Drafting and editing blog posts and email campaigns for Acme."
- **How you want responses** — tone, length, format preferences, and any hard rules ("always use British spelling," "never use emoji").
- **What to prioritize or avoid** — "Favor concrete examples over generalities. Don't invent statistics."

Write these as if onboarding a new hire. The more precisely you describe your standards, the less editing you'll do later. You can revise them anytime as you learn what Claude gets wrong.

## Step 3: Add Project Knowledge

Now upload the reference material Claude should always have on hand. Good candidates:

- Brand or style guidelines
- Product one-pagers and positioning docs
- Example outputs you consider "on target"
- Meeting transcripts or research you'll keep referencing
- Code files or specs, if it's a development Project

Claude's large context window means you can add a meaningful amount, but be selective. A few high-signal documents beat a landfill of everything you have. Curate the knowledge the way you'd brief a smart contractor: give them what matters, not the entire shared drive.

## Step 4: Start Working Inside the Project

Every new chat you start from within the Project inherits both the instructions and the knowledge automatically. Try it: open a chat and ask for a first draft without re-explaining anything. If your setup is good, Claude will already write in your voice and reference your docs.

A few workflows that shine here:

- **Drafting with house style baked in.** Ask for a blog post and Claude applies your guidelines by default. Pair this with our notes on [using Claude for writing](/how-to-use-claude-for-writing/).
- **Consistent editing.** Paste a rough draft and ask Claude to tighten it against your standards.
- **Answering from your own docs.** Ask questions about the uploaded material and get answers grounded in your context rather than generic advice.

When Claude produces something substantial like a document or a code file, it often opens it as an Artifact in a side panel you can iterate on — see our [Artifacts tutorial](/tutorials/27-use-claude-artifacts/) for how to get the most from that view.

## Step 5: Iterate on the Setup

Treat the Project as living. When Claude repeatedly misses in the same way, don't correct it chat by chat — fix the custom instructions so the correction sticks everywhere. When your reference material goes stale, swap the documents. A well-maintained Project gets better over months; a set-and-forget one slowly drifts out of date.

## Tips for Getting More Out of Projects

- **One Project, one job.** Focus beats breadth. Split anything that's grown sprawling.
- **Front-load your standards.** The time you spend writing precise instructions is repaid every single chat.
- **Name chats as you go.** Inside an active Project, conversations pile up fast; clear titles save you later.
- **Use examples, not just rules.** Uploading one great sample output teaches Claude more than a paragraph describing it.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **The junk-drawer Project.** Cramming unrelated work into one Project dilutes the instructions and confuses the knowledge base. Make more, smaller Projects.
- **Vague instructions.** "Be professional" tells Claude almost nothing. "Short paragraphs, no jargon, active voice, examples over adjectives" tells it a lot.
- **Over-stuffing knowledge.** More documents isn't more useful. Irrelevant files add noise. Curate.
- **Forgetting sharing settings.** On Team plans, confirm whether a Project is private or shared before adding anything confidential.
- **Never revisiting the setup.** A Project you built once and never tuned will slowly stop matching how you actually work.

## Wrapping Up

Claude Projects turn a capable general assistant into something tailored to your actual work. The mechanics are simple — name it, write instructions, add knowledge, start chatting — but the payoff scales with how thoughtfully you set them up. Spend ten focused minutes building one properly and you'll stop paying the re-briefing tax on every conversation.
