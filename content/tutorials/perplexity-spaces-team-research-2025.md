Perplexity is great at answering one-off questions, but teams don't work in one-off questions — they work in projects. That's the gap Spaces fills. A Space is a persistent research hub where you set custom instructions once, upload the documents everyone should be reasoning over, and invite your teammates to share it. This guide walks through setting up a Space your team will actually use, based on running several for real research projects.

## Why Spaces Beats Plain Chat for Teams

In a normal Perplexity thread, every conversation starts cold. You re-paste context, re-explain the project, and re-state how you want answers formatted. Multiply that across five teammates and you've got five inconsistent, context-free workflows.

A Space fixes this by making context durable. You define the project's rules and sources once, and every question anyone asks inside the Space inherits them. If you're new to the tool itself, our [Perplexity review](/reviews/perplexity-review/) covers the fundamentals; this guide assumes you know the basics and want to run a shared research workflow.

## Step 1: Create the Space and Name It Clearly

From the Perplexity sidebar, open **Spaces** and create a new one. Give it a name that describes the *project*, not the tool — "Q3 Competitor Research" or "Onboarding Docs," not "Perplexity Space 1." A clear name matters more than it seems: when teammates have several Spaces, the name is how they find the right context fast.

Add a short description too. It's the first thing collaborators read, and one sentence — "Everything we're gathering on the enterprise search market" — orients people instantly.

## Step 2: Write Custom Instructions That Do Real Work

Custom instructions are the highest-leverage part of a Space. They apply to every question asked inside it, so treat them like a standing brief. Good instructions specify:

- **Role and audience** — "You're helping a product team; assume a technical but non-academic reader."
- **Output format** — "Answer in short bullet points with a one-line summary first."
- **Sourcing rules** — "Prioritize primary sources and cite them; flag when something is speculative."
- **Scope boundaries** — "Focus on the enterprise segment; ignore consumer-market news."

Vague instructions produce vague help. The difference between "be helpful" and the specifics above is the difference between a Space that saves time and one that just repeats generic search results.

## Step 3: Upload the Files Everyone Should Reason Over

Spaces let you attach files — reports, briefs, spreadsheets, PDFs — that become part of the Space's context. This is what turns Perplexity from a web search engine into a research assistant grounded in *your* material. Upload the documents your team keeps referencing so questions can draw on both the open web and your private sources in one answer.

A few practical tips:

- **Upload the source of truth, not everything.** Ten focused documents beat fifty noisy ones; irrelevant files dilute answers.
- **Name files descriptively** before uploading so citations are legible.
- **Refresh stale files.** If a strategy doc changes, replace the old upload — the Space won't know a file is outdated.

Higher usage limits on Perplexity Pro help here, since teams hit free-tier upload and search caps quickly when a Space is active.

## Step 4: Choose Your Search Focus and Model

Inside a Space you can steer *how* Perplexity searches. Use a web focus for market and news questions, and lean on the deeper research mode when you need a thorough, multi-source answer rather than a quick lookup. If you're on Pro, you can also select a stronger underlying model for reasoning-heavy questions.

Match the tool to the task: quick factual checks don't need the heaviest research mode, but a "give me a full landscape of X" question does. For a workflow devoted to deep digging, pair this with our guide to [automating research with Perplexity](/tutorials/automate-research-with-perplexity/).

## Step 5: Invite Collaborators

Share the Space with your team so everyone works from the same instructions, files, and thread history. Now when a teammate asks a question, they inherit all the context you configured — no re-briefing required. Shared threads also mean people can see what's already been asked and build on it instead of duplicating research.

Set a light norm for the team: keep questions inside the Space (not in random one-off chats) so the shared history stays useful, and add a note in the description about what belongs there.

## Step 6: Build a Reusable Question Library

The most underused habit: keep a running list of good prompts that work well in your Space. Research projects ask similar questions repeatedly — "summarize the latest developments on X," "compare these two options on cost and risk," "find primary sources for this claim." Saving the phrasings that produce strong answers turns your Space into a repeatable system rather than a place people improvise.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Treating the score of an answer as fact.** Perplexity cites sources — actually click them. AI answer engines can still misread or over-summarize; verify claims that matter.
- **Overstuffing custom instructions.** A wall of rules confuses the model as much as it guides it. Keep instructions tight and specific.
- **Forgetting to update files.** A Space grounded in outdated documents will confidently give outdated answers.
- **Using Spaces for everything.** For quick one-offs, a plain thread is faster. Reserve Spaces for genuine projects.

## Wrapping Up

Perplexity Spaces turns a fast answer engine into a shared research workspace. The payoff comes from the setup: clear custom instructions, the right uploaded files, a sensible search focus, and a team that keeps its work in one place. Do that, and a Space stops being a novelty and becomes the first thing your team opens when a new research question lands. Spend twenty minutes configuring it well, and it pays that back every week.
