Language apps drill you on vocabulary. Textbooks explain grammar. But the thing that actually makes a language stick — messy, improvised conversation with instant feedback — has always required another human. That's the gap ChatGPT fills surprisingly well.

We've spent the past few months using [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) as a daily practice partner for Spanish and Japanese. Here's the routine that works, the prompts to steal, and the mistakes to avoid.

## Step 1: Set up a standing instruction

Don't re-explain your situation every session. Use custom instructions (Settings → Custom Instructions) so every new chat already knows the deal. Something like:

> I'm learning Spanish at an intermediate (B1) level. When we practice, reply in simple Spanish, then add corrections of my mistakes in English at the end of each reply, briefly explaining why. Keep replies short and conversational.

This one change transforms the experience. Every conversation becomes practice by default, and the correction format stays consistent. If you're new to ChatGPT generally, our [getting started guide](/getting-started-with-chatgpt-2023/) covers the basics first.

## Step 2: Calibrate your level

Before drilling, let the model find your level:

> Ask me five questions in French, starting very easy and getting progressively harder. Based on my answers, estimate my CEFR level and tell me what to focus on.

It's not an official placement test, but it's good enough to aim the difficulty correctly — and you can re-run it monthly to feel progress.

## Step 3: Daily conversation role-play (the core habit)

This is the highest-value exercise. Give ChatGPT a scene and a role:

> Role-play with me in German. You're a waiter in a Berlin café and I'm ordering breakfast. Stay in character, use natural casual German, and only correct me at the end of the whole scene, not after every message.

Rotate scenarios that match real life: checking into a hotel, returning a purchase, small talk with a coworker, phone call to reschedule an appointment. Fifteen minutes a day of this beats an hour of flashcards, because you're retrieving words under mild pressure — the thing conversation actually requires.

Two settings to experiment with:

- **Correction timing.** Corrections after every message are great for beginners; end-of-scene corrections keep intermediate conversations flowing.
- **Strictness.** Ask it to "only use vocabulary a first-year student would know" or "speak like a fast native and don't slow down" depending on whether you want comfort or challenge.

## Step 4: Turn your mistakes into drills

After a role-play, mine it:

> List every mistake I made in that conversation. For the three most important, create five fill-in-the-blank exercises each. Quiz me one at a time and don't show answers until I try.

This is the loop most apps can't do: practice → personalized error analysis → targeted drills on *your* weaknesses, not a generic curriculum.

## Step 5: Add voice for speaking practice

If you have ChatGPT Plus, the mobile app's voice conversations turn this whole routine into actual speaking practice. Start a voice chat, use the same role-play prompts, and talk. The speech recognition handles accented, imperfect speech better than we expected, and hearing responses aloud trains your ear between podcast sessions.

Honest caveat: it won't coach your pronunciation with the precision of a human tutor — it understands you or it doesn't, which is coarse feedback. But for fluency (producing sentences in real time without freezing), it's the best always-available option we've found.

The mobile app can also use your camera via image input: snap a menu, a street sign, or a product label and ask for a translation plus vocabulary breakdown. Our [ChatGPT vision guide](/tutorials/chatgpt-vision-guide-2023/) covers what image input can and can't do.

## Step 6: Build a vocabulary system

Ask for vocabulary in context, never as bare lists:

> Give me 8 useful Spanish words for talking about cooking. For each: an example sentence, the register (formal/casual), and a common mistake learners make with it. Then quiz me on yesterday's 8 words first.

Keep one ongoing chat as your "vocab thread" so it can recycle earlier words into later quizzes — a rough approximation of spaced repetition. For serious long-term retention, export words to a proper flashcard app; ChatGPT's memory of a long thread gets unreliable as it grows.

## Step 7: Use it as a grammar explainer of last resort

Where ChatGPT genuinely embarrasses textbooks is answering the questions textbooks won't: "What's the actual difference between *por* and *para* here?" or "Why does this sentence use subjunctive when yesterday's example didn't?" Ask follow-ups until it clicks. Request three different explanations — a rule, an analogy, and five contrasting examples — and one of them will land.

## Step 8: Journal in your target language

Once conversation practice feels routine, add written production. Each evening, write 3-5 sentences about your day in the target language — badly is fine, badly is the point — then paste them in:

> Correct this Spanish journal entry. Show a table: my sentence, the corrected version, and a one-line explanation. Then rewrite the whole entry the way a native speaker my age would casually write it.

That last instruction is the gold. Seeing the gap between "grammatically fixed" and "how a native would actually say it" teaches register and phrasing that no textbook exercise covers. Save the native-style rewrites — after a few weeks they become a personal phrasebook of sentences you already tried to say once, which makes them far easier to remember than generic examples.

You can also flip the exercise for reading practice: paste a paragraph from a news article and ask for a version graded to your level, with the original vocabulary listed underneath. Graded readers on demand, from any text that interests you.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Invented idioms.** ChatGPT occasionally produces expressions that sound plausible but aren't things natives say. Before adopting a colorful phrase, verify it — a quick search or a check against [DeepL](/reviews/deepl-review/) helps.
- **Over-formal register.** Left alone, it speaks like a polite textbook. Explicitly ask for casual, spoken-style language or you'll learn to talk like a business letter.
- **Passive scrolling.** Reading its corrections feels productive but isn't. The value is in *your* production — make sure most of the words in each session are yours.
- **Trusting it on rare languages.** Quality drops off sharply outside major languages. For Welsh or Icelandic, treat it as a supplement to human materials, not a source of truth.
- **No accountability.** ChatGPT will never notice you skipped a week. Pair it with a streak habit, a tutor, or a study partner for the discipline layer.

## A sample 20-minute daily session

1. **2 min** — Quiz on yesterday's vocabulary (from your vocab thread)
2. **10 min** — One role-play scenario, voice if you have it
3. **5 min** — Error review and drills from that conversation
4. **3 min** — Eight new vocabulary words in context

Do that five days a week and you'll feel the difference in a month — not because ChatGPT is magic, but because it removes every excuse between you and daily conversational reps. That was always the hard part.
