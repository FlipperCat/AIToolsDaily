AI chatbots will produce a full trip itinerary in seconds. The problem is that a fast itinerary and a good one aren't the same thing. Raw AI travel plans tend to cram too much into each day, ignore travel time, and confidently recommend places that closed two years ago.

This tutorial walks through a workflow that fixes those problems. It works with any capable assistant, including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Perplexity, and takes about 30-45 minutes for a week-long trip.

## Step 1: Write a trip brief before you prompt

The quality of the itinerary depends on what you tell the AI. Before opening a chatbot, write down:

- **Dates and arrival/departure times** (a 9 p.m. arrival means day one is basically gone).
- **Who's traveling**, including ages, mobility needs, and dietary restrictions.
- **Budget**, as a rough daily figure or total.
- **Pace**: relaxed, moderate, or packed.
- **Interests**, ranked. "Food first, museums second, no shopping" beats a list of ten equal interests.
- **Must-dos and hard no's.**
- **Where you're staying**, if you've already booked.

Paste this brief at the top of your first message. If your assistant supports projects or saved instructions, store it there so you don't need to repeat it.

## Step 2: Ask for a skeleton, not a full itinerary

Don't ask for "a 7-day itinerary for Lisbon" yet. Ask for structure first:

> Using my trip brief, propose a day-by-day skeleton. For each day, give a theme or neighborhood focus, one or two anchor activities, and a rough morning/afternoon/evening split. Keep travel between areas to a minimum. Don't recommend specific restaurants yet.

A skeleton is easy to review and change. You'll quickly spot problems like a day trip scheduled on your arrival day, or two neighborhoods on opposite sides of the city on the same afternoon.

Push back as needed: "Move the day trip to day 4," or "Day 2 is too busy; drop one anchor activity."

## Step 3: Fill in the details one day at a time

Once the skeleton works, expand each day separately. One day per request produces better detail than asking for everything at once:

> Expand day 3. Suggest specific stops in order, with estimated time at each and travel time between them (walking or transit). Include one lunch and one dinner option near where I'll be at that time, in my budget. Flag anything that typically needs advance booking.

Asking for travel time between stops is the single most useful instruction here. It forces the model to account for how long it takes to get around, which is where most AI itineraries fall apart.

## Step 4: Verify everything that can change

This is the step people skip, and it's the one that matters most. AI models can be wrong or out of date on:

- Opening hours and closing days (many museums close one weekday).
- Prices and ticket requirements.
- Whether a restaurant or shop still exists.
- Seasonal closures, renovations, and holidays.
- Transit routes and schedules.

Use a tool with live web access for this. Perplexity is good here because it shows sources for each claim. Our [Perplexity review](/reviews/perplexity-review/) covers its strengths for research. A prompt that works well:

> Check these 8 places for my visit on [dates]: confirm opening hours on that day, whether advance tickets are needed, and current approximate prices. Cite the official site where possible and flag anything you can't confirm.

Then click through to the official sites for anything you plan to book or build a day around. AI-cited sources are a starting point, not proof.

## Step 5: Build the budget

Ask the assistant to turn the itinerary into a rough cost estimate:

> Estimate daily costs for this itinerary for two people: attractions, food at the price level I described, and local transport. Show a low and high range per day, and list the assumptions you made.

Treat the result as a sanity check, not a quote. The ranges are useful for spotting a day that's way over budget so you can swap an expensive activity for a cheaper one.

## Step 6: Stress-test the plan

Before you're done, ask the AI to critique its own work:

> Review this itinerary as a skeptical travel planner. Where is it unrealistic? Which days are overpacked? What happens if it rains on day 2 or 5? Suggest a backup plan for each outdoor-heavy day.

This step catches issues such as back-to-back early mornings, no rest after a long flight, and outdoor plans with no rain backup.

## Step 7: Export it somewhere usable

A chat thread is a bad place to keep a travel plan. Ask for the final version in a format you'll actually use:

- A **table** with day, time, place, address, and booking status to paste into a spreadsheet or notes app.
- A **checklist** of everything that needs booking, sorted by deadline.
- A short **daily summary** you can read on your phone each morning.

Add confirmation numbers and links yourself once you book.

## Which tool to use for which step

| Step | Best fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skeleton and pacing | ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini | Strong at structure and following constraints |
| Day-by-day details | Any general assistant with search on | Needs both planning and recent info |
| Fact-checking | Perplexity or search-enabled chatbot | Sources you can click and verify |
| Maps and travel times | Gemini or a maps app | Tight integration with map data |
| Final document | Any assistant | Formatting is easy for all of them |

## Tips that make a real difference

- **Give the AI your hotel location early.** Everything else should be planned outward from where you sleep.
- **Ask for "local" alternatives** to famous spots. You'll get fewer crowds, though you'll need to verify them more carefully.
- **Plan one flexible half-day** for every three or four days. Real trips need slack.
- **Keep one chat or project per trip** so context doesn't get mixed between plans.
- **Save the good prompts.** The same workflow works for your next trip.

## Common pitfalls

1. **Trusting hours and prices without checking.** This is the most common way AI itineraries go wrong.
2. **Overpacked days.** Models default to ambitious plans. Set a hard limit on activities per day.
3. **Ignoring geography.** Without travel-time instructions, you'll zigzag across the city.
4. **Invented places.** Occasionally a model will recommend a restaurant or viewpoint that doesn't exist. If you can't find it on a map, drop it.
5. **Sharing too much personal data.** You don't need to paste passport numbers or full booking confirmations into a chatbot to plan a trip.

## The bottom line

AI is very good at the tedious parts of trip planning: grouping sights, pacing days, estimating budgets, and formatting a plan you can use. It's unreliable on facts that change. Let it build the plan, verify the details yourself, and you'll get a better itinerary in under an hour.
