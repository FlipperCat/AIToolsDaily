A sharp headshot still does real work in 2023 — on LinkedIn, on a team page, in a speaker bio. The old options were a studio session that costs a few hundred dollars and eats an afternoon, or a cropped phone selfie that looks like a cropped phone selfie. AI headshot tools opened a third lane: upload a handful of selfies, pay a small fee, and get back dozens of studio-style portraits in a few hours.

They're not magic, and the output needs curation. But done right, the result is good enough for professional use. Here's the full workflow — tool choice, input photos, generation, and the editing that separates a usable headshot from an obvious AI miss.

## 1. Pick the right tool

Several services do this well in 2023, and they fall into two camps.

- **Dedicated headshot generators** like HeadshotPro, Aragon AI, and Try It On AI are purpose-built. You upload selfies, they fine-tune a model on your face, and they return business-style portraits with professional backgrounds and attire. These give the most consistent professional results. See our [HeadshotPro review](/reviews/headshotpro-review-2023/) for a deeper look at one of the better options.
- **General avatar and art apps** like [Lensa's Magic Avatars](/reviews/lensa-ai-review-2023/) or a tuned [Leonardo.Ai](/reviews/leonardo-ai-review-2023/) model are more creative and cheaper, but less reliable for straight corporate headshots. They're better when you want stylized profile images than when you need a believable boardroom portrait.

For a LinkedIn or team-page headshot, start with a dedicated generator. For a playful personal brand, the general tools are fine.

## 2. Gather the right input selfies

This is where most people fail, and it's the single biggest lever on quality. The model can only learn the face you show it.

**Aim for 10–20 photos with real variety:**

- Different **angles** — straight on, three-quarter, slight profile.
- Different **expressions** — neutral, slight smile, full smile.
- Different **lighting** — window light, indoor, outdoor shade.
- Different **backgrounds** and clothing.

**Avoid:**

- Sunglasses, hats, or anything covering your face.
- Heavy filters or beauty smoothing — the model learns the filter, not you.
- Other people in frame. Crop to just you.
- 15 near-identical shots from the same session, which make results stiff and repetitive.

Shoot fresh selfies specifically for this if you can. Ten deliberate photos beat twenty random camera-roll grabs.

## 3. Choose styles and settings

Once your photos upload, you'll pick the look of the output: background (office, studio gray, outdoor), attire (business casual, suit, blouse), and often a vibe or lighting style.

- **Match your industry.** A suit reads right for finance and law; business casual suits tech and startups. Don't pick attire that clashes with how your field actually dresses.
- **Order variety.** If the tool lets you request multiple styles, spread your allotment across two or three rather than spending it all on one. You want options to choose from.
- **Keep backgrounds simple.** Clean, slightly blurred backgrounds read as "professional." Busy or dramatic backgrounds date quickly and distract from your face.

## 4. Generate and wait

Dedicated tools train a small model on your face before generating, so expect a wait — typically one to two hours, sometimes more at peak times. You'll get back a batch, often 40–120 images depending on the plan.

Don't judge the tool by its worst outputs. Every batch contains duds. The job now is curation.

## 5. Curate ruthlessly

Set a high bar. From a batch of 100, you're looking for the three to five that are genuinely usable.

Work through them in two passes:

1. **First pass — likeness.** Reject anything that doesn't clearly look like you. Zoom in. AI often drifts on eye shape, face width, and jawline in ways that look fine as a thumbnail and wrong at full size.
2. **Second pass — professionalism.** From the ones that look like you, keep the ones with natural expressions, good lighting, and clean composition. Discard anything with the telltale AI flaws below.

## 6. Fix the flaws

Even your finalists usually need a light touch-up. Common 2023 AI artifacts to check for:

- **Hands and accessories.** If hands or glasses are in frame, inspect them closely — extra fingers and warped frames are classic giveaways. The safest headshots crop at the chest and avoid hands entirely.
- **Teeth and eyes.** Over-generated teeth and asymmetric eyes are common. A general editor's retouch tools, or a quick pass in a suite like [Fotor](/reviews/fotor-ai-review-2023/), can fix minor issues.
- **Over-smoothing.** AI skin often looks plastic. Dial back any extra smoothing so you keep natural texture.
- **Background oddities.** Check for warped doorframes, melted plants, or nonsense text on signage behind you.

A small crop, a slight color correction, and a texture fix are usually all a good finalist needs.

## Tips for better results

- **Run two batches** if the first disappoints. Swapping in better input photos the second time around often transforms the output.
- **Be honest about likeness.** A headshot that looks great but isn't quite you creates an awkward moment when you meet people in person. Prioritize accuracy over flattery.
- **Keep the originals.** Save the full batch; you may want a different shot later for a different context.

## Common pitfalls

- **Garbage in, garbage out.** Low-variety or filtered input selfies cap your results no matter how good the tool is.
- **Accepting the uncanny.** If a finalist feels slightly "off," others will feel it too. Trust that instinct and cut it.
- **Over-editing.** Heavy retouching swings back into fake territory. Light touch-ups only.
- **Ignoring the terms.** Check each tool's rights and data policy before uploading your face, especially for commercial use.

## The bottom line

AI headshots in 2023 are a legitimate option for a professional portrait, at a fraction of studio cost and time — if you treat generation as step one, not the finish line. Feed the model varied, honest selfies; generate a large batch; curate down to a handful; and fix the small artifacts. Do that, and you'll walk away with a headshot that holds up on a profile page. Skip the curation, and you'll get exactly the uncanny results that give AI portraits their reputation.
