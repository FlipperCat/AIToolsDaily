## How to Write a Cover Letter with ChatGPT (2024)

Most cover letters written with ChatGPT are bad, and it is not the model's fault. People type "write a cover letter for a marketing manager job" and paste whatever comes back. The output is grammatically perfect, structurally sound, and completely interchangeable with ten thousand other letters the hiring manager has seen.

The fix is to treat ChatGPT as a fast, tireless drafting partner that knows nothing about you until you tell it. Give it real material, constrain it hard, and edit like you mean it. Here is the process I use, which takes about 20 minutes per application once you have done the setup.

If you are new to the tool, start with our [ChatGPT review](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) for the basics.

## Step 1: Gather your raw material before you open ChatGPT

The quality of the letter is decided here. Collect:

- **The job description**, pasted in full. Strip anything confidential.
- **Your resume**, as plain text.
- **Two or three specific stories** that match the role's top requirements. Each should have a situation, what you did, and a result. Numbers help: "cut onboarding time from three weeks to eight days" beats "improved onboarding".
- **One reason you want this company in particular.** A product you use, a recent launch, a mission that matches your background. If you cannot think of one, that is useful information too.
- **A sample of your own writing.** An email, a LinkedIn post, anything that sounds like you. Two or three paragraphs is enough.

Put all of this in a text file. You will reuse most of it across applications.

## Step 2: Set the frame with a system-style prompt

Start a new chat and paste something like this, filling in your material:

```
You are helping me write a cover letter. Follow these rules strictly:

- Maximum 280 words, three or four short paragraphs.
- Do not open with "I am writing to express my interest" or any variation.
- Do not use these words: passionate, dynamic, leverage, synergy, results-driven, team player, fast-paced, excited.
- Every claim about me must come from the resume or stories I provide. Do not invent achievements, tools, or numbers.
- Match the tone of my writing sample: direct, plain, no exclamation marks.
- End with one sentence about a specific next step, not a generic "I look forward to hearing from you."

Here is the job description:
[paste]

Here is my resume:
[paste]

Here are three stories I want to draw from:
[paste]

Here is a sample of how I write:
[paste]

Why I want this company specifically:
[paste]

Before drafting, list the three requirements from the job description you think matter most and which of my stories maps to each. Wait for me to confirm.
```

That last instruction matters. Making the model show its mapping first catches misreadings before they turn into a full draft. If it picks the wrong story or misses the requirement the posting repeats three times, correct it now.

If you use ChatGPT Plus, the custom instructions feature can hold the tone rules and writing sample permanently so you do not paste them every time.

## Step 3: Generate the first draft

Once the mapping looks right, say "Go ahead and draft it." Read the result once without editing. You are checking for three things:

1. Does the opening line say something only you could say? If it could belong to any applicant, it fails.
2. Does each paragraph point at one requirement from the posting and back it with one story?
3. Did it obey the banned-word list and the length cap? GPT-3.5 in particular will sneak "excited" back in.

## Step 4: Iterate with specific instructions

Do not ask for "another version." Ask for targeted changes, one or two per message:

- "The second paragraph makes two claims. Cut it to the onboarding story only and add the eight-day number."
- "Rewrite the opening line to reference their launch of [product] last month."
- "The closing is generic. Replace it with a sentence proposing a 20-minute call about the Q2 migration mentioned in the posting."
- "Shorten every sentence over 25 words."

Three or four rounds of this usually produces something close. If the model keeps drifting back to clichés, paste the rules again. Long chats lose track of early constraints.

## Step 5: Fact-check every claim

This is the step people skip and regret. Language models fill gaps with plausible fiction. Read the draft line by line and check:

- Every number matches your resume or your stories.
- Every tool, certification, or method named is one you actually used.
- The company details are real. ChatGPT's training data has a cutoff and it will confidently describe products or values that do not exist. Verify against the company's own site.
- The job title and hiring manager's name, if used, are spelled exactly as in the posting.

One fabricated detail, caught in an interview, is worse than no cover letter at all.

## Step 6: Edit it into your own voice

Open the draft in a text editor and rewrite at least the first and last sentences yourself. Read the whole thing aloud. Anywhere you would not say it out loud, change it. Contractions are fine. A slightly imperfect sentence that sounds like you beats a polished one that sounds like a press release.

Keep it under a page and save as PDF unless the application system asks for plain text.

## Tips that make a real difference

- **Write the "why this company" section yourself if you can.** It is the part hiring managers read most closely and the part ChatGPT is worst at, because it has no genuine reason to want the job.
- **Use the same chat for the resume tweak.** Once the model has the posting loaded, ask it which resume bullets to reorder or reword to mirror the job's language. Do not let it add anything you did not do.
- **Save your best prompt as a template.** Swap the job description and company reason per application. The rules, resume, and stories stay the same.
- **Pair it with interview prep.** The stories you gathered in Step 1 are exactly what you need for behavioural questions. Our guide to [practising job interviews with ChatGPT](/tutorials/practice-job-interviews-with-chatgpt/) picks up from here.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Keyword stuffing.** Mirroring the posting's language is smart; repeating "cross-functional stakeholder alignment" four times is not. Applicant tracking systems mostly scan the resume, not the letter.
- **Letting it write about skills you do not have.** If the posting wants SQL and you have never used it, the letter should not imply otherwise. Ask ChatGPT to frame adjacent experience honestly instead.
- **Over-length.** Anything past 300 words is skimmed at best. The model will pad if you let it.
- **Sending the first draft.** Every cover letter that "sounds like AI" is a first draft someone did not edit.
- **Pasting sensitive information.** Keep your address, references, and anything under NDA out of the chat.

## A quick before-and-after

A typical first-attempt opener: "I am writing to express my strong interest in the Marketing Manager position at Acme. As a passionate, results-driven professional with over five years of experience..."

The same opener after this process: "Acme's switch to usage-based pricing last quarter is the kind of change I spent 2023 messaging at Beacon, where I ran the campaign that moved 40 percent of legacy accounts onto the new plan in four months."

Same applicant, same model, different inputs. That gap is the whole point of doing the setup work.
