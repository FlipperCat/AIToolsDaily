## How to Write a Wedding Speech with ChatGPT (2023)

Being asked to give a wedding speech is an honor that arrives with a deadline and a blank page. Most people know what they *feel* about the couple. The hard part is turning that into three minutes that are warm, a little funny, and don't ramble.

ChatGPT is surprisingly good at the parts that trip people up: getting started, finding a structure, and cutting a bloated draft down to size. It's bad at the part that matters most, which is knowing anything about the people getting married. This guide shows how to split the work so the speech ends up sounding like you, not like a greeting card.

If you're new to the tool, start with our [plain-English ChatGPT explainer](/what-is-chatgpt-everything-you-need-to-know-2023/). The free version is all you need here.

## Step 1: Brain-Dump Your Raw Material First

Do this before you open ChatGPT. Spend 15 minutes writing down, in any order and with no polish:

- How you met the person you're speaking for
- Two or three specific stories that show who they are
- When you first met their partner, and your first impression
- A moment you saw them together and thought "this is right"
- Anything the couple says, does, or jokes about that guests will recognize
- What you hope for them

Specific beats general every time. "She's really kind" is forgettable. "She drove four hours to help me move and then refused gas money" is a speech.

This list is the only thing ChatGPT can't produce for you, and it's what makes the speech yours.

## Step 2: Tell ChatGPT Who You Are and Who's Listening

Context changes everything about the output. Start your conversation with something like:

```
I'm writing a best man speech for my brother Sam's wedding to Leah.
I've known Sam my whole life; I met Leah four years ago.
The audience is about 120 people: family, college friends, and some
older relatives. The tone should be warm with some light humor, nothing
embarrassing or crude. Target length is about 3-4 minutes spoken.
Don't write the speech yet. First, ask me any questions you need.
```

The final line matters. Asking ChatGPT to interview you first usually surfaces details you forgot, and it stops the model from immediately producing a generic draft you'll then struggle to escape.

## Step 3: Get a Structure, Not a Script

Paste your brain dump and ask for an outline:

```
Here are my notes. Suggest 2-3 possible structures for the speech.
For each, list the sections in order and which of my stories goes where.
Don't write full sentences yet.
```

A classic structure that ChatGPT will often suggest, and that works:

1. **Opening**: who you are and how you know the couple (one or two sentences)
2. **A story about the person you're speaking for**: one that shows character
3. **Meeting the partner**: your first impression, and how they changed things
4. **The couple together**: the moment you knew
5. **Toast**: a short wish and raised glasses

Pick the structure that fits your strongest story. Your best anecdote should get the most room.

## Step 4: Write the First Draft Yourself, Roughly

This step is optional but recommended. Write a messy version yourself following the outline. Don't worry about wording. It's much easier for ChatGPT to improve *your* draft than for you to make *its* draft sound like you.

If you're truly stuck, ask ChatGPT to draft one section at a time from your notes, then rewrite each section in your own words before moving on.

## Step 5: Use ChatGPT as an Editor

Now paste your draft and give targeted instructions. Useful requests:

- "Cut this to about 550 words without losing any of the stories."
- "The transition between the college story and meeting Leah feels abrupt. Suggest three options."
- "Make the opening line stronger. Give me five alternatives in a conversational tone."
- "Flag any sentence that sounds like a cliché."
- "Which line here is most likely to get a laugh? How could the setup be tighter?"

Ask for several options rather than one rewrite. You're choosing, not outsourcing. The same approach works for other personal writing: our guide to [writing a LinkedIn profile with ChatGPT](/tutorials/write-a-linkedin-profile-with-chatgpt-2023/) uses a similar draft-then-edit loop.

## Step 6: Read It Aloud and Fix What Trips You

Wedding speeches are heard, not read. Read your draft aloud at a natural pace and time it. Mark every spot where you stumble or run out of breath.

Then go back to ChatGPT:

```
These sentences are hard to say out loud: [paste]. Rewrite them to be
easier to speak, with shorter sentences and natural pauses.
```

Spoken language is simpler than written language. Long sentences with clauses that read fine on paper become tongue-twisters with a microphone in your hand and a drink in the other.

## Tips for a Better Speech

**Give it real names and details.** "My brother" produces generic text. "Sam, who once got lost in his own neighborhood" gives ChatGPT something to work with.

**Ask about the audience.** "Is there anything here that might make older relatives uncomfortable?" is a useful sanity check, especially for inside jokes.

**Keep a "no" list.** Tell ChatGPT up front what's off-limits: exes, embarrassing stories, anything the couple wouldn't want shared.

**Save your best line for the toast.** Ask for a few closing wish options, then pick the one that sounds most like something you'd actually say.

## Common Pitfalls

**Accepting the first draft.** ChatGPT's default wedding speech is full of phrases like "two souls," "a journey of love," and "here's to a lifetime of happiness." Guests have heard them all. If a line could appear in any wedding speech, cut it or replace it with a specific detail.

**Letting it invent stories.** If you ask for a speech without giving real anecdotes, ChatGPT will make plausible ones up. Never deliver a story that didn't happen. Someone in the room will know.

**Making it about you.** A common draft problem is too much setup about the speaker. Ask ChatGPT, "Is this speech focused enough on the couple?"

**Going long.** Every extra minute costs goodwill. If your timed read runs past five minutes, ask ChatGPT what to cut, then cut more.

**Pasting private details carelessly.** ChatGPT conversations may be reviewed to improve the service. Use first names and skip anything genuinely sensitive, like health issues or family conflicts.

## The Bottom Line

ChatGPT is a good speechwriting partner if you treat it like an editor with no memories of its own. Bring the stories and the feeling; let it help with structure, transitions, and trimming. Done this way, a speech that might take a week of stalling can come together in an evening, and still sound like it came from you.
