Mock interviews are the single highest-leverage way to prepare for a job interview, and most people do zero of them because recruiting a practice partner is awkward. ChatGPT solves the awkward part. It will play a tough interviewer for as long as you want, and — unlike a friend — it will tell you bluntly when your answer rambles.

Here's the exact workflow. If you're brand new to the tool itself, skim our [ChatGPT review](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) or our [getting started guide](/getting-started-with-chatgpt-2023/) first.

## Step 1: Feed it the job description

Don't start with "ask me interview questions." Generic input gets generic questions. Instead, paste the actual job posting:

> You are helping me prepare for a job interview. Here is the job description: [paste it]. Here is a summary of my background: [2–3 sentences on your experience]. First, list the 8 questions this interviewer is most likely to ask, and note which part of the job description each one maps to.

That last clause matters — seeing *why* each question is likely teaches you to read job postings the way interviewers do.

**Tip:** Strip your name, address, and current employer from anything you paste. It works just as well anonymized, and it's good hygiene not to feed personal details into any AI tool.

## Step 2: Set up the mock interview

Now put ChatGPT in character:

> Act as the hiring manager for this role. Conduct a realistic mock interview with me. Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before continuing. After each of my answers, give brief feedback: one thing that worked, one thing to improve, then ask the next question. Be a fair but demanding interviewer — do not go easy on me.

The "one question at a time" instruction is essential. Without it, ChatGPT dumps ten questions in a single message and the role-play collapses.

## Step 3: Drill the behavioral round with STAR feedback

Behavioral questions ("tell me about a time you...") are where most candidates lose offers, and where ChatGPT's feedback is strongest. After answering, ask for a structured critique:

> Evaluate my answer against the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Which component was weakest? Rewrite my answer in tighter STAR form, keeping my facts but cutting it to under 90 seconds of speaking time.

Then — and this is the part people skip — say the improved answer out loud, and type it again from memory. You're building recall, not collecting scripts.

## Step 4: Run the role-specific round

For technical or specialized roles, run a second session focused on craft:

- **Developers:** "Ask me the kind of system design and debugging questions a mid-level backend engineer would face. Probe my answers with follow-ups."
- **Marketers:** "Ask me to walk through a campaign I'd run for [product], then challenge my channel choices and budget."
- **Analysts:** "Give me a business scenario and ask how I'd structure the analysis. Push back on my assumptions."

ChatGPT's follow-up questions are the real value here. A static list of questions can't interrogate your answer; this can.

## Step 5: Ask for a scorecard

At the end of a session, request an overall assessment:

> Based on this entire mock interview, score me 1–10 on: clarity, structure, specificity, and confidence of language. List my three most repeated weaknesses and one drill for each.

Patterns across sessions matter more than any single score. If "answers lack measurable results" shows up three times, you've found your prep priority.

## Step 6: Flip the table

Interviews end with "any questions for us?" — and weak questions there undo a strong hour. Prompt:

> Suggest 8 insightful questions I could ask the interviewer for this role — questions that signal seniority and genuine interest. Avoid anything answerable from the company website.

Pick two or three that you actually care about. You can also rehearse salary negotiation the same way: "Play a recruiter offering me $X. I want $Y. Negotiate with me."

## Common pitfalls

- **Memorizing scripts.** If you recite a polished ChatGPT-written answer verbatim, interviewers can tell. Use its rewrites to learn the *shape* of a good answer, then say it in your own words.
- **Trusting invented company facts.** ChatGPT will fabricate plausible details about companies, teams, and products. Verify everything independently.
- **Letting it be too nice.** By default ChatGPT drifts toward flattery. Re-instruct it: "Be harsher. Assume I'm competing against strong candidates."
- **Only practicing in text.** Typing answers builds structure, but interviews are spoken. For your final sessions, say answers aloud before typing a condensed version.

## Quick prompt library

Copy-paste starters for common situations:

- **Phone screen:** "Act as a recruiter doing a 20-minute phone screen for this role. Focus on my background, motivation, and salary expectations. One question at a time."
- **Career-change story:** "I'm moving from [field A] to [field B]. Help me build a 60-second answer to 'why the switch?' that frames my old experience as an asset."
- **Weakness question:** "Interview me only on 'what's your greatest weakness?' Reject any answer that sounds like a humble-brag and make me redo it until it's honest and specific."
- **Panel simulation:** "Play three interviewers with different priorities — a skeptical technical lead, a culture-focused HR manager, and a results-obsessed VP. Rotate who asks each question."

## The bottom line

Two or three focused sessions with this workflow — likely questions, a strict mock interview, STAR drills, and a scorecard — will put you ahead of the majority of candidates, who walk in having rehearsed nothing. The tool's writing ability is a side benefit here; what you're really buying is repetition.
