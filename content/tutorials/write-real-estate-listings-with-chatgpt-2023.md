Listing descriptions are the classic real estate time sink: high stakes, tight word limits, and the fifth one you write in a week always comes out flatter than the first. ChatGPT is genuinely good at this job — if you feed it real details and edit like a professional. Here is the workflow, refined over a few dozen test listings.

If you are brand new to the tool itself, start with our [plain-English explainer](/what-is-chatgpt-explained-2023/) and [full ChatGPT review](/reviews/chatgpt-review/), then come back.

## 1. Gather the raw facts first

ChatGPT's output is only as good as your input. Before you open the chat window, collect:

- Address basics: beds, baths, square footage, lot size, year built
- Three to five genuine standout features (chef's kitchen, new roof 2022, walkable to the waterfront)
- Recent upgrades with dates
- Neighborhood facts you can verify
- Target listing length and where it is going (MLS, Zillow, brochure, Instagram)

This step is non-negotiable. Given a vague prompt, ChatGPT invents amenities — and an invented "recently updated HVAC" in a published listing is your problem, not the AI's.

## 2. Use a structured prompt template

Paste something like this, filling in your facts:

```
You are an experienced real estate copywriter. Write a listing
description for the property below.

Facts (use ONLY these — do not add features I have not listed):
- 4 bed / 2.5 bath, 2,150 sq ft colonial, built 1994
- Renovated kitchen (2021): quartz counters, gas range
- Fenced quarter-acre lot, mature oak trees
- New furnace 2022; two-car garage
- 10-minute walk to Main Street shops

Requirements:
- 150-175 words, MLS-appropriate
- Warm but professional tone, no clichés like "must see"
- Lead with the strongest feature, end with a call to action
- Describe the property only — never the ideal buyer or neighbors
```

The two instructions that do the heavy lifting: **"use ONLY these facts"** (curbs invention) and **"describe the property only"** (your first line of fair-housing defense).

## 3. Generate three versions, not one

Ask ChatGPT to "give me three distinct versions: one leading with the kitchen, one leading with the location, one leading with the outdoor space." Picking the best opening from three options takes thirty seconds and beats trying to perfect a single draft through endless regeneration. This works for the same reason it works in [blog writing](/tutorials/02-write-blog-posts-chatgpt/): the model's first idea is rarely its best.

## 4. Run the fair housing pass

This is the step that separates professionals from lawsuits. Read the draft and cut anything that describes *people* rather than *property*:

- "Perfect for a growing family" → describe the four bedrooms instead
- "Safe neighborhood" → name a verifiable feature, like the park two blocks away
- "Walking distance to St. Mary's" → mention the neighborhood, not the church
- Anything about who "will love" the home → delete it

ChatGPT sometimes produces these phrases even when told not to, because they saturate its training data. You are the compliance layer — and when in doubt, ask your broker.

## 5. Fact-check line by line

Compare the draft against your notes from step 1. Verify every number, every date, every claim. ChatGPT rounds, embellishes, and occasionally swaps details between drafts in the same conversation. Two minutes of checking here protects your license.

## 6. Resize for each channel

Once the master version is approved, resizing is where ChatGPT really pays off. In the same conversation:

- "Condense this to 80 words for the MLS remarks field."
- "Write a 2-sentence teaser for an Instagram caption with no hashtags."
- "Turn this into 5 short bullet points for a flyer."
- "Write a friendly email to my buyer list announcing this listing." (Our [ChatGPT email guide](/tutorials/how-to-use-chatgpt-email-writing/) covers that workflow in depth.)

Because the conversation already contains the approved facts, every variant stays consistent.

## Tips that make a real difference

- **Show it your voice.** Paste two of your own past listings and say "match this style." The output stops sounding like a template.
- **Ban the clichés explicitly.** Add "avoid: must see, won't last, oozes charm, entertainer's dream" to your prompt. Appraise the results; expand the ban list over time.
- **Save your template.** Keep the master prompt in a note and reuse it. Consistency of input produces consistency of output.
- **GPT-4 is better at restraint.** If you have Plus, GPT-4 follows the "only these facts" instruction more faithfully than the free model. Worth it in busy season.

## Pitfalls to avoid

1. **Publishing without editing.** AI text has tells — uniform sentence rhythm, empty superlatives. Your edit is what makes it yours.
2. **Letting it guess the neighborhood.** ChatGPT's local knowledge is spotty and outdated. Every area claim comes from you.
3. **One giant prompt for everything.** Draft first, then refine in follow-up messages. Iteration beats mega-prompts.
4. **Forgetting it's a draft tool.** The description sells the showing; the agent sells the house. ChatGPT saves you twenty minutes per listing — it does not replace your judgment about what makes a property move.

Done right, this workflow turns a 45-minute chore into a 10-minute review task, with copy that is often sharper than what most of us write on deadline. That is the honest pitch: not magic, just leverage.
