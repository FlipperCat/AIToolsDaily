Suno has crossed the line from novelty to genuinely usable. With the v4 model, vocals sound less robotic, arrangements hold together, and audio quality is clean enough for background tracks, podcast intros, and — if you're honest with your audience — even releasable songs.

But most people's first Suno track sounds generic, because most people type "a pop song about summer" and take whatever comes out. The gap between that and a track you'd actually use is technique. This tutorial walks the full workflow: prompt, structure, iterate, extend, export.

We reviewed Suno in depth [here](/reviews/suno-ai-review-2024/) if you want the tool overview first; this guide is purely hands-on.

## Step 1: Set Up and Understand Credits

Create an account at suno.com. As of March 2025, the free tier gives you a daily credit allowance — enough for a handful of song generations per day — but no commercial rights. Pro (around $10/month) and Premier (around $30/month) add more credits, priority generation, and commercial use. Prices are approximate; check the current pricing page.

One credit rule that shapes the whole workflow: every generation gives you **two versions** of the song. Always listen to both — they often differ dramatically, and the second take is the keeper more often than you'd expect.

## Step 2: Skip Simple Mode, Use Custom Mode

Simple mode ("describe a song") is fine for a laugh. For real results, toggle **Custom Mode**. It splits the input into two fields, and that split is where all your control lives:

- **Style of Music** — genre, mood, instrumentation, vocal type, era, tempo
- **Lyrics** — your own words, or leave it to Suno per section

Writing your own style description instead of a one-liner is the single biggest quality upgrade in Suno.

## Step 3: Write a Style Prompt That Actually Works

Vague prompts produce the genre's most average possible song. Specific prompts produce character. Compare:

**Weak:** `sad pop song`

**Strong:** `melancholic indie pop, female vocal, breathy and close-mic'd, sparse piano and warm sub bass, slow build to an anthemic final chorus, 80 BPM`

The formula that consistently works:

1. **Genre + subgenre** — "indie pop," "90s boom bap," "melodic techno"
2. **Vocal description** — male/female, raspy/breathy/powerful, or "instrumental"
3. **Two or three instruments** you want front and center
4. **Mood and dynamics** — "slow build," "driving," "stripped back"
5. **Tempo** — a BPM number or "slow/mid-tempo/fast"

Avoid naming real artists — Suno blocks artist names in prompts. Describe the *sound* instead: "stadium rock with layered harmonies" gets you where the band name won't.

## Step 4: Structure Your Lyrics with Metatags

This is the most underused Suno feature. In the lyrics box, use square-bracket tags to tell Suno what each section is:

```
[Verse 1]
Cold coffee on the windowsill
Your name still in my phone

[Chorus]
But I'm not calling, not tonight
I'm learning how to hold my own

[Bridge]
(whispered) maybe someday, maybe not

[Outro]
```

Suno reads these tags and arranges the music accordingly — choruses lift, bridges shift, outros resolve. You can also use performance hints like `[Guitar Solo]`, `[Drop]`, `(harmonies)`, or `(whispered)`. They're suggestions, not commands — the model obeys them maybe three times out of four — but structured lyrics reliably beat a wall of unformatted text.

Writing lyrics is also where you add the most human value. AI-generated lyrics trend cliché; even ten minutes of your own editing gives the song a point of view.

## Step 5: Iterate Like It's Free (Because It Almost Is)

Your first generation is a draft, not a verdict. The professional workflow is volume:

1. Generate 3–5 runs of the same song (6–10 versions total)
2. Shortlist the one or two with the best *vocal performance and chorus* — those are hardest to fix later
3. Tweak the style prompt between runs, changing **one variable at a time** so you learn what each phrase does

If a version is close but flawed, use **Remaster** or regenerate with a small prompt nudge rather than starting over. And if you've built a voice you like, the **Personas** feature lets you carry that vocal character into new songs — invaluable for a podcast with a consistent sonic identity.

## Step 6: Extend to Full Length

Single generations cap out around the two-minute mark. To build a full song:

1. Open your best version and choose **Extend**
2. Add the next lyric section (tagged, as always) — Suno continues from where the track left off
3. Repeat until the song is structurally complete
4. Use **Get Whole Song** to stitch the segments into one continuous track

Tip: extend from a natural boundary (end of a chorus, not mid-word). Transitions are usually seamless, but occasionally the energy dips at the seam — if it does, re-roll the extension rather than accepting it.

## Step 7: Export and Publish

Download as MP3 (or WAV on paid tiers, which you want for any further editing). Before publishing anywhere:

- **Check your rights tier.** Free-tier songs are non-commercial. Monetized YouTube videos count as commercial.
- **Disclose AI use** where platforms require it — streaming services and YouTube have been tightening disclosure rules.
- **Do a final level pass** in any audio editor; AI tracks sometimes come out quiet relative to commercial music.

## Common Pitfalls

- **One-word style prompts.** You'll get the genre's stock photo equivalent.
- **Ignoring the second version** of each generation. It's the better take half the time.
- **Accepting the first decent result.** Version 7 is usually meaningfully better than version 1, and credits are cheap.
- **Fighting the model on niche genres.** Suno is strongest in mainstream styles; for experimental genres, expect more re-rolls — or try Udio, which trades ease of use for finer control (our [Suno vs Udio comparison](/compare/suno-vs-udio/) breaks down when each wins).

## Wrap-Up

The recipe: Custom Mode, a five-part style prompt, metatagged lyrics you've actually edited, ruthless iteration, then extend and stitch. That workflow turns Suno from a toy that makes funny birthday songs into a legitimate production tool for creators.
