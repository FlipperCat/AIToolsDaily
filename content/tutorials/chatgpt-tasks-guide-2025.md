ChatGPT has always had one glaring limitation as an assistant: it only works when you're actively talking to it. **Tasks**, which OpenAI began rolling out in beta this January, changes that. You can now schedule prompts to run automatically — once or on a repeating schedule — and get notified when results are ready.

It's ChatGPT's first real step from chatbot toward something that acts on your behalf. After a few weeks of daily use, here's exactly how to set it up, what it's genuinely good for, and where the beta still stumbles.

## What you need before starting

- A **ChatGPT Plus, Pro, or Team subscription** — Tasks is not on the free tier as of February 2025.
- The **right model selected**: when creating a task, pick the GPT-4o variant labeled for scheduled tasks in the model dropdown. Tasks won't trigger from other models.
- The **mobile app installed** (optional but recommended) so push notifications reach you when tasks complete.

Note the beta limit: **10 active tasks maximum**. Plan accordingly — this guide includes prioritization tips below.

## Step 1: Create your first task

1. Open ChatGPT and select the tasks-enabled GPT-4o model from the model picker.
2. Describe what you want and when, in plain language. For example: *"Every weekday at 7:30 AM, give me a briefing with today's weather in Chicago, my three most important productivity tips for deep work, and one piece of tech news I should know about."*
3. ChatGPT will confirm the schedule — check the time and recurrence it repeats back, because natural-language parsing occasionally misreads things like "every other Friday."
4. The task now appears in the **Tasks** section (look under your profile menu → Tasks) where you can edit, pause, or delete it.

You can also create tasks directly from that Tasks page with an explicit name, instructions, and schedule — better for precision; the conversational route is better for speed.

## Step 2: Set up a daily briefing (the killer use case)

The single most useful task we've built is a morning briefing. The trick is being ruthlessly specific about format:

> "Every day at 7 AM: (1) One-paragraph summary of major AI industry news from the last 24 hours. (2) Three headlines with one-sentence summaries each. (3) End with one actionable insight for someone who runs a small web design business. Keep the whole thing under 250 words."

Without the word cap and numbered structure, briefings balloon into essays you'll stop reading by day three. With them, it's a 60-second read with coffee.

Since tasks can use ChatGPT's browsing capability, news-based briefings pull reasonably current information — though always treat specifics as "verify before acting," the same rule we apply in our [guide to using AI for research](/how-to-use-ai-for-research/).

## Step 3: Build recurring work tasks

Beyond briefings, the patterns that have stuck for us:

**Weekly content ideas.** *"Every Monday at 9 AM, generate five blog post ideas about AI tools for small businesses, each with a working title and two-sentence angle."* Feed the winners into your normal workflow — pairs well with our [content calendar automation guide](/tutorials/26-automate-content-calendar-ai/).

**Practice and learning.** *"Every day at 6 PM, give me one Spanish conversation exercise at intermediate level, then include the answers to yesterday's exercise."* Language drills, coding questions, and interview prep all fit this shape well.

**Deadline reminders with context.** Unlike a dumb calendar ping, a task reminder can include the thing itself: *"On the last day of each month at 4 PM, remind me to send invoices and draft a friendly invoice email template I can adapt."*

**One-time future prompts.** Tasks don't have to repeat. *"Next Friday at 2 PM, remind me to review the quarterly numbers and list the five questions I should ask about them."*

## Step 4: Manage notifications and results

Each run posts its output into a dedicated conversation thread, and you can enable push and/or email notifications per your settings. Three tips:

- **Turn on push for time-sensitive tasks only.** Ten daily notifications train you to ignore all of them.
- **Check the thread even without a notification** — during the beta we've seen occasional notification misses while the task itself ran fine.
- **Review outputs weekly.** Recurring tasks drift; a prompt that produced sharp briefings in week one can get repetitive by week four. Edit the task instructions when quality dips — adding "avoid repeating suggestions from previous days" helps noticeably.

## Step 5: Work within the 10-task limit

The cap forces prioritization, which is honestly healthy. Our recommended split:

- 1–2 daily information tasks (briefing, industry news)
- 2–3 weekly work tasks (content ideas, review prompts)
- 1–2 learning tasks
- Keep 2–3 slots free for one-time reminders

Pause seasonal tasks instead of deleting them — paused tasks keep their instructions for later.

## Common pitfalls

**Expecting actions, not text.** Tasks *generates and delivers text on a schedule*. It cannot send emails on your behalf, post to social media, update spreadsheets, or touch other apps. For automation that acts across your tools, you still need a workflow platform — see our [Zapier AI automation guide](/zapier-ai-automation-guide-2025/) for that layer.

**Vague schedules.** "Every morning" becomes a specific time you didn't choose. Always state exact times, and spot-check the first run — time zones have tripped us twice.

**Set-and-forget syndrome.** An unreviewed recurring task quietly degrades into noise. Calendar a monthly five-minute task audit.

**Trusting news specifics blindly.** Briefing tasks summarize what browsing finds; treat names, numbers, and dates as leads to verify, not facts to repeat.

## Is Tasks worth it?

As a beta, Tasks is more "promising" than "polished" — the 10-task cap, occasional notification hiccups, and text-only outputs keep it short of a true autonomous assistant. But the daily-briefing and scheduled-prompt patterns alone have earned it a permanent place in our routine, and it clearly signals where ChatGPT is heading in 2025: away from a chat window you visit, toward an agent that comes to you.
