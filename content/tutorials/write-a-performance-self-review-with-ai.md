It is review season again, and you have a form with five empty boxes and a deadline on Friday. You know you did good work this year. The problem is remembering what it was, proving it, and writing about yourself without sounding either arrogant or forgettable.

AI assistants are good at this job, as long as you use them for the right part of it. They will not remember your year for you. But they are excellent at turning a messy pile of evidence into clear, specific writing that maps to what your company actually evaluates.

This guide works with any capable assistant, such as [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review-2026-is-it-still-the-best-ai-tool/), Claude, Gemini, or your company's approved enterprise tool.

## Before you start: check the rules

Spend two minutes on this first.

- **Read your company's AI policy.** Many employers allow AI for writing help but restrict which tools can see internal information.
- **Use an approved tool for confidential details.** Revenue figures, customer names, unreleased products, and HR matters should only go into tools your employer has approved.
- **Anonymize when unsure.** "Enterprise customer in healthcare" works just as well as the real company name for drafting purposes.

## Step 1: Gather raw evidence (no AI yet)

This is the step that makes or breaks the review, and most people skip it.

Spend 30 to 60 minutes collecting everything you did since your last review. Do not write sentences yet. Just collect.

Good sources:
- **Your calendar:** Recurring meetings you led, launches, workshops, and interviews you ran.
- **Project tools:** Closed tickets, shipped features, completed tasks.
- **Sent email and chat:** Search for "thanks," "great work," "shipped," and "launched." Screenshot or copy praise from colleagues.
- **Docs you wrote:** Proposals, plans, postmortems, and guides.
- **Metrics dashboards:** Anything that moved because of your work.
- **Last year's goals:** You need these to show progress.

Dump it all into a single document as rough bullet points. Messy is fine.

## Step 2: Let the AI interview you

Your evidence list will have gaps, especially around impact. Instead of asking the AI to write anything yet, have it ask you questions.

```
I'm writing my annual performance self-review. Below are rough notes about
my work this year. Act as an experienced manager preparing me for a review.
Ask me up to 10 questions, one at a time, that would help me surface:
- the measurable impact of this work
- work I've forgotten to list
- how my work helped my team or other teams

Do not write the review yet.

My role: [title, team, level]
My notes:
[paste evidence dump]
```

Answer each question honestly. This step usually turns up the most valuable material, like the process you fixed that saved the team hours every week, which you would never think to write down on your own.

## Step 3: Map evidence to what your company measures

Every company evaluates against something: a career ladder, competencies, values, or goals. Find that document and give it to the AI.

```
Here are the competencies my company uses for reviews at my level:
[paste competencies or ladder text]

Here is my evidence, including my answers to your questions:
[paste]

Organize my evidence under each competency. For each one, list the
strongest 2-3 examples. Flag any competency where my evidence is thin.
Do not invent examples or numbers. If something is missing, say so.
```

That last instruction matters. Assistants will otherwise fill gaps with plausible-sounding achievements you never had.

The flagged gaps are useful, too. They tell you where to dig for more evidence or what to raise as a growth area.

## Step 4: Draft each section in your voice

Now you can write. Give the AI a sample of your own writing so the draft doesn't read like a press release.

```
Write the "[section name]" section of my self-review using the organized
evidence above.

Rules:
- Use first person and plain language. Match the tone of my writing sample.
- Lead each point with the outcome, then explain what I did.
- Use specific numbers only where I provided them.
- Avoid filler phrases like "spearheaded," "drove alignment," "leveraged
  synergies," or "passionate about."
- Stay under [X] words or characters (the form limit).

My writing sample:
[paste a Slack message or email you wrote]
```

Do one section at a time. Separate prompts give you more control than one big "write my whole review" request.

**A good pattern for each accomplishment:** outcome, then action, then evidence. For example: "Cut onboarding time for new support agents from three weeks to ten days by rewriting our training docs and adding a shadowing schedule. Four of the last five hires said the docs were the most useful part of their first week."

## Step 5: Write the growth section honestly

This is where AI help is most useful and most risky.

```
Based on my evidence and the thin areas you flagged, help me write an
honest "areas for growth" section. Pick 1-2 real areas. For each, describe
the gap plainly, give one specific example, and propose a concrete plan for
next year with a way to measure progress. Don't make it sound like a
disguised strength.
```

Managers can tell when "I care too much about quality" is standing in for a real weakness. A clear, owned growth area with a plan builds more credibility than a flawless-sounding review.

## Step 6: Fact-check and de-fluff

Read the full draft against your evidence and check every claim.

- **Every number:** Does it match your source? Did the AI round or inflate anything?
- **Every "led" or "owned":** Did you actually lead it, or contribute to it? Overclaiming shared work is the fastest way to lose a manager's trust.
- **Every adjective:** Cut "significant," "critical," and "exceptional" unless the evidence earns them.
- **Tone:** Read it aloud. Does it sound like you?

## Step 7: Prepare for the conversation

The written review is half the process. Use the same chat to prepare for the meeting.

```
Based on my self-review, act as my manager. Ask me the 5 toughest
follow-up questions you'd likely ask, including at least one that pushes
back on my strongest claim. Then give me feedback on my answers.
```

## Tips for better results

- **Start a brag doc today.** Keep a running note of wins, praise, and metrics through the year. Next review season, Step 1 takes five minutes.
- **Use a project or workspace feature** if your assistant has one. Upload your ladder and evidence once, then draft every section in that context.
- **Ask for multiple versions.** "Give me a version for a reader who skims and one with more detail" helps when your form has strict limits.
- **Credit others.** Naming collaborators makes your review more credible, not less.

## Common pitfalls

- **Letting the AI invent impact.** If you didn't provide a number, it shouldn't appear in the draft.
- **Pasting confidential data into unapproved tools.** Check the policy first.
- **Writing a generic review.** "Consistently delivered high-quality work" says nothing. Specific examples say everything.
- **Over-polishing.** A review that reads like marketing copy invites skepticism. Plain and specific beats impressive and vague.
- **Skipping the evidence step.** AI can only polish what you give it. A thin evidence dump produces a polished but empty review.

Done well, this process takes about two hours and produces a review that is specific, honest, and actually reflects your year. The AI handles the structure and the wording. You supply the facts and make sure every claim holds up.
