Since ChatGPT opened to the public in November, "rewrite my resume" has become one of the most common things people ask it to do. That's a reasonable use. Most resumes fail for boring reasons: vague bullets, duty lists instead of results, and one generic version sent to every job. ChatGPT is good at fixing those kinds of problems.

It's also good at producing a polished resume that sounds like every other polished resume. It'll invent numbers if you let it. This guide shows how to use it as an editor and interviewer, not a ghostwriter. If you've never used the tool, read our [plain-English explainer on what ChatGPT is](/what-is-chatgpt-explained-2023/) and our [getting-started guide](/getting-started-with-chatgpt-2023/) first.

## What ChatGPT can and can't do for a resume

Know the limits before you start:

- **It doesn't know you.** Everything useful it writes comes from what you give it. Thin input gets you generic output.
- **Its knowledge stops in 2021.** It doesn't browse the web, so it can't look up a company, a job posting, or current hiring trends. Paste in what you want it to use.
- **It makes things up confidently.** Ask it to "add metrics" and it'll happily write "increased revenue by 35%" with no basis at all.
- **It forgets in long chats.** Very long conversations can lose earlier details. Start a fresh chat for each job you tailor to.

It's good at turning messy notes into clean bullets, restructuring sections, cutting wordiness, and comparing your resume against a job description.

## Step 1: Build a raw "brag document" first

Don't start with your old resume. Start with a plain list of what you actually did in each role. Write it fast and don't worry about wording:

- Projects you owned or contributed to
- Problems you fixed, and what happened afterward
- Numbers you actually know: team size, budget, volume, time saved, customers handled
- Tools and skills you used every week
- Praise from managers or clients, and any awards or promotions

Before pasting, remove your name, contact details, and anything confidential about your employer. Replace client names with descriptions like "a regional hospital network."

## Step 2: Let ChatGPT interview you

Most people undersell their own work. Ask ChatGPT to dig out the details:

> I'm updating my resume. Below are rough notes about my role as an operations coordinator. Ask me 8 specific questions, one at a time, that would help uncover measurable results and the scope of my responsibility. Don't write any resume content yet.

Answer each question honestly. "I don't know the exact number" is a fine answer; you can estimate conservatively later or leave the number out. This step usually brings up two or three achievements you'd forgotten.

## Step 3: Turn duties into achievement bullets

Now give it the notes and your answers, with firm rules:

> Using only the information I've provided, write 5 resume bullet points for this role. Rules: start each with a strong past-tense verb, include a result or scope where I've given one, keep each under 25 words, and do not invent any numbers, tools, or outcomes. If a bullet would be stronger with a metric I haven't provided, put [METRIC?] in brackets instead.

The `[METRIC?]` placeholder is the most useful part of this prompt. It shows you where a number would help without letting ChatGPT make one up.

A typical before and after:

- **Before:** "Responsible for managing vendor relationships."
- **After:** "Managed relationships with 12 office suppliers and renegotiated two contracts, cutting annual supply costs by [METRIC?]."

If you don't know the savings, rewrite it without the number. A true bullet with less detail beats an impressive one you can't explain in an interview.

## Step 4: Write a summary that isn't a cliché

Resume summaries are where ChatGPT's defaults are worst. Left alone, it writes "Dynamic, results-oriented professional with a passion for excellence." Constrain it:

> Write three alternative 2-sentence resume summaries for me based on the bullets above. Target role: [job title]. Avoid these words: dynamic, passionate, results-driven, seasoned, spearheaded, synergy. Mention my strongest specific achievement and the type of company I want to work for.

Pick the closest one and rewrite it until it sounds like how you'd describe yourself to a friend in the industry.

## Step 5: Tailor to each job description

Start a new conversation for each application. Paste the job description (not your personal details) and ask:

> Here is a job posting. List the 10 most important skills, qualifications, and terms it emphasizes, ranked by how often and how prominently they appear. Then tell me which of them are missing or weak in my resume below.

This gives you a gap list. For each gap, decide:

1. **You have the skill but didn't mention it.** Add it, using the posting's wording if that's accurate.
2. **You have something close.** Describe your actual experience honestly. Don't rename it to match.
3. **You don't have it.** Leave it out. You may want to cover it in a cover letter or interview instead.

Plenty of companies use applicant tracking systems to organize and search applications. Using the same terms as the posting, where they're true, makes your resume easier to find in them. Hiding keywords in white text or listing skills you don't have doesn't help. If a human reviewer doesn't catch it, the interview will.

## Step 6: Handle formatting outside ChatGPT

ChatGPT outputs plain text. That's fine: resumes that are easy for software to read tend to be simple anyway. Take the finished text into Word or Google Docs and:

- Use a single-column layout with standard headings (Experience, Education, Skills)
- Skip tables, text boxes, and graphics for core information
- Use a common font and consistent date formats
- Save as PDF unless the application asks for a Word file
- Keep it to one page for early-career roles, two at most for most others

You can ask ChatGPT to review structure ("Is anything in the wrong section? Is the order logical for a career changer?"), but don't expect it to design the document.

## Step 7: Fact-check and remove the robot voice

Read every line and ask two questions: *Is this true?* and *Could I talk about it for two minutes in an interview?* Delete or rewrite anything that fails either test.

Then do a style pass. Common ChatGPT tells in resumes:

- The same verb opening several bullets ("Leveraged…", "Spearheaded…")
- Stacked adjectives ("innovative, scalable, customer-centric solutions")
- Every bullet the same length and rhythm
- Vague impact phrases ("drove significant improvements")

You can ask ChatGPT to flag these ("List any repeated verbs or vague phrases in this resume"), but make the fixes yourself. Your own phrasing is what makes the resume sound like a person.

## Tips that save time

- **Keep a master resume.** Maintain one long document with every bullet you've ever written, then cut it down for each job. ChatGPT is fast at selecting "the 4 most relevant bullets for this posting."
- **Ask for variations, not one answer.** "Give me 3 versions" gives you something to choose from. One version tends to get accepted without enough scrutiny.
- **Use it for the cover letter too.** The gap list from Step 5 works as an outline for a cover letter that addresses the posting directly.
- **Practice the claims.** Ask ChatGPT to act as an interviewer and question you on specific bullets. If you can't answer, the bullet needs work.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Pasting personal data.** Contact details and employer secrets don't belong in a chat window.
- **Accepting invented metrics.** If you didn't provide the number, it's fiction.
- **One resume for everything.** With ChatGPT's help, tailoring takes minutes rather than an evening, and it's still the step people skip most.
- **Over-polishing.** Keep a few plain, specific sentences. They stand out more than another page of perfect corporate prose.
- **Trusting its advice about specific companies.** It doesn't know a company's current hiring process, and its information is out of date.

## The bottom line

ChatGPT won't make you a stronger candidate, but it can present the experience you already have much more clearly, and it cuts the slow parts of tailoring a resume. Give it detailed, honest input, forbid it from inventing anything, and make the final edit yourself. The result should read like a clear version of you, not like ChatGPT wrote it.
