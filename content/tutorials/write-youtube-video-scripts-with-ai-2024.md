Every YouTuber knows the two hardest sentences to write: the first one, and the one that keeps a viewer from clicking away at the 30-second mark. AI will not deliver your video for you, but used correctly it collapses the blank-page problem and gives you retention structure for free. Here is the workflow, tested across ChatGPT (GPT-4o) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet.

## 1. Start with research, not a prompt

The lazy way — "write me a YouTube script about X" — produces the same generic video everyone else is uploading. Instead, spend fifteen minutes gathering:

- **Transcripts of 2-3 top-performing videos** on your topic (open a video's transcript panel and copy it out)
- **Comment sections** from those videos — unanswered questions are your content gaps
- **Your own take**: the one thing you believe about this topic that the top videos got wrong or missed

Paste all of it into your AI chat as context. Claude's large context window swallows several full transcripts without complaint, which is where it pulls ahead for this step; see our [Claude review](/reviews/claude-review/) for more on its long-document strengths.

## 2. Ask for an outline built around retention, not chapters

Prompt for structure explicitly:

```
Using the research above, outline a 10-minute video on [topic].
Structure it for retention:
- Cold open (15 sec): the payoff, teased
- Context (60 sec): why this matters now
- 3-4 main segments, each ending with an open loop into the next
- A "but here's the problem" turn around the midpoint
- Payoff that resolves the cold open
- End-screen pitch to a related video
Flag where each pattern interrupt or visual change should happen.
```

This is the step most creators skip and the one that matters most. A well-shaped outline means the AI drafts *into* a retention structure instead of producing an essay you then have to bend into one.

## 3. Generate ten hooks before any script

Hooks deserve their own generation pass. Ask for ten cold opens in different modes: a bold claim, a question, a mistake confession, a result-first tease, a "everyone tells you X, here's why that's wrong." Nine will be mediocre. One will be better than what you would have written at 11 p.m. the night before filming.

Test the winner against a simple bar: would *you* keep watching after hearing it with the sound on and the screen off? A hook that only works with visuals is a thumbnail, not a hook — and thumbnails are [their own AI workflow](/tutorials/create-youtube-thumbnails-with-ai-2024/).

## 4. Teach the AI your voice before drafting

The single biggest quality lever: paste a transcript of one of your own videos and say:

```
This is how I actually talk on camera. Note my sentence length,
filler phrases I use on purpose, how I transition between points,
and my level of formality. Draft the script in THIS voice, not
generic YouTuber voice.
```

Without this, every AI script converges on the same breathless "But here's where it gets crazy" cadence viewers have learned to distrust. With it, the draft starts 70% of the way to sounding like you. If you use ChatGPT regularly, saving these voice notes in a custom instruction or a Claude Project means you only do this setup once.

## 5. Draft segment by segment, not all at once

Request the script one section at a time. Two reasons: quality degrades over very long single generations, and reviewing in chunks keeps you editing actively instead of skimming. After each segment, give one concrete note — "tighten this to 90 seconds," "the example is weak, use one about small channels" — before moving on.

Ask for a two-column format as you go: spoken lines on the left, b-roll and on-screen visual cues on the right. Your future editing self will thank you, especially if you later [cut the video into Shorts](/tutorials/turn-long-videos-into-shorts-ai/).

## 6. The read-aloud edit

Print or pull up the draft and read it out loud, once, at performance speed. Mark every place you stumble, every sentence that feels like *writing* instead of *talking*, every fact you have not verified. Then do one final AI pass: paste the marked sections and ask for spoken-language rewrites — shorter sentences, contractions, no subordinate-clause pileups.

Two things stay entirely human in this step: **fact-checking** (GPT-4o and Claude both state wrong specifics with total confidence — verify every number and name) and **the personal stories**. AI can leave slots for your anecdotes; only you can fill them, and they are precisely what makes the video yours.

## Common pitfalls

1. **Publishing draft one.** An unedited AI script reads fine and performs badly. The rewrite is the work.
2. **Letting the AI pick the topic angle.** It will choose the most statistically common angle — which is by definition the most saturated one. Your contrarian take from step 1 is the video.
3. **Scripting word-for-word when you're better loose.** Some creators deliver best from bullet points. If that is you, use this workflow to produce detailed beats, not full prose.
4. **Ignoring the description.** While the conversation has full context, ask for the title options, description, and tags. Thirty extra seconds, and it beats writing metadata cold at upload time.

## The honest math

For a typical 10-minute video, this workflow takes an hour to ninety minutes and saves roughly half of a from-scratch scripting session — with the biggest gains for creators who publish weekly and burn out on the blank page.
