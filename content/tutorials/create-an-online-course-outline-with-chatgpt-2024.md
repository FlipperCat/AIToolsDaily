Most people who want to build an online course don't stall on recording video. They stall on the blank page: what goes in module one, what gets cut, and how to keep a three-hour course from turning into a twelve-hour brain dump.

ChatGPT is good at exactly this kind of structuring. Give it your expertise and a clear target student and it will draft objectives, a module sequence, and lesson-level detail in minutes. Then it can critique its own draft from a confused beginner's point of view. This guide walks through the full workflow.

If you teach in a classroom rather than online, our guide to [writing lesson plans with ChatGPT](/tutorials/how-to-write-lesson-plans-with-chatgpt-2023-a-teachers-step-by-step-guide/) covers the shorter-format version.

## What You Need

- **ChatGPT**, free or Plus. Plus users can use **Canvas**, a side-by-side editor that launched in beta in October, which is useful for revising long outlines. See our [ChatGPT Canvas guide](/tutorials/how-to-use-chatgpt-canvas-2024-a-practical-guide-to-the-new-editing-workspace/).
- **Your raw material:** notes, old workshop slides, blog posts, client FAQs, anything that captures how you already teach the topic.
- **About two hours** for a solid first outline.

## Step 1: Define the Student and the Transformation (Before Opening ChatGPT)

Write two sentences by hand first:

1. **Who is this for?** Be specific. "Freelance designers who know Figma basics but have never run a client discovery call" beats "designers."
2. **What can they do after the course that they couldn't do before?** Say what the student will be able to *do*, not what they'll know.

This matters because ChatGPT fills any vagueness with generic content. A vague student gets you a vague course.

## Step 2: Give ChatGPT the Full Context

Start a new chat and front-load everything:

```
I'm creating a self-paced online course. Here's the context:

Target student: [your sentence from Step 1]
Outcome: [your transformation sentence]
My background: [2-3 lines on your experience teaching this]
Format: video lessons of 5-12 minutes, with worksheets
Target length: about 3 hours of video total
Platform: [Teachable / Thinkific / Kajabi / my own site]

Don't write anything yet. Ask me 5 questions that would help
you design a better course for this student.
```

Asking it to question you first is the most useful move in this workflow. The answers you give, such as common mistakes, what students already tried, and where they quit, become the backbone of the course.

## Step 3: Write Learning Objectives First

Course designers call this *backward design*: decide what students should be able to do, then build the content that gets them there.

```
Based on my answers, draft 5-7 learning objectives for this course.
Use measurable action verbs (e.g., "build," "diagnose," "write,"
"compare"), not "understand" or "learn about." Each objective
should be something a student could demonstrate in an assignment.
```

Edit these hard. Delete any objective you couldn't check with a real task. If an objective reads "understand pricing strategy," rewrite it as "set a project price using a value-based worksheet."

## Step 4: Generate the Module Structure

```
Now design a module structure that achieves these objectives.
For each module give: title, which objective(s) it serves,
the key skill the student practices, and an estimated video length.
Order modules so each builds on the previous one.
Keep the total near 3 hours.
```

Check the draft for:

- **Modules that serve no objective.** Cut them or turn them into a bonus resource.
- **Objectives with no module.** You've found a gap.
- **Front-loaded theory.** ChatGPT tends to open with "History and Background of X." Students want an early win. Move a practical exercise into module one.

## Step 5: Break Modules Into Lessons

Do this one module at a time. Asking for every lesson at once produces shallow output.

```
Expand Module 2 into individual lessons. For each lesson:
- Title
- One-sentence goal
- 3-5 key points to cover
- One example or demo I should show
- A short practice task the student does afterward
Keep each lesson under 12 minutes.
```

Here, swap ChatGPT's generic examples for your own. "Show a sample client email" is a placeholder. "Show the email that got me my first $5,000 retainer, with the client's name removed" is something nobody else can teach.

## Step 6: Add Assessments and Practice

Students remember what they practice, not what they watch.

```
For each module, suggest one assessment that proves the student
met the objective. Mix formats: a worksheet, a short project,
a self-check quiz, a peer-review task. Keep each one achievable
in under 30 minutes.
```

For quiz questions, ask ChatGPT to also explain why each wrong answer is wrong. Those explanations make good feedback text in most course platforms. To help students review after the course, you can also turn your key concepts into [Anki flashcards with ChatGPT](/tutorials/how-to-make-anki-flashcards-with-chatgpt-2024-a-workflow-that-survives-contact-with-exams/).

## Step 7: Stress-Test the Outline

Have ChatGPT critique its own work from a student's point of view:

```
Role-play as my target student: [description]. Read this outline
and tell me:
1. Where would you get confused or lost?
2. Where would you get bored and quit?
3. What question would you be desperate to ask that isn't answered?
4. Which lesson feels like filler?
Be blunt.
```

Then ask it to switch roles:

```
Now act as an experienced instructional designer. Identify the
three biggest structural weaknesses in this outline and how
to fix them.
```

This takes two minutes and reliably catches sequencing problems and bloated modules.

## Step 8: Validate With Real People

AI feedback doesn't replace human feedback. Before recording anything:

- Send the module list to five people who match your target student and ask which module they'd pay for.
- Run a free live workshop on one module and watch where people get stuck.
- Check whether the outline answers the questions you get asked most in DMs and email.

## Step 9: Turn the Outline Into a Production Plan

Once the outline is solid, use ChatGPT for the logistics:

```
Convert this outline into a production checklist: for each lesson,
list what I need to record (talking head, screen recording, slides),
what downloadable resources to create, and a suggested recording order
that batches similar lesson types together.
```

## Tips for Better Results

- **Work in one long conversation.** ChatGPT keeps the context, so later prompts build on earlier decisions.
- **Paste your best existing content.** A popular blog post or client handout tells ChatGPT how you explain things.
- **Ask for three options** when naming modules or the course itself, then combine the best parts.
- **Use Canvas for edits** if you have Plus. Highlighting one section and asking for a fix beats re-pasting the whole outline.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Accepting the first outline.** It will look complete and professional, and it will usually be generic. Most of the value comes from your edits in Steps 3 through 7.
- **Scope creep.** ChatGPT happily adds modules. A course that tries to cover everything rarely gets finished, by you or by your students.
- **Unverified facts.** If ChatGPT suggests statistics, tool features, or "industry standards" for lesson content, check them. It can state outdated or invented details with total confidence.
- **Losing your voice.** Use ChatGPT for structure and keep your own phrasing in the scripts. Students buy courses from people, not from outlines.

## The Bottom Line

ChatGPT won't make you an expert, but it is very good at organizing what you already know. Define the student, work backward from objectives, expand one module at a time, and get it critiqued by both AI and real people. Following those steps turns a month of procrastinating into a course outline you can start recording this week.
