Open LinkedIn in January and you can feel everyone updating their profile at once. New year, new goals, and for a lot of people in tech right now, a layoff email nobody planned for.

ChatGPT, the free chatbot OpenAI released at the end of November, is surprisingly good at the part of this job most people hate: turning a messy career history into tight, readable copy. It is also good at producing the exact "results-driven professional with a passion for excellence" sludge that recruiters scroll past. The difference is almost entirely in how you use it.

This guide walks through a workflow that takes about 45 minutes and produces a headline, an About section, and experience entries you'd actually sign your name to. If you're new to the tool, start with our [plain-English explainer on what ChatGPT is](/what-is-chatgpt-everything-you-need-to-know-2023/).

## What You Need

- **A free ChatGPT account** at chat.openai.com. The tool is in a free research preview and sometimes shows an "at capacity" message at peak hours. Early mornings and late evenings are usually smoother.
- **Your current resume**, or a rough list of jobs, dates, and responsibilities.
- **Two or three job postings** for roles you want next.
- **Any numbers you can dig up**: revenue, team size, tickets closed, time saved, customers supported.

One caution before pasting anything. OpenAI says conversations may be reviewed to improve its systems. Leave out confidential client names, unreleased products, internal financials, and personal details like your address or phone number. Describe sensitive work in general terms ("a national retailer") instead.

## Step 1: Give It Raw Material, Not a Request

The worst prompt is "write my LinkedIn profile." ChatGPT knows nothing about you, so it fills the gap with clichés.

Load context first:

> I'm going to rewrite my LinkedIn profile with your help. Here is my background. Don't write anything yet. Confirm you've read it and summarize my career in three sentences so I can check your understanding.
>
> [paste resume or job history]
>
> Target roles: [paste job titles and two or three postings]

The summary check matters. If those three sentences get your career wrong, say by calling you a marketer when you work in sales operations, correct it now. Everything that follows builds on this understanding.

## Step 2: Let ChatGPT Interview You

Resumes list duties. Good profiles describe outcomes. Most people can't recall their outcomes until someone asks, so make ChatGPT ask:

> Act as a career coach. Ask me five questions, one at a time, to uncover specific accomplishments, numbers, and stories from my last two roles that aren't in what I pasted. Wait for my answer before asking the next question.

Answer in rough, conversational sentences. Typos are fine. Expect questions like "What changed because of the project you led?" and "How large was the team or budget?" Your answers are the material that makes the profile specific.

If you don't know an exact figure, say so: "roughly 30% faster, I never measured it precisely." You want that honesty on record in the chat before Step 5.

## Step 3: Write the Headline

Your headline shows up in search results, comments, and connection requests. LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters, but only the first part appears in most places, so the first few words carry the weight.

> Write 10 LinkedIn headline options for me, each under 220 characters. Lead with the job title recruiters would search for. Then add my specialty and one concrete proof point. No buzzwords like "passionate," "guru," "ninja," or "results-driven." Vary the structure.

Compare the typical results:

- **Weak:** "Passionate Marketing Professional | Driving Growth Through Innovation"
- **Better:** "Email Marketing Manager | Lifecycle and Retention for B2B SaaS | Grew newsletter from 4K to 31K subscribers"

Pick two or three you like and ask it to combine the best parts. Then count the characters yourself. ChatGPT is unreliable at counting and will confidently tell you a 260-character headline is 198.

## Step 4: Draft the About Section

The About section gives you 2,600 characters, but LinkedIn shows only the first few lines before a "see more" link. Those opening lines need to say what you do and for whom.

> Write my LinkedIn About section in first person, under 2,000 characters. Structure: (1) two opening sentences that say what I do and who I help, strong enough to stand alone; (2) a short paragraph on how I work; (3) three or four specific accomplishments from our conversation; (4) what I'm looking for next and how to reach me. Conversational and confident. Short paragraphs. Use only facts I've given you.

The first draft will be about 70% of the way there. Improve it with targeted follow-ups instead of starting over:

- "Make the opening less formal."
- "Cut the second paragraph in half."
- "Replace 'leveraged' and 'spearheaded' with plain verbs."
- "This sounds like a brochure. Rewrite it the way I'd explain my job to a friend at dinner."

That last one works better than it has any right to.

## Step 5: Rewrite Experience Entries as Accomplishments

For each role, paste your current description and ask:

> Rewrite this role as a two-sentence summary plus four bullets. Each bullet starts with a plain action verb and ends with a result. Use only numbers I gave you. Where a number would help but I haven't provided one, write [NEED NUMBER] instead of inventing it.

The placeholder instruction is the most important line in this guide. Left alone, ChatGPT will happily write "increased conversion by 37%" when you never said any such thing. A made-up metric on a public profile is a problem waiting for an interview question.

The same approach works for the document you attach to applications. Our guide to [writing a resume with ChatGPT](/tutorials/how-to-write-a-resume-with-chatgpt-2023-a-step-by-step-guide-that-keeps-it-honest/) goes deeper on tailoring bullets to a specific posting.

## Step 6: Pull Keywords and Skills From Real Postings

Recruiters search LinkedIn by keyword, so the terms on your profile should match the terms in the jobs you want.

> Here are three job postings for my target role. List the 15 skills and keywords that appear most often. Mark which already appear in my draft profile and which are missing. For the missing ones, suggest where I could add them truthfully, and flag any I don't seem to have experience with.

Add the skills you really have to LinkedIn's Skills section. You can list up to 50, though the top few get the attention. Work the most important ones into your headline and About text. Skip anything you'd struggle to discuss in an interview.

## Step 7: Do a De-Robot Pass

Before you publish, run one more prompt:

> Review the full profile below. List every phrase that sounds generic or could appear on anyone's profile, and suggest a specific replacement drawn from my actual experience.

Then read the whole thing out loud. Cut anything you wouldn't say to a colleague. Finally, rewrite at least a few sentences by hand. Your own phrasing, even when it's slightly less polished, is what makes the profile sound like a person.

## Tips That Make a Real Difference

- **Stay in one conversation.** ChatGPT remembers earlier messages in the same chat, so your interview answers carry forward. If a long thread starts dropping details, paste the key facts again.
- **Ask for options, not answers.** Ten headlines beat one. Three openings beat one.
- **Give it a voice sample.** Paste an email or post you wrote and say "match this tone."
- **Reuse the work.** The same chat can produce a short bio, a note to recruiters, or [cold emails to hiring managers](/tutorials/write-cold-emails-with-chatgpt-2023/).
- **Practice next.** Once the profile is live, use the same accomplishments to [rehearse interviews with ChatGPT](/tutorials/how-to-practice-job-interviews-with-chatgpt-2023-a-step-by-step-guide/).

## Common Pitfalls

**Invented facts.** Mentioned above and worth repeating. Check every number, title, tool, and date against reality.

**Buzzword drift.** After a few rewrites, "synergy" creeps back in. Restate your banned-word list when it does.

**Outdated knowledge.** ChatGPT's training data stops in 2021 and it can't browse the web. Don't rely on it for current LinkedIn features, salary data, or what's trending in your industry.

**Sameness.** Plenty of people are using the same tool with the same lazy prompt. The interview step and your hand edits are what separate your profile from theirs.

**Keyword stuffing.** A profile crammed with search terms reads badly to the human who eventually opens it. Write for the recruiter first and the search box second.

**Lost drafts.** Conversations are normally saved in the sidebar, but the service does go down under heavy load. Copy drafts into a document as you go.

## The Bottom Line

ChatGPT doesn't know what you accomplished, and it can't decide what you want next. What it does well is the grind in between: asking questions, structuring answers, producing ten variations in seconds, and trimming text to fit a limit.

Treat it as an editor who interviews you, verify every claim, and finish by hand. You'll end up with a profile that's sharper than what most people write alone and still sounds like you.
