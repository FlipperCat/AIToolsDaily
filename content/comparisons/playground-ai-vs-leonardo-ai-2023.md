If you want to generate AI images without paying for Midjourney or setting up Stable Diffusion on your own GPU, two browser tools come up first: Playground AI and Leonardo AI. Both run on Stable Diffusion-family models, both have unusually generous free tiers, and both offer more control than a simple prompt box.

They are built for different people, though. Playground feels like a lightweight editor for casual creators and designers. Leonardo feels like a production tool for game artists and people who want consistent, stylized output at volume.

We used both side by side for several weeks this fall, running the same prompts through each across portraits, product shots, fantasy illustration, game assets, and social media graphics. Here's how they compare.

## Quick comparison

| | Playground AI | Leonardo AI |
|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Beginners, quick edits, social graphics | Game assets, stylized art, consistent series |
| **Free tier** | Large daily image allowance | Daily token allowance (about 150 tokens) |
| **Models** | Stable Diffusion 1.5, SDXL, Playground's own model, DALL-E 2 option | Platform-tuned models plus community models, with SDXL-based options |
| **Editing** | Canvas with inpainting, outpainting, and image combining | AI Canvas with inpainting and outpainting |
| **Custom training** | Not a core feature | Train your own model on your images |
| **Quality boost** | Filters (style presets) | Alchemy pipeline (paid plans) |
| **Paid plan from** | About $15/month | About $10/month |
| **Learning curve** | Gentle | Moderate |

## Image quality

On raw output, Leonardo usually wins, especially with Alchemy turned on. Alchemy is Leonardo's enhanced generation pipeline. It costs more tokens per image, but it produces noticeably sharper detail, better faces, and more coherent compositions. For fantasy characters, game concept art, and stylized illustration, Leonardo's fine-tuned models produced the kind of polished look people associate with Midjourney more often than Playground did.

Playground holds its own on photographic and design-oriented prompts, particularly with SDXL selected. Its filters are curated style presets you can apply with a click, and they are a big part of why beginners get good results quickly. A plain prompt with a well-chosen filter often beat a carefully worded prompt on Leonardo's base settings.

Both struggle with the usual problems for this generation of models: hands, readable text inside images, and complex scenes with several specific subjects. Neither is clearly better at text. If text in images matters to you, neither tool solves that yet.

**Edge: Leonardo**, for peak quality and stylized art. Playground is close for photos and simple graphics.

## Free tier and limits

This is where the two tools take very different approaches.

**Playground** gives free users a large daily allowance of image generations, far more than most competitors. For hobbyists, that effectively means unlimited experimentation. The tradeoffs are slower generation at busy times and some limits on advanced options and image sizes.

**Leonardo** uses tokens. Free accounts get a daily refill of about 150 tokens, and each generation costs tokens depending on resolution, model, number of images, and features. A standard image is cheap. High-resolution or premium settings use up the daily allowance quickly. Free generations are also public.

In practice, a free Playground user can generate many times more images per day than a free Leonardo user. A free Leonardo user, however, can produce higher-quality images when they choose settings carefully.

**Edge: Playground**, by a wide margin, for volume on a $0 budget.

## Editing and canvas tools

Both tools let you go beyond single prompts.

**Playground's Canvas** is the stronger editor. You can outpaint past an image's borders, mask a region and regenerate only that area, and combine several generations on an infinite board. It works more like a lightweight Photoshop with a generator built in. For social media graphics, thumbnails, and quick mockups, it is the most practical feature either tool offers.

**Leonardo's AI Canvas** also supports inpainting and outpainting, and it works well, but it feels more like an add-on to the generation workflow than a main workspace. Leonardo also includes image-to-image with strength controls, upscaling, and a texture generator for 3D models, which is a niche feature but useful for game developers.

**Edge: Playground**, for everyday editing. Leonardo for 3D textures.

## Models and customization

This is Leonardo's biggest advantage.

Leonardo lets you **train your own model** on a set of your images. Upload a dozen or more consistent examples, such as a character, a product, or an art style, and you get a model that reproduces that look across new prompts. Combined with a large library of community-trained models, this makes Leonardo far better at producing *consistent* output across a series. That matters for game assets, children's book illustrations, and brand imagery.

Playground offers model switching between Stable Diffusion 1.5, SDXL, its own in-house model, and DALL-E 2. That variety is useful, but it does not offer the same depth of custom training. Getting the same character across ten images on Playground still takes careful prompting, seeds, and luck.

If you want full control, including LoRAs, ControlNet, and any checkpoint you like, running [Stable Diffusion XL](/reviews/stable-diffusion-xl-review-2023/) locally still beats both. Our [Midjourney vs Stable Diffusion comparison](/compare/midjourney-vs-stable-diffusion/) covers that tradeoff.

**Edge: Leonardo**, clearly.

## Ease of use

Playground is the friendlier first experience. You see a prompt box, a filter picker, and a few sliders. Sensible defaults mean a first-time user gets an attractive image in under a minute.

Leonardo shows more controls up front: model selection, Alchemy, Prompt Magic, guidance scale, dimensions, negative prompts, and token costs for each setting. None of it is hard, but it takes an afternoon to learn which combinations are worth the tokens. Its community feed is a good learning resource, because you can copy a prompt and settings from an image you like.

**Edge: Playground**, for beginners. Leonardo rewards people who put in the time.

## Pricing (as of November 2023)

Both products change their plans often, so treat these numbers as approximate:

**Playground AI**
- **Free:** large daily image allowance, filters, Canvas access
- **Pro:** around $15/month for higher limits, faster generation, private images, and clearer commercial terms

**Leonardo AI**
- **Free:** about 150 tokens per day, public generations
- **Apprentice:** around $10/month for a larger monthly token pool, private generations, and Alchemy
- **Artisan:** around $24/month for more tokens and faster processing
- **Maestro:** around $48/month for heavy users

On commercial rights, both tools have generally granted usage rights on paid plans, while free-tier terms have been less clear and have changed over time. If you are producing client work, read the current license and keep records of what you generated and when.

**Edge: Tie.** Playground is the better deal if you stay free. Leonardo's entry plan is cheaper if you plan to pay.

## Community and ecosystem

Leonardo has built an active community around shared models and a public feed of generations with visible prompts. That makes it easy to learn by example and find a model tuned for a specific look, such as anime, isometric game art, or photorealistic portraits.

Playground also has a public feed and makes it easy to remix other people's images. It feels more like a social design app, while Leonardo feels more like an asset library for creators.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Playground AI if:**
- You are new to AI image generation and want good results fast
- You need lots of images on a $0 budget
- You make social graphics, thumbnails, or mockups and need in-browser editing
- You want to switch between SDXL and DALL-E 2 without juggling accounts

**Choose Leonardo AI if:**
- You need consistent characters, styles, or products across many images
- You are a game developer or illustrator who needs stylized assets or 3D textures
- You are willing to spend about $10/month for Alchemy-level quality
- You want to train a custom model without running your own hardware

**Use both if** you can. Many creators generate and iterate on Leonardo, then bring the best image into Playground's Canvas for outpainting and final touches. Both free tiers are generous enough that this costs nothing.

## Verdict

Playground AI is the best free starting point. It has the most generous free allowance, the easiest interface, and a Canvas editor that is useful for real work. Leonardo AI is the better production tool, with higher peak quality, custom model training, and stronger consistency across a series.

If you only try one, start with Playground to learn the basics. Once you need a consistent look across many images, move to Leonardo.
