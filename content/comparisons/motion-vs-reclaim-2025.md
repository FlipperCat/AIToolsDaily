# Motion vs Reclaim: The AI Scheduling Showdown

Both Motion and Reclaim promise the same magic: hand them your tasks and they'll slot the work into your calendar automatically, reshuffling as your day changes. But under that shared pitch they're built on different philosophies. Motion wants to be your entire productivity system. Reclaim wants to be the smart layer that defends the calendar you already live in.

I've run both against real weeks of meetings, deadlines, and the usual chaos. Here's how they actually differ in 2025.

## Quick Comparison Table

## Philosophy: Replace vs Enhance

The single biggest difference isn't a feature — it's intent.

**Motion** wants to replace your task manager, your calendar app, and your project tracker. You put tasks in Motion, and its AI builds and continuously rebuilds your schedule around them, deadlines, and meetings. When something slips, Motion re-plans the rest of your day automatically. It's a commitment: to get the value, you have to actually run your work through it.

**Reclaim** takes the opposite stance. It assumes you'll keep using Google Calendar or Outlook as your home base, and it quietly makes that calendar smarter — defending focus blocks, scheduling habits, and finding time for tasks without asking you to move in. It's additive rather than all-consuming.

That framing predicts almost everything else. If you want one tool to rule your productivity, lean Motion. If you want to keep your setup and add intelligence, lean Reclaim.

## Auto-Scheduling

Both tools auto-schedule, but the scope differs.

Motion's scheduling is holistic and aggressive. It looks at every task, deadline, and meeting and constructs a complete plan for your day, then reflows it in real time as things change. When it works, it's genuinely impressive — you stop deciding what to do next because Motion already decided. The tradeoff is that a busy, shifting calendar can feel like it's being rearranged out from under you.

Reclaim's scheduling is more targeted. It specializes in finding and protecting the right time for specific things — a task due Friday, a recurring habit, a one-on-one. It's less "plan my entire existence" and more "make sure the important blocks survive contact with a chaotic week." For many people that restraint feels safer and more predictable.

## Task and Project Management

This is where Motion pulls clearly ahead. It includes a real project management layer — projects, task dependencies, assignees, and views that let a small team run actual work inside the tool. If you've been juggling a separate task app and calendar, Motion collapses them into one.

Reclaim is lighter here by design. It handles task lists and integrates with tools you already use, but it isn't trying to be your project tracker. It schedules tasks well; it doesn't aim to manage complex projects. If robust task management matters, that gap is decisive.

## Habits and Recurring Time

Reclaim wins this category decisively. Its habit feature is a standout: define recurring commitments — "workout three times a week," "no meetings before 10," "lunch every day" — and Reclaim intelligently fits them into your week, flexing around meetings rather than sitting on rigid fixed slots. For protecting the routines that keep you sane, nothing here beats it.

Motion handles recurring tasks, but its strength is deadline-driven planning, not defending flexible habits. If your goal is guarding personal time and building consistent routines, Reclaim is the more thoughtful tool.

## Integrations and Team Use

Both sync with Google Calendar and Outlook, which covers most people. Reclaim leans into calendar-centric features like scheduling links and smart team availability, and since joining Dropbox it fits naturally into a broader productivity stack. Motion's integrations serve its all-in-one model — the point is to bring work *into* Motion rather than to spread across many tools.

For teams, the choice again follows philosophy. Motion suits a small team that wants a shared operating system for work. Reclaim suits a team that wants smarter individual calendars and easy coordination without abandoning existing tools.

## Pricing

Prices are approximate and change — treat this as a snapshot as of early 2025.

Motion has no free tier. After a trial, it runs roughly $19–34 per user per month depending on annual versus monthly billing and plan. It's priced as a system you commit to.

Reclaim offers a genuinely useful free tier, with paid plans in the rough range of $8–18 per user per month that unlock more habits, longer scheduling horizons, and team features. You can get real value without paying, which lowers the risk of trying it.

The honest read: Reclaim is easier to justify financially because you can start free and upgrade only if it earns it. Motion asks for a bigger commitment up front, and the value depends on you fully adopting it. ## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Motion if:**

- You want one tool to replace your task manager, calendar, and project tracker.
- You're deadline-driven and want AI to plan your entire day for you.
- You run a small team and want a shared productivity system.
- You're willing to fully commit — Motion rewards adoption and punishes half-measures.

**Choose Reclaim if:**

- You want to keep your existing calendar and just make it smarter.
- Protecting focus time and building consistent habits is your top priority.
- You want to start free and pay only if it proves its worth.
- You prefer a tool that enhances your workflow rather than replacing it.

The deciding question is really about commitment. Motion is a bet on consolidation — powerful if you go all in, underwhelming if you don't. Reclaim is a lower-risk enhancement that shines at defending time without demanding you rebuild your habits around it.

For most people dipping a toe into AI scheduling, I'd start with Reclaim's free tier to see whether auto-scheduling clicks for you at all. If it does and you find yourself wanting one system for tasks, projects, and calendar together, Motion is the natural upgrade.
