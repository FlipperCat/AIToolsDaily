If you build training videos for a living, you've probably narrowed your shortlist to these two. Synthesia is the category's best-known name, used by large companies for onboarding, compliance, and internal communications. Colossyan has spent the last few years positioning itself squarely at learning and development teams, with features like branching scenarios and quizzes built into the video itself.

Both turn a script into a presenter-led video without a camera, a studio, or an actor. The differences show up in the details that matter to L&D teams: interactivity, LMS export, how conversation scenes look, how translation works, and how many people can collaborate without stepping on each other. We built the same three training modules in both (a compliance refresher, a software walkthrough, and a customer-service role-play) to see where each one pulls ahead.

## Quick Comparison

| Factor | Synthesia | Colossyan |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **Best for** | Large-scale corporate video, internal comms, multilingual rollouts | L&D teams building interactive training |
| **Avatar quality** | Polished, very consistent | Good, slightly less natural in close-ups |
| **Avatar library** | Larger | Smaller but varied, includes side-view angles |
| **Conversation scenes** | Possible, more manual | Built-in multi-avatar scenes |
| **Interactivity** | Clickable elements in its player | Quizzes and branching scenarios |
| **LMS export** | Available on higher tiers | Available on higher tiers, central to the pitch |
| **Translation** | Strong, with video translation and dubbing | Auto-translate built into the editor |
| **Collaboration** | Mature workspace, comments, brand kits | Solid, fewer enterprise controls |
| **Entry price (approx.)** | ~$20-30/month | ~$20-30/month |

Pricing is approximate as of December 2025 and varies by billing period and seat count.

## Avatar Quality and Realism

Synthesia's avatars are the more polished of the two. Lip sync is tight, head and hand movement looks natural for presenter-style delivery, and its more expressive avatars handle tone shifts better than older "newsreader" styles. If the video will be seen by customers or executives, Synthesia's output is the safer bet.

Colossyan's avatars are good, and for most internal training nobody will complain. In tight close-ups, though, movement can look a little more mechanical. Colossyan offsets this with variety: side-view avatar angles and multi-avatar scenes make conversations feel more like a scene and less like a slideshow with a talking head.

Both platforms offer custom avatars of real employees, which many teams use for a familiar face from leadership or the training team. Expect consent requirements, extra cost or higher tiers, and some turnaround time on both.

**Edge: Synthesia** for realism. **Colossyan** for staging conversations.

## Interactivity and Learning Features

This is where the two diverge most.

Colossyan treats a video as a learning object. You can add multiple-choice questions mid-video and build branching scenarios where a learner's choice sends them down a different path. That's ideal for soft-skills training: "The customer is angry. Do you apologize, escalate, or offer a refund?" Each choice plays a different scene. Building that in Colossyan took us an afternoon. Doing it in a traditional authoring tool would take far longer.

Synthesia has added interactive elements to its video player, such as clickable buttons and links, and it works well alongside authoring tools. But if you want quizzes and branching to live inside the video itself, Colossyan is more purpose-built.

**Edge: Colossyan**, clearly, for interactive training.

## LMS and SCORM Export

For L&D teams, a video that can't report completion to your LMS is only half done. Both platforms offer SCORM export on higher-tier plans, so videos can be uploaded to an LMS and tracked like other courses. Colossyan's interactive elements and export are designed together, so quiz results and branching paths fit naturally into an LMS workflow.

Synthesia's export works fine for standard video modules. Teams that build interactivity in a separate authoring tool often treat Synthesia as the video engine and do assessments elsewhere.

**Edge: Colossyan** for all-in-one interactive modules. **Tie** for plain video modules.

## Translation and Localization

Both platforms are strong here, which is a big part of why companies use avatar video at all. Re-recording a human presenter in ten languages is expensive. Translating a script and re-rendering is not.

Synthesia's language and voice coverage is among the widest in the category, and its video translation features can dub existing videos, not just re-render avatar scenes. For global companies rolling out the same content across many regions, that breadth matters.

Colossyan's auto-translate is built into the editor and handles the common workflow of duplicating a video, translating it, and reviewing it. Coverage is broad, though Synthesia generally offers more language and voice options.

Either way, have a native speaker review translated scripts, especially for compliance content, where a mistranslation becomes a legal problem. Our [guide to translating videos with AI](/tutorials/how-to-translate-videos-with-ai-2025-a-step-by-step-guide/) covers a review workflow that works with both.

**Edge: Synthesia**, for breadth and dubbing.

## Editor and Ease of Use

Both editors feel like slide software with an avatar on top. You write or paste a script per scene, choose an avatar and background, and add text, images, and screen recordings.

Synthesia's editor is more refined, with a large template library and AI script assistance. It also imports existing documents and slide decks, which helps when you're converting existing training content.

Colossyan's editor is similarly easy, with PowerPoint and document import that turns existing material into a first draft quickly. We found ourselves moving slightly faster in Synthesia for straightforward presenter videos, and faster in Colossyan for scenes with multiple characters. Our earlier [Colossyan review](/reviews/colossyan-review-2023/) covers its editor in more depth.

**Edge: Tie**, depending on the type of video you make most.

## Collaboration and Enterprise Controls

Synthesia is built for large organizations: shared workspaces, commenting, brand kits that lock fonts and colors, user roles, and the security documentation enterprise procurement teams ask for. If your rollout involves dozens of editors across departments, Synthesia's maturity shows.

Colossyan supports team workspaces, brand kits, and collaboration too, and its enterprise plan covers what most mid-sized companies need. Very large organizations with strict IT requirements may find Synthesia's controls more complete.

**Edge: Synthesia** for large enterprises.

## Pricing

As of December 2025, both platforms start at roughly $20-30 per month for an entry plan with a limited number of video minutes, with mid-tier plans in the $60-90 per month range. Features L&D teams care most about, like SCORM export, custom avatars, more minutes, and advanced collaboration, typically sit on higher tiers or custom enterprise plans.

Two things to check before buying:

1. **Minute limits.** Both platforms cap video minutes per month or year on lower tiers. Estimate your real production volume, including re-renders after edits.
2. **What's enterprise-only.** Make a list of must-have features (SCORM, custom avatars, SSO, brand locks) and confirm which plan includes each one.

Both vendors change plans often, so check current pricing pages and ask for a trial.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Synthesia if:**

- You need the most polished, realistic avatars for customer-facing or executive communication
- You're localizing content into many languages or dubbing existing videos
- You're rolling out across a large organization with strict IT, security, and brand requirements
- Your interactivity lives in a separate authoring tool, and you mainly need a strong video engine

**Choose Colossyan if:**

- You're an L&D team building interactive training with quizzes and branching scenarios
- Role-play and conversation scenes are a big part of your content
- You want interactive modules that export to your LMS without extra tools
- You're a mid-sized team that values training-specific features over enterprise breadth

**Consider something else if** your videos are for marketing, sales outreach, or social media. Personalized and more expressive avatar tools fit those jobs better. ## Verdict

There's no wrong choice here, only a wrong fit. Synthesia is the better general-purpose avatar video platform: more realistic, more languages, and more enterprise-ready. Colossyan is the better training tool: interactivity, branching, and conversation scenes are built in rather than bolted on.

If your job title includes "learning" or "instructional design," start your trial with Colossyan. If you're producing video for a whole company, across departments and regions, start with Synthesia. Either way, build one real module in each before you sign an annual contract. A free trial on your own content will tell you more than any feature table.
