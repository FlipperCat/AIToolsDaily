## ElevenLabs vs Descript Overdub (2023): Which AI Voice Should You Use?

AI voice went from novelty to usable in about six months. Two tools sit at the front of that shift, and they are aimed at completely different problems.

ElevenLabs is a speech synthesis engine: type text, get audio that sounds startlingly close to a real person. Descript Overdub is a feature inside a full audio and video editor: it patches your own voice into a recording so you can fix a flubbed line by typing.

Both do "AI voice." Choosing between them is really a choice between generating narration and repairing recordings.

## Quick Comparison

| | ElevenLabs | Descript Overdub |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary job** | Generate speech from scratch | Patch and fix existing recordings |
| **Product type** | Standalone TTS + voice cloning | Feature inside a full editor |
| **Raw voice quality** | Best available right now | Good in short patches, weaker in long passages |
| **Cloning setup** | Minutes, from a short sample | ~10 minutes reading a consent script |
| **Emotional range** | Strong, sometimes unpredictable | Flat but consistent |
| **Editing tools** | None — audio out only | Full transcript-based editor |
| **Entry price** | Free tier; paid from ~$5/mo | Free tier; paid from ~$12/user/mo (annual) |
| **Best for** | Narration, audiobooks, characters, apps | Podcasts, screencasts, course videos |

## Voice Quality

ElevenLabs is, as of this writing, the most natural-sounding text-to-speech available to individuals. Its advantage is prosody — it varies pace, adds breath, and lands emphasis in places that sound intentional rather than computed. Read a paragraph with a comma-heavy sentence and it phrases the clauses properly instead of marching through them.

That expressiveness is also its weakness. Run the same text twice and you can get meaningfully different reads. Occasionally it invents an emotional inflection you did not ask for, or mispronounces a proper noun with total confidence. For long-form narration you will regenerate sections and cherry-pick takes.

Overdub is tuned for a different bar: it needs to sound like *you*, in the middle of *your* recording, for about eight words. Judged that way it is very good. Judged as a narrator for a full paragraph, it flattens out — the intonation is even, the energy does not rise or fall, and listeners start to notice. That is a reasonable trade for what the feature is for.

**Winner: ElevenLabs**, decisively, for generated speech. Overdub wins on the narrow task of matching an existing recording.

## Voice Cloning and Setup

ElevenLabs' instant cloning takes a short sample — a minute or two of clean audio — and produces a usable clone almost immediately. That speed is the single biggest reason it spread so quickly. It also raises obvious consent problems, and the platform has been visibly tightening verification requirements in response. Expect the rules here to keep moving.

Descript takes the opposite approach deliberately. To create an Overdub voice you record roughly ten minutes reading a consent script that includes you stating permission aloud. It is slower and mildly tedious, and it makes casual misuse much harder. If you are producing content for a client or an employer, that paper trail is a feature, not friction.

Descript also offers stock Overdub voices if you do not want to clone yourself at all — useful, but this is where the quality gap with ElevenLabs is widest.

**Winner: depends on your risk posture.** ElevenLabs for speed, Descript for defensibility.

## Workflow

This is the real dividing line.

ElevenLabs hands you a WAV or MP3. Everything after that — timing, trimming, music, mixing, sync to video — happens somewhere else. If you already work in an editor, that is fine. If you do not, you have just acquired a second problem.

Descript wraps voice inside transcript-based editing, which remains the best idea in audio software in years: delete a word from the transcript and it disappears from the audio. Add filler-word removal, Studio Sound cleanup, and multitrack video, and Overdub becomes one tool in a pipeline rather than a standalone step. Our [Descript review](/reviews/04-descript-review/) goes deeper on the editor itself, and the [podcast editing walkthrough](/tutorials/edit-a-podcast-with-descript-2023/) shows the loop in practice.

The practical version: fixing a mispronounced name in a 45-minute podcast takes about twenty seconds in Descript. Doing the same with ElevenLabs means generating a clip, importing it, matching level and room tone, and nudging it into place.

**Winner: Descript**, and it is not close.

## Languages and Range

ElevenLabs is English-first, with multilingual capability only beginning to appear in beta. Voice variety is its strength — a large shared voice library plus your own clones, spanning ages and accents, which makes it viable for character work and dialogue. If you want to see how it compares against the more corporate-narration end of the market, our [ElevenLabs vs Murf comparison](/compare/elevenlabs-vs-murf/) covers that axis.

Overdub is effectively single-voice by design. You clone yourself, and that is the point. There is no meaningful character or accent range to explore.

**Winner: ElevenLabs.**

## Pricing (Approximate, as of April 2023)

ElevenLabs runs a free tier with a modest monthly character allowance, then paid plans starting around $5/month for a small character quota with cloning, roughly $22/month at the tier most creators land on, and higher tiers at approximately $99 and $330/month for publishing and business volumes. Billing is by characters generated, which is a genuinely hard unit to forecast until you have run a project through it — a 20-minute narration script is far more characters than people expect.

Descript has a free tier, with paid plans around $12/user/month billed annually (higher month-to-month) and a professional tier roughly double that. Overdub is included, with the more generous limits on the upper tier. Because you are paying for an editor, the AI voice is effectively bundled rather than metered.

Both vendors have changed pricing more than once in the past year. Treat every number here as directional and check current rates.

**Winner: Descript** on predictability; ElevenLabs on entry cost for light use.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose ElevenLabs if** you are generating voice that never existed — audiobook narration, YouTube voiceover where you do not want to record, character dialogue for games or animation, IVR prompts, or app-embedded speech. It is also the right pick if you need many distinct voices, or if you want the highest possible quality per clip and are willing to regenerate takes to get it. See the [full ElevenLabs review](/reviews/05-elevenlabs-review/) for the detail.

**Choose Descript Overdub if** you record yourself regularly and your pain is corrections, not creation. Podcasters, course creators, and anyone shipping screencasts get more value from the surrounding editor than from the voice model. The consent-script requirement is also the easier story to tell a client or legal team.

**Use both if** you produce narrated video: draft and clean in Descript, and pull in ElevenLabs when you need a voice that is not yours or a read with more performance than Overdub can manage. They cost little enough together that this is a realistic setup for a solo creator.

The one thing neither tool solves is disclosure. Synthetic voice is now good enough that audiences cannot reliably tell, which makes it your call — and increasingly your obligation — to say when a voice is generated.
