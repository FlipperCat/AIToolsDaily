AI meeting notes have quietly become one of the most useful categories of productivity software, and two tools keep coming up: Granola and Otter.ai. They both promise to save you from typing while half-listening, but they take opposite approaches to getting there.

Granola is a bot-free notepad that enhances what you jot down. Otter is a transcription service that joins your calls and captures everything. Choose Granola for frictionless, private, well-structured notes; choose Otter for complete transcripts, team archives, and searchable call history.

## Quick Comparison

| Factor | Granola | Otter.ai |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **Approach** | Local audio, no bot joins | OtterPilot bot joins calls |
| **Core output** | Enhanced structured notes | Full transcript + summary |
| **Visibility to others** | Invisible to attendees | Visible assistant in call |
| **Transcription depth** | Good, summary-focused | Winner, verbatim + speakers |
| **In-person meetings** | Excellent | Supported |
| **Team features** | Growing | More mature |
| **Best for** | Individuals, small teams | Teams, sales, archives |
| **Pricing** | Free tier + per-seat plan | Free tier + Pro/Business |

**TL;DR:** Granola for clean private notes. Otter for full transcripts and team search.

## How Each One Works

### Granola

Granola runs on your computer and listens to the meeting's audio directly, no bot required. You take rough notes the way you normally would, and after the call it merges your notes with the transcript to produce a clean, structured summary. Because it captures audio locally rather than joining as a participant, nobody in the meeting sees a recording bot, and it works just as well for in-person conversations as for video calls.

The experience is deliberately minimal: it feels like a notepad that happens to be very good at filling in what you missed. You can apply templates, and you can ask questions across your past meetings. If you want the full breakdown, see our [Granola review](/granola-ai-meeting-notes-review-2026/).

### Otter.ai

Otter is the more established product and takes the classic transcription route. Its OtterPilot assistant joins Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams calls, transcribes everyone in real time with speaker labels, and produces summaries and action items afterward. Otter AI Chat lets you query the transcript conversationally, and everything lands in a searchable workspace. Our [Otter.ai review](/otter-ai-review-meeting-transcription/) goes deeper on the day-to-day experience.

## Accuracy and Notes Quality

This is where the philosophies diverge.

Otter is built for the transcript. If you need a near-verbatim record with speaker attribution, a searchable archive, and the ability to go back and find the exact moment someone committed to a deadline, Otter's years of transcription investment show. It is the better system of record.

Granola is built for the takeaway. Its transcript exists mainly to feed the summary, and the summary is what shines: tight, structured, and shaped by the notes you actually cared enough to type. For most one-on-ones and internal meetings, that polished summary is more useful than a wall of raw transcript.

## Privacy and Meeting Etiquette

Granola's no-bot design is more than a gimmick. Some clients and colleagues are uncomfortable when a recording assistant visibly joins a call, and in certain contexts a visible bot changes how candidly people speak. Because Granola captures audio locally and stays invisible, it sidesteps that friction entirely.

Otter's visible assistant is the opposite trade: everyone knows they are being transcribed, which is more transparent but occasionally more awkward. Neither approach removes your responsibility to follow local consent laws and tell people when you are recording.

## Integrations and Team Use

Otter is the stronger choice for teams today. It has more mature shared workspaces, sales-oriented features, and integrations designed around capturing every call into a common archive. If your goal is organizational memory, that maturity matters. Teams weighing this often also look at [Otter vs Fireflies](/compare/otter-ai-vs-fireflies-2026/), the other big transcription rivalry.

Granola's team features are growing, and it is superb for individuals and small teams who want great notes without administration. But if you need dozens of seats feeding a searchable, permissioned archive, Otter is further along.

## Pricing

Pricing is approximate and as of mid-2026; confirm current rates before subscribing.

- **Granola:** A free tier with a monthly meeting limit, plus a per-seat paid plan in the high-teens per month for unlimited meetings and more features.
- **Otter:** A free tier with a monthly transcription-minute cap, a Pro plan roughly in the $8-17 per month range, and Business tiers around $20-30 per user per month with team controls.

Both are affordable relative to the time they save. The deciding factor is usually features, not price.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Granola if you:**

- Want polished, structured notes with almost no friction
- Prefer no visible bot in your meetings
- Take a lot of in-person or sensitive calls
- Are an individual or small team focused on takeaways, not transcripts

**Choose Otter if you:**

- Need complete, searchable transcripts with speaker labels
- Run a team that wants a shared archive of every call
- Have sales or customer workflows built around recorded conversations
- Value a mature, established platform with broad integrations

Both are excellent, and they are not really competing for the same job. Granola is the best notepad in the room; Otter is the best system of record.
