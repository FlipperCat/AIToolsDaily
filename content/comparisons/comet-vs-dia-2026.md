The browser is quietly becoming the main battleground for AI assistants. Instead of copying text into a chatbot tab, AI browsers put the assistant inside the page — reading what you read, summarizing what you skim, and increasingly clicking buttons on your behalf.

Two of the most talked-about options in 2026 are **Perplexity Comet** and **Dia** from The Browser Company (the team that built Arc). We've reviewed both individually — see our [Comet review](/reviews/perplexity-comet-browser-review-2026/) and [Dia review](/reviews/dia-browser-review-2026/) — but the question we keep getting is simpler: if you're going to switch browsers once, which one deserves it?

Here's the head-to-head.

## Quick comparison

| Dimension | Perplexity Comet | Dia |
|---|---|---|
| Maker | Perplexity | The Browser Company |
| Engine | Chromium | Chromium |
| Core idea | Answer engine + agentic assistant | Chat with your tabs |
| Default search | Perplexity | Configurable (AI chat is the star) |
| Agentic actions | Yes — can navigate and act on pages | Limited — mostly reads and writes, not clicks |
| Personality | Research and task-completion | Writing, learning, everyday browsing |
| Extensions | Chrome extensions supported | Chrome extensions supported |
| Platforms | Desktop-first, rollout still expanding | Mac-first, rollout still expanding |
| Price | Free tier; advanced features tied to Perplexity plans | Free tier; Pro subscription for heavy AI use |

Both are Chromium underneath, so your bookmarks, passwords, and most extensions migrate easily from Chrome. The differences are all in what the AI layer does.

## Search and answers

**Comet** is Perplexity all the way down. The address bar is an answer engine: type a question and you get a cited answer, not ten blue links. If you already like Perplexity's approach to research — sourced answers, follow-up threads — Comet feels like that product finally living where it always belonged. Our [Perplexity review](/reviews/perplexity-ai-review-2026/) covers why the citation-first model works so well for research-heavy users.

**Dia** treats search as more of a conversation starter. Its signature move is chatting with your open tabs: highlight a paragraph and ask about it, or ask a question across seven tabs of research and get a synthesized answer. It's less about replacing Google and more about making sense of what you've already opened.

**Edge: Comet for finding things, Dia for digesting things.**

## Agentic capabilities

This is the biggest philosophical split.

Comet leans hard into the agent framing. The assistant can take multi-step actions — open pages, fill in details, work through a comparison shopping task, or handle tedious sequences like unsubscribing from newsletters. It doesn't always succeed; agentic browsing in 2026 is still the kind of thing you supervise rather than trust. Complex flows (multi-page checkouts, sites with aggressive bot detection) fail often enough that you'll learn where its ceiling is within a week. But when it works on well-structured sites, it genuinely saves time.

Dia is deliberately more conservative. Its AI mostly reads, summarizes, and writes — drafting an email based on a tab's contents, turning notes into a plan, explaining a concept from a page. Skills let you save reusable prompt workflows, which is quietly one of its best features. But it isn't trying to click around the web for you to nearly the same degree.

Which approach is "right" depends on your risk tolerance. Agents that act on real websites can act wrongly on real websites. Comet's ambition cuts both ways.

**Edge: Comet, with an asterisk about reliability.**

## Everyday browsing feel

Dia inherits The Browser Company's design DNA, and it shows. It's the more polished, opinionated product — clean interface, thoughtful touches, an AI that feels woven in rather than bolted on. Arc fans will recognize the taste level, even though Dia is intentionally simpler than Arc was.

Comet feels more utilitarian. It's a competent Chromium browser with an exceptional assistant attached. If you spend your day in research mode, that's fine. If you care about how your tools feel, Dia is the nicer place to live.

**Edge: Dia.**

## Privacy and trust

An AI browser sees everything you browse — that's the whole point, and also the whole concern.

Both companies let you control when the assistant can read pages, and both process a meaningful amount in the cloud, so neither is the choice for the truly privacy-paranoid (they should stick with a standard browser and a separate chatbot). Perplexity has faced more public scrutiny here, partly because Comet's agentic features require deeper access to be useful, and partly because Perplexity's ad ambitions make people nervous about incentives. Dia's simpler AI footprint means a somewhat smaller surface area of concern, though The Browser Company's acquisition by a larger enterprise software company in late 2025 introduced its own set of "what happens to this product now?" questions.

Read each privacy policy yourself if this matters to you. Neither is egregious; neither is a privacy product.

**Edge: roughly even, with different anxieties.**

## Pricing

As of August 2026, both follow a similar shape: free to download and use with meaningful limits, paid tiers for heavy AI usage.

- **Comet**: free with a Perplexity account; the most capable models, higher agent usage, and priority features track with Perplexity's paid plans (roughly $20/month for Pro, with a much pricier Max tier for power users).
- **Dia**: free tier with usage caps on AI features; a Pro subscription (also in the ~$20/month range) removes the ceilings.

Treat all of this as approximate — AI browser pricing has been reshuffled repeatedly, and both companies are still figuring out what the market will pay. If you already subscribe to Perplexity Pro, Comet is effectively a free upgrade, which is a genuine tiebreaker.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Perplexity Comet if:**

- You already use (or pay for) Perplexity and live in research mode
- You want an assistant that *does* things, not just explains things, and you're willing to supervise it
- Cited, sourced answers matter to you more than interface polish

**Choose Dia if:**

- Your AI use is mostly reading, writing, and synthesizing across tabs
- You value design quality and a calmer, less ambitious AI layer
- You were an Arc user mourning its deprioritization and want the successor

**Stick with Chrome + a chatbot tab if:**

- You're privacy-sensitive, or your workplace hasn't approved AI browsers
- Your browsing is mostly apps (Gmail, Figma, dashboards) where an AI layer adds little

Our honest take: Comet is the more consequential product, Dia is the more pleasant one. For most readers of this site — people who research tools, compare options, and dig through documentation — Comet's answer-engine-plus-agent combination delivers more practical value per day, and that's where we'd point you first. But Dia is the browser we'd recommend to a friend who just wants AI to make normal browsing a bit smarter, and it's the one that feels better crafted.

The good news: both are free to try, both import from Chrome in minutes, and switching back costs you nothing. Run each for a week and see which one you reach for.
