## ChatGPT Team vs Claude Team (2025)

Both OpenAI and Anthropic sell essentially the same thing to small companies: their consumer assistant, plus an admin console, plus a promise not to train on your data. Both land around the same price per seat. The marketing pages are nearly interchangeable.

The actual products are not. After running both across a small team's writing, research, and coding work, the differences that matter are not on the pricing page at all — they're in seat minimums, how each handles shared context, and what happens when someone hits a usage limit mid-task.

Here's the honest breakdown.

## Quick Comparison

| | ChatGPT Team | Claude Team |
|---|---|---|
| **Seat minimum** | 2 | 5 |
| **Approx. price/seat** | ~$25/mo annual, ~$30 monthly | ~$25/mo annual, ~$30 monthly |
| **Effective monthly floor** | ~$50 | ~$125 |
| **Trained on your data?** | No, by default | No, by default |
| **Shared workspace assets** | Custom GPTs shared to workspace | Projects with shared knowledge |
| **Context window** | Large; varies by model | Around 200K tokens |
| **Image generation** | Yes, built in | No |
| **Web browsing** | Yes | Yes |
| **Code execution / data analysis** | Yes, sandboxed Python | Analysis via Artifacts, more limited |
| **Voice conversation** | Yes | Limited |
| **Admin console** | Yes | Yes |
| **Best at** | Breadth of features | Long-document reasoning and writing |

## Price and the Seat Minimum Trap

Per-seat pricing is close enough to be a wash — both hover around $25 per user per month on annual billing, closer to $30 month-to-month.

The seat minimum is where they diverge, and for small teams it's decisive. ChatGPT Team starts at two seats. Claude Team starts at five. If you are three people, Claude Team means paying for five seats — roughly $125/month for three users, versus about $75 for the same three on ChatGPT Team.

That gap closes entirely once you're past five people. But a lot of teams evaluating these plans are two or three people, and for them the sticker price is misleading.

Worth noting: two individual Pro subscriptions cost about the same as two Team seats and give you nearly the same capability minus the admin console and shared workspace. If you don't need centralized billing, shared assets, or the data guarantee in writing, individual plans are not a bad answer.

## Model Quality and What Each Is Actually Good At

Both give you the vendor's current frontier models. The capability gap between them on general tasks is small enough that benchmark arguments are mostly noise at this point. Where they differ is temperament.

**Claude is better at long-form text.** Feed it a 60-page contract, a research paper, or a messy 40-message email thread and ask for analysis, and it holds the whole thing coherently. Its writing needs less editing — fewer lists where prose belongs, less throat-clearing, better instruction-following on tone. For teams whose work product *is* writing, this is the whole ballgame.

**ChatGPT is better at breadth.** Image generation, sandboxed Python for data analysis, voice conversation, and a much larger third-party ecosystem all live in one subscription. When someone on your team needs to make a chart from a CSV, generate a quick illustration, and draft an email in the same session, ChatGPT does all three without switching tools.

Reasoning modes exist on both sides now — extended thinking on Claude, dedicated reasoning models on ChatGPT — and both meaningfully improve hard analytical work at the cost of speed. Neither has a decisive edge for everyday team use.

## Shared Knowledge: Projects vs Custom GPTs

This is the feature that justifies a team plan at all, and the two approaches are philosophically different.

**ChatGPT Team** centers on **custom GPTs** published to your workspace. You configure instructions, upload reference files, optionally wire up actions to external APIs, and share the result. It's a *packaged tool* model: someone builds "Proposal Writer," everyone uses it.

**Claude Team** centers on **Projects**. A project holds shared knowledge — documents, style guides, codebases — that every conversation inside it can draw on. It's a *shared context* model: the knowledge lives in the workspace, and each person's conversations start already informed.

Which is better depends on your work. Packaged tools win for repeatable tasks with a fixed shape (support replies, standard document formats). Shared context wins for open-ended work against a large body of reference material (legal, research, consulting, engineering against a codebase).

Claude's project knowledge also benefits from the large context window — you can put substantially more reference material in play at once. We covered the mechanics of both approaches in more depth in [Claude Projects vs Custom GPTs](/compare/claude-projects-vs-custom-gpts/).

## Admin, Security, and Data Handling

Roughly at parity, and both are adequate for a small business:

- Workspace data excluded from model training by default on both
- Admin console with seat management, role assignment, and usage visibility
- SSO available, though often gated to larger or enterprise-tier plans
- SOC 2 Type II compliance on both sides
- Domain-based user provisioning

ChatGPT Team's admin tooling is somewhat more mature, reflecting a longer head start on enterprise sales. Claude's is functional but thinner on analytics.

Neither team tier gives you the full audit logging, retention controls, or compliance API that enterprise plans do. If you're in a regulated industry, you're likely looking at the enterprise tier on either side regardless, and pricing there is negotiated.

## Usage Limits and Throughput

Both plans advertise "higher limits" than individual tiers without publishing exact numbers, which is frustrating but standard.

In practice, Claude's limits are the ones people bump into. Long conversations with large documents in context consume budget fast, and hitting a cap mid-analysis with a multi-hour wait is a genuinely disruptive experience — it's the most common complaint from teams doing heavy document work.

ChatGPT's caps are generally more forgiving for typical usage patterns, partly because typical usage involves shorter conversations. Heavy users of the reasoning models will still find ceilings.

Budget for this honestly: if your team does deep document analysis all day, test the limits during a trial month before committing to an annual term.

## Ecosystem and Integration

ChatGPT wins on breadth. More third-party connectors, a larger community of shared custom GPTs, deeper integration into tools your team may already use, and a mature API story if you eventually build something.

Claude's ecosystem is smaller but has become interesting for technical teams, particularly around standardized tool connections that let the assistant reach into local files and services. If your team is engineering-heavy, that matters more than the raw connector count.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose ChatGPT Team if:**
- You have fewer than five people — the seat minimum alone decides it
- Your team's work spans writing, images, data analysis, and quick automation
- You want one subscription that covers the widest range of requests
- Non-technical staff need something approachable with obvious guardrails

**Choose Claude Team if:**
- Your core work product is written: legal, research, consulting, editorial, technical documentation
- You routinely work with long documents and need the model to hold all of it at once
- Output quality per token matters more than feature breadth — you want less editing, not more options
- You have five or more people, so the seat minimum is moot

**Choose neither if:** you're two people who just need better AI. Two individual Pro subscriptions cost about the same and the admin console won't change your life at that size.

**Consider both if:** you're above about ten people and split between writing-heavy and generalist roles. Running Claude seats for the writers and researchers and ChatGPT seats for everyone else is a real pattern, and at these prices it's cheaper than forcing one tool to be adequate at everything.

## The Bottom Line

ChatGPT Team is the safer default. It does more things, the seat minimum is friendlier, and no one on your team will be blocked by a missing feature.

Claude Team is the better *specialist* purchase. For teams whose output is prose and analysis, the quality difference compounds daily in editing time saved, and that's worth more than image generation you'll use twice a month.

Both offer trials. Run the same week of real work through each, count how many outputs you had to substantially rewrite, and let that number decide. Prices and limits shift frequently on both sides, so verify current terms before you commit to an annual plan.
