AI video went from novelty to usable tool in the first half of 2023. Two names come up constantly: **Runway**, whose Gen-2 model brought text-to-video to the public in June, and **Kaiber**, the animation tool behind a wave of stylized music videos, including Linkin Park's "Lost."

They get compared because both turn prompts into moving images. In practice they solve different problems. Runway is a broad creative suite with a general-purpose video model at its center. Kaiber is a focused tool for animating images and restyling footage, with a strong pull toward music. Here's how they stack up after putting both through the same set of projects.

## Quick Comparison

| | Runway | Kaiber |
|---|---|---|
| **Core strength** | Text/image-to-video (Gen-2), video-to-video (Gen-1), full editing suite | Animating images and restyling video with strong visual styles |
| **Typical clip length** | About 4 seconds per Gen-2 generation | Longer sequences, often matched to a full song |
| **Audio reactivity** | No | Yes, visuals can sync to an uploaded track |
| **Realism** | Better at realistic, cinematic shots | Leans stylized, painterly, and illustrative |
| **Editing tools** | Extensive (background removal, inpainting, slow motion, timeline editor) | Minimal |
| **Learning curve** | Moderate | Low |
| **Free option** | Limited one-time credits | Free trial |
| **Paid plans (approx., July 2023)** | ~$12-$28/user/month billed annually | ~$5-$30/month |

## What Each Tool Actually Does

**Runway** is a browser-based video platform with dozens of AI "magic tools" plus a timeline editor. The headline features right now are its two video models:

- **Gen-1** restyles existing footage. Upload a clip, give it a reference image or text prompt, and it re-renders the video in that style while keeping the original motion.
- **Gen-2** generates short clips from a text prompt, an image, or both. This is the one getting attention, since it's one of the first text-to-video models regular people can use.

Around those sit practical tools that have nothing to do with generation: green screen and background removal, inpainting to erase objects, frame interpolation, and more. For a closer look at the model itself, see our [first look at Runway Gen-2](/runway-gen-2-video-ai-2023/).

**Kaiber** works from a starting image or video and animates it through a style you describe. Its main modes:

- **Flipbook** creates frame-by-frame animation with a hand-drawn, morphing look, the style most people associate with Kaiber.
- **Motion** produces smoother, more continuous movement.
- **Transform** restyles an uploaded video, similar in spirit to Runway's Gen-1.

Kaiber's standout feature is **audio reactivity**. Upload a song and the animation pulses and shifts with the beat. For musicians, that one feature explains most of its popularity.

## Output Quality

For **realistic or cinematic shots**, like a drone sweep over a coastline or a slow push-in on a face, Runway's Gen-2 is ahead. Clips have more believable lighting and camera motion. They're still short, and they show the usual artifacts of current video models: melting hands, drifting faces, and objects that change shape mid-shot.

For **stylized animation**, Kaiber is more reliable. Because it doesn't try to be photoreal, its artifacts read as part of the aesthetic. A morphing oil-painting cityscape looks intentional in a way that a morphing "realistic" person doesn't.

Neither produces footage you'd mistake for camera work. Plan around their limits: short shots, abstract or stylized subjects, and edits that cut before things fall apart.

## Control and Workflow

**Runway gives you more control.** You can seed Gen-2 with an image to lock in composition, adjust settings, and bring clips straight into the editor to trim, sequence, and composite. If you're building a sequence from many short shots, doing it all in one place is a real advantage.

**Kaiber is simpler.** You pick a starting frame, write a prompt, choose a style, set a length, and generate. There's less to tune, which makes it fast for beginners and frustrating when you want a precise result. Getting a specific camera move or keeping a character consistent across scenes is mostly trial and error.

A common workflow for both is to create a strong still first, often in Midjourney, then animate it. Starting from a good image improves results in either tool more than any prompt trick. Our [Midjourney beginner guide](/midjourney-beginner-guide-2023/) covers that first step.

## Length and Music Videos

This is the biggest practical difference.

Runway's Gen-2 generates clips of about four seconds. A one-minute video means stitching together 15 or more generations and hoping the style stays consistent between them. It's doable, and Runway's editor makes it easier, but it takes time and credits.

Kaiber generates longer continuous sequences and can tie the length and motion to an audio track. For a lyric video, a visualizer, or a three-minute stylized music video, Kaiber gets you there with far less assembly. That's why so many independent artists have adopted it.

## Pricing

Both use credit systems, and prices are approximate as of July 2023:

**Runway**
- **Free:** a one-time allotment of credits, enough to try the tools, with watermarked exports
- **Standard:** about $12 per user per month billed annually (about $15 monthly)
- **Pro:** about $28 per user per month billed annually (about $35 monthly), with more credits and storage
- Gen-2 burns credits by the second, so heavy text-to-video use drains a plan quickly

**Kaiber**
- **Free trial:** a short trial with limited credits
- **Paid tiers:** roughly $5 to $30 per month depending on credit volume
- Longer videos and higher resolutions cost more credits

For occasional experiments, Kaiber is cheaper to get into. For regular production, Runway's price covers a whole editing suite, which matters if you'd otherwise pay for separate tools.

## Ease of Use

Kaiber wins for beginners. Its guided flow of image, prompt, style, and length produces something shareable on the first try.

Runway takes an afternoon to learn. The number of tools can be overwhelming, and Gen-2 prompting takes practice. Once it clicks, the extra control pays off.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Runway if you:**
- Want text-to-video or image-to-video with the most realistic output available to consumers right now
- Need editing tools like background removal, object removal, and a timeline alongside generation
- Are making short-form content, ads, or experimental film from many short shots
- Already work in video and want AI inside a familiar editing workflow

**Choose Kaiber if you:**
- Are a musician or make visuals for music: lyric videos, visualizers, stylized music videos
- Want a strong, painterly, animated look rather than realism
- Need longer continuous sequences without stitching clips together
- Want the fastest path from idea to finished clip with minimal learning

**Use both if** you make music content professionally. Kaiber handles audio-synced animated sections, and Runway handles cleanup, compositing, and realistic cutaway shots.

## Verdict

Runway is the more capable and versatile platform. Gen-2 is currently the most useful consumer text-to-video model, and the editing suite around it makes it a real production tool rather than a toy. Kaiber is the better specialist: if your project is set to music and you want a distinctive animated style, it gets you a finished video faster and cheaper.

Both are improving quickly, and the gaps described here could look different in a few months. For now, match the tool to the job: Runway for realism and control, Kaiber for style and music. If you need talking-head presenter videos instead, neither is the right tool, and something like [D-ID](/reviews/d-id-review-2023/) is a better fit.
