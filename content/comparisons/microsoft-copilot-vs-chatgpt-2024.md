Here's the strange thing about comparing Microsoft Copilot and ChatGPT in early 2024: under the hood, they're largely the same technology. Microsoft's assistant runs on OpenAI's GPT-4 family — the same models that power ChatGPT. So this isn't a comparison of intelligence. It's a comparison of packaging, pricing, and where each assistant actually lives in your day.

That turns out to matter a lot. One gives away GPT-4 for free; the other charges $20/month for it but wraps it in a far richer toolbox. Here's how they stack up.

## Quick comparison

| | Microsoft Copilot | ChatGPT |
|---|---|---|
| Underlying model | GPT-4 / GPT-4 Turbo | GPT-3.5 free; GPT-4 with Plus |
| Free tier | GPT-4 access (with limits at peak times) | GPT-3.5 only |
| Paid tier | Copilot Pro, ~$20/month | ChatGPT Plus, ~$20/month |
| Web access | Built in (Bing-grounded, with citations) | Included with Plus (Browse) |
| Image generation | DALL·E 3 via Image Creator, free | DALL·E 3, Plus only |
| Custom bots | Copilot GPTs (early, limited) | Custom GPTs + GPT Store |
| Data analysis / file uploads | Limited | Advanced Data Analysis with Plus |
| Office integration | Deep (Word, Excel, Outlook — extra cost) | None native |
| Where it lives | Windows, Edge, Bing, mobile apps | Web app, mobile apps |

## Model access: Copilot's free tier is the story

The single most disruptive fact in this matchup: **Copilot gives free users GPT-4-class answers.** ChatGPT's free tier is still GPT-3.5, which is noticeably weaker on reasoning, math, and nuanced writing. If you've only ever used free ChatGPT, free Copilot will feel like an upgrade — same lineage as what Plus subscribers pay for.

The asterisks: free Copilot can get deprioritized during peak hours, conversations have tighter turn limits, and the Bing-flavored wrapper sometimes steers answers toward search-results summaries when you wanted pure reasoning. Copilot Pro (~$20/month, launched in January 2024) removes the peak-time throttling and adds faster access to GPT-4 Turbo.

ChatGPT Plus, at the same ~$20/month, gets you GPT-4 with a message cap, plus the broader toolkit covered below.

## Web grounding and citations

Copilot is search-grounded by default: answers pull from live Bing results and come with footnoted links. For "what happened this week" or "compare current prices" questions, that's genuinely better than ChatGPT's default behavior, and the citations make quick fact-checking easier.

ChatGPT Plus can browse, but it treats browsing as an occasional tool rather than a default posture, and it's slower when it does. For research-style queries with sources, Copilot wins; dedicated tools like Perplexity push this further (see our [Perplexity vs ChatGPT comparison](/compare/perplexity-vs-chatgpt/)), but between these two, Copilot is the researcher.

The flip side: that same grounding makes Copilot worse at long creative or analytical sessions. It keeps wanting to be a search engine. ChatGPT is the better *conversation* — longer context, better memory of the thread, less tendency to bail out of a hard question by quoting a webpage.

## The paid-tier toolboxes

This is where ChatGPT Plus earns its keep. For the same ~$20:

- **Custom GPTs and the GPT Store.** Build your own assistant with instructions and knowledge files, or grab purpose-built ones from the store that opened in January. This is the closest thing to an app ecosystem in AI right now. (Our guide to [custom instructions](/chatgpt-custom-instructions-guide-2024/) covers the lighter-weight version.)
- **Advanced Data Analysis.** Upload spreadsheets and documents, get real Python-powered analysis and charts. Nothing on the Copilot side matches this today.
- **Voice conversations** on mobile that feel surprisingly natural.
- **DALL·E 3** image generation inline.

Copilot Pro counters with: priority GPT-4 Turbo access, more Image Creator "boosts" for faster DALL·E 3 generations, and — the big one — **Copilot inside Microsoft 365 apps** (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook) *if* you also have a Microsoft 365 Personal or Family subscription. Drafting a document in Word with AI in the margin, or asking Excel to explain a formula, is a real workflow change. But note the stacked cost: Copilot Pro plus a 365 subscription before any of it lights up.

Copilot GPTs (custom bots) exist but are early and shallow compared to OpenAI's.

## Writing, coding, and everyday quality

In our side-by-side testing, output quality is close — unsurprising, given the shared models — but with flavor differences:

- **Writing:** ChatGPT (GPT-4) produces longer, more structured drafts and follows detailed style instructions more faithfully. Copilot trends shorter and more conservative, with a tone that's harder to budge.
- **Coding:** Both are capable. ChatGPT Plus is more pleasant for iterative debugging because of the longer conversational context; Copilot's citations occasionally surface useful Stack Overflow threads. (For in-editor coding, GitHub Copilot is the different, better answer — a separate product despite the shared branding.)
- **Current events and lookups:** Copilot, clearly.
- **Long multi-step tasks:** ChatGPT, clearly.

It's worth saying that neither of these is the only game in town — Anthropic's Claude has become a serious third option for writing-heavy work (see [ChatGPT vs Claude](/compare/chatgpt-vs-claude/)), and Google relaunched Bard as Gemini with its own paid tier (our [Gemini Advanced review](/reviews/gemini-advanced-review/) covers it).

## Which should you choose?

**Choose free Copilot if you're paying nothing.** This one's easy: GPT-4-class answers for free beats GPT-3.5 for free. For students, casual users, and anyone who mostly asks questions and drafts short text, free Copilot is the best zero-dollar assistant available right now.

**Choose ChatGPT Plus if you're paying $20 for an AI to *work* in.** Custom GPTs, data analysis, voice, longer context, better creative stamina — the Plus toolbox is simply deeper, and power users will feel the difference daily.

**Choose Copilot Pro if you live in Microsoft Office.** If Word, Excel, and Outlook are where your workday happens and you already carry a Microsoft 365 subscription, AI inside those apps is worth more than any chat interface. That's the one user for whom Copilot Pro beats Plus.

**If you're a researcher who needs citations**, Copilot free may cover you — try it before paying anyone.

The uncomfortable truth for Microsoft: for most people weighing the two paid tiers, ChatGPT Plus is the stronger $20. The uncomfortable truth for OpenAI: Microsoft is giving away the thing OpenAI charges for, and for a large share of casual users, free Copilot means never needing to subscribe at all.
