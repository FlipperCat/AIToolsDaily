Paraphrasing tools have quietly become the most-used category of AI writing software. Not everyone wants an AI to write for them — but almost everyone has a sentence that reads clunky, an email that sounds too blunt, or a paragraph that needs to be half as long. That's the job Wordtune and QuillBot both compete for, and they approach it differently enough that the right pick is rarely obvious.

We've spent several weeks running both against the same emails, essays, and blog drafts. Here's where each one wins.

## Quick comparison

| Dimension | Wordtune | QuillBot |
|---|---|---|
| Core strength | Natural, context-aware sentence rewrites | Fast paraphrasing with many adjustable modes |
| Rewrite quality | Higher — reads like a human editor | Good, but can drift mechanical on long text |
| Modes / controls | Casual, formal, shorten, expand, Spices | Standard, Fluency, Formal, Simple, Creative, Expand, Shorten + synonym slider |
| Extra tools | Wordtune Read (summarizer), Spices suggestions | Grammar checker, summarizer, citation generator, Co-Writer |
| Free plan | ~10 rewrites per day | Paraphraser capped at 125 words at a time |
| Approximate price | ~$10–25/month depending on billing | ~$10/month, or roughly $4–5/month billed annually |
| Best for | Professionals polishing important writing | Students and high-volume rewriting on a budget |

## Rewriting quality: Wordtune sounds more human

Put the same awkward sentence through both tools and the difference shows up quickly. Wordtune's suggestions tend to restructure the sentence the way a good editor would — reordering clauses, cutting filler, keeping your voice. It offers several alternatives per sentence and lets you pick, which keeps you in control of tone.

QuillBot's paraphraser is more aggressive about word substitution. On short passages it's perfectly solid, and the synonym slider lets you dial how far it strays from your original. But push a long passage through at higher settings and you start seeing thesaurus-itis — technically-correct synonyms that no human would choose. For academic text where you want variation from a source, that's sometimes fine. For an email to your boss, Wordtune's output needs less cleanup.

That said, QuillBot's mode system is genuinely useful. Fluency mode is a light-touch cleanup that fixes grammar while barely changing wording, and Simple mode is great for making dense text readable. Wordtune's controls are fewer but better tuned.

## Beyond rewriting: two different bundles

Neither tool is just a paraphraser anymore, and the bundles are where they diverge most.

**Wordtune** added Spices this year — inline suggestions that go beyond rephrasing to *continue* your writing: add an example, a counterargument, a statistic, an analogy. It's a clever middle ground between a rewriter and a full generator like [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/). There's also Wordtune Read, a separate summarizer that condenses long documents and articles — useful, though it lives apart from the main editor.

**QuillBot** is building a full writing suite. Alongside the paraphraser you get a grammar checker, a summarizer, a citation generator (APA, MLA, Chicago), and Co-Writer, a document editor that pulls all the tools into one workspace. The grammar checker isn't at the level of [Grammarly](/reviews/grammarly-review/) — see our [Grammarly vs ProWritingAid comparison](/compare/grammarly-vs-prowritingaid/) for the dedicated options — but as a bundled extra it covers the basics.

For students, QuillBot's bundle is hard to beat: paraphrase a source, check the grammar, generate the citation, all in one tab. For working professionals, Wordtune's narrower, higher-quality focus tends to matter more.

## Where you can use them

Both offer Chrome extensions and web editors, and both work inside Google Docs. Wordtune's extension is the smoother of the two — it pops suggestions inline on most websites, including Gmail, LinkedIn, and Twitter, which makes it feel like an ambient editor rather than a destination site. QuillBot has Chrome and Microsoft Word extensions, and the Word integration is a real advantage if your life happens in Office.

One practical note: both tools send your text to their servers for processing. If you handle confidential material, check your organization's policy before pasting it into either.

## Free plans and pricing

The free plans are structured differently and it changes who each tool suits.

- **Wordtune free** gives you about 10 rewrites per day. That's enough to polish a few important sentences, not enough to work through an essay. Premium runs roughly $10/month billed annually, more month-to-month (approximate, as of May 2023).
- **QuillBot free** limits the paraphraser to 125 words at a time and restricts modes, but there's no daily cap — you can chunk a long document through it patiently. Premium is about $10/month, dropping to roughly $4–5/month on an annual plan (approximate, as of May 2023).

On pure value, QuillBot wins. Its annual plan costs less than half of Wordtune's, and the free tier is more usable for volume work. Wordtune is betting you'll pay a premium for output quality — which, for some users, is exactly right.

## Speed and workflow

QuillBot is the faster tool for bulk work: paste, pick a mode, done. Rewriting a whole section takes seconds. Wordtune works sentence-by-sentence, which is slower but more deliberate — you're reviewing alternatives rather than accepting a wholesale transformation. Which rhythm you prefer probably predicts which tool you'll stick with better than any feature list does.

## What about just using ChatGPT?

The question everyone asks in 2023. Yes, ChatGPT can paraphrase, and it's free. But the workflow is clunkier for editing: you're copy-pasting between tabs, writing prompts to control tone, and getting one rewrite at a time with no inline integration. Purpose-built rewriters earn their keep through interface, not intelligence — suggestions where you're already writing, multiple alternatives at a glance, one-click tone shifts. If you rewrite occasionally, ChatGPT is fine. If it's a daily habit, the dedicated tools pay for themselves in friction saved.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Wordtune if:**

- You write emails, proposals, or anything where tone and polish matter
- You want suggestions that preserve your voice rather than replace it
- Spices' "add an example / counterargument" prompts appeal to how you write
- You'd rather pay more for output you barely need to edit

**Choose QuillBot if:**

- You're a student or on a budget — the annual price is genuinely cheap
- You process a lot of text and want speed plus a no-daily-cap free tier
- The bundled grammar checker, summarizer, and citation generator would replace other tabs
- You live in Microsoft Word

**Our verdict:** Wordtune is the better rewriter; QuillBot is the better deal. For professional writing where quality per sentence matters, Wordtune's suggestions are a class above and worth the premium. For everyone else — especially students — QuillBot's combination of price, volume, and bundled tools makes it the sensible default.
