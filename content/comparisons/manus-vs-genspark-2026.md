The "general-purpose AI agent" category went from demo-week hype to actual product shelf in about eighteen months, and two names defined it: **Manus**, whose invite-only launch in early 2025 briefly broke the AI internet, and **Genspark**, which answered weeks later with its Super Agent and a suite of output tools. Both promise the same headline: give the agent a goal, walk away, come back to finished work.

We've run both on real tasks — research reports, competitor analysis, slide decks, data cleanup — and they are more different than their pitches suggest. Here's the honest comparison.

## Quick comparison

| | Manus | Genspark |
|---|---|---|
| **Core approach** | Autonomous agent in a cloud computer (browser, code, files) | Mixture-of-agents routing across models + built-in tools |
| **Signature outputs** | Research reports, working files, small web apps/dashboards | AI Slides, AI Sheets, reports, calls/booking tasks |
| **Session style** | Long-running, watchable, replayable | Faster, more task-shaped |
| **Speed** | Slower — thoroughness over turnaround | Generally faster per task |
| **Pricing model** | Credits; ~$39/mo entry, ~$199/mo pro (approx.) | Free daily credits; Plus ~$25/mo (approx.) |
| **Free tier** | Limited trial credits | Yes, recurring daily credits |
| **Best for** | Deep multi-step projects with file deliverables | Everyday delegated tasks, decks and spreadsheets |

## How they actually work

**Manus** gives its agent a full cloud computer. When you hand it a goal — "research these 12 competitors and build me a comparison site" — it opens a browser, visits pages, writes and runs code, creates files, and assembles deliverables, all visible in a live session you can watch or replay later. That replay is more than a party trick: it's how you audit *how* the agent reached its conclusions, which matters when you're deciding whether to trust them.

**Genspark** takes a routing approach: its Super Agent decomposes your task and farms the pieces out across multiple models and purpose-built toolkits — slides, spreadsheets, search, even placing phone calls for things like reservations. You see a plan and progress, but the experience is more "capable assistant with power tools" than "watching a remote worker."

The philosophical difference shapes everything downstream: Manus optimizes for *depth of autonomy*, Genspark for *breadth of useful output, fast*.

## Output quality

For **deep research and multi-step projects**, Manus produces the more impressive ceiling. Given a meaty task and enough credits, it returns organized reports with working artifacts — spreadsheets it built from scraped data, a functioning dashboard, cited findings. When it lands, it genuinely feels like work you'd have paid a junior analyst for. When it misses, it misses expensively: a wrong turn early in a long session burns credits on a confidently wrong deliverable.

For **structured business outputs**, Genspark's dedicated tools give it the edge. AI Slides produces presentable decks with less cleanup than a general agent gluing a deck together from scratch, and AI Sheets handles data tasks in a familiar grid. The outputs are more predictable — a narrower band between best and worst case.

Both share the category's chronic weakness: surface-level sourcing. Each will occasionally lean on thin web sources and present the result with unearned confidence. Whatever agent you use, spot-check anything that feeds a real decision — the same discipline we recommended in our [Operator review](/openai-operator-review-2026/) applies across the whole category.

## Speed and reliability

Genspark is usually the faster path from request to usable output — minutes rather than the better part of an hour. Manus sessions run long by design; that's tolerable for a task you'd otherwise spend a day on and frustrating for something you needed in ten minutes.

Reliability is the category's tax. Both fail on some fraction of complex tasks — dead-ending on a site that blocks automation, misreading intent, or delivering something adjacent to what you asked. Genspark's failures tend to be cheaper and faster to detect. Manus's failures cost more credits and more waiting, though the replay makes diagnosis easier.

## Pricing

Both use credit systems, and both change their schemes often enough that you should treat the following as approximate as of August 2026.

**Manus** offers limited free credits to trial, with paid plans starting around **$39/month** and a pro tier around **$199/month** for heavier credit allowances. Complex sessions consume credits quickly — a substantial research task can eat a meaningful chunk of a starter plan's monthly budget. Price it against the value of the task, not against other subscriptions.

**Genspark** stands out for its **recurring free daily credits** — enough to actually use it lightly every day, not just trial it once. The Plus tier around **$25/month** unlocks heavier use. For experimenting with what agents can do without a purchase decision, Genspark is the lowest-friction entry in the category.

If your agent needs are really *research* needs, also consider whether a dedicated deep-research mode in a chatbot subscription you already pay for covers you — our [Gemini Deep Research review](/reviews/gemini-deep-research-review-2025/) is a good reality check before adding another subscription.

## Trust, transparency, and control

Manus wins on auditability: the session replay shows every page visited and every step taken, which is the closest this category comes to showing its work. Genspark shows plans and progress, but the mixture-of-agents routing is inherently more opaque — you see what came back, less of how.

Neither should be pointed, unsupervised, at anything involving your credentials, purchases, or outbound communication without reading the current permissions model carefully. The category has improved its guardrails, but "autonomous" and "unattended" are still different things.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Manus if:**

- Your tasks are genuinely multi-step projects — market research, data collection and analysis, building small tools — where depth beats turnaround
- You want file deliverables and working artifacts, not just answers
- You value being able to audit how the agent got there
- You'll use it enough to justify $39+/month in credits

**Choose Genspark if:**

- Your delegated work skews toward decks, spreadsheets, and everyday research
- You want results in minutes and predictable output quality
- You want a real free tier to learn the category before paying
- One subscription covering many light tasks fits you better than credits burned on a few heavy ones

**Honest defaults:** most individual users should start with Genspark's free daily credits and upgrade only if they hit the ceiling weekly. Most people with actual analyst-shaped workloads — founders, consultants, researchers — should trial Manus on one real project and judge the deliverable against what their time is worth. And if what you really wanted was an agent for your *existing* business workflows rather than open-ended tasks, purpose-built platforms are often the better fit — see our [Lindy review](/lindy-ai-agents-review-2026/) for that end of the spectrum.

The category is real now. Neither tool replaces judgment, but both, pointed at the right tasks, return more than they cost — which is more than could be said in the demo-week days.
