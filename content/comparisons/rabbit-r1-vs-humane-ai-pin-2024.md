Two dedicated AI gadgets were supposed to make 2024 the year we started looking at our phones less. The **Humane AI Pin** is a screenless wearable that clips to your shirt. The **Rabbit R1** is a small orange handheld with a scroll wheel and a push-to-talk button. Both shipped this spring, and both got rough reviews.

We've spent time with both. This comparison covers what each does well, where each falls short, and whether either is worth buying now, with Apple and OpenAI both moving to put better AI assistants on phones people already own.

## Quick Comparison

| | Humane AI Pin | Rabbit R1 |
|---|---|---|
| Price (as of June 2024) | $699 + ~$24/month subscription | $199, no required subscription |
| Form factor | Screenless wearable, magnetic clip | Handheld with small touchscreen |
| Display | Laser projection onto your palm | ~2.9" screen |
| Input | Voice, touchpad, gestures | Push-to-talk button, scroll wheel, touchscreen |
| Camera | Fixed, front-facing | Rotating camera |
| Connectivity | Built-in cellular (via the subscription) | Wi-Fi or your own SIM |
| Phone calls / texts | Yes, with its own number | No |
| App actions | Limited built-in services | Spotify, Uber, DoorDash, Midjourney at launch |
| Biggest complaints | Heat, battery life, latency, price | Limited usefulness, battery (partly fixed), "why not an app?" |

## Design and Hardware

**Humane AI Pin.** The hardware looks great. It attaches magnetically to clothing with a battery "booster" behind the fabric, and the "Laser Ink" display projects a green interface onto your palm. In practice the projection is hard to read in bright light, palm gestures take practice, and many reviewers reported the Pin getting warm during heavy use. A wearable that heats up against your chest is a hard sell.

**Rabbit R1.** Designed with Teenage Engineering, the R1 is playful and easy to grip. The push-to-talk button is simple and reliable, and the rotating camera works for both "look at this" questions and selfies. The scroll wheel works well, though the small touchscreen is used for so little that it can feel like an afterthought. Build quality matches the price: fine, not premium.

**Edge: Rabbit R1.** It's less ambitious, but you actually want to pick it up.

## The AI Experience

Both devices send your request to cloud AI models and read the answer back. That's where most of the frustration comes from.

**Humane AI Pin** answers general knowledge questions, does live translation, describes what its camera sees, and handles messages and calls. When it works, asking a question without taking out a phone feels new. The problem is **latency**. Responses often take several seconds, sometimes longer, and that kills the advantage of not reaching for your phone. Reviewers also reported confidently wrong answers, especially for visual questions.

**Rabbit R1** is generally quicker to respond, and its vision features, such as describing a scene or reading a menu, are fun. Rabbit partnered with Perplexity for up-to-date answers (early buyers also got a year of Perplexity Pro), so current-events questions do better than you'd expect. But it shares the same basic limits: it misunderstands requests, has no context about your life, and can't take over when the AI fails.

**Edge: Rabbit R1**, mostly on speed.

## Doing Things (the Real Promise)

The pitch for both devices is that they *do things for you* rather than just answer questions.

**Rabbit** has made its "Large Action Model" the center of its marketing: an AI that learns to operate apps the way a person would. At launch, that meant connecting a handful of services through its web portal: Spotify, Uber, DoorDash, and Midjourney. Results have been mixed. Some requests work, many are slow or fail, and the bigger "teach it any app" vision hasn't shipped yet. Reports that the R1's software could run as an ordinary Android app on a phone added to the skepticism.

**Humane** takes a different approach: built-in capabilities instead of app control. It handles calls, texts, notes, photos, music through Tidal, and translation. But it doesn't connect with the apps most people rely on, and without a screen even simple tasks like checking what you just captured are awkward.

**Edge: Neither.** Rabbit promises more and Humane does a few things more reliably, but neither replaces opening an app on your phone.

## Battery and Reliability

Battery life was a common complaint about both at launch.

- **Humane AI Pin:** the booster system lets you hot-swap batteries, which is clever, but you'll need it. Many reviewers found it hard to get through a full day. In early June, Humane also told owners to stop using the charging case because of a safety issue with a battery cell from a supplier, which hurt confidence further.
- **Rabbit R1:** early units drained fast even when idle. A software update improved standby battery life noticeably, and the device is easy to charge, but it still isn't an all-day device under heavy use.

**Edge: Rabbit R1**, after the update.

## Price and Ongoing Cost

This is where the R1 clearly wins.

- **Humane AI Pin:** $699 up front plus roughly $24/month for the required subscription, which covers cellular service and cloud features. Over two years that's well over $1,200, more than most flagship phones.
- **Rabbit R1:** $199, no required subscription. Bring your own SIM or use Wi-Fi.

**Edge: Rabbit R1**, by a wide margin.

## The Real Competitor: Your Phone

Here's the uncomfortable part for both companies. The phone in your pocket is getting smarter quickly:

- OpenAI's [GPT-4o announcement](/news/news-032-gpt4o-announcement/) showed fast, natural voice conversation with live camera input, the same experience these gadgets are trying to deliver, in an app on a device you already own.
- Apple just announced [Apple Intelligence](/news/news-046-apple-intelligence/), with a smarter Siri that can act within apps and an option to hand questions to ChatGPT.
- Assistants like [Meta AI](/reviews/meta-ai-review-2024/) are now built into apps people use every day, and Meta's camera glasses offer hands-free AI in a more familiar form factor.

A dedicated device needs to be much faster, more convenient, or more capable than the phone app. Right now, neither the Pin nor the R1 is.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Buy the Rabbit R1 if:**
- You're an early adopter who enjoys experimental hardware and accepts that it may not work well.
- You want a fun, low-cost AI gadget, ideally one that also gets you a year of Perplexity Pro.
- You're curious about where AI agents and app automation are going and don't mind waiting for updates.

**Buy the Humane AI Pin if:**
- You strongly want a screenless, hands-free wearable and can live with heat, battery, and speed limits.
- The cost, including the monthly fee, doesn't matter much to you.
- You mainly want quick questions, translation, and capture rather than app control.

**Skip both if:**
- You want something that reliably saves time today. A good phone assistant app does more, faster, for free or less than either device costs.

**Our verdict:** the **Rabbit R1** is the better buy because it costs far less and is more fun to use. But "better" is relative here. Both are first-generation products whose promises are ahead of the software. If you're curious, the R1 is a $199 experiment. The Humane AI Pin is a $699-plus-subscription experiment, and that's much harder to justify. For most people, the smarter move is to wait and see what this year's phone AI updates can do.
