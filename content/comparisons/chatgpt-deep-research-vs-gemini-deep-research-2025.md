The hottest feature category of early 2025 isn't chat — it's the **agentic research report**. Both OpenAI and Google now ship a "Deep Research" mode that takes a question, spends anywhere from five to thirty minutes browsing dozens or hundreds of sources, and returns a long, cited report instead of a quick answer.

Same name, same promise. After running both on the same set of real research tasks — market landscapes, technical literature summaries, purchasing decisions, and competitive analyses — the differences are bigger than the branding suggests.

## At a glance

| | ChatGPT Deep Research | Gemini Deep Research |
|---|---|---|
| **Provider** | OpenAI | Google |
| **Launched** | February 2025 | December 2024 |
| **Underlying approach** | o3-based reasoning agent | Gemini reasoning model + Google Search infrastructure |
| **Typical run time** | ~10–30 minutes | ~5–15 minutes |
| **Report style** | Dense, analytical, long | Broad, structured, more survey-like |
| **Plan editing** | Refines via clarifying questions first | Shows an editable research plan before running |
| **Access** | Plus (small monthly quota), Pro (much larger quota) | Gemini Advanced (~$20/mo); limited free trial access rolling out |
| **Citations** | Inline, generally precise | Comprehensive source list, sometimes looser inline linking |
| **Best at** | Depth, analysis, technical synthesis | Breadth, speed, source coverage |

## Report quality and depth

**ChatGPT Deep Research** reads like it was written by a sharp analyst who actually formed an opinion. Reports are long — often several thousand words — with genuine synthesis: it compares claims across sources, flags contradictions, and draws conclusions rather than just cataloguing findings. On a technical question (comparing vector database architectures), it went several layers deeper than I expected, surfacing tradeoffs that would have taken me a day of reading to assemble.

**Gemini Deep Research** produces well-organized surveys. It's excellent at mapping a topic — here are the players, here are the categories, here's what each source says — but it editorializes less. On the same vector database task it covered more products than ChatGPT did, yet the analysis stayed closer to summarization. For getting oriented in an unfamiliar domain fast, that's exactly what you want; for a decision-ready brief, it leaves more thinking to you.

**Winner: ChatGPT** for analysis and depth; **Gemini** if what you need is a comprehensive map rather than a verdict.

## Sources and citations

Gemini's superpower is the browsing muscle behind it — it routinely touches far more pages per run, and its coverage of recent web content is excellent. The full source list on a typical report is long enough to be a research artifact in itself. The weakness: inline citation precision. Occasionally a specific claim traces back to a source that only loosely supports it.

ChatGPT visits fewer sources but cites more surgically — claims link to the specific supporting page, which makes verification faster. It's also more willing to lean on primary sources (papers, documentation, filings) over SEO content. The flip side: on very fresh topics, its sweeps sometimes miss things Gemini catches.

Both still hallucinate occasionally at the *synthesis* layer — a correct source, slightly overstated conclusion. Spot-check anything that will end up in front of a client. Our guide to [using AI for research](/how-to-use-ai-for-research/) covers verification workflows that apply directly here.

## Speed and workflow

Gemini is meaningfully faster — many runs finish inside ten minutes — and its **editable research plan** is a genuinely great design choice. Before browsing, it shows you the outline of what it intends to investigate; you can strike sections or redirect emphasis. That one interaction prevents most wasted runs.

ChatGPT instead asks clarifying questions up front, then disappears for what can be half an hour. The questions are good, but you can't see or steer the plan mid-flight, and a misunderstood brief costs you a long wait and a quota slot.

**Winner: Gemini**, comfortably, on workflow.

## Limits and pricing

As of April 2025 (approximate — both companies have already adjusted these once):

- **ChatGPT**: Plus subscribers (~$20/month) get a small number of Deep Research runs per month — enough to make each one feel precious. The Pro tier (~$200/month) raises the quota to genuinely-usable-daily territory. That pricing gap is hard to swallow for individuals.
- **Gemini**: Deep Research is included with Gemini Advanced (~$20/month) with much more generous practical limits, and Google has begun letting free users sample it.

On value per dollar, this isn't close: **Gemini** delivers 80% of the capability at a tenth of the cost of unrestricted access. Worth noting [Perplexity](/reviews/perplexity-review/) also shipped its own deep research mode recently at an aggressive price point — see our [Perplexity vs ChatGPT comparison](/compare/perplexity-vs-chatgpt/) for how the lighter-weight option stacks up.

## Where each one stumbles

**ChatGPT Deep Research:**
- Quota anxiety on Plus makes you hoard runs for "worthy" questions.
- Long runs occasionally overthink simple questions into 4,000-word essays.
- Slower to include very recent sources on fast-moving topics.

**Gemini Deep Research:**
- Synthesis is shallower; you'll do more of the final thinking.
- Inline citations need more spot-checking against the claims they support.
- Report prose can be repetitive — sections sometimes restate each other.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose ChatGPT Deep Research if:**
- You need decision-ready analysis, not just organized information.
- Your questions are technical or require reconciling conflicting sources.
- You're on Pro anyway, or your handful of monthly Plus runs maps to a handful of genuinely hard questions.

**Choose Gemini Deep Research if:**
- You run research frequently and need volume at a sane price.
- Speed and steering (that editable plan) matter to your workflow.
- Your typical task is "map this space for me" — competitor scans, topic orientation, buying research.

**Use both if:** you can justify two subscriptions, they layer beautifully — Gemini for the broad sweep, ChatGPT for deep dives on what the sweep surfaces. And for academic work specifically, pair either with the workflow in our [literature review with AI guide](/tutorials/how-to-do-a-literature-review-with-ai-2025/), because neither tool's citations are yet trustworthy enough to paste into a bibliography unchecked.

My own default after a month: Gemini for the first pass on everything, ChatGPT Deep Research saved for the two or three questions a month where the answer actually changes a decision. The agentic research category is barely six months old and already genuinely useful — the gap between these two will be worth rechecking by fall.
