If you make podcasts or video content, two names come up constantly in 2024: **Descript** and **Riverside**. They overlap enough to be confusing but are built around different core strengths. Descript is an editing powerhouse that treats audio and video like a text document. Riverside is a recording platform obsessed with capturing broadcast-quality audio and video, even over shaky internet. This comparison breaks down where each one wins so you can pick without buyer's remorse.

## Quick Comparison

| Dimension | Descript | Riverside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core strength | Editing by editing text | High-quality remote recording |
| Recording quality | Good, screen + mic | Excellent, local track recording |
| Transcription | Fast, editable, core feature | Included, solid |
| AI features | Overdub, filler-word removal, Studio Sound | AI show notes, clip generation |
| Video editing | Full timeline + text-based | Lighter, recording-focused |
| Best for | Editing and repurposing | Recording remote interviews |
| Learning curve | Moderate | Easy |
| Pricing model | Free tier + paid plans | Free tier + paid plans |

## Recording: Riverside's Home Turf

Riverside was built to solve one painful problem: recording remote guests without the audio turning to mush when someone's Wi-Fi hiccups. It does this by recording each participant's audio and video **locally** on their own device, then uploading the high-quality files afterward, rather than relying on the compressed stream you hear during the call. The result is up to broadcast-grade tracks even if the live connection was rough.

For interview-based shows with remote guests, this is a genuine advantage. You get separate tracks per speaker, which makes later editing far cleaner, and the video quality holds up well enough for YouTube.

Descript can record too — including screen recordings, which are great for tutorials — but recording is not its reason for existing. If your workflow is "get two people on a call and capture it perfectly," Riverside is the more reassuring choice.

## Editing: Descript Runs Away With It

This is where the tables turn completely. Descript transcribes your recording and lets you edit the audio or video by editing the **transcript**. Delete a sentence of text and the corresponding audio disappears. Cut filler words like "um" and "uh" across an entire episode with one click. It genuinely changes how fast you can edit, and it is why so many creators keep Descript in their stack even if they record elsewhere. We go deeper on it in our [Descript vs CapCut comparison](/compare/descript-vs-capcut/).

Descript's AI extras pile on top: **Studio Sound** cleans up muddy audio, **Overdub** can generate speech in a cloned voice to fix a flubbed word, and its filler-word and pause removal save hours. Riverside has added AI features too — automatic show notes, transcripts, and clip generation — but its editing environment is lighter and more focused on trimming and quick clips than full production.

If your bottleneck is editing and repurposing, Descript is the clear winner. Many creators pair the two, which we'll come back to.

## Transcription and AI Show Notes

Both tools transcribe, and both do it well enough for practical use. Descript's transcription is central to the whole product, so it is deeply integrated and immediately editable. Riverside's transcription is a supporting feature but perfectly serviceable, and its AI show-note and clip tools are handy for getting social snippets out the door quickly.

For accuracy-critical transcription work you might still lean on dedicated tools — see our [best AI transcription tools](/best-ai-transcription-tools/) roundup — but for most podcasters, either app's built-in transcription is good enough.

One practical difference: because Descript's transcript *is* the editing surface, cleaning up a messy transcript directly improves your edit. In Riverside, the transcript is more of a byproduct you use for show notes and clips. Neither approach is wrong — it just reflects the tools' different centers of gravity. If you plan to publish full transcripts alongside episodes for accessibility or SEO, Descript's tighter integration will save you a step.

## Video Quality and Repurposing

Riverside captures cleaner source video thanks to local recording, which matters if polished talking-head footage is your goal. Descript, meanwhile, is stronger at *turning* footage into finished, repurposed content — pulling clips, adding captions, and cutting a long episode into shorts using the text-based workflow.

So the honest framing is: Riverside gives you better raw material, Descript does more with it. That is exactly why the two are so often used together.

## Ease of Use

Riverside is the easier tool to pick up. You send a guest a link, they join in a browser, and you hit record. There is not much to learn.

Descript has a steeper (though still reasonable) learning curve because it does more. The text-based editing concept clicks quickly, but the full feature set — Overdub, Studio Sound, multitrack video, publishing — takes time to explore. It is worth it if editing is a big part of your week.

## Pricing

As of mid-2024, both offer a **free tier** with limits, then tiered paid plans that scale with features and usage (recording hours, transcription minutes, higher export quality, and team seats). Descript's paid plans unlock more AI editing capacity and remove watermarks; Riverside's paid plans raise recording hours and unlock higher-resolution exports and separate track downloads. Prices here are approximate and change over time, so check each site's current plans before subscribing. Neither is dramatically cheaper than the other for a typical solo creator — the decision should come down to features, not a few dollars.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Riverside if** your top priority is recording remote interviews at the highest quality. If your show is guest-heavy, or you produce video where clean source footage matters, Riverside's local recording is the safer foundation.

**Choose Descript if** editing and repurposing are your biggest time sinks. Solo podcasters, course creators, and anyone cutting long recordings into clips will get more day-to-day value from Descript's text-based editing and AI cleanup tools.

**Use both if** you can swing it. A very common 2024 workflow is: **record in Riverside** for pristine tracks, then **edit in Descript** for speed. It is more expensive, but for creators who care about both capture quality and editing efficiency, it is the best of both worlds.
