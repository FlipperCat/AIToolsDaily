Removing a background used to mean ten minutes with the Pen tool in Photoshop. Now it takes a few seconds, and two names come up more than any others: **Remove.bg**, the single-purpose cutout tool that made one-click background removal popular, and **Photoroom**, a mobile-first editor that has grown into a full product-photo studio.

Both are good at the core job. They're built for different people, though. We ran both through a mix of product shots, portraits, and awkward edge cases (hair, glass, fur, and transparent packaging) to see where each one is the better choice.

If you want our standalone take on one of them, see the [Remove.bg review](/reviews/remove-bg-review-2023/).

## Quick Comparison

| | Photoroom | Remove.bg |
|---|---|---|
| Core focus | Product photo editor and studio | Background removal only |
| Platforms | iOS, Android, web | Web, desktop app, plugins |
| Cutout quality | Excellent on products | Excellent on people and hair |
| AI backgrounds | Yes, generated scenes | Basic colors and stock backgrounds |
| Batch processing | Yes (paid) | Yes (desktop app, API) |
| Templates and resizing | Yes, marketplace-ready | Minimal |
| API | Yes | Yes, mature and widely integrated |
| Pricing model | Subscription | Credits or subscription |
| Best for | Ecommerce sellers, social content | Designers, developers, high volume |

## Cutout Quality

This is the dimension most people care about, and the honest answer is that both are very good. On a clean product shot (a sneaker on a kitchen counter, a candle on a wooden table) we'd struggle to pick a winner in a blind test.

The differences appear at the edges:

- **Hair and fur:** Remove.bg had a slight edge on portraits and pets. Flyaway strands were preserved more naturally and there was less of the "cut-out sticker" halo.
- **Products with soft shadows:** Photoroom tended to handle these better, partly because it can re-create a clean shadow afterward instead of trying to keep the original one.
- **Glass and transparent items:** Both struggled. Expect to do some manual touch-up on bottles, glassware, and clear packaging whichever tool you pick.
- **Busy backgrounds:** Both did well, but Remove.bg occasionally kept small background fragments around complex objects, and Photoroom occasionally clipped thin parts like straps and cables.

Both include manual erase and restore brushes for fixing mistakes. Photoroom's are easier to use on a phone. Remove.bg's editor is more precise with a mouse.

## Editing After the Cutout

This is where the two tools stop being similar.

**Remove.bg** mostly stops at the cutout. You can drop in a solid color, blur the original background, or choose a stock photo background. Then you download and do the rest in Photoshop, Canva, Figma, or whatever you already use. For designers, that's a feature: it does one job and hands off cleanly.

**Photoroom** treats the cutout as step one. After removing the background you can:

- Generate an **AI background** from a text prompt or preset scene (marble countertop, beach, studio gradient)
- Add **AI shadows** and reflections so the product looks grounded rather than pasted on
- Apply **templates** sized for Instagram, Etsy, eBay, Amazon, Shopify, and other marketplaces
- Retouch small blemishes and remove unwanted objects
- Add text, logos, and stickers for social posts

For a small seller shooting on a phone, that saves a whole round-trip into a second app. For a designer with an established Photoshop workflow, most of it is redundant.

## Batch Processing and Volume

If you're processing a whole catalog, this matters more than cutout quality.

**Remove.bg** has a desktop app for batch processing a folder of images, plus an API that's been around long enough to be built into many tools and plugins. If you have hundreds or thousands of images a month, or want removal to happen automatically inside another system, Remove.bg is the more established option.

**Photoroom** offers batch editing in its paid plan. You can apply the same cutout, background, and template to a set of product shots in one go, which is great for keeping a store catalog visually consistent. It also has an API, which has become a serious option for developers building ecommerce tools.

Our take: for "remove 2,000 backgrounds and export PNGs," choose Remove.bg. For "make 40 product photos look like one consistent catalog," choose Photoroom.

## Ease of Use

Photoroom is clearly built for phones. You take a photo, it removes the background almost instantly, and you swipe through background and template options. Someone with no design experience can produce a decent listing image in under a minute.

Remove.bg is even simpler, because it does less. You upload an image, the background disappears, and you download the result. There's almost nothing to learn. The trade-off is that you need another tool to finish the image.

## Integrations

Remove.bg plugs into a wide range of workflows, including a Photoshop plugin, design tool integrations, automation platforms, and the desktop app. If you live in Adobe tools or run automated pipelines, that matters.

Photoroom's integrations focus on selling: export presets and sizing for major marketplaces, plus its API. It fits less naturally into a traditional design studio's workflow.

## Pricing (approximate, as of October 2024)

**Photoroom**
- **Free:** Background removal and basic editing, with limits and watermarking on some exports and features.
- **Pro:** Roughly $10–13 per month, or less when billed annually. Unlocks watermark-free exports, AI backgrounds, batch editing, and higher-resolution output.
- **Team/Business options:** Priced higher, with shared brand kits and collaboration.

**Remove.bg**
- **Free:** Unlimited low-resolution preview downloads.
- **Subscription:** Roughly $9 per month for a modest monthly bundle of full-resolution credits, with larger bundles at lower per-image cost.
- **Pay-as-you-go:** Credit packs for occasional use. These cost more per image than a subscription but the credits don't expire monthly.

A rough rule: if you process a steady number of images every month, compare the cost per image. If you edit sporadically, Remove.bg's credit packs can work out cheaper. If you need finished, styled product photos, Photoroom's flat subscription is usually better value because it replaces a second editing tool.

Both companies adjust plans regularly, so confirm current pricing before you commit.

## Privacy and Rights

Both services process images in the cloud. Check each provider's data-retention policy before uploading anything sensitive, such as unreleased products, client photos, or images of children. For commercial use, the cutout itself is your image. With AI-generated backgrounds, review Photoroom's terms for how generated content can be used, especially in paid ads.

## Alternatives Worth Knowing

- **[Clipdrop](/reviews/clipdrop-review-2023/):** Background removal plus relighting, upscaling, and cleanup tools in one place.
- **Canva's background remover:** Convenient if you already pay for Canva Pro and design there anyway.
- **Adobe Photoshop's Remove Background and Generative Fill:** The best choice if you need full control and already have a Creative Cloud subscription.
- **[Cleanup.pictures](/reviews/cleanup-pictures-review-2023/):** For removing objects and blemishes rather than whole backgrounds.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Photoroom if:**
- You sell on Etsy, eBay, Amazon, Shopify, Depop, or Poshmark and shoot products on your phone
- You want finished, on-brand listing images, not just transparent PNGs
- You need consistent catalog styling across many products
- You don't have a separate design tool or the time to learn one

**Choose Remove.bg if:**
- You're a designer who finishes images in Photoshop, Figma, or Canva
- You process high volumes and care about cost per image
- You need a reliable API for an automated pipeline
- Your images are mostly people, pets, or subjects with tricky hair and fur

**Use both if:** you run a store with high volume. Some sellers use Remove.bg's API for bulk cutouts and Photoroom for hero images and social posts that need styling.

Neither tool is a bad choice. The question is whether you want a fast **cutout utility** (Remove.bg) or a **product-photo studio** (Photoroom).
