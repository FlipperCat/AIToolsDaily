If your calendar looks like a game of Tetris played by other people, you've probably come across Reclaim.ai and Clockwise. Both connect to Google Calendar, both promise to carve out time for deep work, and both do it by moving events around automatically.

They start from different places, though. **Reclaim** is built around *your* priorities: habits, tasks, and routines that it defends on your calendar. **Clockwise** is built around *your team*: it rearranges flexible meetings across several people's calendars so everyone gets longer stretches of uninterrupted time.

We ran each one for three weeks on real work calendars. One tester was an individual contributor with a task-heavy week, and the other was a manager with more than 20 meetings a week. Here's how the two compare once the novelty wears off.

## Quick comparison

## How each one works

### Reclaim: your priorities as flexible events

Reclaim's building blocks are **Habits** and **Tasks**. A Habit is a recurring block with a range, not a fixed time. For example, "Lunch, 45–60 minutes, sometime between 11:30 and 2:00" or "Deep work, 2 hours, mornings preferred." Reclaim finds a slot and puts it on your calendar.

The clever part is how it defends that slot. Early in the week, a Habit shows as *free*, so colleagues can still book over it, and Reclaim quietly moves it somewhere else. As open time runs out, Reclaim switches the event to *busy* so it stops getting eaten. That escalation is what makes it feel smart instead of rigid.

Tasks work the same way, with a duration and a due date. Connect Todoist, Asana, Jira, Google Tasks, or one of several other tools, and Reclaim pulls tasks in and schedules work sessions before each deadline, splitting long tasks into chunks.

### Clockwise: rearranging the team's meetings

Clockwise looks at a group of calendars together. When you mark a meeting as **flexible**, you give Clockwise permission to move it within a window you set (for example, any time on Tuesday) to a slot that works better for everyone. Its goal is to cluster meetings so the gaps between them become two- and three-hour blocks, which it then protects as **Focus Time**.

The more of your team uses it, the better it works. One person on Clockwise sees modest gains. A whole department on Clockwise lets it shift recurring syncs and 1:1s in ways no single person could manage by hand.

## Focus time

This is why most people install either app, and the approaches are different.

Clockwise treats focus time as a first-class feature. You set a weekly target, and it books Focus Time holds in the largest open blocks. You can mark them busy and have Clockwise update your Slack status while they run. Because Clockwise can also move flexible meetings, it *creates* focus time instead of only finding it.

Reclaim handles focus time with a Habit. That works well, and the free-to-busy escalation is a good defense. But Reclaim won't move a colleague's meeting to open up a better block. If other people's meetings have already fragmented your calendar, Reclaim can only work with the gaps you have.

**Edge: Clockwise**, especially for people whose calendars are full of internal meetings.

## Tasks and personal routines

Here the roles reverse. Reclaim is effectively a time-blocking system for your to-do list. Our individual-contributor tester moved a Todoist list of about 15 tasks into Reclaim and, within an hour, got a realistic view of which ones would not fit before Friday. That's useful, and a little humbling.

Clockwise has no task scheduling. It can hold time for lunch and add travel time around meetings that have a location, but it doesn't know about your to-do list or personal routines such as a workout or a daily planning session.

**Edge: Reclaim**, by a wide margin.

## 1:1s and meeting scheduling

Reclaim's **Smart 1:1s** schedule recurring one-on-ones within a flexible window and reschedule them automatically when one person has a conflict. For managers with several direct reports, that alone saves a surprising amount of back-and-forth. Reclaim also includes **scheduling links** for booking with people outside your company.

Clockwise handles internal meetings well through flexible meetings, including recurring ones. Its focus is internal coordination, though. If you book a lot of external calls, compare its current options with a dedicated booking tool before you cancel that tool.

**Edge: Reclaim** for managers and anyone who books external meetings. The two are roughly even for internal scheduling.

## Team features

Clockwise was built for organizations. It supports shared **no-meeting days**, team calendars, and analytics that show how much focus time a team gets and where meeting load piles up. Admins can roll it out across a company.

Reclaim has team plans and a few shared features, but most of its value lands at the individual level. Two Reclaim users on the same team don't get much more than two separate Reclaim users.

**Edge: Clockwise.**

## Setup and day-to-day feel

Both connect through Google sign-in and add their own events to your calendar. Neither supports Outlook at the time of writing, which rules both out for Microsoft 365 shops.

Reclaim takes longer to set up because you need to configure Habits, working hours, and task integrations. After that, it runs in the background. Its Slack status sync, which shows colleagues when you're in a meeting or focusing, is a nice extra.

Clockwise is faster to start. The main new habit is marking meetings as flexible, and its Chrome extension shows its suggestions inside Google Calendar.

Both share one adjustment period: a calendar that changes itself takes getting used to. In week one, both testers were surprised by events that had moved. By week three, that was the point. Colleagues who don't use the tool may still find the auto-generated events confusing, so tell your team what the colored blocks mean.

## Privacy and permissions

Both apps need broad read and write access to your calendar, and both can see meeting titles and attendees. That comes with what they do. If you work somewhere with strict data rules, check with IT before you connect a work account. Both companies publish security documentation you can hand to IT.

## Pricing (as of January 2023)

Both use a free plan plus per-user paid tiers, and both adjust their plans from time to time, so check the current pricing pages before you buy.

- **Reclaim:** The free plan covers Habits, basic task scheduling, and calendar sync, with limits. Paid tiers run roughly **$8–$12 per user per month** billed annually and unlock more Habits, more integrations, Smart 1:1s, and scheduling links.
- **Clockwise:** The free plan includes Focus Time and flexible meetings for individuals. Paid team and business tiers run roughly **$7–$12 per user per month** billed annually and add team analytics, organization-wide scheduling features, and admin controls.

For a solo user, both free plans are generous enough to test properly. Cost only becomes a real factor when you roll one out across a team.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Reclaim if:**
- You're an individual contributor or freelancer with more tasks than time.
- You want routines like lunch, exercise, or deep work defended automatically.
- You manage several direct reports and want 1:1s that reschedule themselves.
- You regularly book meetings with people outside your company.

**Choose Clockwise if:**
- Your calendar is dominated by internal meetings you don't control.
- Your team or company will adopt it together.
- You want no-meeting days and team-level visibility into meeting load.

**Consider both:** Some people run both, using Clockwise for team meeting optimization and Reclaim for tasks and habits. If you try this, set the same working hours in each and watch for both tools trying to hold the same time.

Our individual contributor stayed with Reclaim. Our manager stayed with Clockwise, partly because their direct reports were already using it. That split matches our advice: pick the tool that fits *whose* calendar is the problem.
