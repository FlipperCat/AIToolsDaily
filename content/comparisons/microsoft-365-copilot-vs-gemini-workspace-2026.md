# Microsoft 365 Copilot vs Gemini for Google Workspace (2026): Which Office AI Earns Its Keep?

For most teams, choosing between Microsoft 365 Copilot and Gemini for Workspace isn't really about which AI is smarter. It depends on where your email, files, and meetings already live. Both assistants are most useful inside their own suite, grounded in your own documents and conversations, and much less useful outside it.

Still, pricing, depth, and data handling differ in ways that matter when you pay per seat. We've used both across email, documents, spreadsheets, and meetings. Here's how they compare as of fall 2026.

## Quick comparison

| | Microsoft 365 Copilot | Gemini for Google Workspace |
|---|---|---|
| **Pricing model** | Paid add-on per user, on top of a Microsoft 365 license | Included in most Business and Enterprise Workspace plans |
| **Free tier at work** | Copilot Chat (web-grounded) included with work accounts | Gemini app included; features vary by plan |
| **Email** | Copilot in Outlook | Gemini in Gmail |
| **Documents** | Word | Docs |
| **Spreadsheets** | Excel (deepest analysis features) | Sheets |
| **Presentations** | PowerPoint | Slides, plus Google Vids for video |
| **Meetings** | Teams recaps, live Q&A | Meet "Take notes for me," translated captions |
| **Org knowledge** | Microsoft Graph (mail, chats, files, calendar) | Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Chat |
| **Research tools** | Researcher and Analyst agents | Deep Research, NotebookLM |
| **Custom agents** | Copilot Studio | Gems, Flows, and Google's enterprise agent platform |
| **Best for** | Microsoft-centric orgs with heavy Excel and Teams use | Google-centric teams wanting AI without a big add-on bill |

## Pricing and licensing

This is the biggest practical difference, and it often decides the matter on its own.

**Gemini** has been bundled into Google Workspace Business and Enterprise plans since early 2025. Google raised plan prices when it made the change, but there's no separate AI line item for most features. Lower tiers get a reduced feature set, and some advanced capabilities are reserved for higher plans. If you already pay for Workspace, you probably already have Gemini.

**Microsoft 365 Copilot** is still a separate per-user add-on. The enterprise price has historically been around $30 per user per month on an annual commitment, and Microsoft has since introduced lower-priced options and promotions aimed at smaller businesses. Every Microsoft 365 work account also gets **Copilot Chat** at no extra cost, which is a web-grounded chat assistant. The full in-app experience that reads your mail, files, and meetings requires the paid license.

As of October 2026, rolling Gemini out to a 50-person Google shop costs little or nothing extra. Rolling out full Copilot to a 50-person Microsoft shop is a real budget decision. Pricing and bundles change often in this category, so check both vendors' current pages before committing.

**Edge: Gemini**, on cost.

## Email: Outlook vs Gmail

Both handle the basics well: summarize a long thread, draft a reply in your tone, and pull action items out of a messy conversation.

**Copilot in Outlook** is strong at catching up. Ask it to summarize what you missed while you were out, and it can pull from email, Teams chats, and meeting notes together. That cross-app view is Copilot's signature strength. Its drafting coach, which flags tone and clarity before you send, is useful for sensitive messages.

**Gemini in Gmail** feels faster and more lightweight. Thread summaries appear right at the top of long conversations, and contextual reply suggestions are good enough to use with light editing. Searching your inbox in natural language ("what did the vendor say about the delivery date?") works well.

**Edge: Copilot** for cross-app catch-up; **Gemini** for speed in daily use.

## Documents: Word vs Docs

**Copilot in Word** can draft a document from a prompt plus references to existing files, such as "write a proposal based on this brief and last quarter's report." It's good at restructuring and summarizing long documents, and at turning a Word doc into a PowerPoint deck.

**Gemini in Docs** offers similar drafting and rewriting through a side panel that can reference other files in Drive. It's quicker for short edits like rephrasing, shortening, and changing tone, and it's well integrated with Google's collaborative editing.

In practice, draft quality is close. The bigger factor is how well your file storage is organized, because both assistants ground their drafts in the documents they can find.

**Edge: Tie.**

## Spreadsheets: Excel vs Sheets

Excel is where Copilot most clearly justifies its price. Copilot in Excel can explain formulas, build complex ones from a description, add calculated columns, highlight trends, and generate charts. The **Analyst** agent can work through messier, multi-step analysis on larger datasets. Teams that live in Excel models will feel the difference.

**Gemini in Sheets** handles formula help, table generation, and quick analysis competently, and it has improved steadily. For heavy financial modeling or large datasets, though, it's still a step behind. If spreadsheets are a core job function, test both on your real workbooks. Our [Copilot review](/reviews/microsoft-copilot-review-2025/) goes deeper into the Excel experience.

**Edge: Copilot.**

## Presentations

**Copilot in PowerPoint** builds a draft deck from a Word document or prompt using your organization's templates. Treat it as a starting point, not a finished deck.

**Gemini in Slides** generates slides and images and helps with speaker notes. Google's **Vids** app adds AI-assisted video creation for internal training and updates, which Microsoft's suite doesn't directly match.

**Edge: Slight Copilot** for decks; **Gemini** if you want video.

## Meetings: Teams vs Meet

Meeting features are among the most-used AI tools in either suite.

**Copilot in Teams** produces recaps with action items and lets you ask questions during a live meeting ("what did we decide about the budget?"), which helps if you join late. Recaps also feed into Copilot's cross-app answers later.

**Gemini in Meet** offers "Take notes for me," which saves structured notes to a Google Doc and shares them with attendees, plus translated captions for multilingual teams. It's simple and reliable, and it requires no setup.

**Edge: Copilot** for in-meeting Q&A; **Gemini** for low-effort notes.

## Research and company knowledge

Copilot's foundation is the **Microsoft Graph**, which connects your email, chats, calendar, and files across SharePoint and OneDrive. Its **Researcher** agent can combine internal content with web sources into a structured report. When your organization's data is well organized, this is very powerful.

Google's equivalents are **Deep Research** in the Gemini app and **NotebookLM**, which is excellent for working with a defined set of sources. Our [NotebookLM review](/reviews/notebooklm-review/) covers why so many teams use it. Gemini's grounding in Gmail and Drive works well, but Microsoft's single cross-app context is still more integrated.

**Edge: Copilot**, for organizations with well-organized data.

## Agents and automation

**Copilot Studio** is the more mature platform for building custom agents. It connects to business systems, supports approval workflows, and lets IT govern which agents people can use.

Google offers **Gems** (reusable custom assistants), **Flows** for automating multi-step Workspace tasks, and an enterprise agent platform for data outside Workspace. It's improving quickly but feels less unified.

**Edge: Copilot.**

## Security, privacy, and governance

Both vendors commit that business customer data isn't used to train their general models. Both also respect existing file permissions, and that's where the real risk is. **An AI assistant can surface anything a user has access to.** If your SharePoint or Drive sharing is a mess, the assistant will make that obvious, sometimes embarrassingly so.

Microsoft offers deep governance tooling through Purview and SharePoint management features, which large regulated organizations tend to prefer. Google offers data loss prevention, Drive labels, and client-side encryption options that keep certain content out of AI processing entirely.

Before rolling out either one, audit sharing permissions and write down what employees should and shouldn't paste into AI tools. Our [guide to writing an AI usage policy](/tutorials/ai-usage-policy-small-business-2026/) is a practical starting point.

**Edge: Tie.** Microsoft's tooling is deeper for large enterprises; Google's is simpler for smaller teams.

## Which should you choose?

For most organizations, **stay with the suite you already use**. Switching email, files, and calendars to get a different AI assistant almost never pays off. The more useful questions are how much to spend and who should get it.

- **You're on Google Workspace:** Turn Gemini on, train people on the five or six features that matter for their roles, and add NotebookLM for research-heavy teams. You're probably already paying for it.
- **You're on Microsoft 365:** Start everyone on the free Copilot Chat, then buy full Copilot licenses for the roles that clearly benefit: finance and analysts in Excel, managers who live in Teams and Outlook, and anyone who writes a lot of documents. Measure usage after 60–90 days before expanding.
- **You're a small business choosing a suite from scratch:** Google Workspace with Gemini is the lower-cost route to capable built-in AI. Choose Microsoft if your work depends on Excel or your clients expect Office files.
- **You care most about chat quality, not suite integration:** A standalone assistant may serve you better. Our [ChatGPT Team vs Claude Team comparison](/compare/chatgpt-team-vs-claude-team-2025/) covers that route.

Our take: Gemini is the better value, and Copilot is the deeper tool. Both improve on a monthly cadence, so revisit the decision when your contract comes up for renewal.
