AI meeting notetakers have become standard equipment. Join almost any external call and at least one bot is already there recording. Two of the names you'll see most often are **Fireflies.ai** and **Read.ai**.

Both join Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams calls, transcribe them, and send out summaries afterward. Past that, they're built around different ideas. Fireflies wants to be a **searchable archive of every conversation**, with deep integrations into sales and project tools. Read.ai wants to tell you **how your meetings went**: who was engaged, who talked too much, and how you come across.

We ran both on the same set of internal standups, client calls, and sales demos for several weeks. Here's how they compare.

## Transcription Accuracy

On clean audio with one speaker at a time, both are accurate enough that you'll rarely need to fix the transcript. The differences showed up on harder calls:

- **Crosstalk:** Both had trouble when people talked over each other. Fireflies tended to merge the overlapping speech into one speaker's turn. Read.ai was a bit better at separating speakers but sometimes dropped short interjections.
- **Jargon and names:** Fireflies did slightly better with product names and acronyms after a few meetings on the same topic. Both still misspell unusual surnames.
- **Languages:** Fireflies supports a long list of languages and is the safer choice for multilingual teams. Read.ai's language support is narrower, so check that your languages are covered before you commit.

**Edge:** Fireflies, narrowly, mainly on language support and file uploads. You can upload recorded audio or video to Fireflies, which matters if you also want to transcribe podcasts, interviews, or in-person recordings.

## Summaries and Action Items

This is the feature most people actually use, and both do it well.

**Fireflies** gives you an overview, a bullet outline with timestamps, action items, and keywords. Its **smart search filters** are useful. One click shows every question, date, metric, or task mentioned in a meeting, which helps when you're skimming a 60-minute call for the one number that matters.

**Read.ai** produces a "meeting report" with a summary, chapters, action items, and key questions. The reports are well organized and easy to scan. Read also sends a summary to participants after the meeting, which teams either love or find intrusive (more on that below).

**Edge:** Tie. Fireflies is better for digging through a call. Read.ai's reports are easier to read at a glance.

## Analytics and Coaching

This is where the two differ most.

**Read.ai** was built around meeting analytics. It scores engagement and sentiment through the call, shows talk-time balance, and offers **speaker coaching**: feedback on your pace, how much you dominated the conversation, and how engaged people were while you spoke. Managers running lots of internal meetings, and anyone trying to present better, will find this useful.

**Fireflies** has conversation intelligence too, including talk-time ratios, topic trackers (for example, flag every time a competitor or "pricing" comes up), and sentiment analysis on higher plans. It's aimed at sales and customer teams tracking what was said, not at coaching you on how you said it.

**Edge:** Read.ai, clearly, if analytics and coaching are why you're buying. Treat engagement scores as directional, not precise. Inferring "engagement" from audio and video signals is useful, but it's still a guess.

## Search and Knowledge Retrieval

**Fireflies** is strong at turning your meeting history into something you can query. **AskFred**, its built-in assistant, answers questions about a single meeting or across many ("What did the client say about the Q2 timeline?"), and its global search reliably surfaces past conversations. If you also record client calls with tools like [Tactiq](/reviews/tactiq-review-2023-meeting-transcripts-without-a-bot-in-the-room/), Fireflies will feel familiar but goes further.

**Read.ai** has been expanding past meetings, adding summaries for email and messaging and a search assistant that works across those sources. It's an ambitious plan. In our testing, meeting search worked well and the newer cross-source features were still settling in.

**Edge:** Fireflies for meeting search today. Read.ai if you want one tool that also summarizes your inbox and chat, and you're willing to grant broader access.

## Integrations and Workflows

**Fireflies** has the bigger integration catalog: Salesforce, HubSpot, and other CRMs, Slack, Notion, Asana, Trello, and Zapier. Sales teams can push call notes and action items straight into deal records, which removes a real chore. Our [Fireflies review](/reviews/fireflies.ai-review-2026-ai-meeting-notes-and-conversation-intelligence/) covers the CRM workflow in more detail.

**Read.ai** covers the basics: calendar, Slack, CRMs, and common productivity tools. It's less of a workflow hub. Its value lives mostly inside Read's own reports.

**Edge:** Fireflies, especially for sales and ops teams.

## Privacy and Meeting Etiquette

Both tools have drawn complaints about bots joining meetings that not everyone expected. The usual cause is an auto-join setting tied to someone's calendar. Read.ai's habit of sending reports to attendees has been a particular sore point for some users.

Before rolling out either tool:

- Turn off auto-join for external meetings, or limit it to meetings you host.
- Decide who receives summaries automatically. "Only me" is the safest default.
- Tell participants up front, and know your local recording-consent rules.
- Check data retention settings, and whether recordings are used to improve the vendor's models. Both vendors document their policies, and enterprise plans add more controls.

**Edge:** Tie. Both are workable with sensible settings. Neither is safe to leave on its defaults.

## Pricing (Approximate, as of January 2025)

**Fireflies.ai**
- **Free:** limited transcription credits and storage, basic summaries.
- **Pro:** ~$10/user/month billed annually (~$18 monthly). Unlimited transcription, more storage, AI summaries.
- **Business:** ~$19/user/month billed annually (~$29 monthly). Adds video recording, conversation intelligence, and team features.
- **Enterprise:** ~$39/user/month billed annually, with admin, security, and compliance controls.

**Read.ai**
- **Free:** a small number of meetings per month with core reports.
- **Pro:** ~$15/user/month billed annually (~$20 monthly). Unlimited meetings, full reports, coaching.
- **Enterprise / Enterprise+:** roughly $22–30/user/month billed annually, with admin controls and broader integrations.

Prices change often, and both vendors run promotions. Check current pricing pages before buying. On a per-seat basis, Fireflies usually costs less for basic transcription. Read.ai's price includes analytics that Fireflies reserves for its higher tiers.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Fireflies.ai if:**
- You're a sales, customer success, or recruiting team that wants call notes synced into a CRM.
- You need a searchable archive and want to ask questions across months of calls.
- You want to upload recordings from outside live meetings.
- You need broad language support or the lowest per-seat price.

**Choose Read.ai if:**
- You manage people and want insight into meeting health and engagement.
- You want to get better at presenting and running meetings, and coaching feedback appeals to you.
- You like polished, skimmable reports more than a deep archive.
- You're interested in one assistant across meetings, email, and chat.

- You mostly want free, lightweight notes for Zoom. Look at our [Fathom vs Otter comparison](/compare/fathom-vs-otter.ai-2024-which-ai-meeting-assistant-should-you-use/).

## Verdict

These two tools overlap less than they seem to. **Fireflies is the better system of record.** It captures, searches, and pushes meeting data into the rest of your stack. **Read.ai is the better mirror.** It shows how your meetings actually go and how you come across in them.

For most teams that just need reliable notes and searchable history, Fireflies is the safer default at a lower price. If you're a manager or a team trying to run fewer, better meetings, Read.ai's analytics justify the extra cost. Both have free plans, so run them on the same week of meetings and compare the output before you decide.
