If you've given up on scrolling through ten blue links, you've probably landed on one of two tools: **Perplexity** or **Bing Chat**. Both answer questions in plain language, both cite their sources, and both can use GPT-4. The similarity mostly ends there.

Bing Chat is Microsoft's attempt to put a conversational layer on top of an entire search engine and browser. Perplexity is a smaller, focused product built around one idea: give a direct answer and show exactly where it came from. We used both for several weeks of everyday research, including product comparisons, technical questions, news catch-up, and quick fact checks. Here's how they compare.

## Quick comparison

| | Perplexity | Bing Chat |
|---|---|---|
| **Core idea** | Answer engine with citations | Chat layer over Bing search |
| **Free tier** | Yes, unlimited standard searches | Yes, free with a Microsoft account |
| **Paid tier** | Perplexity Pro, ~$20/month | None for consumers |
| **GPT-4 access** | Limited free Copilot uses; much more on Pro | Included free |
| **Model choice** | Pro: GPT-4 or Claude 2 | No choice; conversation styles instead |
| **Citations** | Numbered sources, shown up front | Footnotes plus "Learn more" links |
| **Source filters** | Focus modes (Academic, Reddit, YouTube, etc.) | None |
| **Image generation** | No | Yes, via Bing Image Creator |
| **Image input** | No | Yes, visual search in chat |
| **File uploads** | Pro: PDFs and text files | Edge sidebar can read open PDFs and pages |
| **Best experience** | Web, iOS, Android | Microsoft Edge and the Bing app |
| **Business option** | No dedicated enterprise plan | Bing Chat Enterprise |

## Answer quality

Both tools draw on GPT-4 for their best answers, so on straightforward questions the quality gap is small. The differences show up in how each one gathers and frames information.

**Perplexity** reads like a well-sourced summary. It usually pulls from five or more sources, writes a compact answer, and lists the sources above the text. On multi-part questions, its **Copilot** mode asks a clarifying question first ("Are you looking for budget options or high-end?") and then searches more deeply. That extra step often produces a more targeted answer than a one-shot query.

**Bing Chat** is more conversational and more variable. Its three conversation styles change the output a lot. **Precise** gives short, cautious, factual answers. **Balanced** is the default middle ground. **Creative** is looser and better for brainstorming, but more likely to drift from the sources. In Precise mode, Bing is a strong research tool. In Creative mode, double-check everything.

**Edge:** Perplexity, for research you need to trust. Bing Chat's Precise mode is close, but Perplexity is more consistent.

## Citations and trust

This is Perplexity's strongest advantage. Sources sit at the top of every answer, numbered and clickable, and inline markers show which sentence came from which page. It's easy to check a claim in seconds.

Bing Chat also cites sources as footnotes, and it links out generously. But its answers are more likely to mix sourced facts with the model's own general knowledge without making the line between them clear. In our testing, Bing sometimes cited a page that only loosely supported the sentence it was attached to.

Neither tool is immune to errors. Both occasionally summarize a source incorrectly. Perplexity simply makes verification faster. For more on how Bing handles this, see our [Bing Chat review](/reviews/bing-chat-review-2023/).

## Controlling where answers come from

Perplexity's **Focus** modes are something Bing doesn't have. You can restrict a search to:

- **Academic**: papers and scholarly sources
- **Reddit**: real user discussions and opinions
- **YouTube**: video content
- **Wolfram|Alpha**: math and computation
- **Writing**: no web search, just the model

Focus on Reddit is surprisingly useful for product research, because you get what owners say instead of what affiliate listicles say. Academic focus is a good first pass for literature questions, though it isn't a replacement for a proper database search.

Bing Chat always searches the whole web. You can steer it with wording ("using only peer-reviewed sources"), but that's a request, not a filter.

**Edge:** Perplexity.

## Extras and ecosystem

Bing Chat is part of Microsoft's much bigger push, and it shows in the feature list:

- **Image creation.** Ask for an image in chat and Bing Image Creator generates it, now powered by DALL-E 3. Perplexity doesn't create images.
- **Visual search.** Upload a photo and ask about it: identify a plant, read a chart, get ideas for a recipe from a picture of your fridge.
- **Edge sidebar.** In Microsoft Edge, Bing Chat can summarize the page or PDF you have open, which is useful for long reports.
- **Windows and Office.** Microsoft is folding the same assistant into Windows 11 and its Office apps under the broader "Copilot" name, so Bing Chat is likely to show up in more places over time.

Perplexity's extras are smaller but focused: **Collections** to organize research threads, shareable thread links, a clean mobile app, and (on Pro) file uploads so you can ask questions about your own PDFs.

**Edge:** Bing Chat, if you want one assistant that does many things. Perplexity, if you only want research.

## Limits and friction

Bing Chat is free, but it comes with friction. The best experience is in Microsoft Edge. It works in other browsers now, with some restrictions. Conversations have a cap on turns, after which you need to start over, and it's sometimes quicker than Perplexity to decline a topic or end a conversation.

Perplexity's free tier gives you unlimited standard searches, but the GPT-4-powered Copilot searches are rationed. You get a handful every few hours before it falls back to the standard model. Heavy users will feel that limit quickly.

## Pricing (as of October 2023)

Prices are approximate and change often, so check the official pages.

- **Bing Chat:** Free for consumers with a Microsoft account. Businesses can use **Bing Chat Enterprise**, which adds commercial data protection and is included with many Microsoft 365 business plans.
- **Perplexity:** Free tier with unlimited standard searches and limited Copilot uses. **Perplexity Pro** costs about **$20 per month** (or around $200 per year). It adds far more Copilot searches, a choice between GPT-4 and Claude 2, and file uploads.

On paper, Bing Chat is the better deal: GPT-4 for free. In practice, whether Perplexity Pro is worth it depends on how much you value its sourcing and focus modes.

## Privacy and data

Bing Chat for consumers is tied to your Microsoft account and Microsoft's general privacy policy. For work data, Bing Chat Enterprise is the version to use, because Microsoft says chat data isn't saved or used to train its models there.

Perplexity works without an account for basic use, but your threads are saved if you sign in. It doesn't yet offer an enterprise-grade plan with contractual data protections, so companies with strict policies will likely lean toward Microsoft's offering.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Perplexity if:**

- Research is your main use case, and you want sources you can check at a glance.
- You want to filter by source type, such as academic papers, Reddit, or YouTube.
- You'd like to choose between GPT-4 and Claude 2 and are willing to pay about $20 a month.
- You don't use Microsoft Edge and don't want to.

**Choose Bing Chat if:**

- You want GPT-4-powered answers without paying anything.
- You'd use image generation and image understanding in the same chat.
- You already work in Edge, Windows, and Microsoft 365.
- Your company needs an assistant with commercial data protection today.

**Use both if** you do serious research. Many people run the same question through both tools and compare. When they disagree, that's usually a sign to go read the sources yourself.

## Verdict

Perplexity is the better **research tool**: more consistent, easier to verify, and more controllable. Bing Chat is the better **free all-rounder**: GPT-4 at no cost, plus images, visual search, and deep Microsoft integration. For most people the practical answer is to start with the free tiers of both and let a week of real questions decide.
