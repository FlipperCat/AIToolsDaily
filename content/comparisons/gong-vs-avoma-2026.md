Gong and Avoma both record your sales calls, transcribe them, and use AI to turn conversations into notes, deal insights, and coaching. On a feature checklist they look close. In practice they're built for different buyers.

Gong is the enterprise revenue intelligence platform: deep analytics, forecasting, and a sales process built around its data, sold through custom contracts. Avoma is the mid-market alternative: AI meeting notes, conversation intelligence, and revenue features in one product, with published per-seat pricing you can buy without a procurement cycle.

This comparison covers how they differ on capture, AI notes, deal intelligence, coaching, setup, and cost, and which one fits which team. For a deeper look at Gong alone, see our [Gong review](/reviews/gong-review-2024/).

## Capture and Transcription

Both tools join meetings on Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, record them, and produce speaker-separated transcripts. Both connect to common sales dialers so phone calls land in the same library as video meetings.

Gong captures more surrounding activity by default, pulling in email and calendar data to build a full picture of each deal. Avoma also syncs calendar and CRM context, but its center of gravity is the meeting itself.

Transcription quality is comparable for clear audio. Neither is immune to errors with crosstalk, heavy accents, or niche jargon. Both let you set up custom vocabulary, which is worth doing in week one.

**Edge:** Gong, for breadth of captured activity. Roughly even on the transcripts themselves.

## AI Notes and Summaries

This is where Avoma shines. Its notes are structured by meeting type: a discovery call produces sections for pain points, current tools, decision process, and next steps, while a demo or onboarding call uses a different template. You can customize these templates, and notes sync into the CRM so reps don't retype them.

Gong generates call summaries with key points and next steps, and its strength is answering questions across many calls: "what has this account said about budget?" or "which competitors came up this quarter?" It's built for managers looking across deals rather than reps documenting one call.

If your main pain is reps not logging good notes, Avoma solves it more directly. If it's leaders not knowing what's happening across the pipeline, Gong's cross-call Q&A is stronger.

**Edge:** Avoma for per-meeting notes; Gong for cross-deal questions.

## Deal Intelligence and Forecasting

Gong is the category leader here. Its deal boards combine CRM stages with real engagement: who's been involved, how recently, how many stakeholders, and what risks came up on calls. Forecasting is a full module that lets reps and managers submit numbers with activity evidence alongside.

Avoma offers deal insights and pipeline views too, including deal risk indicators and forecasting features. They're useful, especially for a growing team that's never had any of this, but they're less configurable and less deep than Gong's.

For a 10-rep team, Avoma's version may be all you need. For a 100-rep org with layered forecast calls, Gong's depth starts to matter.

**Edge:** Gong, clearly.

## Coaching and Conversation Analytics

Both tools track talk-to-listen ratios, monologue length, questions asked, and keyword or topic mentions. Both support scorecards and time-stamped comments on calls so managers can coach specific moments.

Gong goes further with analytics comparing top performers to everyone else and more mature tools for building call libraries for onboarding. Avoma covers the essentials well: scorecards, comments, shareable snippets, and playlists of good calls.

**Edge:** Gong for large enablement programs; Avoma is sufficient for most teams under a few dozen reps.

## Extras: Scheduling and Lead Routing

Avoma includes a meeting scheduler and inbound lead routing, which can replace a separate scheduling tool for some teams. It's a nice consolidation for small companies watching their tool count.

Gong instead extends into sales engagement (sequences and outreach) for teams that want one vendor across more of the revenue workflow.

**Edge:** Depends on what you'd otherwise buy separately.

## Setup and Administration

Avoma can be running in an afternoon: connect calendars, the CRM, and the video platform, pick note templates, and go. Configuration grows as you add scorecards and trackers, but the starting point is light.

Gong rewards investment. Mapping CRM fields, building trackers, designing scorecards, and training managers to run pipeline reviews from Gong data takes real time. Teams that assign a sales ops or enablement owner get far more from it. Teams that don't often end up using an expensive tool as a call recorder.

**Edge:** Avoma for speed and low overhead.

## Pricing

Prices change, so treat this as approximate and as of October 2026:

- **Avoma** publishes per-seat pricing. Entry plans built around AI meeting notes cost a modest amount per user per month, with conversation intelligence and revenue intelligence features on higher tiers or as add-ons. Monthly billing and free trials make it easy to test.
- **Gong** doesn't publish pricing. Buyers typically report per-seat annual licenses plus a platform fee, annual or multi-year contracts, and separate pricing for some modules. For small teams, first-year costs can run to five figures.

Once Gong's platform fee is included, Avoma typically works out to a fraction of Gong's per-seat cost. Whether Gong's extra depth is worth the difference depends on the size of your pipeline and how much forecast accuracy is worth to you.

**Edge:** Avoma, by a wide margin, on cost and buying friction.

## Privacy and Consent

Both tools use a bot or integration to record calls, and both offer settings for consent notices and excluding certain meetings. Recording laws differ by country and state, so set a clear disclosure policy before rollout regardless of which you choose. Also check data residency and retention controls if you sell into regulated industries.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Avoma if:**

- You're a startup or SMB sales team, roughly under 30 reps.
- Your biggest problem is inconsistent call notes and CRM hygiene.
- You want conversation intelligence without a long contract or procurement process.
- You'd like a scheduler and lead routing in the same tool.
- You don't have a dedicated sales ops person to run a heavy platform.

**Choose Gong if:**

- You manage a larger sales org with multiple teams and forecast layers.
- Forecast accuracy and deal risk visibility are board-level concerns.
- You have an ops or enablement owner who can configure and maintain it.
- You run Salesforce and want deep, mature integration.
- You're building a formal coaching program around call libraries.

**Consider neither if** you mostly need meeting notes for internal calls or occasional client meetings. A general AI meeting assistant like [Fathom](/reviews/fathom-ai-review-2026/) or [Read AI](/reviews/read-ai-review-2025/) will cover that at a lower price, sometimes free.

One more practical note: both tools are only as useful as your CRM data. If you're still choosing a CRM, settle that first. Our [HubSpot vs Salesforce comparison](/compare/hubspot-vs-salesforce/) can help.

## Verdict

Gong is the more powerful platform, and for large sales organizations its deal intelligence and forecasting justify the cost. Avoma delivers most of what smaller teams need, especially well-structured AI notes and practical coaching tools, at a much lower price and with far less setup.

For most teams under a few dozen reps, start with Avoma. Move to Gong when your pipeline is big enough that forecast misses cost more than the license, and when someone on your team has time to run it.
