## Adobe Podcast Enhance vs Descript Studio Sound (2023)

Two tools have changed what "bad audio" means for podcasters and video creators this year. Adobe Podcast's Enhance Speech takes a noisy, echoey recording and returns something that sounds like it was captured in a treated room. Descript's Studio Sound does a similar job, built into an editor that many creators already use.

Both are one-click AI speech enhancers. Both can make a laptop mic sound dramatically better. But they fit into very different workflows, and they fail in different ways. We ran the same set of recordings through both: a laptop-mic interview in a kitchen, a USB-mic recording with room echo, a phone voice memo outdoors, and a well-recorded studio track. Here's how they compare.

## Quick Comparison

| | Adobe Podcast Enhance | Descript Studio Sound |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Standalone web tool | Feature inside the Descript editor |
| **Workflow** | Upload file, download result | Apply to clips or tracks while editing |
| **Strength control** | None (as of late 2023) | Intensity control |
| **Best at** | Rescuing bad recordings | Polishing audio inside an edit |
| **Video support** | Audio files only | Works on video projects too |
| **Price (approx., Nov 2023)** | Free in beta, with usage limits | Included in Descript plans; paid tiers ~$12-$24/mo billed annually |
| **Batch processing** | No | Per-project, track by track |
| **Learning curve** | Almost none | Moderate (you're learning Descript) |

## Sound Quality

This is the question most people care about, and the honest answer is: **Adobe Enhance is more aggressive, and on truly bad audio, it usually wins.**

On the kitchen interview and the outdoor voice memo, Enhance produced results that were hard to believe. Background hum, fridge noise, and much of the room reflection disappeared, and the voice gained a close, broadcast-style presence. Studio Sound cleaned the same files noticeably, but left more room tone behind, and the voice sounded improved rather than transformed.

On moderately good audio (the USB mic with some echo), the gap narrowed. Both produced usable results. Studio Sound sounded slightly more natural; Enhance sounded slightly more "produced."

On the already-good studio track, the picture flipped. Enhance's processing added a faint synthetic quality, and on a couple of words it altered the character of the voice in a way we didn't want. Studio Sound at a lower intensity left the track largely alone while tidying the noise floor.

Both tools share one failure mode: on very poor audio, they can produce artifacts, including warbly consonants, clipped word endings, or a voice that sounds slightly "rebuilt." Enhance's artifacts are rarer but more noticeable when they happen. ## Control

This is Studio Sound's clearest advantage.

**Adobe Enhance gives you no controls.** You upload, you wait, you get a result. If it's too much, there's no wet/dry slider and no setting to dial it back. Your options are to accept it, skip it, or blend the enhanced file with the original yourself in another editor.

**Studio Sound lets you set the intensity**, and because it lives inside an editor, you can apply it to one speaker's track and not another, or to one section of a recording and not the rest. For a two-person interview where only the guest had a bad mic, that flexibility matters.

If you care about how your audio sounds and like to make adjustments, Studio Sound is easier to live with. If you want a fix with zero decisions, Enhance's lack of options is almost a feature.

## Workflow

The tools are built for different moments in production.

**Adobe Enhance is a pre-processing step.** You take a raw file, run it through the web tool, download the cleaned version, and then edit it wherever you normally do. That suits creators who edit in Audition, Audacity, GarageBand, or Premiere. The upload-and-wait loop is simple, but it adds a round trip for every file, and as of late 2023 there's no batch mode.

**Studio Sound is part of editing.** If you already edit in Descript, applying it is a toggle on a track. It can be adjusted later, removed, or applied to only the parts that need it. It also works when you're editing video, since Descript handles video projects. The tradeoff is that you're committing to Descript as your editor. If you're not already using it, adopting a whole editor for one feature is a big ask.

## Limits and Reliability

**Adobe Enhance** is in beta and free, which comes with caveats. There are per-file length and size limits (roughly an hour per upload in our testing), so long recordings need splitting. During busy periods, processing can be slow. And because it's a beta, there's no guarantee the free access or current behavior will stay the same.

**Studio Sound** is a stable, shipping feature in a paid product. Its limits are tied to your Descript plan rather than per-file caps, with the free tier mainly limiting transcription hours and export options. It's more predictable for regular production work.

## Pricing

Pricing is approximate as of November 2023 and changes often.

**Adobe Podcast Enhance** is free to use with an Adobe account during the beta. Adobe has indicated that premium options with higher limits are part of its plans, so don't build a business workflow on free access lasting forever.

**Descript** has a free plan that lets you try Studio Sound with limited transcription. Paid plans run roughly $12 to $24 per month billed annually, and also include transcription hours, filler-word removal, and Overdub voice cloning. Studio Sound alone doesn't justify the cost, but if you'd use Descript's editing features anyway, it comes along for free.

## Alternatives Worth Knowing

Neither tool is the only option for AI audio cleanup:

- **[Auphonic](/reviews/auphonic-review-2023-the-set-and-forget-audio-engineer-for-podcasters/)** handles leveling, loudness normalization, and noise reduction automatically, with a free monthly allowance. It's subtler than either tool here but excellent for consistent, platform-ready loudness.
- **[Cleanvoice AI](/reviews/cleanvoice-ai-review-2023/)** focuses on removing filler words, mouth sounds, and dead air rather than room noise.
- **iZotope RX** remains the professional choice for detailed audio repair, at a much higher price and learning curve.

A common setup is Enhance or Studio Sound for cleanup, followed by Auphonic for loudness.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Adobe Podcast Enhance if:**
- You need to rescue a genuinely bad recording, like a laptop mic, a noisy room, or a remote guest on earbuds
- You already edit in another app and just want cleaner source files
- You want a free tool and can accept some uncertainty about the beta
- You want the strongest possible result and don't need fine control

**Choose Descript Studio Sound if:**
- You already edit in Descript, or you're considering it for text-based editing
- You want to adjust intensity or apply enhancement to specific tracks only
- Your source audio is decent and needs polish rather than rescue
- You edit video as well as audio and want one tool for both

**Use both if:** you edit in Descript but occasionally receive a recording that's beyond what Studio Sound can fix. Run that one file through Enhance first, import the result into Descript, and leave Studio Sound off for that track.

**Our verdict:** For pure rescue power, Adobe Podcast Enhance is the most impressive free audio tool of 2023. For day-to-day production, Descript Studio Sound is the more practical choice, because control and integration matter more over dozens of episodes than maximum strength on a single file. Neither replaces recording well in the first place. A decent mic in a quiet room still beats any amount of AI processing.
