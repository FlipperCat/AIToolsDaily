AI resume builders are a crowded, noisy category with a lot of tools that are really just a GPT wrapper and a template. Teal and Rezi are the two that consistently come up as genuinely useful, and they are useful in different ways. One is a job search command center that happens to include a resume builder. The other is a resume builder that is very focused on one thing.

Here is how they actually compare after running the same job search through both.

## At a Glance

| | Teal | Rezi |
|---|---|---|
| **Core idea** | Job search workspace with resume builder attached | Focused ATS-oriented resume builder |
| **Job tracking** | Strong — Chrome extension, kanban board, status stages | Minimal |
| **Keyword matching** | Per-job match score against saved postings | Keyword targeting plus a 0–100 Rezi Score |
| **AI writing** | Bullet rewriting, summaries, cover letters | Bullet generation, summaries, cover letters |
| **Templates** | Cleaner, more modern | Plain by design, parse-safe |
| **Free tier** | Generous and genuinely usable | Limited, enough to evaluate |
| **Paid pricing (approx.)** | ~$9/week or ~$29/month for Teal+ | ~$29/month, or a one-time lifetime option around $129 |
| **Best for** | Active searches with many applications | Getting one resume as tight as possible |

Prices are approximate as of December 2024 and both companies change them regularly.

## Job Tracking

This is Teal's real product and it is not close.

Teal's Chrome extension saves a job posting in one click from LinkedIn, Indeed, or a company careers page, storing the full description rather than a link — which matters when postings disappear. Saved jobs land on a kanban board with stages from Saved through Applied, Interviewing, and Offer, plus notes, contacts, and follow-up reminders. For anyone running twenty or more applications at once, this replaces the spreadsheet everyone builds and then abandons.

It also feeds the rest of the tool. Because Teal has the full job description stored, its match analysis compares your resume against that specific posting rather than a generic keyword list.

Rezi has some job management, but it is a secondary feature and feels like it. If application organization is your bottleneck, Teal wins this dimension outright.

## Keyword Matching and "ATS Optimization"

Both tools sell ATS optimization, and both oversell it. Worth being clear about what is real:

Applicant tracking systems parse resumes into structured fields and let recruiters search and filter. A resume that parses badly — because of multi-column layouts, text in graphics, unusual section headings, or a PDF exported oddly — can genuinely fail to show up in a recruiter's search. That is a real, fixable problem. What is not real is the widely repeated claim that a robot scores you and auto-rejects you below a threshold.

**Teal** shows a match score for your resume against a specific saved posting, with the keywords from that posting highlighted as present or missing. Because it is anchored to a real job description, the output is directly actionable: these six terms are in the posting and not in your resume, decide which ones honestly apply to you.

**Rezi** gives a Rezi Score out of 100 built from its own content rules — bullet structure, use of metrics, section completeness, word choice, length. It is less about a specific job and more about general resume hygiene. The score is somewhat gamified, but the underlying checks are reasonable: it will catch that half your bullets have no measurable outcome, which is the most common resume problem there is.

Different philosophies, both defensible. Teal is better when you are tailoring per application. Rezi is better when you are fixing one resume properly.

## AI Writing Quality

Both use large language models to rewrite or generate bullet points, and the quality gap is small. In side-by-side testing on the same input, both produced competent results and both produced the same failure: inflated, unverifiable claims.

Feed either tool "managed the company blog" and you will frequently get back something like "spearheaded a comprehensive content strategy driving a 40% increase in organic traffic." The structure is right. The number is invented. You have to catch these every time, because a fabricated metric in an interview is worse than a vague bullet on a resume.

**Rezi** is slightly more aggressive about forcing metrics into bullets, which is good discipline when you have real numbers and dangerous when you do not.

**Teal** is a bit more conservative and better at matching the tone of your existing bullets, so a partially rewritten resume reads more consistently.

Both handle cover letters adequately and neither is better than drafting one yourself with a general-purpose assistant — see our walkthrough for [writing a cover letter with ChatGPT](/tutorials/write-a-cover-letter-with-chatgpt-2024/), which gives you more control over tone for roughly no extra cost.

## Templates and Export

Rezi's templates are deliberately plain: single column, standard headings, conventional fonts. That is a feature for ATS parsing and a liability if you are applying anywhere design-conscious.

Teal's templates look better and give more layout control, with a live editor that is pleasant to use. A few of the more visual options introduce parsing risk, so if you pick one, run the exported PDF through a text extractor and confirm the output reads in a sensible order.

Both export clean PDFs. Neither replaces laying out your own document if presentation is part of what you are being judged on.

## Pricing

**Teal** has the more generous free tier by a wide margin — tracker, extension, builder, and basic matching all work unpaid. Teal+ unlocks unlimited AI generation and deeper analysis at roughly $9 per week or around $29 per month. The weekly option is the one to watch: it is convenient for a two-month search and quietly expensive if you forget to cancel. Set a reminder the day you subscribe.

**Rezi** gates more behind payment, with a limited free tier that is enough to evaluate the product. Pro runs around $29 per month, and Rezi has offered a one-time lifetime purchase in the $129 range. If you expect to job hunt more than once in the next several years, the lifetime option is the better economics in this category by some distance.

All figures approximate as of December 2024. Verify current pricing before buying.

## Where Both Fall Short

Neither tool fixes a weak underlying record. AI can rewrite "responsible for social media" into something sharper, but it cannot manufacture the results that make a resume competitive. If your bullets lack outcomes because the work lacked measurable outcomes, the honest fix is to go find the numbers in old dashboards and reports, not to let a model guess them.

Neither is a substitute for research either. The highest-leverage resume change is usually reordering and rewriting to match what a specific team actually cares about, which requires reading the posting carefully and ideally talking to someone who works there. Both tools help you execute that; neither does it for you.

And both encourage a volume mindset — track more jobs, generate more variants, apply more. For most searches, fifteen carefully targeted applications beat a hundred generated ones. ## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Teal if** you are running an active, high-volume search and your real problem is organization. The free tier alone is worth installing, the job tracker is the best in the category, and per-posting match analysis fits a tailoring workflow. Use the weekly plan only if you will genuinely cancel.

**Choose Rezi if** you want one excellent resume rather than a job search system, you are early-career and need structural guidance on what a good bullet looks like, or you want to pay once and stop thinking about it. The lifetime option makes it the cheaper choice over a multi-year horizon.

**Choose neither if** your resume is already strong and your problem is interviews or networking. A rewriting tool will not move that needle, and your time is better spent on [interview preparation](/tutorials/practice-job-interviews-with-chatgpt/) or on reaching people directly.

The most cost-effective setup for most people: Teal's free tier for tracking, and one careful pass on your resume — with either tool or with a general-purpose AI assistant — checking every generated claim against something you can actually defend in an interview.
