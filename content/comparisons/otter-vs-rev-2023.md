Otter.ai and Rev both turn speech into text, and both show up in nearly every "best transcription tool" list. In practice they solve different problems. Otter is a live, AI-only note-taker built around meetings. Rev is a transcription service that sells both machine and human-made transcripts, with captions and subtitles alongside.

Choosing the wrong one usually means paying for accuracy you don't need, or publishing quotes you can't trust. Here is how they compare in early 2023, and which one fits which workload.

|---|---|---|
| **Core model** | Subscription AI transcription | Pay-per-minute service (AI or human) |
| **Live meeting transcription** | Yes, real-time in Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams | No live note-taker; works on recordings |

## How Each One Works

**Otter.ai** listens live. Its meeting assistant can join scheduled Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams calls from your calendar and transcribe in real time, labeling speakers as it goes. Participants can follow along, highlight lines, and add comments during the call. You can also record in person with the mobile app or upload existing audio files.

**Rev** works on files. You upload a recording, or link one from a source like Zoom or YouTube, choose a service level, and get a transcript back. The machine tier is fast and cheap. The human tier sends your audio to professional transcribers who produce a cleaned-up, time-stamped transcript. Rev also sells closed captions and translated subtitles as separate products.

That one difference, live AI versus after-the-fact service, drives most of the comparison.

## Accuracy

On a clear, two-person call with decent microphones, Otter's transcripts are good enough to skim and search. Names, product jargon, and acronyms are the usual misses, and Otter lets you add custom vocabulary to reduce them.

Accuracy drops on the audio that real meetings often produce: people talking over each other, laptop microphones in echoey rooms, strong accents, or a phone call. The transcript remains useful for remembering what was discussed, but you would not quote from it without checking the recording.

Rev's machine transcription is in the same general league as Otter's on clean audio. The real difference is the human tier. Professional transcribers handle crosstalk, accents, and technical terms far better, and they flag inaudible sections instead of confidently guessing. Rev advertises very high accuracy for human transcripts, and in my tests that held up on difficult interview audio where AI tools struggled.

**Winner:** Rev, specifically the human service, when accuracy matters. For everyday meetings, the gap rarely justifies the price.

## Speed and Workflow

Otter wins on immediacy. The transcript exists while the meeting is happening, and a searchable record is ready the moment it ends. For people with back-to-back calls, that is the whole appeal.

Rev's machine transcripts come back within minutes of upload. Human transcripts usually take hours to about a day for standard turnaround, longer for long or difficult files, with faster options at a higher price. That is fine for a podcast interview or a research recording. It is useless for "what did we just agree to?"

**Winner:** Otter.

## Meeting Features

This is not close. Otter is built around meetings: calendar sync, auto-joining calls, live captions, speaker labels, shared notes, and a searchable archive of every conversation. Teams on the Business plan get admin controls and shared vocabulary.

Rev is not a meeting assistant. You can feed it meeting recordings, but there is no live experience and no running archive of your calls.

One caution with Otter: an assistant that automatically joins and records calls raises consent questions, especially with external participants. Tell people when you are recording, and check your organization's policy. If a bot in the room is the problem, a browser-extension approach like [Tactiq](/reviews/tactiq-review-2023-meeting-transcripts-without-a-bot-in-the-room/) captures captions without a visible participant.

**Winner:** Otter.

## Captions, Subtitles, and Publishing

For anything that will be published, such as video captions, podcast transcripts, journalism, research quotes, or legal records, Rev has the stronger offering. It sells closed captions in standard formats, foreign-language subtitles, and transcripts in clean or verbatim styles. The human review means fewer embarrassing errors in public.

Otter can export text and caption files, but its output is designed for notes, not broadcast. You would need a careful manual pass before publishing.

**Winner:** Rev.

## Pricing (Approximate, as of March 2023)

**Otter.ai:**
- **Basic (free):** a few hundred transcription minutes per month, short per-conversation limits, and limited file imports
- **Pro:** roughly $17/month billed monthly, or about $8-10/month billed annually, with far more minutes and longer conversations
- **Business:** roughly $30/user/month monthly, or about $20/user/month billed annually, with higher limits and admin features

**Rev:**
- **AI transcription:** around $0.25 per audio minute
- **Human transcription:** around $1.50 per audio minute
- **Captions and foreign subtitles:** priced per minute, with subtitles costing more

The math is straightforward. A person with 20 hours of meetings a month pays a flat Otter subscription. The same 20 hours through Rev's human service would cost well over a thousand dollars. On the other hand, a journalist with two one-hour interviews a month pays about $30 for Rev machine transcripts, or under $200 for human transcripts, and never needs a subscription.

If you are comfortable with a bit of setup and your audio is clean, OpenAI's open-source Whisper model is a free third option. Our [Whisper transcription guide](/tutorials/how-to-transcribe-audio-for-free-with-openai-whisper-2023-a-step-by-step-guide/) walks through running it locally.

## Privacy and Security

Both services process your audio in the cloud. Otter stores your meeting archive on its platform, which is the point of the product but also means a growing record of sensitive conversations. Rev's human tier means people outside your company hear the audio. Rev states that its transcribers work under confidentiality agreements, but for highly sensitive material, such as legal privilege or medical details, check both vendors' current terms and your own compliance requirements before uploading.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Otter.ai if:**
- Most of your audio is internal meetings
- You want a searchable record without doing anything after the call
- You need live captions during calls
- You want a predictable monthly cost

**Choose Rev if:**
- You are publishing the transcript, quotes, or captions
- Your audio is messy, multi-speaker, or full of jargon
- You transcribe occasionally and prefer paying per file
- You need subtitles in other languages

**Use both if** you run meetings daily and also produce content. Many teams let Otter handle internal calls and send the handful of interviews or webinars that will be published to Rev's human service.
