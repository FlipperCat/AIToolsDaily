Presentation software hasn't changed much in years. Tome and Beautiful.ai are two of the first serious attempts to change that with AI, but they come at it from opposite ends. Tome starts from a prompt and writes the whole story for you. Beautiful.ai starts from design rules and keeps your slides from looking amateur, and it has recently added an AI assistant on top.

I built the same three decks in both tools: a startup pitch, an internal quarterly update, and a training session. Here's how they compare in early 2023.

## Quick comparison

| Dimension | Tome | Beautiful.ai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core idea | Prompt-to-story generator with a page-based canvas | Smart templates that auto-format as you add content |
| AI generation | Full outline, copy, and images from one prompt | DesignerBot builds draft slides from a description |
| Design control | Loose; layouts are simple tiles | Tight; layouts adapt automatically, limited freeform |
| Output feel | Web-native, scrolling story | Classic 16:9 slide deck |
| Export | Shareable link; PDF on paid plans | PowerPoint and PDF export |
| Collaboration | Comments and shared workspaces | Team themes, shared slide libraries, brand controls |
| Best for | Fast first drafts, pitch narratives, async sharing | Polished slides that must live in PowerPoint workflows |
| Pricing (approx., as of March 2023) | Free tier with AI credits; paid around $8-10/mo | Pro around $12/mo billed yearly; Team around $40/user/mo |

## AI generation: Tome does more of the writing

Tome's whole pitch is that you type something like "a 10-page pitch for a meal-kit startup aimed at college students" and get a complete draft. It writes the outline, fills each page with copy, and generates images to match. The first time you see it work, it's impressive.

The output is a first draft, though, and it reads like one. The copy is generic, the claims are vague, and the AI-generated images can look a little off. I rewrote most of the text on every page. What Tome actually saves you is the blank-page problem: you get structure and a narrative arc in under a minute, and editing is faster than starting cold.

Beautiful.ai's DesignerBot is newer and more modest. You describe the deck you want and it creates a set of slides with suggested layouts and placeholder text. It's useful for getting started, but it doesn't try to write your story. In my tests it was better at choosing a sensible layout for a chart or a timeline than at producing usable copy.

**Winner:** Tome, if you want the AI to do the heavy lifting on content.

## Design quality and control

This is where the two tools are furthest apart.

Beautiful.ai's "smart slides" are the product. Add a fourth bullet and the layout reflows. Drop in a logo and it resizes and aligns. Spacing, fonts, and colors stay consistent across the deck without you thinking about it. The downside is that you can't easily break the rules. If you want an element in a specific spot that the template doesn't allow, you'll fight it.

Tome is looser. Pages are built from tiles (text, image, video, embeds), and you can arrange them fairly freely. That flexibility makes it easier to produce something that looks off-brand or inconsistent, and the visual range is narrower than Beautiful.ai's template library.

For decks that go in front of executives or clients, Beautiful.ai's guardrails are a real advantage. For a quick narrative you're sharing as a link, Tome's looser canvas is fine.

**Winner:** Beautiful.ai for consistent, professional-looking slides.

## Format: web story vs slide deck

Tome output feels like a web page. It's designed to be shared as a link and read on screen, with embedded videos, live web content, and a scrolling layout. That works well for async pitches and investor follow-ups, and less well if someone expects a `.pptx` file.

Beautiful.ai produces traditional slides. You can present from the browser, but you can also export to PowerPoint and PDF, which matters in companies where decks get passed around, edited by others, and dropped into larger presentations.

If your organization runs on PowerPoint, this one dimension may decide it for you.

## Collaboration and team features

Beautiful.ai is more mature here. The Team plan includes shared themes, locked brand colors and fonts, a shared slide library, and admin controls. A marketing or sales team can keep every deck on brand without policing it manually.

Tome supports shared workspaces and comments, and it's fine for small teams passing drafts back and forth. It doesn't have the same depth of brand management yet, which reflects that it's a younger product.

**Winner:** Beautiful.ai for teams with brand requirements.

## Learning curve

Both tools are easier than PowerPoint for beginners. Tome is the faster start: type a prompt, get a draft, edit. Beautiful.ai takes a bit longer, because you need to learn which smart slide does what, but once you know the library you can build clean decks quickly.

Neither tool is great for heavy data work. Beautiful.ai's chart slides are decent for simple bar and line charts. For anything complex, you'll still build the chart elsewhere and paste it in.

## Pricing

As of March 2023, approximately:

- **Tome:** A free plan with a limited number of AI generation credits, plus a paid Pro tier in the ~$8-10/month range that adds more AI usage, PDF export, and branding options.
- **Beautiful.ai:** No permanent free plan (there's a trial). Pro is around $12/month billed annually, and Team is around $40/user/month billed annually.

Prices for AI tools move fast, so check both sites before you commit. If you're only making a few decks a year, Tome's free tier is the cheaper way to experiment.

## Limitations worth knowing

**Tome:**
- AI copy needs heavy editing and fact-checking. It will confidently invent statistics.
- AI-generated images are hit or miss for business use.
- The link-first format doesn't suit every audience.

**Beautiful.ai:**
- Strict templates can feel limiting for creative work.
- DesignerBot is early and doesn't replace writing your own content.
- No free plan, so casual users pay or go elsewhere.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Tome if:**
- You mostly need a fast first draft and a narrative structure.
- You share decks as links (investor updates, async pitches, internal memos).
- You're a founder or solo creator who'd rather edit than build from scratch.

**Choose Beautiful.ai if:**
- Your decks need to look polished and consistent every time.
- You work in a team with brand guidelines.
- Recipients expect PowerPoint or PDF files they can edit.

**Use both if:** you like Tome for brainstorming the story and Beautiful.ai for the final, presentable version. Drafting in Tome and rebuilding the strongest pages in Beautiful.ai is a reasonable workflow, if a bit redundant.
