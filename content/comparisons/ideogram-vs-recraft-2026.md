Most AI image generator comparisons obsess over photorealism. But if you're making *design assets* — logos, posters, social graphics, icons, packaging mockups — the questions are different: Can it spell? Can it export something a client can actually use? Can it stay on brand across fifty assets?

That's the fight between **Ideogram** and **Recraft**, the two generators that carved out the design niche while [Midjourney and Flux battled over aesthetics](/compare/midjourney-vs-flux-2026/). We've used both extensively for real design tasks. Here's how they compare in 2026.

## Quick comparison

| | Ideogram | Recraft |
|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Text-heavy graphics, posters, logos concepts | Brand systems, vector assets, icons, mockups |
| **Text rendering** | Excellent — the long-time leader | Very good, occasionally less reliable on long text |
| **Vector (SVG) export** | No true native vector workflow | Yes — genuine editable vector output |
| **Editing tools** | Magic Fill, Canvas, remix, upscale | Full editor: inpainting, style tools, mockups, upscaling |
| **Style consistency** | Style references and codes | Custom styles from your own images; brand-oriented |
| **API** | Yes | Yes |
| **Free tier** | Yes, limited daily generations | Yes, limited credits |
| **Paid from (approx., Aug 2026)** | ~$8/month | ~$12/month |

## Text rendering

Ideogram built its reputation on being the first generator that could reliably spell, and that lead has held up remarkably well. Headlines, multi-line poster copy, stylized lettering — Ideogram gets it right on the first or second attempt more often than anything else we've tested. When the text *is* the design, it's still the safest pick.

Recraft is no slouch — it's firmly in the top tier and miles beyond general-purpose models — but on longer passages or unusual layouts it fumbles a bit more often, and you'll do a few more regenerations.

**Winner: Ideogram**, though the gap has narrowed to "noticeable" rather than "decisive."

## Vector output and design workflows

This is Recraft's trump card, and it's not a gimmick. Recraft can generate **actual vector graphics** — editable SVGs with clean paths you can open in Illustrator or Figma and rework like any designer-made asset. For logos, icons, and illustrations destined for print or scaling, this changes what "AI-generated" means: you get a starting file, not a flattened picture of one.

Ideogram outputs raster images. They're high quality, and upscaling covers many use cases, but if a client asks for the vector file, you're tracing or rebuilding. Third-party raster-to-SVG converters exist, but the results need cleanup and never match native vector generation.

Recraft also leans into production workflows: icon sets in a consistent style, product mockups, and background removal live inside the tool rather than requiring a round-trip to other software.

**Winner: Recraft**, comfortably. If "vector" is in your requirements, this comparison is already over.

## Editing and iteration

Ideogram's Magic Fill (inpainting) and Canvas give you a capable workspace for extending images, fixing regions, and combining elements. The remix workflow — feeding an image back in with a modified prompt — is fast and intuitive, and the community feed is genuinely useful for finding prompt styles that work.

Recraft's editor feels more like a lightweight design tool than a prompt playground. You can select and regenerate regions, adjust individual elements in vector pieces, apply styles, and set exact brand colors. The learning curve is slightly steeper; the ceiling is higher.

**Winner: tie**, leaning Recraft for designers, Ideogram for speed.

## Style control and brand consistency

Both tools understand that design work means making asset #40 match asset #1.

Ideogram offers style references and reusable style codes, which work well for keeping a campaign visually coherent. It's a prompt-centric approach: flexible, fast, occasionally drifty.

Recraft lets you build **custom styles from your own uploaded images** and reuse them across generations, plus define brand color palettes that generations actually respect. For agencies and in-house teams producing branded assets at volume, this is the more serious system. It pairs well with the kind of multi-tool stacks we covered in our [AI tools for designers guide](/ai-tools-designers-2026/).

**Winner: Recraft** for brand systems; Ideogram is fine for one-off campaign coherence.

## Image quality and range

For general aesthetic quality — photorealism, illustration, atmosphere — both are strong, and both have improved substantially since their breakout releases (see our standalone [Ideogram review](/reviews/ideogram-review-2026/) and [Recraft review](/recraft-ai-image-design-review-2026/) for deeper dives). Ideogram tends to produce livelier, more varied compositions from loose prompts. Recraft tends toward cleaner, more controlled output that reads as "designed."

Neither is the tool we'd pick for cinematic concept art — that's still Midjourney/Flux territory. These two win when the output needs to function as a design asset.

**Winner: tie** — different flavors, both excellent within the design lane.

## API and pricing

Both offer APIs with per-image pricing, and both are realistic options for building image generation into a product. Recraft's vector generation via API is a differentiator if your product needs scalable assets.

On subscriptions, as of August 2026 (approximate — both change plans regularly): Ideogram's paid plans start around **$8/month** with higher tiers around $20–$60 adding more priority generations and features. Recraft starts around **$12/month** with team-oriented tiers above that. Both free tiers are genuinely usable for evaluation — Ideogram's daily free generations are a bit more generous for casual use.

One licensing note: check each tool's current terms on commercial use for free-tier images before shipping client work. Paid tiers on both are commercially safe; free tiers have differed on this historically.

**Winner: Ideogram** on price for individuals; **Recraft** justifies its premium for teams that use the vector and brand features.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Ideogram if:**

- Text accuracy is your #1 requirement — posters, thumbnails, ads, memes, event graphics
- You want the lowest-friction path from prompt to finished graphic
- You're an individual creator and price sensitivity matters
- You iterate fast and loose rather than working within a brand system

**Choose Recraft if:**

- You need vector output — logos, icons, illustrations for print or product
- You're producing branded assets at volume and consistency is non-negotiable
- You want mockups, background removal, and design editing in one place
- You're a designer who wants AI as a production tool, not just an idea generator

**Use both if** you're an agency or full-time designer: Ideogram for text-led concepts and rapid exploration, Recraft for production assets. At roughly $20/month combined for entry tiers, the pairing costs less than most single premium AI subscriptions and covers the whole design pipeline.

For everyone else: be honest about whether you need vectors. If yes, Recraft. If no, Ideogram's text mastery and lower price make it the better default.
