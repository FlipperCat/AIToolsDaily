## vidIQ vs TubeBuddy (2025): Which YouTube Growth Tool Earns Its Subscription?

vidIQ and TubeBuddy have been the two default YouTube growth extensions for most of a decade. Both sit inside YouTube Studio, both show you keyword scores and competitor stats, and both have spent the last two years bolting AI onto everything they can reach.

They have also drifted apart. vidIQ has bet on AI-driven ideation and coaching. TubeBuddy has doubled down on channel management and bulk operations. That makes the choice less about which one is "better" and more about what kind of creator you are, so this comparison is organised around the jobs you would actually hire either tool to do.

## At a glance

| | vidIQ | TubeBuddy |
|---|---|---|
| **Core strength** | Ideation, research, AI coaching | Channel management, bulk edits, testing |
| **Where it runs** | Browser extension, web app, mobile app | Browser extension, mobile app |
| **Keyword research** | Strong, with competition and trend scoring | Strong, with search volume and weighted scores |
| **AI features** | AI Coach chatbot, daily video ideas, title and thumbnail generation, outlier finder | AI title generator, thumbnail generator, Suggested Shorts, thumbnail analysis |
| **Thumbnail A/B testing** | No | Yes, on higher tiers |
| **Bulk editing** | Limited | Extensive |
| **Free tier** | Yes, usable | Yes, limited |
| **Paid entry point (approx., mid-2025)** | Higher, roughly $49/month monthly for the main tier | Lower, under $10/month for the entry tier |
| **Best for** | Solo creators deciding what to make | Creators managing a large back catalogue |

## Keyword and topic research

Both tools do the basics well. Type a phrase and you get a search volume estimate, a competition score, related terms, and a list of videos already ranking. Both also inject a stats panel next to any video you watch, showing tags, view velocity, and engagement rates.

vidIQ's research surface is broader. Its keyword tool leans on trend data, so you can see whether interest in a topic is climbing or fading, and the Outliers feature surfaces videos that are massively outperforming their channel's average. That is the single most useful research view either tool offers, because an outlier from a small channel tells you a topic has untapped demand.

TubeBuddy's Keyword Explorer is more conservative and more transparent. It shows a weighted score and breaks it down into search volume, competition, and optimisation strength so you can see why a term scores the way it does. Its Search Explorer also lets you filter by video length, upload date, and channel size, which is handy for finding gaps that a new channel can realistically compete in.

**Edge: vidIQ** for finding topics. **TubeBuddy** if you want to understand the score rather than trust it.

## AI ideation and coaching

This is where the two tools have diverged most.

vidIQ has built its 2025 pitch around AI. The daily ideas feed generates video concepts based on your channel and niche, the AI Coach is a chatbot that answers questions using your own analytics ("why did my last three videos underperform?"), and there are one-click generators for titles, descriptions, and thumbnail concepts. The quality is uneven. The title generator is genuinely useful for getting unstuck. The idea feed produces a lot of generic suggestions that you will scroll past. The coach is best treated as a way to query your analytics in plain English rather than as a strategist.

TubeBuddy has AI too, but it is narrower: a title generator, a thumbnail generator, an AI-assisted description writer, and Suggested Shorts, which scans a long video and proposes clips to cut. The Suggested Shorts feature is more practically valuable than most of vidIQ's ideation, because it turns work you already did into more uploads. The titles are fine, similar to what you would get by pasting your topic into ChatGPT.

If you want AI to help write the actual video, neither tool is the right place. Our guide to [writing YouTube scripts with AI](/tutorials/write-youtube-video-scripts-with-ai-2024/) covers that workflow separately.

**Edge: vidIQ** on breadth. **TubeBuddy** on the one AI feature that reliably pays for itself.

## Thumbnails and testing

TubeBuddy has offered A/B testing of thumbnails and titles for years, on its higher tier. You upload two versions, it alternates them, and it reports which one won on click-through rate. This used to be a decisive advantage.

It matters less in 2025 because YouTube Studio now has its own built-in Test & Compare feature that runs thumbnail tests natively, on every channel, for free. TubeBuddy's version still has advantages: it can test titles as well as thumbnails, and it gives more detailed reporting. But the moat has shrunk.

vidIQ does not do A/B testing at all. It compensates with an AI thumbnail generator and a preview tool that shows your thumbnail next to competitors' in a simulated search page. That preview is worth using every upload. The generator produces backgrounds and layouts that need work before they are publishable; our [AI thumbnail workflow](/tutorials/create-youtube-thumbnails-with-ai-2024/) explains how to composite properly rather than trusting a one-shot generation.

**Edge: TubeBuddy**, though YouTube's own testing tool has narrowed the gap.

## Channel management and bulk tools

If you have more than fifty videos, this section probably decides it.

TubeBuddy's Bulk Processing tools let you find and replace text across every description on your channel, add or remove end screens and cards in bulk, update tags across a whole playlist, and apply changes to hundreds of videos in one pass. If you have ever needed to swap an expired affiliate link across three years of uploads, this is the feature that justifies the subscription on its own.

TubeBuddy also handles scheduling, comment filtering and canned responses, playlist actions, and a "best time to publish" recommendation based on your audience's activity.

vidIQ has nothing comparable. It offers a few management conveniences, like a channel audit and competitor tracking, but it is built around deciding what to make, not maintaining what you already made.

**Edge: TubeBuddy**, decisively.

## Analytics and competitor tracking

Both add a stats bar to every video page and a dashboard summarising your channel. vidIQ's competitor tracking is stronger: you can add channels to a watchlist and get alerts when they upload or when one of their videos takes off, and the Trend Alerts feature notifies you when something in your niche is spiking.

TubeBuddy's Competitor Scorecard covers similar ground with less polish, and its Videolytics panel gives a good per-video breakdown when you are studying a specific competitor's upload.

**Edge: vidIQ.**

## Pricing

Both change prices regularly, and both discount heavily for annual billing, so treat these as approximate as of July 2025.

**vidIQ** has a free tier that includes the stats overlay and limited keyword research. The main paid plan, Boost, runs in the region of $49 a month billed monthly and substantially less on an annual plan, and includes the full AI feature set. Higher tiers add one-on-one coaching sessions.

**TubeBuddy** has a limited free tier. Its Pro plan costs under $10 a month and covers most research and productivity tools. The Legend tier, which adds A/B testing and unlimited bulk processing, sits in the low-to-mid $30s per month, again with an annual discount. TubeBuddy has historically offered a discount for channels under a thousand subscribers, which makes it very cheap for a new creator.

On price alone, TubeBuddy wins at the entry point. At the top tiers they are closer than they look.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose vidIQ if:**

- You are a solo creator whose main problem is deciding what to make next.
- You want competitor and trend alerts to catch topics early.
- You will actually use the AI coach to interrogate your analytics.
- You are willing to pay more for research depth.

**Choose TubeBuddy if:**

- You have a large back catalogue that needs periodic maintenance.
- You want to test titles, not just thumbnails.
- You want to mine long videos for Shorts without editing each one by hand.
- You are on a tight budget or under a thousand subscribers.

**Use both if:** you run a channel professionally and the combined cost is small relative to revenue. Plenty of full-time creators run vidIQ for research and TubeBuddy for management. The extensions coexist in the same browser without conflict.

**Skip both if:** you upload occasionally and mostly want ideas. YouTube Studio's own Inspiration tab and analytics have absorbed a surprising amount of what these tools used to be the only source for, and a general-purpose AI chatbot handles title brainstorming for free. Our broader roundup of [AI tools for YouTubers](/ai-tools-for-youtubers/) covers the alternatives worth pairing with either extension.

The honest summary: vidIQ is a better research tool, TubeBuddy is a better operations tool, and in 2025 the question is which of those two problems is actually holding your channel back.
