Two AI image generators dominate casual conversation right now. **Midjourney** is the one behind most of the jaw-dropping images on social media, especially since version 5 arrived in March. **Bing Image Creator**, which Microsoft launched the same month, runs on an advanced version of OpenAI's DALL-E and is free to anyone with a Microsoft account.

One costs at least $10 a month and lives inside Discord. The other is free and opens in a browser tab. That difference alone settles it for some people. For everyone else, the details matter. We ran both through portraits, product shots, illustrations, and a few deliberately awkward prompts to see where each one wins.

## Quick Comparison

| | Midjourney (v5.1) | Bing Image Creator |
|---|---|---|
| **Underlying model** | Midjourney's own model | Advanced version of OpenAI's DALL-E |
| **Where you use it** | Discord (bot commands) | Bing.com/create, Bing Chat, Edge sidebar |
| **Price (as of June 2023)** | ~$10, $30, or $60/month; no free trial | Free with a Microsoft account |
| **Images per prompt** | 4-image grid, then upscale or vary | 4 images |
| **Aspect ratios** | Almost any, via `--ar` | Square only |
| **Fine control** | Many parameters, image prompts, `/blend`, `/describe` | Prompt text only |
| **Speed** | Fast GPU hours; relaxed mode on higher plans | Fast with "boosts," slower without |
| **Privacy** | Images public by default; Stealth mode on top plan | Images saved to your account |
| **Commercial use** | Allowed on paid plans (with conditions) | Terms limit use to personal, non-commercial purposes |
| **Best for** | Artists, designers, serious hobbyists | Casual users, quick one-off images |

## Image Quality

**Midjourney wins, and it isn't close for most styles.** Version 5 was a big step up: more photorealistic lighting, better skin texture, and much better hands than earlier versions. Version 5.1, now the default, adds a stronger built-in sense of style, and its RAW mode tones that down when you want something more literal.

Midjourney's output has a polish that's hard to match. Lighting looks deliberate, compositions feel framed, and even simple prompts come back looking like finished art. The downside is a recognizable "Midjourney look." It tends to add drama you didn't ask for.

**Bing Image Creator is good, but less consistent.** It handles straightforward prompts well, such as illustrations, cartoons, simple scenes, and stock-style objects. It's also more literal than Midjourney, which helps when you need the prompt followed exactly. Where it falls behind is photorealism and fine detail: faces can look waxy, textures smear, and complex scenes get muddled.

**Text in images** is weak in both. Expect garbled letters on signs, posters, and logos. Neither tool is ready for typography work yet.

## Prompt Following

This one is closer than the quality gap suggests. Bing, built on DALL-E, tends to include the specific objects you list and place them roughly where you asked. Midjourney sometimes treats your prompt as a mood board, giving you a gorgeous image that skips the third thing you mentioned.

If your prompt is "a red bicycle leaning against a blue door with a cat on the seat," Bing is more likely to give you all three elements. Midjourney is more likely to give you an image you'd frame, minus the cat.

## Control and Customization

**Midjourney has far more control.** Through parameters added to your prompt, you can set:

- **Aspect ratio** (`--ar 16:9`, `--ar 2:3`, and so on)
- **Stylization strength** (`--stylize`) to dial its artistic flair up or down
- **Variety** (`--chaos`) to get more different results in each grid
- **Negative prompts** (`--no text, people`)
- **Seeds** for more repeatable results

You can also use images as prompts, merge several images with `/blend`, and use `/describe` to have Midjourney suggest prompts from an image you upload. After each grid, you can upscale an image or create variations of it, which makes iteration fast. For a deeper look, see our [Midjourney v5 review](/reviews/midjourney-v5-review-2023/).

**Bing gives you a text box.** No aspect ratio options (everything is square), no parameters, and no image uploads. You change results by rewriting the prompt. That's a feature for beginners and a dealbreaker for anyone producing images for a specific layout, like a blog header or a phone wallpaper.

## Ease of Use

**Bing Image Creator wins clearly.** Go to the site, sign in with a Microsoft account, type a prompt, and get four images. You can also generate images inside Bing Chat by asking for them in conversation, or from the sidebar in Microsoft Edge.

**Midjourney's Discord interface is its biggest barrier.** You join a Discord server, find a newbie channel or message the bot directly, and type `/imagine` commands while other people's images scroll past. It works, and power users like it, but many beginners give up before their first image. Midjourney has a web gallery for browsing your work, but generation still happens in Discord.

## Pricing

**Midjourney** (approximate, as of June 2023):

- **Basic, ~$10/month:** a limited monthly amount of fast generation time, roughly a couple hundred images.
- **Standard, ~$30/month:** more fast hours plus unlimited "relaxed" generations, which are queued and slower.
- **Pro, ~$60/month:** even more fast hours and **Stealth mode**, which keeps your images out of the public gallery.

Annual billing lowers these prices. Midjourney paused its free trial in spring 2023 after a surge of misuse, so right now you have to pay to try it.

**Bing Image Creator is free.** You get "boosts" that put your request in the fast lane; when they run out, images still generate, just more slowly. Boosts replenish over time, and you can earn more through Microsoft Rewards. For occasional use, you may never notice the limit.

## Commercial Use and Ownership

This matters if you're making images for a business.

**Midjourney** lets paid subscribers use their images commercially, with a condition for larger companies: at the time of writing, businesses above a revenue threshold need the Pro plan. Remember that images are public by default, so other users can see your prompts and results unless you pay for Stealth mode.

**Bing Image Creator's terms** have so far framed use as legal, personal, and non-commercial. If you're creating client work, ads, or product imagery, read the current terms before relying on it. They're the most likely part of this comparison to change.

In both cases, the copyright status of AI-generated images is still unsettled. Don't assume you can register or exclusively protect them.

## Content Rules

Both tools filter prompts and refuse explicit, violent, or otherwise restricted content. Bing is noticeably stricter. It sometimes blocks harmless prompts that happen to include a flagged word, and you get a "content warning" with little explanation. Midjourney also bans words and moderates its community, but in normal creative work its filter gets in the way less often.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Midjourney if:**

- Image quality and style are your top priority.
- You need specific aspect ratios for social posts, thumbnails, or print.
- You want to iterate with variations, upscales, and image prompts.
- You plan to use images commercially and can pay for a plan.
- You don't mind learning Discord commands.

**Choose Bing Image Creator if:**

- You want good images for free, today, without setup.
- You're making personal projects, presentations, or quick concept sketches.
- You value literal prompt-following over artistic polish.
- You already use Edge or Bing Chat.

**Our take:** Start with Bing Image Creator. It's free, and it will quickly show whether AI images fit your workflow. If you find yourself fighting the square format, wishing for more realism, or making images for paying clients, Midjourney's Basic plan is the logical upgrade.
