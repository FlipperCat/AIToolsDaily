Picking a newsletter platform used to come down to one question: do you want to pay a
monthly fee or give up a cut of your subscription revenue? That's still the core of
**Beehiiv vs Substack**, but the decision has more parts now. Beehiiv has built out AI
writing helpers, ad tools, and growth features. Substack has leaned into being a social
network for writers, with discovery, notes, video, and recommendations.

Many newsletter writers also draft with AI, whether in the platform or in a separate
assistant, so we looked at how each platform fits that workflow as well as the usual
questions about money and growth.

## Quick Comparison

| Dimension | Beehiiv | Substack |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Business model | Monthly subscription tiers; free starter plan | Free to start; takes a percentage of paid subscription revenue |
| Built-in AI tools | AI writing, editing, and image helpers in the editor | Minimal native AI; most writers draft elsewhere |
| Discovery / network | Recommendations network, boosts, referral program | Large reader network, app, recommendations, Notes |
| Monetization | Paid subs, ad network, boosts, sponsorships | Paid subs, founding members; sponsorships handled yourself |
| Design control | Flexible templates, custom website builder | Clean but uniform look; limited customization |
| Analytics | Detailed (segments, clicks, acquisition sources) | Basic to moderate |
| Ownership / exit | Export list and content | Export list and content; network followers are harder to move |
| Best for | Operators building a newsletter business | Writers who want an audience and community fast |

Pricing and features change often. Treat everything here as accurate in broad strokes
as of October 2026, and check each platform's current pricing page.

## Pricing and Fees

**Beehiiv** uses a tiered SaaS model. There's a free plan with a subscriber cap and
limited features. Paid tiers unlock advanced growth tools, ad monetization, automations,
and premium subscriptions, and the price rises with your subscriber count. Beehiiv
doesn't take a percentage of your paid subscription revenue, though standard payment
processing fees still apply.

**Substack** costs nothing to start. If you turn on paid subscriptions, it takes a
percentage of that revenue (historically around 10%) plus payment processing fees.

The math is simple. A small or free newsletter costs nothing on Substack. Once paid
revenue grows, a percentage-based fee can cost far more than a flat Beehiiv plan. Run
your own numbers at your projected paid subscriber count.

## AI Writing and Editing

This is where the two platforms differ most in 2026.

**Beehiiv** includes AI helpers in its editor: drafting from a prompt, rewriting or
shortening sections, adjusting tone, generating subject lines, and creating images. The
convenience is real. You don't copy text between tabs, and subject-line suggestions are
useful for quick testing. Quality is roughly what you'd expect from embedded AI: fine
for tightening and brainstorming, generic when you ask it to write a whole issue.

**Substack** offers little native AI. Most Substack writers who use AI draft or edit in
an assistant like ChatGPT or Claude and paste the result in (our
[ChatGPT vs Claude](/compare/chatgpt-vs-claude/) comparison covers which suits long-form
writing). There's also a cultural point: Substack's audience leans toward personal,
voice-driven writing, and readers there are often quick to notice and dislike text that
reads as AI-generated.

Our take: native AI shouldn't decide this choice. A separate assistant works with either
platform, and the best results come from a deliberate workflow (research, outline, your
own draft, AI edit) rather than an in-editor button. Our
[AI newsletter workflow tutorial](/tutorials/build-ai-newsletter-workflow/) walks
through one.

## Growth and Discovery

**Substack's** biggest advantage is its network. Readers already have accounts, the app
surfaces new publications, recommendations from other writers drive real subscriber
growth, and Notes works as a built-in social feed. For a new writer with no audience,
that discovery loop is hard to match.

**Beehiiv** offers growth tools rather than a destination. Its recommendations network,
paid "boosts" (where other newsletters promote yours for a fee per subscriber), referral
programs, and landing pages are strong. But you are building traffic yourself rather
than tapping into a reader app.

If you already bring an audience from social, a podcast, or a business, Beehiiv's tools
help you convert and grow it efficiently. If you're starting from zero, Substack's
network gives you more of a tailwind.

## Monetization

**Substack** is built around paid subscriptions. Free and paid tiers, founding member
pricing, and a frictionless checkout for readers who already have a Substack account.
Sponsorships are possible, but you arrange them yourself.

**Beehiiv** offers more revenue paths: premium subscriptions, a built-in ad network that
matches newsletters with advertisers, boosts (earning by recommending others), and
tools for selling your own sponsorships. For newsletters monetized mainly through ads,
such as niche industry roundups, Beehiiv is the more natural fit.

## Design, Website, and Analytics

**Beehiiv** gives you more control: customizable email templates, a website builder for
your newsletter's home, custom domains, and detailed analytics on opens, clicks,
segments, and where subscribers came from. Operators who A/B test subject lines and
track acquisition costs will want this.

**Substack** keeps things deliberately simple. Every publication looks broadly similar:
clean, readable, and recognizably Substack. Analytics cover the basics. For many writers
the simplicity is a feature, since there's less to fiddle with and more time to write.

## Ownership and Lock-In

Both platforms let you export your subscriber list and content, which matters if you
ever leave. The difference is subtle. On Substack, some of your audience may be app
followers or reach you through the network rather than as plain email subscribers, and
that relationship doesn't fully move with you. On Beehiiv you're operating more like a
traditional email platform, so your list is the asset.

## Pros and Cons at a Glance

**Beehiiv**
- Pros: no revenue cut, built-in AI helpers, deep analytics, ad network, strong design
  control.
- Cons: monthly cost rises with list size, no built-in reader app, and more settings to
  manage.

**Substack**
- Pros: free to start, strong discovery network, easy paid subscriptions, minimal setup.
- Cons: percentage fee grows with revenue, limited design and analytics, little native
  AI, and some audience relationships are tied to the network.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Substack if:**
- You're a writer starting with little or no audience and want discovery built in.
- Your newsletter is personal, essay-driven, or opinion-led, and community matters.
- You want to start free and only pay (via the revenue share) once you earn.

**Choose Beehiiv if:**
- You're running the newsletter as a business, with ads, sponsorships, or a funnel.
- You already have traffic from social, a website, or a product.
- You want detailed analytics, design control, and AI helpers in the editor.
- Your projected paid revenue makes a percentage fee more expensive than a flat plan.

**Either works if** you mainly want a reliable way to send a free weekly email. Then
pick the editor you enjoy writing in.

Whichever you choose, keep AI as an editor rather than the author. The newsletters that
grow on both platforms are the ones with a recognizable human voice and a clear reason
to open the next issue.
