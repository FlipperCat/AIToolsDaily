If you want to edit video without paying for Premiere Pro, two free editors come up again and again: **Clipchamp**, Microsoft's browser-based editor that now ships with Windows 11, and **CapCut**, ByteDance's editor that became the default for TikTok and Reels creators.

Both have added AI features quickly: automatic captions, text-to-speech, background removal, and one-click edits. They're aimed at different people, though. Clipchamp is a clean, safe choice for office and school work. CapCut is built for short-form creators who want trends, effects, and speed.

We edited the same set of projects in both: a talking-head explainer, a product demo, and a batch of vertical social clips. Here's how they compare.

## Ease of use

**Clipchamp** feels like a simplified desktop editor: media library on the left, preview in the middle, timeline at the bottom. Nothing is hidden behind gestures, and it's hard to get lost. If you've used PowerPoint, you can produce something presentable in Clipchamp in an afternoon.

**CapCut** packs much more onto the screen. The desktop app has deep menus for effects, text animations, keyframes, and audio tools. There's more to learn, but experienced editors will move faster in it. On mobile, CapCut is clearly better. Clipchamp's mobile story is much weaker.

**Winner:** Clipchamp for beginners, CapCut for anyone who edits regularly.

## AI features

This is where the two differ most.

### Captions

Both generate automatic subtitles from speech with good accuracy on clear audio. CapCut's advantage is styling: animated word-by-word captions, highlight colors, and presets that match what's popular on TikTok. Clipchamp's captions are accurate and easy to edit, but the styling is more conservative. That's fine for a training video and plain for Reels.

### Text-to-speech

Clipchamp's text-to-speech covers a wide range of languages and natural-sounding voices. It's useful for narrating slides or product walkthroughs when you don't want to record your own voice. CapCut's voices include the familiar social media narrators along with more neutral options. For corporate narration, Clipchamp sounds more appropriate. For social content, CapCut's voices fit the format.

### Automated editing

Clipchamp's AutoCompose builds a draft video from your uploaded clips and photos based on a style you pick. It's a decent starting point for a quick recap video, but you'll usually rework it. Clipchamp also has tools for cleaning up audio, such as reducing background noise.

CapCut goes further. It can cut a long video into short clips automatically, reframe horizontal footage for vertical formats, remove backgrounds without a green screen, and isolate vocals from music. If you turn podcasts or webinars into shorts, CapCut covers what a dedicated clipping tool would do.

**Winner:** CapCut, by a clear margin on breadth.

## Templates and assets

Clipchamp includes a stock library of video, audio, and images, with more on Premium. Its templates lean toward business uses such as intros, announcements, slideshows, and social ads.

CapCut's template library is enormous and tied to current trends. That's a real advantage if your goal is reach on TikTok or Instagram. The downside is that trend templates go stale quickly, and a growing share of the best effects and assets carries a Pro badge.

**Winner:** CapCut for social content, Clipchamp for business.

## Export quality and limits

Clipchamp's free plan exports at 1080p without a watermark, which is generous. Premium adds 4K export, a brand kit, premium stock, and content backup.

CapCut's free tier also exports at good quality, but check for Pro-only effects, filters, or assets before you finish. If you've used them, CapCut will ask you to upgrade or remove them at export. That's not a deal-breaker, but it can interrupt a deadline if you didn't notice the badges while editing.

**Winner:** Clipchamp, for fewer surprises at export.

## Privacy, policy, and workplace fit

For many teams, this section decides the choice.

**Clipchamp** sits inside the Microsoft ecosystem. Business and education accounts use Microsoft 365 sign-in, OneDrive storage, and the admin controls IT teams already manage. If your company runs on Microsoft 365, Clipchamp is the easiest editor to get approved.

**CapCut** is owned by ByteDance, the company behind TikTok. Some organizations restrict ByteDance apps on work devices, and a US law passed in 2024 requires ByteDance to divest its US apps or face removal from app stores, with a deadline in early 2025. How that affects CapCut in practice is still uncertain. If you depend on CapCut for client work, keep your raw footage and project assets backed up outside the app.

As with any cloud editor, read the terms of service before you upload client footage. Pay particular attention to the license you grant over your content.

**Winner:** Clipchamp for business use.

## Pricing (approximate, as of November 2024)

- **Clipchamp:** Free plan with 1080p exports. Premium costs around $12 per month, less with annual billing. Some Microsoft 365 plans include Clipchamp features.
- **CapCut:** Free plan with ads for some features and Pro-only assets. CapCut Pro costs roughly $8 to $10 per month, with discounts for annual billing that vary by platform and region.

Both companies change plans and features often. Check current prices before you commit.

## Where each falls short

**Clipchamp:**

- Browser-based editing struggles with long 4K projects.
- Effects and transitions are basic compared with CapCut.
- Weak mobile editing.

**CapCut:**

- More paywalled features than before, and they aren't always obvious until export.
- Ownership and regulatory uncertainty in some markets.
- Its trend-driven look can feel wrong for corporate or educational content.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Clipchamp if:**

- You're making training videos, internal updates, school projects, or simple marketing videos.
- Your organization runs on Microsoft 365 and IT approval matters.
- You want reliable free 1080p exports and a gentle learning curve.

**Choose CapCut if:**

- You publish short-form video on TikTok, Reels, or Shorts every week.
- You want animated captions, trend templates, and automatic clipping from long videos.
- You edit on your phone as much as on a computer.

## Verdict

CapCut is the more capable AI editor. Its captions, automatic clipping, and effects library are ahead of Clipchamp, and for social-first creators it's the default for a reason. Clipchamp is the safer and simpler choice: clean free exports, Microsoft account integration, and fewer surprises. Choose based on where your videos will be published and who needs to approve the tools you use.
