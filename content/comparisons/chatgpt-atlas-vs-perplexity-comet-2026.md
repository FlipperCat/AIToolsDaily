A year ago, the AI browser was a curiosity: a Chromium shell with a chatbot bolted to the side. Now two of the biggest names in AI ship one, and both want to be the window you live in all day. **Perplexity Comet** arrived first, in the summer of 2025, and went free to everyone that October. **ChatGPT Atlas** launched a few weeks later, macOS first, with OpenAI's weight behind it.

Both have had roughly a year of updates since. We have run them side by side as daily browsers for the last several weeks. The short version: they are built on the same engine and promise similar things, but they make opposite bets about what an AI browser is for. That difference decides which one you should install.

For a wider view of the category, our [Comet vs Dia comparison](/compare/comet-vs-dia-2026/) covers the other serious contender.

## The core bet

**Atlas** treats the browser as a ChatGPT client that happens to render web pages. The sidebar is the same ChatGPT you already use, with your account, your projects, and your memory. The new tab page is a ChatGPT prompt box. The pitch is continuity: you stop copying text into a chat tab because the chat is already looking at the tab.

**Comet** treats the browser as a search product first. The address bar sends you to Perplexity, with cited answers rather than ten blue links. The Comet Assistant sits beside the page and can summarize, compare across tabs, and, when asked, click through sites on your behalf. The pitch is replacing the research workflow, not the chat window.

If you already pay for ChatGPT, Atlas feels like coming home. If you already use Perplexity as your search engine, Comet feels like the obvious upgrade. If you use neither, read on.

## Assistant quality

Both sidebars read the current page and answer questions about it well. Summaries, "what does this clause mean," "turn this table into a spreadsheet," and "draft a reply to this email" all work in both.

Atlas has the edge on writing. Cursor chat, which lets you highlight text in any input field and ask ChatGPT to rewrite it in place, is the feature we used most. Composing in Gmail, Notion, or a CMS without leaving the field is a small thing that compounds over a day. It also benefits from ChatGPT's memory: if you have told ChatGPT how you like emails written, Atlas already knows.

Comet has the edge on research. Ask it to compare three product pages open in different tabs and it does so with citations back to each. Ask a follow-up question and it keeps the sources straight. Perplexity's search backbone shows here. Atlas can search too, but the results read like ChatGPT with web access, which is a different texture than a sourced answer.

Neither assistant is immune to hallucination when summarizing long or badly formatted pages. We caught both inventing a detail from a PDF that was not there. Verify before you forward.

## Agent mode

This is the headline feature for both and the one to be most skeptical about.

In both browsers, agent mode lets the assistant take over the tab: navigate, click, fill forms, add items to carts, and work through multi-step tasks while you watch. Both pause on sensitive sites like banking and payments, both ask before completing purchases, and both will stop and hand control back when confused.

Atlas's agent inherits OpenAI's work on ChatGPT agent, covered in our [ChatGPT agent mode guide](/tutorials/chatgpt-agent-mode-guide-2026/). It is methodical, narrates what it is doing, and is better at recovering when a page does not behave as expected. The fuller agent experience is reserved for paid ChatGPT tiers.

Comet's assistant is faster to start a task and better integrated with search, so "find me three hotels under $200 near the venue and open them" flows naturally. Perplexity's higher tiers add background tasks that run without occupying your visible tab. The free tier gets a limited version.

Both are slower than doing simple tasks yourself. Agent mode earns its keep on tedious, repetitive, low-stakes work: unsubscribing from newsletters, filling out the same form across several sites, collecting prices into a sheet. It does not earn its keep for anything you would be upset to see done wrong.

## Security and prompt injection

This matters more in an AI browser than anywhere else. An agent that reads web pages and takes actions can be manipulated by hidden instructions on those pages. Security researchers demonstrated this against agentic browsers, including Comet, in 2025, and both companies have shipped mitigations since: confirmations before sensitive actions, restrictions on what the agent can read while logged in, and modes that watch rather than act on high-risk sites.

Neither has solved the problem, because it is not fully solvable with current models. Our practical advice is the same for both: keep agent mode off by default, turn it on for specific tasks, and never let it operate in a logged-in session of your bank, your password manager, or your work admin console.

## Memory and privacy

Atlas's browser memories are optional and off until you turn them on. When enabled, ChatGPT remembers things about the sites you visit and what you did there, which makes later questions like "what was that article about tax changes I read last week" actually answerable. You can view, delete, and disable memories, and there is an incognito mode that logs you out of ChatGPT entirely. Browsing content is not used for training by default, though check the current settings, since defaults can change.

Comet's equivalent lives on the paid side: personal search across your history and connected accounts like email and calendar. Perplexity also ships a built-in ad and tracker blocker, which Atlas does not. For users who care about the surveillance side of the web, that is a concrete point for Comet.

Both companies collect data to run the product. If you are evaluating either for a regulated workplace, read the data processing terms rather than the marketing page.

## Everyday browser quality

Both are Chromium, so extensions, passwords, and bookmarks import cleanly from Chrome. Tab management, performance, and compatibility are essentially Chrome-level in both.

Comet has had longer to mature as a browser and it shows in small ways: more settings, a steadier update cadence on Windows, and fewer rough edges in the sidebar layout. Atlas launched tighter but narrower, and while its platform coverage has grown since the macOS-only debut, Windows users should confirm the current status before planning a switch.

## Pricing

Both browsers are free to download and use.

- **Atlas**: works with a free ChatGPT account. Paid ChatGPT plans (Plus, Pro, Business) unlock the fuller agent mode and higher usage limits. See our [ChatGPT Plus review](/reviews/chatgpt-plus-review-2026/) for what the subscription includes beyond the browser.
- **Comet**: free, with Perplexity Pro and Max unlocking more agent capacity, background tasks, personal search, and the Comet Plus publisher bundle. Our [Comet review](/reviews/perplexity-comet-browser-review-2026/) breaks down the tiers.

Neither browser alone justifies a subscription. Buy the subscription for the assistant you want, and the browser comes along.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose ChatGPT Atlas if:**

- You already pay for ChatGPT and use its memory and projects.
- You write a lot in web apps and want inline rewriting everywhere.
- You want a single assistant with one personality across desktop, mobile, and browser.
- You are on a Mac.

**Choose Perplexity Comet if:**

- Research and comparison shopping are most of your browsing.
- You want cited answers as your default search experience.
- You value a built-in ad blocker.
- You need Windows support today without checking a roadmap.

**Choose neither yet if:**

- You rely on a browser with strong enterprise management and policy controls.
- Your work involves sensitive logged-in sessions you cannot risk exposing to an agent.
- You just want Chrome with a chatbot. Chrome itself has Gemini built in now, and Edge has Copilot mode. The switching cost may not be worth it.

Our pick for most people: **Comet** as a daily browser, because its search-first design improves the thing you do most, and its agent features degrade gracefully when you ignore them. **Atlas** is the better choice for committed ChatGPT users, especially writers, and it is improving quickly. Install both, use each for a week, and keep the one you stop noticing.
