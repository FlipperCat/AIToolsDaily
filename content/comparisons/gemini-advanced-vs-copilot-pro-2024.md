Within a few weeks of each other, Google and Microsoft both launched a $20-a-month consumer AI subscription. **Copilot Pro** arrived in January, giving individuals priority access to GPT-4 Turbo and — for the first time — Copilot inside Word, Excel, and the rest of Office for non-enterprise users. **Gemini Advanced** followed in February, putting Google's flagship Gemini Ultra model behind a Google One AI Premium plan.

Same price, same pitch — "the good model, plus your productivity apps" — but the two products have meaningfully different shapes. We've spent several weeks with both. Here's how they actually compare.

## At a glance

| | Gemini Advanced | Copilot Pro |
|---|---|---|
| **Price (as of March 2024)** | ~$19.99/mo (Google One AI Premium) | ~$20/mo |
| **Flagship model** | Gemini 1.0 Ultra | GPT-4 Turbo (priority access) |
| **Office/app integration** | Gemini in Gmail, Docs & more rolling out to subscribers | Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, OneNote* |
| **Image generation** | Imagen-based, currently limited | DALL·E 3, 100 daily "boosts" |
| **Extras** | 2TB Google One storage, longer conversations | Copilot GPT Builder, faster peak-time performance |
| **Free fallback** | Free Gemini (Pro model) | Free Copilot (GPT-4 off-peak) |

*Copilot in Office apps requires a separate Microsoft 365 Personal or Family subscription.

That asterisk is doing a lot of work, and we'll come back to it.

## Model quality

**Copilot Pro** runs on GPT-4 Turbo with priority access during peak hours. It's the same model class that powers [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) Plus, and it behaves like it: strong reasoning, reliable instruction-following, mature tooling. If you've used ChatGPT Plus, you know exactly what you're getting — which is both the compliment and the criticism.

**Gemini Advanced** runs Gemini 1.0 Ultra, Google's first model pitched directly at the GPT-4 tier. In day-to-day use it's a genuine peer: excellent at creative writing and brainstorming, strong at explaining and summarizing, and noticeably good at tasks with a research flavor, where its Google Search grounding helps. It's less consistent than GPT-4 Turbo on multi-step logic and code — it can be brilliant one prompt and sloppy the next — and it still refuses harmless requests more often than it should.

Worth noting: Google has already announced Gemini 1.5 Pro with a dramatically longer context window, currently in limited developer preview. It's not what you get in Gemini Advanced today, but it signals where the product is headed.

**Edge: Copilot Pro, narrowly** — for consistency rather than peak capability.

## Productivity app integration

This is the real battleground, because both companies are betting you'll pay for AI *where you already work*.

**Copilot Pro** lights up Copilot buttons across Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and OneNote. Draft a document from a prompt in Word, summarize a long email thread in Outlook, generate a deck outline in PowerPoint, ask Excel questions about a table. Quality varies — Word drafting and Outlook summarization are the standouts; Excel's Copilot is still labeled preview and feels like it — but when it works, it's the closest thing yet to AI woven into mainstream office software. The catch: **you need a separate Microsoft 365 Personal or Family subscription** for the Office integration. Without one, Copilot Pro is mostly "faster GPT-4 with DALL·E 3."

**Gemini Advanced** ships as part of Google One AI Premium, and Google has been rolling Gemini into Gmail and Docs for subscribers — the successor to what was called [Duet AI](/reviews/google-duet-ai-workspace-review-2023/) last year. Where it's live, "Help me write" in Gmail and Docs is convenient for drafts and replies. But the integration is shallower than Microsoft's today: less coverage across Sheets and Slides for consumers, and features arriving gradually rather than all at once. What Google bundles instead is concrete: **2TB of Google One storage**, which many households already pay ~$10/month for on its own.

**Edge: Copilot Pro** if you live in Office *and* already pay for Microsoft 365. **Gemini Advanced** if you live in Gmail and Docs — the storage bundle softens the wait for deeper integration.

## Image generation

Copilot Pro includes DALL·E 3 through Designer with 100 daily boosts — fast, reliable, and good at following detailed prompts. Gemini's image generation is in worse shape: Google paused its people-image generation in February after embarrassing historical-accuracy failures, and as of this writing it hasn't fully returned. Even setting that incident aside, DALL·E 3 is the stronger, more controllable generator.

**Edge: Copilot Pro, clearly.**

## Ecosystem and extras

- **Copilot GPT Builder** lets Pro subscribers create custom Copilots, mirroring OpenAI's custom GPTs. It's early but points somewhere useful.
- **Gemini Advanced** supports longer conversations and file-based work, and its mobile story is strong — the Gemini app can replace Google Assistant on Android, which makes it the best phone-native AI assistant we've used.
- Both offer free tiers (free Gemini runs the Pro model; free Copilot offers GPT-4 at off-peak times), so neither subscription is the only way in — you're paying for the top model and the app hooks.

For the broader landscape of how Microsoft's assistant stacks against OpenAI's own product, see our [Copilot vs ChatGPT comparison](/compare/microsoft-copilot-vs-chatgpt-2024/).

## Privacy and trust

Neither company is a privacy charity, but read the settings on both. Gemini conversations may be reviewed to improve the product unless you adjust activity controls; Microsoft's consumer Copilot has its own data-use policies distinct from the enterprise "commercial data protection" pitch. If your prompts include sensitive material, spend five minutes in each product's privacy settings before you commit either way.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Copilot Pro if:**

- You already pay for Microsoft 365 and spend your day in Word, Excel, Outlook, or PowerPoint — this is the only way to get Office Copilot as an individual
- You want the most consistent GPT-4-class model without surprises
- Image generation matters to you (DALL·E 3 with generous limits)

**Choose Gemini Advanced if:**

- Your life runs on Gmail, Docs, and Drive rather than Office
- The 2TB storage bundle offsets a subscription you already pay for
- You're on Android and want a genuinely capable assistant on your phone
- You value research-flavored answers grounded in Google Search

**Choose neither if:** you don't need AI inside your documents and email. ChatGPT Plus remains the strongest standalone chatbot subscription, and the free tiers of both Gemini and Copilot are good enough to cover casual use.

## Bottom line

Copilot Pro is the safer $20 today: a proven model plus real (if uneven) Office integration — provided you're already inside the Microsoft 365 tent, which quietly makes its true cost higher. Gemini Advanced is the more interesting bet: a capable model, the best mobile assistant story, a storage bundle with concrete value, and a fast-improving roadmap — but its app integration is still catching up to its marketing. Pick based on whose apps you open every morning, because that — not benchmark deltas — is what you'll notice every day.
