Perplexity and Kagi are two of the most common destinations for people who are tired of Google, but they answer different questions.

**Perplexity** asks, "What's the answer?" It reads the web for you and writes a summary with numbered citations. **Kagi** asks, "Where's the best page?" It's a paid, ad-free search engine that puts ranked links first and makes AI optional.

We used each as our default search tool for two weeks of normal work: technical troubleshooting, product research, fact-checking, and everyday lookups. Here's where each one earns its place.

## Quick comparison

| | Perplexity | Kagi |
|---|---|---|
| Primary output | Written answer with numbered citations | Ranked list of links, AI summary on request |
| Business model | Free tier plus subscriptions; has tested advertising | Subscription only, no ads |
| Free option | Yes, with limits on advanced searches | Trial searches only |
| Paid plans (approx.) | Pro about $20/month; Max about $200/month | About $5, $10, or $25/month |
| AI chat | The core product; model choice on paid plans | Kagi Assistant; the broadest model access is on Ultimate |
| Deep research | Dedicated research mode for multi-step reports | More limited |
| Personalization | Spaces, custom instructions, source filters | Block, raise, or lower domains; Lenses; bangs |
| Browser | Comet | Orion (Mac and iPhone) |
| Best for | Getting answers and summaries fast | Finding and evaluating sources yourself |

## The core difference

This matters more than any feature list. With Perplexity, the answer is the product and the sources are supporting evidence. With Kagi, the sources are the product and the AI answer is an optional shortcut.

That changes how you work. Perplexity is faster when you trust the summary. Kagi is faster when you want to judge the sources yourself, because the links are already sorted, cleaner, and shaped by your own preferences.

## Answer quality and citations

Perplexity is very good at questions that require reading several pages. For example, "What changed between these two library versions?" or "Compare these three standing desks on warranty and weight capacity." It pulls from several sources, writes a structured answer, and handles follow-up questions well.

Its weakness is the same as every answer engine's. The summary can sound more certain than its sources are. In our testing, most citations supported the claims next to them, but some supported only part of a sentence, and a few pointed to thin, SEO-driven pages. For anything that matters, click through.

Kagi's **Quick Answer**, triggered by ending a query with a question mark, produces a shorter cited summary above the normal results. It's less ambitious than Perplexity, but the full results sit right below it, so checking a claim takes one click.

**Edge: Perplexity** for synthesis. **Kagi** for answers you can check quickly.

## Finding specific pages

When you want a URL rather than an answer, such as documentation, a forum thread, a company's support page, or an old blog post you half-remember, Kagi is clearly better. Its results carry fewer listicles and affiliate content farms, and there's no shopping clutter at the top of the page. Its **Small Web** results also surface independent sites that big engines tend to bury.

Perplexity can find pages, but it's awkward for this. You get a paragraph about the page when you only wanted a link to it.

**Edge: Kagi.**

## Personalization and control

This is Kagi's strongest advantage. You can **block** domains you never want to see, **lower** ones you distrust, and **raise** or **pin** ones you rely on. After a few weeks, your results reflect your own judgment. **Lenses** narrow a search to forums, academic sources, PDFs, programming sites, or a custom list of domains you define. **Bangs** such as `!w` for Wikipedia or `!yt` for YouTube send a query straight to another site.

Perplexity offers control at a different level. You can choose source types, such as the web, academic papers, or social discussions. **Spaces** let you group threads with custom instructions and uploaded files for an ongoing project. What you can't do is permanently train it to ignore a specific site the way Kagi can.

**Edge: Kagi.**

## AI models and chat

On paid plans, Perplexity lets you choose from its own Sonar models and third-party models from companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. The lineup changes often. Paid plans also add file uploads, image generation, and **Labs**, which builds reports, spreadsheets, and simple dashboards from a single prompt.

**Kagi Assistant** also offers models from several providers, and it can ground answers in Kagi's own search results, including your domain rankings. You can create custom assistants with their own instructions. The full model lineup and highest usage limits are tied to the Ultimate plan, and lower plans get more limited access. Check the current plan page for details, because Kagi has adjusted this over time.

**Edge: Perplexity** for a deeper AI toolset. **Kagi** if you want multi-model chat bundled with a search engine you already pay for.

## Research workflows

For long research tasks, Perplexity is ahead. Its research mode runs many searches, reads dozens of sources, and produces a structured report in a few minutes. Spaces keep project material together. For a first pass on an unfamiliar topic, it saves real time. See how it compares with a general chatbot in our [ChatGPT vs Perplexity comparison](/compare/chatgpt-vs-perplexity/).

Kagi works better as a tool for doing research yourself. Clean results, Lenses, and the **Universal Summarizer**, which condenses long articles, PDFs, and videos, make manual research faster without handing off the judgment.

**Edge: Perplexity** for delegated research. **Kagi** for research you want to control.

## Privacy and business model

Kagi's pitch is simple: you pay, so there are no ads and no incentive to profile you. Its privacy policy says it doesn't keep search history tied to your account, and it has added support for anonymous-token searching (Privacy Pass) for people who want extra separation between their account and their queries.

Perplexity funds its free tier with subscriptions and outside investment, and it has experimented with sponsored follow-up questions. Account settings let you limit how your data is used to improve its models. Perplexity has also faced criticism and legal challenges from publishers over how it uses their content, which some users weigh when choosing a tool.

**Edge: Kagi.**

## Pricing (as of September 2026)

Prices are approximate and change often, so check each company's pricing page before you subscribe.

**Perplexity**
- **Free:** unlimited basic searches, with a daily cap on advanced searches
- **Pro:** about **$20/month** (discounted annually), with more advanced searches, model choice, file uploads, and Labs
- **Max:** about **$200/month** for the highest limits and early access to new features
- **Enterprise:** per-seat business plans with admin and security controls

**Kagi**
- **Starter:** about **$5/month** for a capped number of searches (around 300 per month)
- **Professional:** about **$10/month** for unlimited searches
- **Ultimate:** about **$25/month** for unlimited searches plus the full Kagi Assistant model lineup
- Family and team plans are also available

The interesting comparison is Kagi Ultimate at about $25 versus Perplexity Pro at about $20. Both give you multi-model AI chat. Kagi adds a full ad-free search engine, and Perplexity adds the stronger research tools.

## Browsers

Both companies make a browser. **Comet**, Perplexity's Chromium-based browser, builds its assistant into every page and can carry out multi-step tasks such as comparing products across tabs. We covered it in our [Comet browser review](/reviews/perplexity-comet-browser-review-2026/). **Orion**, Kagi's WebKit-based browser for Mac and iPhone, focuses on speed, low memory use, and zero telemetry, and it supports many Chrome and Firefox extensions.

Neither browser is required to use the search product, but each shows where its company is heading: Perplexity toward an AI agent that browses for you, and Kagi toward a quieter web you browse yourself.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Perplexity if:**
- You mostly want answers, not links.
- You regularly research unfamiliar topics and value a cited first draft.
- You want a capable free tier, or you'd use Labs and the research mode.

**Choose Kagi if:**
- You search heavily for work and want cleaner, ad-free results.
- You want control over which sites appear in your results.
- Privacy and a subscription-only business model matter to you.
- You're a developer or researcher who needs to find specific pages quickly.

**Use both:** This is a common setup, and it works well. Set Kagi as your browser's default search engine, and keep Perplexity's free tier open for questions that need synthesis.

In our two weeks, Kagi became the tool we reached for dozens of times a day, and Perplexity became the one we opened a few times a day for bigger questions.
