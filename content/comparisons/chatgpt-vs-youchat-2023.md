Two months after ChatGPT went viral, the most common question we get is some version of "is there anything else like it?" The most usable answer right now is **YouChat**, the conversational assistant built into the You.com search engine. It launched in December, a few weeks after ChatGPT, and takes a noticeably different approach: it tries to be a search engine that talks, not a chatbot that happens to know things.

We've used both daily for most of January on real tasks: research questions, email drafts, code snippets, and explanations. Here's how they compare.

## Quick Comparison

| | ChatGPT | YouChat |
|---|---|---|
| **Maker** | OpenAI | You.com |
| **Underlying model** | GPT-3.5 series, fine-tuned for dialogue | Large language model (You.com hasn't detailed it) |
| **Web access** | None. Knowledge stops in 2021 | Yes, pulls in current search results |
| **Citations** | No | Often, as links to sources |
| **Price** | Free research preview | Free |
| **Account required** | Yes | No |
| **Availability** | Frequent "at capacity" lockouts | Generally available |
| **Best at** | Long-form writing, reasoning, code, back-and-forth | Quick factual lookups, recent topics |

## Answer Quality and Writing

On pure language ability, ChatGPT is clearly ahead. Ask both to draft a polite follow-up email to a client who's gone quiet, and ChatGPT gives you something you could send with light edits: the right tone, a sensible structure, and a clear ask. YouChat's version is serviceable but flatter, and it's more likely to pad the response or drift into generic phrasing.

The gap widens as tasks get longer. ChatGPT keeps track of a multi-turn conversation well: "make it shorter," "now more formal," "add a line about the invoice" all land. YouChat handles follow-ups, but it loses the thread more often and sometimes answers the follow-up as if it were a brand-new search.

For teachers, marketers, and anyone who writes for a living, this is the dimension that matters most, and ChatGPT wins it. (We've covered practical writing workflows in our guide to [writing lesson plans with ChatGPT](/tutorials/write-lesson-plans-with-chatgpt-2023/).)

## Current Information and Web Access

This is YouChat's reason to exist. ChatGPT's knowledge ends in 2021 and it can't browse, so it has no idea about anything recent: last week's news, current product prices, this season's sports results. Worse, it sometimes answers confidently anyway, with details that sound right but are out of date or invented.

YouChat blends live search results into its answers. Ask about a recent product launch or a news event from this month and you'll usually get a relevant summary. It isn't flawless. It can merge details from different sources or lean on a low-quality page. But the answer is at least connected to the present.

If your questions are about the current state of the world, YouChat is the more honest tool by design.

## Citations and Trust

Both chatbots can be wrong while sounding confident. The difference is whether you can check them.

ChatGPT gives you no sources. If it tells you a statistic, you have to find it yourself, and sometimes you'll find it doesn't exist. Ask it for references and it may produce convincing-looking citations to papers that were never published. Treat any factual claim from ChatGPT as a lead to verify, not an answer.

YouChat often includes links alongside its answers. Those links are the real value: you can click through and confirm the claim in seconds. The caveat is that a citation doesn't guarantee the summary is faithful to the source, so it's worth spot-checking anything important. Still, having a verification path is a meaningful advantage for research.

## Coding Help

Developers have adopted ChatGPT quickly, and for good reason. It writes working functions in common languages, explains error messages, converts code between languages, and walks through its reasoning when asked. It also makes mistakes: invented library functions, subtle off-by-one errors, outdated APIs. But as a first-draft generator and rubber duck, it's excellent. Our [guide to debugging code with ChatGPT](/tutorials/debug-code-with-chatgpt-2023/) covers how to get the most from it.

YouChat can produce code and will sometimes surface relevant documentation or Stack Overflow-style results, which helps when you need an up-to-date API. Its generated code is less consistent, though, and it's weaker at multi-step debugging conversations. You.com also offers separate developer-focused search features, but for "write and iterate on this with me," ChatGPT is stronger.

If you want code completion inside your editor rather than a chat window, a dedicated tool is a better fit. See our [GitHub Copilot vs Codeium comparison](/compare/github-copilot-vs-codeium/).

## Reliability and Access

ChatGPT's popularity is its biggest practical problem. At peak hours, you'll regularly hit the "ChatGPT is at capacity right now" screen, sometimes for long stretches. Conversations can also error out mid-response. OpenAI has signaled that a paid tier with more reliable access is coming, but for now, the free preview is the only option and access isn't guaranteed.

YouChat has been consistently available in our testing. You don't even need an account: go to You.com, switch to the chat tab, and start typing. For anyone who needs a chatbot *right now* and not after a lockout, that counts for a lot.

## Privacy

Both are free products in an early phase, and both say conversations may be reviewed to improve their systems. Don't paste in confidential client data, passwords, unreleased financials, or personal information about others, whichever tool you're using. This is a sensible default for every AI chatbot at this stage.

## Limitations Worth Knowing

**ChatGPT:**
- No knowledge past 2021, and no browsing.
- Confident fabrication, especially of sources, numbers, and niche facts.
- Capacity lockouts at busy times.
- Occasionally overcautious refusals on harmless requests.

**YouChat:**
- Weaker writing and reasoning quality.
- Can blend or misread search sources.
- Loses context in longer conversations.
- Answers feel more like search snippets stitched together than a considered response.

## Which Should You Choose?

The honest answer for most people is **both**, used for different jobs. They're free, and they complement each other well.

**Choose ChatGPT if you:**
- Write emails, posts, outlines, or reports and want a strong first draft.
- Need help brainstorming, explaining concepts, or thinking through a problem.
- Write code and want an interactive helper.
- Can tolerate the occasional capacity lockout.

**Choose YouChat if you:**
- Ask questions about recent events, products, or anything after 2021.
- Want links you can use to verify answers.
- Need something that works immediately, with no account and no waiting.
- Mainly use AI as a smarter search box.

**Our workflow:** use YouChat to gather current facts and sources, then take those verified facts to ChatGPT to shape them into polished writing. That plays to each tool's strength and avoids its biggest weakness.

This category is moving quickly. Microsoft's deepening partnership with OpenAI has fueled widespread speculation about chat features coming to search, and other companies are clearly working on their own assistants. We'll revisit this comparison as things change. For now, ChatGPT is the better thinker and writer, and YouChat is the better window onto the present.
