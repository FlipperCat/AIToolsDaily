If you write fiction and you've tried general chatbots for it, you've probably hit the same wall: tidy, cautious prose that reads like a book report. Two tools built specifically for storytellers take a different approach. **Sudowrite** is a writing assistant designed around the novelist's revision process. **NovelAI** is a story-continuation engine running its own in-house models, with an emphasis on privacy and deep control.

Both help you write fiction. They go about it in very different ways. Here's how they compare as of March 2023.

## At a Glance

| | **Sudowrite** | **NovelAI** |

| Core idea | Toolkit of writing actions (Write, Describe, Rewrite, Brainstorm) | Continuous story co-writing in one document |
| Underlying models | Built on large third-party language models | Its own models trained for storytelling |

## Writing Approach

**Sudowrite** treats the AI as a set of specialized tools. Highlight a passage and hit *Describe* to get sensory details (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste) you can work into your prose. Use *Rewrite* to try the same paragraph more vividly, with more tension, or from a different angle. *Write* continues from your cursor in your voice, offering a few options to choose from. *Brainstorm* generates character names, plot twists, or places on demand.

That structure maps closely to how many novelists actually work: draft, stall, poke at a scene, revise. You stay the author, and the AI is a room full of suggestions.

**NovelAI** is closer to a collaborative typewriter. You write, the model continues, you edit its output or regenerate, and the story grows in a single document. The AI's job is to keep going in the voice and direction you've set, and its models are trained heavily on fiction, so the output tends to read like a story instead of an essay.

**Verdict:** Sudowrite is better for revising and enriching a draft you own. NovelAI is better for co-drafting long passages in flow.

## Prose Quality

Both produce more natural fiction than a general assistant like [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/), which tends toward summarizing and moralizing unless heavily prompted.

Sudowrite benefits from large, capable underlying models, so its prose is fluent and its descriptive suggestions are often excellent. The weakness is a familiar "AI voice" that shows up if you accept output uncritically: a few too many adjectives, predictable metaphors.

NovelAI's output depends more on setup. With a good opening, a filled-in memory field, and sensible settings, it can match an established style closely over long stretches. With a blank page and default settings it can wander or lose track of details. In our testing, its ceiling was high but its floor was lower.

**Verdict:** Sudowrite is more consistent out of the box. NovelAI rewards effort with more stylistic flexibility.

## Control and Memory

This is NovelAI's biggest advantage. Its **Memory** field holds persistent context. The **Author's Note** steers tone and direction near the point of generation. The **Lorebook** stores entries for characters, places, and concepts that get pulled into context automatically when their keywords appear. You can also adjust sampling settings like randomness and repetition penalties, or train custom modules on a style.

Sudowrite keeps things simpler. You can supply context about characters and genre, but you're mostly relying on its tools to do the right thing. That's a feature for writers who don't want to tinker, and a limit for those who do.

**Verdict:** NovelAI for control freaks and world-builders; Sudowrite for writers who want results without configuration.

## Content Policies and Privacy

Genre writers often care about both, for different reasons.

NovelAI is notably permissive. Dark themes, violence, and adult content in fiction are generally allowed, and stories are encrypted locally before being stored on its servers, so the company says it can't read them. For horror, romance, and grimdark fantasy writers, and for anyone uneasy about unpublished manuscripts sitting on a server, that's a strong selling point.

Sudowrite runs on third-party language models and inherits some of their content restrictions. Most mainstream fiction is fine, but very explicit or extreme material can trigger refusals or watered-down output. Its privacy approach is that of a standard cloud service.

**Verdict:** NovelAI wins clearly on both counts if these matter to you.

## Ease of Use

Sudowrite is approachable from the first minute: paste in a chapter, highlight, click. The interface is designed for writers, not tinkerers.

NovelAI takes longer to learn. The defaults work, but getting the best from it means understanding memory, lorebook entries, and generation settings. There's a lively community sharing presets and tips, which helps a lot.

**Verdict:** Sudowrite.

## Extras

NovelAI includes an image generator, popular for anime-style character art and scene illustrations, which is useful for world-building and visualizing characters. Sudowrite focuses purely on text but includes some genuinely useful touches for writers, like feedback on a passage and tools for varying word choice.

If you want a distraction-free drafting space with light AI help rather than a fiction specialist, [Lex](/reviews/lex-ai-writing-editor-review-2023/) is worth a look as a third option.

## Pricing

As of March 2023, both use tiered monthly subscriptions in a similar range, roughly $10 to $25 per month. Sudowrite's tiers are based on how many AI words you can generate each month. NovelAI's tiers differ in model access, context length, and image-generation allowance, and it offers a limited free trial. Prices change often in this space, so check both sites before subscribing.

Heavy Sudowrite users can burn through a lower tier's word allowance quickly during intense drafting. NovelAI's text generation is more generous on higher tiers, which makes it better value for high-volume co-writing.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Sudowrite if:**
- You already have a draft and want help revising, describing, and getting unstuck.
- You want great results without learning settings.
- You write mainstream literary, commercial, or genre fiction without explicit content.

**Choose NovelAI if:**
- You like co-drafting long passages in a flowing, back-and-forth style.
- You're building a big world with many characters and want the AI to remember them.
- You write dark or mature fiction, or you want your manuscript encrypted.
- You enjoy tuning tools and are willing to invest time in setup.

**Consider both if** you're a prolific writer: some authors draft scenes in NovelAI and polish them with Sudowrite's rewriting and description tools.
