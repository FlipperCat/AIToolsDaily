Zoom quietly changed the meeting-assistant market when it started bundling AI Companion into paid Zoom plans at no extra cost. Suddenly every team already paying for Zoom has a free AI notetaker — which raises an obvious question: why keep paying for Otter? We've been running both side by side across a month of real meetings. The answer is less obvious than "free wins," but for some teams it genuinely is that simple.

## Quick comparison

| | Otter.ai | Zoom AI Companion |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Free tier (300 min/mo); Pro from roughly $8–17/mo | Included with paid Zoom plans |
| **Works with** | Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams | Zoom only |
| **Live transcript** | Yes, real-time with speaker labels | Captions live; full transcript with recording |
| **Meeting summary** | Yes, with action items | Yes, with next steps and chapters |
| **Ask questions mid-meeting** | Yes (Otter AI Chat) | Yes ("catch me up" style questions) |
| **Searchable archive** | Yes, across all meetings | Tied to Zoom recordings/inbox |
| **Extras** | Slide capture, channels, cross-meeting chat | Smart recording chapters, chat/email compose |

## Transcription quality

On raw accuracy, the two are closer than Otter fans might expect. Both handle clear audio well, both stumble on crosstalk, heavy accents, and niche jargon. Otter's speaker identification is more mature — it learns voices over time and labels speakers reliably once trained. Zoom's speaker attribution works, but corrections are clunkier.

Where Otter clearly wins is the *live* experience. The real-time transcript streams into the Otter panel as people speak, and you can highlight moments, drop comments, and tag teammates during the meeting. AI Companion's live features center on asking questions ("What did I miss?" when you join late — genuinely useful), but the full transcript experience mostly arrives after the meeting ends, attached to the recording.

## Summaries and action items

Both produce solid post-meeting summaries, and both occasionally hallucinate an action item nobody actually agreed to — trust but verify. Zoom's summaries are well-structured and land in your email or Team Chat automatically, which is exactly the frictionless experience most people want. Its smart recording chapters are a sleeper feature: long recordings get split into skimmable, titled sections.

Otter's summaries are comparable in quality, but the surrounding workflow is deeper. Every meeting lands in a searchable workspace, and Otter AI Chat can answer questions *across* your meeting history — "What did the client say about the Q3 budget in any call this month?" is a query Zoom simply can't answer, because AI Companion's context is one meeting at a time. If your meetings are a knowledge base, Otter treats them like one.

## The platform question (this decides it for most teams)

AI Companion is Zoom-only. That single fact should drive your decision more than any feature comparison.

If your organization lives entirely inside Zoom, the bundled tool covers 80% of what most people used third-party notetakers for — and it does it without a bot visibly joining the call, which clients sometimes find off-putting.

But most of us don't control what platform our meetings happen on. Clients send Google Meet links; partners insist on Teams. Otter's assistant joins all three, and every conversation ends up in one searchable archive regardless of where it happened. Mixed-platform work is Otter's structural advantage, and it's the same reason dedicated notetakers like Fathom keep thriving — see our [Fathom vs Otter comparison](/compare/fathom-vs-otter-2024/) for how Otter stacks up against another independent option.

## Pricing and value

As of May 2024, Otter's free tier gives you around 300 transcription minutes a month with per-meeting caps — fine for testing, tight for real use. Pro runs roughly $8–10/month billed annually (more month-to-month), and Business plans land around $20/user/month with admin controls.

AI Companion's price is the whole pitch: if you're on any paid Zoom plan, it costs nothing extra to switch on. For a 20-person team already paying for Zoom, replacing Otter Business with AI Companion saves real money — likely thousands per year. That math is hard to argue with if Zoom is your only meeting platform.

One caveat: "free" requires a paid Zoom account. Free-tier Zoom users get nothing, while Otter's free tier works regardless of which platform hosts the meeting.

## Integrations

Otter has had years to build out its ecosystem: calendar auto-join from Google and Outlook, summary pushes to Slack, CRM syncing on business plans, and a Chrome extension. AI Companion integrates deeply with exactly one ecosystem — Zoom's own chat, mail, and whiteboard products — which is either perfectly sufficient or a dealbreaker depending on how much of your stack is Zoom-branded. If your post-meeting workflow lives in Slack or a CRM, Otter slots in with far less friction.

## Privacy and admin control

Both vendors say customer meeting content isn't used to train their models — but your compliance team will want to verify current terms themselves. Practical differences: AI Companion inherits Zoom's admin console, so IT can enable or disable features org-wide, which enterprises like. Otter is an additional vendor to review, an additional data processor storing your conversations, and its bot joining calls announces itself to (and is visible to) external participants. Some teams consider that transparency a feature; sales teams often consider it friction.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Zoom AI Companion if:**

- Your team is on paid Zoom plans and meetings happen overwhelmingly on Zoom
- You want summaries with zero extra cost, zero extra vendors, and zero bots in the room
- Your needs are per-meeting (summary, action items, catch-up) rather than building a searchable archive

**Choose Otter.ai if:**

- Your meetings are spread across Zoom, Meet, and Teams — this alone justifies it
- You want cross-meeting search and chat over your entire conversation history
- You rely on the live transcript during meetings, not just the recap after
- You're an individual whose company controls the Zoom account but not your personal tooling

**Consider running both** during a transition: AI Companion for internal Zoom standups, Otter for external and cross-platform calls. Plenty of teams land there permanently.

The bigger picture: bundled AI is commoditizing basic meeting notes, and standalone tools now have to win on depth — cross-platform reach, archives, integrations.
