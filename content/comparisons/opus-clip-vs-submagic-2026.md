Short-form video has a predictable bottleneck. Recording is fun, posting is fun, and everything in between — finding the good moments, cutting them to vertical, captioning them, adding enough visual motion to survive a thumb scroll — is tedious. Opus Clip and Submagic both exist to remove that middle, and because they overlap more every year, creators keep asking which one to pay for.

The short version: they started from opposite ends of the pipeline and are meeting in the middle. Opus Clip began as a "find the clips" tool and added editing. Submagic began as a "polish the clip" tool and added clipping. Which one you should choose depends on which end of that pipeline is actually costing you time.

## Clip Selection: Opus Clip Wins

If you publish podcasts, webinars, interviews, or anything over ten minutes, this is the dimension that matters most. Opus Clip has spent years on it. Upload a 60-minute episode and it returns a stack of candidate clips, each with a "virality score," a suggested title, and a reframed vertical cut that keeps the speaker centered. You can tell it what to look for — "moments about pricing," "the funniest parts" — and it re-scans with that lens.

The scoring is not magic. Our [full Opus Clip review](/opus-clip-review-2026/) found the top-scored clips are usually *decent* starting points rather than guaranteed hits, and it has a bias toward moments with confident, declarative speech. But the hit rate is good enough that a 90-minute recording becomes a week of posts in under an hour.

Submagic's clipping feature works but feels like what it is: a later addition. It identifies reasonable segments and reframes them, but the ranking is shallower and the search-by-topic controls are thinner. If you mostly upload already-cut 30-second clips, you will never notice. If you upload hour-long files, you will.

## Captions and Visual Polish: Submagic Wins

Flip the question and the answer flips too. Submagic's captions are the reason people pay for it. Word-level timing is tight, the templates look like what is actually trending on TikTok and Reels rather than a year behind, and the control is granular — highlight keywords, switch fonts and animation per word, add emojis automatically or not, and fix a mis-transcribed word by clicking on it.

The automated polish layer is also stronger. Auto-zooms land on emphasis points, sound effects are tasteful by default, and the AI B-roll picks are more relevant with easier swapping when they miss. Our [Submagic review](/submagic-captions-review-2026/) called it the cleanest caption-and-polish tool we have used, and that still holds.

Opus Clip's captions are perfectly fine — accurate, several styles, multilingual on Pro — and its B-roll and zoom features have closed part of the gap this year. They are simply less refined. Side by side, the Submagic output looks more like something a human editor finished.

## Editing Control

Neither tool is a replacement for CapCut or Premiere, but both now include a timeline.

Submagic's editor is more complete: trim, split, reorder, adjust individual caption words, move zoom points, and swap B-roll from within the same screen. It is built for someone who wants to touch every clip before posting.

Opus Clip's editor has improved considerably and covers the essentials — trimming, caption fixes, reframing adjustments, brand templates — but it is still designed around the assumption that you will accept most of what the AI produced and fix a few things. For volume publishers that is actually the right assumption. For creators who care about every cut, it will feel constraining.

## Reframing and Multi-Speaker Video

Both tools auto-reframe horizontal footage to vertical and track the active speaker. Opus Clip handles two-person podcast layouts better, with a split-screen option that switches or stacks speakers sensibly. Submagic reframes single-speaker content cleanly but is less consistent with multiple people in frame.

Screen recordings and slide-heavy webinars are a weak spot for both. Expect to manually adjust the crop on anything where the important content is not a face.

## Speed and Workflow

Processing time is comparable: a one-hour upload takes several minutes in either tool. The real workflow difference is in what happens after.

Opus Clip is built around batch output. Review a dozen clips, approve eight, schedule them to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram, and LinkedIn from inside the app. The scheduling and publishing integration is genuinely useful if you post daily.

Submagic assumes one clip at a time and rewards attention. You can process in bulk, but the interface pushes you toward opening each one.

## Pricing

**Opus Clip** (approximate, August 2026):
- Free: 60 upload minutes per month, watermarked
- Starter: about $19/month, 150 minutes, no watermark
- Pro: about $29/month, 300 minutes, 1080p, AI B-roll, multilingual captions
- Streamer: about $79/month, 1200 minutes, livestream clipping

Pricing counts uploaded minutes. A 90-minute podcast costs 90 minutes regardless of how many clips you export, so long-form publishers burn through Starter quickly.

**Submagic** (approximate, August 2026):
- Starter: about $13/month, 5 hours of video
- Pro: about $25/month, 20 hours, AI B-roll, advanced templates
- Business: about $42/month, 60 hours, team workspaces

Submagic's entry price is lower and its hour allowances are generous if you upload short clips. If you upload full-length episodes for clipping, compare the hour cap against Opus Clip's minute cap at your real volume — they can come out surprisingly close.

Neither tool has a meaningful free tier for sustained use. Opus Clip's free plan is enough to evaluate clip selection on one or two videos; Submagic's trial is enough to see the caption styles.

## Where Both Fall Short

- **Hooks.** Neither tool writes a good first three seconds. Their suggested titles and on-screen hooks are generic. That part is still on you.
- **Non-talking-head content.** Gameplay, tutorials, B-roll-heavy footage, and anything without a clear speaker confuse the clip selection and reframing in both.
- **Caption accuracy on jargon and names.** Both mis-transcribe technical terms and proper nouns regularly. Plan to proofread.
- **Lock-in.** Brand templates, caption presets, and scheduling connections do not export. Switching later means rebuilding.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Opus Clip if:**
- Your source material is long — podcasts, interviews, webinars, streams.
- You publish at volume and want to approve and schedule in batches.
- You clip livestreams.
- Two-speaker reframing matters to you.

**Choose Submagic if:**
- You already know which moments to post and want them to look finished.
- Caption style is a core part of your brand.
- You want to touch every clip before it goes out.
- You are budget-conscious and mostly upload short files.

**Use both if:**
- You run a podcast or show and care about polish. A common workflow is Opus Clip for selection and reframing, then Submagic for captions and effects on the clips that made the cut. It is two subscriptions, but for a weekly show producing 20-plus shorts it is still cheaper than an editor.

The tools are converging, and by next year the gap in each one's weak area will probably be narrower. For now, the decision is simple: pay for the half of the pipeline that is eating your time.
