Three months after ChatGPT went public, every AI writing tool faces the same awkward question: why pay for this when ChatGPT exists? Copy.ai and Writesonic are two of the most popular answers. Both are built on OpenAI's language models, both have free plans, and both have added chat interfaces to keep up. Day to day, though, they feel quite different to use.

We ran both through the same set of real tasks: product descriptions, ad variations, cold emails, a 1,500-word blog post, and a handful of social captions. Here's how they compare, and when either one is worth paying for instead of just using ChatGPT.

New to the underlying tech? Our explainer on [what ChatGPT actually is](/what-is-chatgpt-explained-2023/) covers the basics that both of these tools build on.

## Quick comparison

| | **Copy.ai** | **Writesonic** |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest at | Short-form marketing copy | Long-form articles and SEO content |
| Templates | 90+ copywriting tools | 100+ templates |
| Long-form workflow | Blog wizard plus freeform editor | Multi-step AI Article Writer |
| Chat assistant | Copy.ai Chat (new in 2023) | Chatsonic, with Google search data |
| Image generation | No | Yes (Photosonic) |
| Free plan | ~2,000 words/month | Limited trial word allowance |
| Paid entry (approx.) | ~$49/month Pro, unlimited words | ~$13–20/month, word-capped |
| Pricing model | Flat rate | Word allowance, scaled by quality level |
| Learning curve | Very low | Low to moderate |

Pricing is approximate as of March 2023. Both companies change plans often.

## Short-form copy

Copy.ai is the clear winner for short-form work. Its template library covers ad headlines, product descriptions, Instagram captions, email subject lines, value propositions, and dozens of other formats. You fill in a few fields, pick a tone, and get several variations in seconds.

What stands out is the volume of usable options. When we asked both tools for Facebook ad headlines for a meal-kit brand, Copy.ai's batch had more lines we'd actually test. Writesonic's were competent but leaned more generic. That fits each product's focus. Copy.ai was built as a copywriter's brainstorming partner, and it shows.

**Edge: Copy.ai.**

## Long-form content

Here the positions flip. Writesonic's AI Article Writer walks you through a structured process: pick a topic, generate and choose a title, build or edit an outline, then generate the full article section by section. Because you approve each stage, the final draft follows your structure, not the model's whims.

Copy.ai has a blog post wizard and a freeform editor, and both work. But long pieces need more manual assembly and the output drifts more as articles get longer. In our 1,500-word test, Writesonic's draft needed fewer structural fixes. Copy.ai's needed more rewriting to stay on topic.

Neither produced publish-ready long-form content. Both drafts needed fact-checking, sharper intros, and real examples. Writesonic just gave us a better starting skeleton.

**Edge: Writesonic.**

## Chat assistants

Both tools now have a ChatGPT-style chat mode, which tells you a lot about how much ChatGPT has reshaped this market in a few months.

**Chatsonic** is Writesonic's chat product, and its main selling point is pulling in Google search data so it can talk about recent events. ChatGPT's knowledge stops at its training cutoff, so that's a meaningful difference for topical content. In practice, the answers are sometimes thin and the sources need checking, but it's useful for news-adjacent work.

**Copy.ai Chat** is newer and more focused on marketing tasks. It's useful for back-and-forth on campaign ideas and works well alongside the templates, but it doesn't offer the same live-web angle.

**Edge: Writesonic**, because of the web data.

## Output quality and editing load

Underneath, both tools draw on similar OpenAI model families, so raw writing quality is closer than the marketing suggests. The differences come from prompting, templates, and workflow design.

A few patterns held across our tests:

- **Copy.ai** produced punchier short lines but more repetition across variations.
- **Writesonic** produced more structured long drafts but leaned on stock phrases ("in today's fast-paced world") more often.
- **Both** occasionally stated made-up facts with confidence. Neither should be trusted with statistics, product specs, or claims without verification.

Writesonic also lets you choose a quality level for generations. Higher levels give noticeably better results but draw down your word allowance faster. It's a fair system once you understand it, but it adds a decision Copy.ai doesn't make you think about.

## Ease of use

Copy.ai is the easier tool to pick up. The interface is clean, the templates are self-explanatory, and you can get value in five minutes without reading anything.

Writesonic has more features to learn, including the article workflow, quality levels, Chatsonic, Photosonic, and SEO-oriented tools. That's not a flaw, but new users should expect a short learning period before the long-form workflow clicks.

**Edge: Copy.ai.**

## Pricing

This is where the decision often gets made.

**Copy.ai** keeps it simple: a free plan with around 2,000 words per month, then a Pro plan at roughly $49/month (less with annual billing) that includes unlimited words. If you write a lot, that flat rate is easy to budget and removes any word-counting anxiety.

**Writesonic** starts cheaper, with entry paid tiers somewhere around $13–20/month depending on billing and word allowance, but pricing scales with how many words you generate and which quality level you use. Light users pay less than they would for Copy.ai Pro. Heavy users, especially those who use top-quality settings for long-form work, can end up paying as much or more.

A simple rule: if you generate tens of thousands of words a month, Copy.ai's unlimited plan is likely the better deal. If you write a few articles a month, Writesonic's lower tiers go further.

## The ChatGPT question

You can't compare these tools in 2023 without asking whether you need either one. ChatGPT is free, and ChatGPT Plus costs $20/month. With good prompts, it can write ad copy, outlines, and blog drafts too. Our [ChatGPT Plus vs Free comparison](/compare/chatgpt-plus-vs-free-2023/) covers what the paid tier adds.

What Copy.ai and Writesonic sell is mostly **workflow**: templates that save prompting effort, structured long-form processes, saved projects, team seats, and in Writesonic's case, web-connected chat and image generation. If you're comfortable writing your own prompts (our [ChatGPT getting-started guide](/getting-started-with-chatgpt-2023/) is a good primer), ChatGPT may cover most of what you need. If you want repeatable, structured output without thinking about prompts, the dedicated tools still earn their keep.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Copy.ai if:**

- Most of your work is short-form: ads, product descriptions, emails, social posts.
- You want the fastest possible learning curve.
- You write in high volume and want unlimited words at a flat rate.
- You mainly use AI as a brainstorming partner for marketing lines.

**Choose Writesonic if:**

- You produce blog posts and SEO articles regularly.
- You want a guided outline-to-article process with control at each step.
- You'd use web-connected chat for topical content.
- Your volume is modest and you want a cheaper entry point.

**Choose neither (yet) if:** you're comfortable prompting ChatGPT directly and don't need templates or team features. Start with ChatGPT, note where you keep repeating the same prompts, and come back to these tools if the friction adds up.

Both offer free tiers, so the cheapest way to decide is to run the same three or four real tasks from your own workload through each. The one whose drafts you edit less is the one to pay for.
