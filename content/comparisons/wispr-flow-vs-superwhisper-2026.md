Voice dictation quietly became one of the highest-leverage AI upgrades of the past two years. Modern speech models don't just transcribe what you say — they strip the "ums," fix the false starts, and format the result so it reads like something you typed. Wispr Flow and Superwhisper are the two names that come up in every conversation about this, and they represent genuinely different philosophies: polished cloud service versus private local processing. We've used both daily for months. Here's how to pick.

## Quick comparison

| | Wispr Flow | Superwhisper |
|---|---|---|
| **Platforms** | macOS, Windows, iOS | macOS, iOS |
| **Processing** | Cloud | On-device (local models), cloud optional |
| **Works offline** | No | Yes |
| **Auto-editing** | Yes — filler removal, formatting, tone | Yes — via configurable modes and prompts |
| **Custom vocabulary** | Yes, learns names/jargon over time | Yes, plus per-app modes |
| **Pricing model** | Free tier + subscription (~$12–15/mo) | Subscription (~$8.50/mo) or one-time lifetime option |
| **Best for** | Cross-platform users who want zero setup | Mac users who want privacy, offline use, and control |

## The core philosophical difference

**Wispr Flow** is a product in the modern SaaS mold: install it, press the hotkey, talk, and beautifully formatted text appears in whatever app your cursor is in. Your audio goes to their servers, gets processed by their models, and comes back cleaned up. Everything is tuned for the median user having a great experience with zero configuration.

**Superwhisper** is a power tool. It runs speech models directly on your Mac — you choose the model, trading speed against accuracy — and your audio never has to leave the device. "Modes" let you define how output is processed per context: a messaging mode that keeps things casual, an email mode that formats professionally, a custom-prompt mode that can translate or restructure as it transcribes. It's the difference between an appliance and a workbench.

Neither philosophy is wrong. But they sort users pretty cleanly.

## Accuracy and output quality

Raw transcription accuracy is close — both are dramatically better than the built-in dictation on any OS, and both handle natural, rambling speech well. The differences show at the edges.

Wispr Flow's cloud processing gives it consistently strong cleanup: it's notably good at turning a meandering spoken thought into tight written prose, and its tone adaptation (matching how you write in Slack versus email) works with no configuration. Its dictionary quietly learns your coworkers' names and your project jargon, which pays off more each week.

Superwhisper's ceiling depends on which model you run. The larger local models rival Wispr Flow's accuracy but respond a beat slower on older Macs; smaller models are snappy but sloppier with jargon. The mode system is the trump card: because you control the processing prompt, you can get output Wispr Flow simply won't produce — structured meeting notes from a rambling voice memo, or dictation that comes out in another language. If you've read our [Whisper transcription guide](/whisper-ai-transcription-guide/), Superwhisper is that ecosystem productized.

## Privacy and offline use

This section decides it for a lot of people.

Superwhisper's on-device processing means dictation works on a plane, in a dead zone, and — critically — in workplaces where sending audio to a third-party cloud is a compliance non-starter. Lawyers, doctors, and anyone under NDA should start here. Local processing isn't a feature for these users; it's the requirement.

Wispr Flow requires an internet connection and cloud processing. The company states dictation audio isn't used to train models without consent, but policies evolve — verify the current terms if your work is sensitive. For most everyday users writing emails and messages, the cloud tradeoff is invisible and the polish is worth it.

## Platform support

Wispr Flow wins this one outright: native apps for macOS, **Windows**, and iOS mean one dictation tool with one learned dictionary across your whole setup. Superwhisper remains Mac and iPhone only — if any part of your life runs on Windows, that's the whole ballgame.

On the Mac itself, both integrate deeply: system-wide hotkeys, dictation into any text field, and no per-app setup. iOS is more constrained for both (keyboard extensions have limits Apple imposes), but both are usable for messages and notes on the go.

## Pricing

As of August 2026, approximate pricing: Wispr Flow offers a free tier with a weekly word allowance — genuinely enough to evaluate it — with Pro at roughly $12–15/month. Superwhisper runs about $8.50/month, with a lifetime license option (historically in the ~$200–250 range) that's compelling if you know you'll use it for years; a limited free mode lets you test smaller models.

The math: Wispr Flow costs more over time and you're renting it. Superwhisper's lifetime option means heavy dictators can stop paying entirely after year one or two. Factor in that Superwhisper's local models cost the developer nothing per use, so the pricing gap is structural, not promotional.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Wispr Flow if:**

- You use Windows anywhere in your setup — Superwhisper isn't an option there
- You want the best zero-configuration experience: install, talk, done
- Automatic tone matching and cross-app polish matter more to you than control
- You're new to voice dictation — the free tier is the easiest on-ramp in the category (start with our [Wispr Flow review](/wispr-flow-voice-typing-review-2026/))

**Choose Superwhisper if:**

- Privacy or compliance requires audio to stay on your device
- You need offline dictation — travel, spotty internet, air-gapped work
- You want modes and custom prompts that transform speech, not just transcribe it
- You prefer paying once over subscribing forever

**A note on scope:** neither is a meeting transcription tool — they're for dictating *your* voice into apps. For transcribing calls and recordings, that's a different category; see our roundup of [the best AI transcription tools](/best-ai-transcription-tools/).

Our take: Wispr Flow is the better default for most people, and it's what we recommend to anyone asking "which one?" with no further context — cross-platform reach plus effortless quality wins the median case. But Superwhisper is the better *tool*: more private, more controllable, cheaper long-term, and the only real choice when your audio can't leave your machine. Power users on Macs shouldn't be surprised if they try both and keep the workbench.
