Pitch and Gamma both want to replace PowerPoint and Google Slides, but they come at it from opposite directions.

**Pitch** is a collaborative presentation tool that added AI. It's built around slides, brand templates, and teams working on decks together, with an AI generator layered on top.

**Gamma** is an AI tool that happens to make presentations. You type a prompt or paste notes, and it generates a full deck, document, or web page made of flexible "cards" instead of fixed slides.

That difference matters more than any feature checklist. We built the same three decks in each tool (an investor update, a sales pitch, and an internal strategy review) to see where each one wins.

## Quick Comparison

| | Pitch | Gamma |
|---|---|---|
| **Core idea** | Collaborative slide editor with AI assist | AI-first generator for decks, docs, and pages |
| **Format** | Traditional fixed-size slides | Scrollable cards that can also present as slides |
| **AI generation** | Generates a first-draft deck from a prompt | Generates decks, docs, and pages from prompts, notes, or imported files |
| **Design control** | High: precise layout, brand templates | Moderate: theme-driven, less pixel control |
| **Collaboration** | Strong: real-time editing, comments, assignments, statuses | Good: comments and shared editing |
| **Brand consistency** | Workspace templates, fonts, and colors | Custom themes on paid plans |
| **Exports** | PDF and PowerPoint | PDF and PowerPoint (layout fidelity varies) |
| **Sharing and analytics** | Share links with engagement analytics on paid tiers | Share links; view analytics on paid tiers |
| **Free plan** | Yes, with limits | Yes, with a one-time AI credit allowance |
| **Best for** | Teams producing recurring, on-brand decks | Individuals who need a good deck fast |

## AI Generation

**Gamma wins clearly.** Generation is the whole product. You can start from a one-line prompt, paste a messy outline, or import a document, and Gamma returns a structured deck with headings, layouts, and images in under a minute. You can then use AI to rewrite a card, change the tone, condense text, or add a section.

In our tests, Gamma's first drafts got most of the way to a usable deck. The structure was logical, and the layouts varied enough to avoid "wall of bullets" syndrome. The weak spots were generic phrasing and stock-feeling imagery, so plan on rewriting the key slides in your own voice.

**Pitch's AI generator** produces a reasonable first draft from a prompt, choosing a template and filling in placeholder content. It's helpful for getting past the blank page, but the output needs more editing than Gamma's, and the AI is less woven into day-to-day editing. Pitch treats AI as a starting point. Gamma treats it as the main way you work.

## Design Control

**Pitch wins.** It's a real slide editor. You can position elements precisely, build custom layouts, and lock brand templates so every deck from your team looks consistent. Designers on our team were comfortable in Pitch within an hour.

Gamma's card-based format is flexible but less precise. You choose a theme, and Gamma handles most layout decisions. That's great when you don't want to think about design, and frustrating when you need an element in a specific spot. Some layouts also reflow in ways you don't expect when you add or remove content.

**Rule of thumb:** if a designer will care how the deck looks, use Pitch. If speed matters more than pixel precision, use Gamma.

## Collaboration and Workflow

**Pitch wins for teams.** Collaboration is where Pitch feels most mature. You get real-time co-editing, comments, the ability to assign slides to teammates, and slide statuses (like "in progress" or "done"). For a sales team building a deck from shared templates or a marketing team producing a monthly report, this workflow is smooth.

Gamma supports shared editing and comments, and it works fine for a few people reviewing a deck. It doesn't have the same project-management layer for a team splitting up a big deck.

## Format and Presenting

The two tools think about "a presentation" differently.

**Pitch decks are slides.** They present the way audiences expect, they look the same on any screen, and they export cleanly to PDF and PowerPoint.

**Gamma decks are closer to web pages.** Cards can hold more content than a slide and scroll if needed. You can present them as slides, but they also work well as a link someone reads on their own, like a doc that looks designed. Gamma can also produce standalone documents and simple web pages from the same editor.

That makes Gamma excellent for async sharing (send a link instead of a PDF) and a bit less predictable for live presenting on a projector, where a card that overflows the screen looks awkward.

## Exports

**Pitch is more reliable.** PDF and PowerPoint exports from Pitch held their layout well in our tests, which matters if a client insists on a .pptx file.

Gamma exports to PDF and PowerPoint as well, but because cards don't map one-to-one onto fixed slides, exported decks sometimes need cleanup: text boxes resize, spacing shifts, or long cards split awkwardly. If your workflow ends in PowerPoint, budget time for fixes or stick with Pitch.

## Sharing and Analytics

Both tools let you share a live link instead of an attachment, and both offer viewing analytics on paid plans so you can see whether someone opened your deck and how far they got.

Pitch's analytics are well-suited to sales and fundraising, where knowing that a prospect spent time on the pricing slide is useful. Gamma's analytics are simpler but cover the basics. Neither replaces a dedicated document-tracking tool if that's central to your sales process.

## Pricing (approximate, as of January 2025)

Both tools have free plans and change pricing fairly often, so treat these as rough ranges and check the current pricing pages.

| | Pitch | Gamma |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | Unlimited presentations with core features and limits on advanced options | Free account with a one-time bank of AI credits |
| **Entry paid** | Roughly $20–25/month for a Pro workspace | Roughly $8–10/month per user (Plus), billed annually |
| **Higher tier** | Business tier for larger teams, priced higher with custom options | Roughly $15–20/month per user (Pro), billed annually |
| **What paid adds** | Custom branding, analytics, more workspace members, version history | Unlimited AI generation, custom themes and branding, analytics, removing Gamma branding |

For a solo user, Gamma is cheaper. For a team that would use Pitch's collaboration features heavily, Pitch's workspace pricing can be good value, depending on headcount.

## Learning Curve

**Gamma is faster to start.** You can have a decent deck in five minutes without any design skill. The tradeoff shows up later, when you want to fine-tune something and fight the auto-layout.

**Pitch takes a little longer** because it's a full editor, but anyone who has used Google Slides or Keynote will feel at home. The payoff is control.

## Where Each One Falls Short

**Pitch:**

- AI is a starting point, not a core workflow
- Less useful for quick one-off decks where you don't want to touch layout
- Higher entry price for solo users

**Gamma:**

- Less precise design control
- Exports to PowerPoint can need cleanup
- AI output can sound generic without heavy editing
- Card format can look odd when presenting live on a fixed screen

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Pitch if:**

- Your team produces recurring decks (sales pitches, monthly reports, board updates) that must stay on-brand
- A designer or brand team cares about exact layout
- Several people collaborate on the same deck and need assignments and statuses
- You often deliver final decks as PowerPoint files

**Choose Gamma if:**

- You need a good-looking deck fast and don't want to design it
- You share decks mostly as links for people to read on their own
- You're a solo founder, consultant, or educator on a budget
- You also want to create docs or simple one-page sites from the same tool

**Consider using both.** A common pattern we've seen: draft in Gamma to get structure and content quickly, then rebuild the final version in Pitch (or your company's template) for polish and brand consistency.

## Bottom Line

Pitch is the better presentation tool. Gamma is the better AI tool. If your bottleneck is getting from a blank page to a solid draft, Gamma will save you the most time. If your bottleneck is producing polished, consistent decks as a team, Pitch is the stronger choice and its AI features are a helpful bonus.
