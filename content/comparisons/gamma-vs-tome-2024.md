AI presentation tools had a real moment over the past year, and two names kept coming up: Gamma and Tome. Both promise the same magic trick — type a prompt, get a finished-looking deck in seconds — but they make very different bets about what a presentation should be. This 2024 comparison breaks down how they actually differ and who each one is for.

I've built real decks in both. Here's the honest rundown.

## Quick comparison

| Dimension | Gamma | Tome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core idea | Flexible "cards" for decks, docs, and web pages | AI-native storytelling and presentations |
| AI generation | Strong; generates full decks from a prompt or outline | Strong; generation plus a narrative tilt |
| Output format | Decks, documents, simple web pages | Presentation-style "tomes" |
| Editing model | Card-based, flexible blocks | Page/tile-based, story-first |
| Templates & themes | Good selection, easy theming | Sleek, design-forward defaults |
| Export | PDF and PowerPoint export, plus shareable links | Shareable links, with export options |
| Best for | Generalists who want decks and docs in one tool | Story-driven pitches and polished visuals |
| Pricing (approx., as of 2024) | Free credits, paid tiers in the ~$8–15/mo range | Free tier, paid plans in a similar range |

## How AI generation compares

Both tools nail the core promise: give them a topic or an outline and they produce a structured, decent-looking deck fast. The difference is in feel.

**Gamma** treats generation as a starting point you'll heavily edit. It builds your content into "cards" — flexible blocks you can rearrange, nest, and restyle. It's less precious about being a "presentation" and more about getting structured content on the screen quickly, which you then shape.

**Tome** leans into narrative. Its generations feel story-first, with a strong design opinion baked in. The output often looks more polished out of the box, but it nudges you toward Tome's way of telling a story rather than a blank-canvas free-for-all.

If you want raw flexibility, Gamma's generation gives you more to work with. If you want something that looks finished with minimal fiddling, Tome's defaults are hard to beat.

## Output format and flexibility

This is the biggest philosophical split.

**Gamma is multi-format.** The same tool makes slide-style decks, scrolling documents, and basic web pages. That's genuinely useful if you bounce between a pitch deck on Monday and an internal one-pager on Tuesday. The card system means a deck and a doc are really just different arrangements of the same building blocks.

**Tome is presentation-focused.** It's built around making one beautiful, scrollable presentation. It does that one thing with real polish, but it's not trying to be your document tool too.

So the question is partly: do you want a Swiss Army knife (Gamma) or a specialist (Tome)?

## Templates, themes, and design

Both look good, but with different personalities.

Tome's defaults are sleek and design-forward — you can hand a Tome to someone and it reads as intentional without much work. Gamma's themes are clean and easy to apply across a whole deck in one click, and the editing is forgiving for non-designers. Gamma feels a bit more "productivity tool that happens to look nice"; Tome feels a bit more "design tool that happens to be easy."

## Export and sharing

Sharing is similar — both give you a clean link you can send, and both render nicely in the browser.

Export is where Gamma has an edge for many workflows: PDF and PowerPoint export means you can take your deck into the broader Microsoft and Google ecosystem, hand it to a colleague who lives in PowerPoint, or print it. If your organization standardizes on PowerPoint, that matters. Tome's strength is the in-app web experience; if you mostly present from a link, that's fine, but check current export options if offline files are a hard requirement for you.

## Pricing

As of mid-2024, both follow the now-familiar pattern: a free tier with limited AI credits, then paid plans roughly in the eight-to-fifteen-dollars-per-month range depending on billing and features. Gamma's free credits are a reasonable way to test full deck generation; Tome's free tier likewise lets you build real presentations before paying.

Treat all of these figures as approximate — pricing in the AI tools space changes often. The practical advice is the same for both: build one real deck on the free tier before you subscribe. You'll learn more from that than from any feature list, including this one.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Gamma if:**

- You want one tool for decks, documents, and quick web pages.
- You value flexible editing and plan to restructure the AI's output heavily.
- PowerPoint or PDF export is part of your workflow.
- You're a generalist who'd rather not learn two tools. Gamma also pairs well with broader [AI presentation tools](/best-ai-presentation-makers/) if you're still shopping around.

**Choose Tome if:**

- Your main job is telling a polished, visual story — pitches, narratives, portfolios.
- You want output that looks finished with minimal editing.
- You mostly present from a shareable link rather than exporting files.
- A strong, opinionated design default appeals to you more than a blank canvas.

**The honest summary:** Gamma is the better *generalist* — more formats, more flexibility, friendlier export. Tome is the better *specialist* for design-forward, story-driven presentations. Neither is a bad choice in 2024; they're optimized for different jobs.

If you're not sure, start with Gamma for its versatility. If you find yourself wishing the output looked more polished out of the box and you only ever make presentations, give Tome a serious look. Both have free tiers, so the real test costs you nothing but an afternoon. And if AI deck builders in general feel like overkill for your needs, a traditional tool plus a dose of [Notion AI](/reviews/15-notion-ai-review/) for the writing might be all you need.
