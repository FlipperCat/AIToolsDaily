Merlin and Monica are two of the most popular "AI everywhere" browser extensions. Both put a chat sidebar on every web page, both bundle models from several AI labs under one subscription, and both promise to summarize, translate, and write for you without switching tabs.

On paper they look almost identical. After using each as our main browser assistant for two weeks, we found the differences are in the details: how credits are counted, how polished the everyday workflows feel, and how much extra you get outside the browser.

This comparison covers what matters when choosing between them, with recommendations by use case at the end.

## Quick comparison

|---|---|---|
| **Core product** | AI sidebar extension plus web app | AI sidebar extension plus web, desktop, and mobile apps |
| **Models** | Several models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others | Several models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others |

## Models and answer quality

Both tools resell access to frontier models rather than training their own. You pick a model from a menu, and higher-end models consume more of your allowance.

That means answer quality is broadly the same when you pick the same model in each. The differences come from system prompts, how much page context each tool sends, and how long conversations can run before context gets trimmed. In our testing, neither was consistently better at reasoning. Both occasionally gave shorter, less thorough answers than the same model in its native app, which is common for wrapper products that manage costs carefully.

If you already pay for ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, the main thing these extensions add is convenience, not intelligence. Factor that into whether you need either one.

**Verdict:** Tie. Pick based on workflow and price, not model quality.

## Browsing, summaries, and research

This is where both tools earn their keep.

**Merlin** is quick and focused. Its page summaries are tight, and its YouTube summarizer is one of the best we have used: it pulls the transcript, gives a structured summary, and lets you ask follow-up questions about the video. It also adds an AI answer panel next to search engine results, which is handy for quick lookups.

**Monica** offers the same core features with more options. You can summarize a page, chat with it, translate it side by side, or save highlights to its memo feature. The extra options are useful, but the interface is busier, and it took us longer to find the action we wanted.

For deeper research with sources, both trail dedicated answer engines. Use Perplexity or a deep-research feature in a frontier model's own app when citations matter.

**Verdict:** Merlin for speed and YouTube; Monica for breadth.

## Writing tools

Both let you select text on any page and rewrite, shorten, expand, or translate it. Both can draft replies in email and social media.

**Monica** goes further here. It has more preset writing modes, a dedicated composing panel, and better-organized options for tone and length. If you write a lot of email or social content in the browser, it feels more complete.

**Merlin** covers the basics well and stays out of the way. It is a good fit if you mainly want quick rewrites rather than a full writing workspace.

Neither replaces a dedicated grammar tool if you need consistent style and correctness checks. In-browser AI writing help is not new, either; tools like [HyperWrite](/reviews/hyperwrite-review-2023/) were doing autocomplete-style writing in the browser well before these all-in-one sidebars took off.

**Verdict:** Monica.

## Apps and platforms

**Monica** has the wider footprint, with desktop and mobile apps alongside the extension and syncing between them. If you want one assistant on your laptop and phone, that matters.

**Merlin** is centered on the browser extension, with a web app for longer chats. It is lighter, but less useful away from your desktop browser.

Monica also has an interesting connection to the wider AI agent market: the team behind it went on to launch Manus, the general-purpose agent that drew a lot of attention this year. Monica itself is still a sidebar assistant, not an autonomous agent. If you are interested in agents, see our [ChatGPT Agent vs Manus comparison](/compare/chatgpt-agent-vs-manus-2025-which-general-ai-agent-gets-real-work-done/).

**Verdict:** Monica.

## Usage limits and pricing

This is the area that causes the most frustration with both products.

Both use allowances rather than truly unlimited use. Basic models are cheap or generous; advanced models consume far more of your allowance per message. Heavy users of the top models can run through a month's allowance surprisingly quickly, and the exact rules change often.

Pricing as of August 2025, approximate:

- **Merlin:** A limited free tier with a daily query allowance. Paid plans run roughly $15–20/month month-to-month, with discounts for annual billing.
- **Monica:** A limited free tier. Paid tiers run roughly $10–20/month depending on level, with meaningful discounts for annual billing.

Monica's entry paid tier is usually cheaper, but compare the advanced-model allowances, not the headline price. Read the current plan pages carefully, and start month-to-month until you know your usage pattern.

**Verdict:** Monica is usually cheaper to start. For heavy advanced-model use, check current allowances for both before you commit.

## Privacy and security

Any AI sidebar that can read the page you are on needs broad browser permissions. That applies equally to both tools.

Practical precautions:

- Do not use either on pages containing passwords, financial data, or confidential client information.
- Check your employer's policy before you install them on a work machine. Many companies block extensions with this level of access.
- Review each company's data retention and training policies, and use any opt-out settings available.
- Turn the sidebar off on sites where you do not need it.

**Verdict:** Tie. Treat both with the same caution as any extension that can read every page.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Merlin if:**

- You mostly summarize articles, research topics, and digest YouTube videos.
- You want a fast, lightweight sidebar that does not get in the way.
- You live in a desktop browser and rarely need an assistant on your phone.

**Choose Monica if:**

- You write lots of email and social content in the browser and want richer writing tools.
- You want one assistant across browser, desktop, and mobile.
- You want the lower-cost entry plan.

**Choose neither if:**

- You already pay for ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, or Gemini and are happy switching tabs. The native apps usually give fuller answers and more features for the same model.
- You work in a locked-down environment where broad-permission extensions are not allowed.

## Bottom line

Merlin and Monica are closer than their marketing suggests. Model quality is effectively the same, so the decision comes down to workflow. Merlin is the sharper research and summary tool. Monica is the more complete all-round assistant, especially for writing and for users who want it on every device.

Whichever you try, start on the free tier, watch how quickly you use your advanced-model allowance, and only then choose a paid plan.
