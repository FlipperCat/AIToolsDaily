Most people don't need a transcript of their meetings. They need the three decisions that got made and the list of who promised to do what. That gap is what Supernormal is built for. Instead of handing you a 9,000-word wall of text, it listens to the call and produces structured notes: a short summary, key points, and action items, formatted like a competent colleague wrote them.

We used Supernormal across a few weeks of internal standups, client calls, and one-on-ones to see whether the notes are good enough to send without rewriting. Mostly, they are, with some caveats worth knowing before you roll it out to a team.

## What Is Supernormal?

Supernormal is an AI meeting assistant that joins your calls on Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams, captures what's said, and turns it into notes using OpenAI's GPT models. When the meeting ends, the notes land in your Supernormal workspace and can be emailed to attendees or pushed to other tools automatically.

The pitch is simple: stop splitting your attention between listening and typing. If you've read our explainer on [what ChatGPT is and how it works](/what-is-chatgpt-everything-you-need-to-know-2023/), you already understand the engine. Supernormal's value is the wrapper: getting the audio in, structuring the output, and getting the notes to the right people without extra steps.

## Key Features

**Automatic notes, not just transcripts.** The default output is a summary, a list of discussion points grouped by topic, and action items. The summary is usually three to five sentences, and it's the part people actually read.

**Meeting templates.** You can choose different note formats for different meeting types, such as a standup, a sales call, a one-on-one, or an interview. A sales template pulls out things like pain points and next steps; a standup template focuses on blockers and owners. You can also edit the sections to match how your team works.

**Auto-sharing.** Notes can go out to attendees by email right after the call. This is the feature that changes behavior. When everyone gets the same recap within minutes, there's less "wait, what did we agree?" in the follow-up thread.

**Calendar integration.** Connect your calendar and Supernormal can join scheduled meetings automatically. You can set rules so it only joins certain calls, which matters if you don't want a notetaker in every sensitive conversation.

**Editable output.** Notes are fully editable before or after sharing, so you can fix a misheard name or delete a tangent that shouldn't be in writing.

**Integrations.** Supernormal connects to a growing set of workplace tools so notes can flow into where your team already works. The list is expanding quickly, so check the current integrations page against your stack before committing.

## How the Notes Hold Up

On clear, structured meetings, like a weekly standup with five people taking turns, the notes were usually good enough to forward with little or no editing. The model is good at separating "we discussed X" from "we decided X," which is the hardest part of meeting notes.

Quality drops in predictable places:

- **Crosstalk and fast back-and-forth.** When three people talk over each other, attribution gets shaky and some points collapse together.
- **Jargon and names.** Product names, acronyms, and unusual surnames get mangled. You'll fix the same internal terms repeatedly.
- **Implied action items.** If someone says "I can probably look into that," the AI sometimes logs it as a firm commitment, and sometimes misses it entirely. Review the action items before you send.
- **Long, unstructured calls.** A 90-minute brainstorm produces notes that are accurate sentence by sentence but don't capture the arc of the conversation.

None of this is unique to Supernormal. It's the current state of AI meeting notes. But it means you should treat the output as a strong first draft rather than the final record.

## Pros

- **Notes-first output** saves real time. You get something readable instead of a transcript you'll never revisit.
- **Templates fit different meeting types** and noticeably improve relevance compared with a generic summary.
- **Auto-sharing builds a habit** of consistent recaps across a team with almost no effort.
- **Works across the big three platforms**, so mixed Zoom/Meet/Teams calendars aren't a problem.
- **Fast setup.** Connecting a calendar and getting the first notes takes a few minutes.

## Cons and Limitations

- **A bot in the room.** Like most meeting assistants, Supernormal shows up as a participant. Some clients find that off-putting. If that's a dealbreaker, a browser-extension approach like [Tactiq](/reviews/tactiq-review-2023-meeting-transcripts-without-a-bot-in-the-room/) avoids the visible bot.
- **Not a transcript archive.** If you need searchable, timestamped transcripts across months of calls, transcript-first tools are stronger. Our [Otter.ai vs Rev comparison](/compare/otter-vs-rev-2023/) covers that side of the market.
- **Accuracy depends on audio.** Bad microphones and noisy rooms degrade everything downstream.
- **Privacy review required.** Meeting content passes through a third-party service and an AI model. Teams handling legal, medical, or HR conversations need to check data handling terms before turning on auto-join.
- **Auto-sharing can backfire.** Sending notes to every attendee immediately is great until the AI includes an off-hand comment that shouldn't be in an email. Consider sharing manually for sensitive meetings.

## Who Supernormal Is For

**Good fit:**

- Managers and team leads who run several recurring meetings a week and want consistent recaps
- Sales and customer success reps who need clean notes for follow-ups and CRM updates
- Consultants and agencies who want to send professional call summaries to clients quickly

- People whose meetings are mostly sensitive and shouldn't be processed by an external AI service

## Tips for Better Results

1. **Pick the right template per meeting type.** A sales template on a standup produces odd output. Set templates once per recurring meeting.
2. **State decisions out loud.** Saying "So we're deciding X, and Sam owns it by Friday" gives the AI a clean signal and makes the action items far more reliable.
3. **Spend 60 seconds reviewing before sharing.** Check names, numbers, and action items. That's usually all the editing it needs.
4. **Set auto-join rules.** Exclude one-on-ones, HR calls, and anything confidential by default.
5. **Tell people it's there.** A quick "I have a notetaker running, notes will go out after" avoids awkwardness and covers consent.

## Verdict

Supernormal does what it promises: it turns meetings into readable, shareable notes with very little effort. The notes-first approach is the right call for most knowledge workers, and templates plus auto-sharing make it more useful than a raw transcript tool for day-to-day work.

It isn't a perfect record. You'll still review action items, fix names, and make judgment calls about what should go in writing. But if you spend hours a week in meetings and your current notes are scattered or nonexistent, Supernormal's free tier is worth a two-week trial. If you find yourself reading and forwarding the notes, the paid plan pays for itself quickly.

**Rating: 4.1/5.** Strong, practical notes for everyday meetings. Not a substitute for a verbatim transcript.
