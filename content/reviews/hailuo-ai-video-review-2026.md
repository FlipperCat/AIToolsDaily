If you follow AI video at all, you know the big names: Veo, Sora, Runway, Kling. Hailuo AI — built by Chinese AI lab MiniMax — gets less airtime in Western coverage, and that's a mistake. It has quietly become one of the best tools for one specific thing: making people move like people.

I've spent a couple of weeks generating clips across Hailuo, [Kling](/kling-ai-video-review-2026/), and [Google Veo 3](/reviews/google-veo-3-review-2026/) for the same prompts. Here's where Hailuo wins, where it loses, and who should actually pay for it.

## What Hailuo AI Is

Hailuo is a text-to-video and image-to-video generator from MiniMax, accessible through a web app (and an API for developers). You type a prompt or upload a reference image, pick a duration and resolution, and get a short clip back — typically 6 to 10 seconds per generation.

The workflow will feel familiar if you've used any modern video generator: prompt box, style options, a queue, and a library of your past generations. There's no timeline editor built in — Hailuo makes clips, not finished videos. You assemble those clips elsewhere.

## Key Features

**Strong human motion.** This is Hailuo's calling card. Walking, dancing, gesturing, fighting, facial expressions — its character animation looks less "floaty" than most competitors. Where other models produce that telltale slow-motion drift, Hailuo's subjects move with believable weight.

**Image-to-video with subject reference.** Upload a photo of a character and Hailuo can keep that character reasonably consistent across generations. It's not perfect — outfits and fine details still drift — but it's good enough for storyboards and short character-driven content.

**Camera control via prompt.** You can direct pans, zooms, tracking shots, and push-ins with plain-language camera instructions, and Hailuo follows them more reliably than older-generation models did.

**Fast iteration on lower tiers.** Standard-resolution generations come back quickly, which matters more than people admit. AI video is a numbers game — you generate ten clips to keep two — and a fast queue changes how the tool feels.

**API access.** MiniMax offers Hailuo through an API, which makes it a practical option if you're building video generation into a product rather than clicking around a web app.

## What I Like

- **Best-in-class body mechanics for the price.** For dance clips, sports motion, and expressive characters, Hailuo output regularly beat Kling in my side-by-side tests, and Kling is no slouch.
- **Prompt adherence on action.** Ask for "a chef flipping a pancake, catching it in the pan" and you'll usually get that actual sequence of events, not a vague kitchen vibe.
- **Value.** The paid tiers cost meaningfully less than the premium plans of Runway or the subscriptions that gate Veo's highest quality. If you're a solo creator generating daily, that difference compounds.
- **Image-to-video quality.** Animating a still portrait or product shot produces less warping than I expected. Faces mostly stay faces.

## What I Don't Like

- **Clip length.** You're working in short segments. Longer narratives mean stitching clips, and continuity between them is on you.
- **Fine detail degradation.** Hands, text, jewelry, and background faces still smear under motion, especially at standard resolution. Every AI video tool has this problem; Hailuo hasn't solved it either.
- **No native audio.** Veo 3 generates synchronized sound and dialogue; Hailuo clips are silent. You'll add music, foley, or voiceover in post.
- **Queue times spike on the free tier.** During peak hours, free generations can sit in the queue long enough that you'll wonder if it failed.
- **Data jurisdiction.** MiniMax is a Chinese company. That's irrelevant for a hobbyist making meme clips; it may matter to enterprises with strict data policies. Read the privacy terms before uploading client assets.

## Tips for Better Generations

A few things that measurably improved my hit rate:

- **Describe the action as a sequence, not a state.** "A woman turns from the window, smiles, and walks toward the camera" beats "a smiling woman by a window." Hailuo is good at executing beats in order — give it beats.
- **One subject, one action per clip.** Multi-character interactions are where coherence falls apart fastest, on Hailuo and every rival. Split complex scenes into separate shots.
- **Use image-to-video for anything brand-critical.** Starting from a still you control (a product render, a character sheet) constrains the model and cuts your re-roll count roughly in half.
- **Specify the camera explicitly.** Clips without camera direction default to a static or gently drifting shot. "Slow push-in," "handheld tracking shot," and "orbit around the subject" all work reliably.
- **Generate at standard resolution first.** Iterate cheap, then re-run only the winning prompt at high resolution.

## Pricing

As of July 2026, Hailuo runs a credit-based subscription model: a limited free tier, and paid plans that start around $10–15/month for casual use, scaling to roughly $50–95/month for heavy users who need faster queues, higher resolution, and watermark-free commercial output. Annual billing discounts apply. Treat these as ballpark figures — AI video pricing shifts constantly, so check the current page before subscribing.

Compared with the field: cheaper than Runway's upper tiers, in the same neighborhood as Kling, and much cheaper than accessing top-tier Veo quality through Google's premium AI plans.

## Who It's For

- **Short-form content creators** making TikTok/Reels/Shorts clips where human motion sells the video
- **Storyboarders and previz artists** who need to test how a scene moves before committing budget
- **Indie music video makers** — dance and performance shots are Hailuo's sweet spot
- **Developers** who want video generation behind an API without enterprise-tier pricing

Skip it if you need long-form continuity, built-in audio, or an all-in-one editor. Pair it with a traditional editor, or look at [Runway](/reviews/runway-review-2026/) if you want generation plus editing tools in one place. And if cinematic polish with sound is the priority and budget isn't, Veo remains the benchmark — see our [Veo vs Sora comparison](/compare/google-veo-vs-sora-2026/) for how the top tier shakes out.

## Verdict

Hailuo AI is the best AI video generator most people haven't tried. It won't dethrone Veo on cinematic quality, and the short-clip, no-audio workflow keeps it in the "shot generator" category rather than "video maker." But for animating people — realistically, expressively, affordably — it's arguably the strongest option per dollar in 2026.

**Rating: 4/5.** A specialist tool that's genuinely excellent at its specialty. If human motion is what your content needs, start your trial here.
