Most AI audio tools are built for producers. Moises is built for the person sitting on the edge of the bed with a guitar, trying to learn a solo that is buried under three other instruments. That focus is why it has built such a large following among gigging and hobby musicians, and why it deserves a closer look than "another vocal remover."

We used Moises for about three weeks across iOS, Android, and the web app. The test material included pop, funk, metal, jazz trio recordings, a few live bootlegs, and our own rough demos. Here is what it does well, where it falls short, and whether the subscription is worth it.

## What Moises is

Moises is an AI-powered practice and audio app. At its core is a neural-network stem separator: upload a song and it splits the mix into parts such as vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano, and "other." Around that core, it adds tools musicians actually use when learning or rehearsing material: chord detection, key and tempo detection, speed and pitch control, a click track, and looping.

It runs on phones, tablets, and in a browser, and your library syncs between them. That matters more than it sounds. You can split a track on your laptop and then practice along to it on your phone in the rehearsal room without re-uploading anything.

## Key features

### Stem separation

You choose a separation type when you upload. The simplest option splits vocals from everything else. Paid plans add four-stem and five-stem options (vocals, drums, bass, and the rest, plus guitar or piano), and you can mute, solo, or rebalance each stem with on-screen faders.

Quality is strong on modern studio recordings. Vocals come out clean on most pop and hip-hop tracks, and bass isolation is good enough to transcribe a line by ear. Drums are the most reliable stem overall. Dense distorted guitars, heavy reverb, and older mono recordings produce more artifacts, which is true of every splitter we have tried.

### Chord and key detection

Moises analyzes the song and shows a scrolling chord chart synced to playback. On straightforward pop and rock it was right most of the time. On jazz with extended harmony it simplified voicings or guessed wrong, so treat it as a starting point rather than a transcription. Key detection was reliable in our testing.

### Tempo, pitch, and metronome

You can slow a track down without changing pitch, or shift pitch without changing speed. Slowing a solo to 70% while keeping it in tune is the single most useful practice feature here. A click track locks to the detected beat grid, and a count-in helps when you loop a section.

The time-stretching is good at moderate settings. Push it below about half speed and you will hear the usual smearing and warble.

### Looping and sections

You can set A–B loops to drill a passage. The app also tries to mark song sections, which makes jumping to the second chorus faster than scrubbing.

### AI mastering

Moises includes a simple mastering tool for your own recordings. It is fine for making a rough demo louder and more balanced before you send it to bandmates. It is not a substitute for a mastering engineer or a dedicated mastering service, and it gives you only a few controls.

## Pros

- **Built around practice, not just splitting.** Separation, chords, tempo control, and a click track in one app removes a lot of friction.
- **Good separation quality** on most modern recordings, with drums and bass standing out.
- **Cross-platform library** that syncs between phone, tablet, and web.
- **Very approachable.** No DAW, no plugins, no command line.
- **Cheap paid tier** compared with most AI audio subscriptions.

## Cons and limitations

- **Artifacts on hard material.** Live recordings, lo-fi tracks, and wall-of-sound mixes still produce watery vocals and bleed between stems.
- **Chord detection simplifies.** Fine for pop and rock, unreliable for complex harmony.
- **Upload caps and length limits.** The free tier runs out quickly if you are learning a full setlist.
- **Limited export control for producers.** If you want lossless multitrack exports in bulk for a session, a dedicated splitter or local tool fits better.
- **Cloud processing.** Your audio goes to Moises' servers. For unreleased material, check you are comfortable with that.

## How it compares

If you only need stems occasionally, LALAL.AI is a strong pay-as-you-go alternative with a slightly more producer-oriented feel. Our [LALAL.AI review](/reviews/lalal-ai-review-2023/) covers its separation quality in more detail.

Open-source models like Meta's Demucs and Deezer's Spleeter cost nothing and can match commercial tools on some material, but they need a command line or a third-party wrapper, and they give you no practice features at all. ## Pricing

Pricing as of October 2023, approximate and subject to change:

| Plan | Approx. price | What you get |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| Free | $0 | A handful of uploads per month, vocal/backing split, length limits |
| Premium | Roughly $4/month billed annually (more month-to-month) | More uploads, multi-stem separation, chords, pitch and speed control, higher-quality audio |
| Pro / higher tiers | Noticeably more | Larger upload allowances and features aimed at heavier and professional use |

Moises also offers education and team options. Check the current pricing page before you subscribe, because plan names and limits change often.

For a musician who practices most days, the Premium tier is one of the better-value AI subscriptions available. If you split two songs a month, the free tier may be all you need.

## Who it's for

- **Instrumentalists learning songs by ear.** Mute your part, slow it down, loop the hard bar.
- **Singers** who want instrumental backing tracks for practice.
- **Cover bands** preparing setlists and checking parts.
- **Music teachers** building practice material for students.
- **Songwriters** who want a quick, rough master of a demo.

It is less suited to:

- **Producers who need clean, lossless stems in bulk** for remixing. A dedicated splitter or a local Demucs setup fits better.
- **Anyone needing accurate jazz or classical transcription.** The chord chart is a helper, not an answer key.

## Verdict

Moises succeeds because it understands its audience. Stem separation on its own is becoming a commodity, and Moises' separation is good rather than clearly best in class. What sets it apart is everything around the split: synced chords, clean time-stretching, a click that follows the song, and a library that follows you between devices.

If you play an instrument and learn music from recordings, it will likely save you hours each month for the price of a coffee. If you are a producer who just needs stems, you can skip the practice features and pick a splitter that charges by the minute.

**Rating: 4.4 / 5.** The best all-round AI practice app we have tested this year, held back mainly by artifacts on difficult recordings and by chord detection that struggles with complex harmony.
