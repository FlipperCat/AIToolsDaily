Most AI writing tools ask you to come to them. You open a web app, paste in context, generate text, and copy it back to where you were working. HyperWrite takes the opposite approach: it lives in your browser and tries to help wherever you are already typing.

We used HyperWrite daily for three weeks on email, LinkedIn posts, documentation, support replies, and a few blog drafts. We also spent time with its experimental Personal Assistant, one of the first consumer AI agents that can take actions in a web browser. Here is what is useful, what is still rough, and who should pay for it.

## What HyperWrite is

HyperWrite is an AI writing assistant from OthersideAI, delivered mostly through a Chrome extension and a web dashboard. It has three main parts:

1. **TypeAhead:** autocomplete that suggests the next words as you type, in almost any text field.
2. **AI tools library:** a large collection of single-purpose generators for emails, rewrites, summaries, essays, social posts, and more.
3. **Personal Assistant:** an experimental agent that can control your browser to research and complete simple tasks.

The underlying models come from OpenAI, with the higher tiers getting access to more capable models and higher usage limits.

## Key features

### TypeAhead autocomplete

This is HyperWrite's best feature. Start typing a reply in Gmail and a grey suggestion appears; press a key to accept it, or keep typing to ignore it. It reads the surrounding context, so a reply to a meeting request gets suggestions about scheduling rather than generic filler.

In practice, it is most useful for routine writing: confirmations, follow-ups, short updates, and product descriptions. On anything that needs your specific opinion or knowledge, suggestions drift toward plausible but vague. You will reject a lot of them, but accepting the good ones still saves noticeable time across a day.

### AI tools library

HyperWrite ships with a long list of task-specific tools: rewrite in a different tone, expand bullet points into paragraphs, summarize a page, write a cover letter, draft a LinkedIn post, and so on. Each tool is a form with a few fields.

The tools are convenient, but most are thin wrappers around a prompt. If you are already comfortable writing prompts in ChatGPT, you will not find much here you could not do yourself. If you are not, the forms remove guesswork. Template-based writers such as [Rytr](/reviews/rytr-review-2023-is-the-9-ai-writer-good-enough/) take a similar approach at a lower price.

### Chat with web access

HyperWrite includes a chat assistant that can search the web and cite sources. It is handy for quick factual lookups while drafting. Answers were generally sensible but occasionally leaned on a weak source, so check citations before you rely on them.

### Personal Assistant (experimental)

The Personal Assistant is the most ambitious part of HyperWrite. You describe a task, such as "find three Italian restaurants near me with outdoor seating and summarize their reviews," and it opens tabs, clicks, scrolls, and reads pages in your browser while you watch.

When it works, it feels like a preview of how AI tools will operate in the future. In our testing, it handled simple research tasks reasonably well. It struggled with logins, pop-ups, complex forms, and anything with more than a few steps. It sometimes looped, or reported success on a task it had not finished.

It is worth trying for curiosity's sake. We would not trust it with purchases, account changes, or anything you cannot easily check.

## Pros

- **Works where you write.** TypeAhead in Gmail, Docs, LinkedIn, and web forms removes most copy-and-paste.
- **Genuinely time-saving on routine text.** Short replies and boilerplate get faster.
- **Large tool library** that helps users who do not want to write prompts.
- **Early access to agent-style automation** that few other consumer tools offer.
- **Usable free tier** for testing.

## Cons and limitations

- **Suggestions can be generic.** TypeAhead often proposes safe filler, which can make your writing sound bland if you accept too much.
- **Browser-dependent.** The best features need the Chrome extension, so it is less useful in desktop apps.
- **Privacy tradeoff.** An extension that reads what you type on many sites needs broad permissions. Check your company's policy before installing it on a work machine.
- **Agent is unreliable.** The Personal Assistant is a demo of the future, not a dependable tool today.
- **Overlap with ChatGPT.** Many of the library tools duplicate what a good prompt can do.

## Pricing

Pricing as of July 2023, approximate and subject to change:

| Plan | Approx. price | What you get |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| Free | $0 | A limited number of generations per month, TypeAhead, basic tools |
| Premium | Around $20/month | Much higher usage limits, more capable models, full tool library |
| Ultra | Around $45/month | Highest limits and the most access to the Personal Assistant |

Annual billing lowers the effective monthly cost. Usage limits are the main thing that differs between plans, so think about how many generations you would use in a busy week before choosing.

## Who it's for

- **Professionals who write lots of short text** across email, LinkedIn, and web apps.
- **Support and sales teams** answering similar messages all day, if company policy permits the extension.
- **Non-technical users** who prefer forms to prompts.
- **Early adopters** curious about browser agents and willing to supervise them.

It is less suited to:

- **Long-form writers**, who will be better served by a dedicated editor or a chat assistant.
- **Privacy-sensitive environments**, where a broad-permission extension is a problem.
- **Users who already prompt ChatGPT fluently** and do not mind switching tabs.

## Verdict

HyperWrite's TypeAhead is a smart idea: put AI where the writing actually happens rather than in yet another tab. For routine text, it saves real time. The tool library is useful but not unique, and the Personal Assistant is a compelling preview that is not yet dependable.

If you write lots of short messages in your browser, the free tier is worth installing to see how often you accept suggestions. If that number is high, Premium is a fair price. If you mostly write long documents, a chat assistant or a dedicated editor will serve you better.

**Rating: 3.9 / 5.** Excellent in-browser autocomplete, a decent tool library, and an exciting but unreliable early agent.
