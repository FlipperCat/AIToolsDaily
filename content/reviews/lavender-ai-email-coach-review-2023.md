Most sales emails don't fail because of grammar. They fail because they're too long, all
about the sender, and end with a vague ask. **Lavender** is built around that
observation. Rather than generating emails from scratch the way general AI writers do,
it watches you write and coaches you toward something a busy stranger might answer.

We used it for several weeks of real outbound to see whether the coaching changes
behavior or just adds a scoreboard to your inbox.

## What it is

Lavender is a browser extension that adds a side panel to Gmail and Outlook on the web.
As you type, it scores the draft out of 100 and lists specific fixes: cut this sentence,
the subject line is too long, this phrase sounds like a template, the reading level is
too high for a cold email. It also pulls context about the recipient so you can open
with something specific instead of "Hope this finds you well."

It sits in the same broad family as general AI writing assistants, but it's much
narrower. Grammarly or Wordtune will polish any text (we compared them in
[Grammarly vs Wordtune](/compare/grammarly-vs-wordtune-2023/)). Lavender only cares
about one question: will this email get a reply?

## Key Features

### Email scoring

The score is the core of the product. It weighs word count, reading level, subject
line length, the number of questions, how much the email talks about "I/we" versus
"you," and tone. The feedback is concrete. Instead of "make it better," you get "this
email is 180 words; aim for under 100."

### Personalization assistant

The panel surfaces public information about the recipient and their company, such as
role, recent posts, company news, and hiring signals. It's useful for finding one real
hook quickly. It doesn't replace research for high-value accounts, but it cuts the
"open ten tabs" routine for mid-priority prospects.

### AI writing help

A GPT-powered assistant can suggest an opening line, simplify a clunky sentence, or
rewrite a paragraph in a more casual tone. It works best on small pieces. Asking it to
write the whole email gives you something generic that the scoring engine then
politely criticizes.

### Mobile preview

A large share of email gets read on a phone. Lavender shows how your draft looks on a
small screen, which quickly exposes walls of text and long subject lines.

### Tone and personality hints

Lavender can estimate how your recipient prefers to communicate (direct, detail-heavy,
friendly) and suggest adjustments. Treat these hints as guesses. They're based on
limited public signals, and they're occasionally wrong in obvious ways.

### Team coaching

On team plans, managers can see score trends across reps. That's useful for onboarding
new SDRs. It can also turn into score-chasing if leadership treats the number as the
goal rather than replies and meetings.

## Pros

- **Feedback you can act on.** Every suggestion is specific enough to fix in seconds.
- **It changes habits.** After a couple of weeks we were writing shorter emails before
  the panel said anything. That's the real value.
- **Works where you already write.** No new app, no copying text into a separate tool.
- **Fast personalization research** for accounts that don't justify 20 minutes each.
- **Generous free tier** for learning the basics.

## Cons and Limitations

- **The score isn't the outcome.** It can't evaluate targeting, offer, or timing, and
  those matter more than word count.
- **It pushes everyone toward one style.** Short, casual, and question-led works for
  many cold emails. It's not always right for enterprise buyers, follow-ups to warm
  leads, or industries with formal norms.
- **Browser-only.** Desktop Outlook users and people who write inside sales platforms
  that aren't supported get less out of it.
- **Privacy review needed.** The extension reads your drafts, so check with IT or
  security before rolling it out across a company, especially in regulated sectors.
- **AI drafting is average.** If you want a tool to generate full emails, a general
  writer like [Writer](/reviews/writer-ai-review-2023/) or a budget option like
  [Rytr](/reviews/rytr-review-2023/) does more of the drafting itself.

## How We'd Use It

1. **Write the email yourself first.** Don't start with the AI assistant.
2. **Fix the top two or three flags.** Usually length and the ask. Don't chase 100.
3. **Add one real personalization hook** from the research panel.
4. **Check the mobile preview** and cut anything that scrolls.
5. **Track replies, not scores.** If a 75-point email outperforms a 95-point one for your
   audience, trust the replies.

## Who It's For

- **SDRs and BDRs** sending dozens of manual emails a day.
- **Founders doing their own outbound** who never learned cold email and want fast
  feedback.
- **Recruiters** writing to passive candidates, where the same rules (short, specific,
  low-pressure ask) apply.
- **Sales managers** onboarding new reps who need consistent coaching.

It's a weaker fit for people who mostly send automated sequences without editing them,
or for anyone whose emails are long-form by nature, such as proposals or support
replies.

## Verdict

Lavender is one of the more useful AI sales tools we've tested in 2023, mainly because
it teaches rather than replaces. The scoring makes cold email principles visible as you
type, and most people write noticeably tighter emails within a few weeks. The limits are
real: the score can't judge targeting, the house style doesn't suit every audience, and
the browser-only setup excludes some workflows.

Start on the free plan. If your reply rates move and outbound is a core part of your
job, the paid tier pays for itself quickly. If you send a handful of emails a week, the
free tier plus a general assistant like ChatGPT (see how it stacks up in
[ChatGPT vs YouChat](/compare/chatgpt-vs-youchat-2023/)) for occasional rewrites will
cover you.
