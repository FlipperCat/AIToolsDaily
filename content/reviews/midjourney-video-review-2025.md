Midjourney spent three years as the image generator people recommended when they wanted pictures that looked good without much fuss. On June 18, 2025, it shipped its first video model, V1, and the pitch is similar. It isn't the most capable video tool on the market. It's the easiest way to make a good-looking image move, at a price that undercuts most of the field.

A week after launch, here's where it stands: what it does well, where it falls short of Veo 3, Runway, and Kling, and who should bother with it.

## What It Is

V1 is an image-to-video model built into the Midjourney website. There's no separate product and no separate subscription. You generate an image as usual, or upload one, click **Animate**, and get four five-second clips back.

There is no text-to-video in the strict sense. Every clip starts from a still frame. In practice that's a two-step workflow: get the picture right, then set it in motion.

Midjourney describes V1 as an early step toward a much bigger goal, models that simulate interactive 3D worlds in real time. This release is the affordable first installment, and it's best judged that way.

If you're new to the platform, our [Midjourney review](/reviews/midjourney-review-2026-the-leading-ai-art-generator-for-designers/) covers the image side, which you'll need to be comfortable with first.

## Key Features

### Animate: automatic or manual

In **automatic** mode, Midjourney writes its own motion prompt and decides how the scene should move. It's a one-click option and a reasonable default for atmospheric shots.

In **manual** mode, you describe the motion yourself: what the subject does, how the camera moves, how the scene develops. This is where you get some directorial control, though it's control by description. There are no sliders or camera paths.

### Low motion and high motion

Two settings govern how much moves:

- **Low motion** suits ambient scenes. The camera stays mostly still and the subject moves slowly or deliberately. The downside is that some clips barely move at all.
- **High motion** moves both subject and camera. It's more dynamic and more likely to produce glitches, warped limbs, and odd physics.

### Extend

Like a clip? Extend it by about four seconds at a time, up to four times. That puts the ceiling at roughly 21 seconds.

### Bring your own image

You can upload an image made elsewhere, mark it as the start frame, and add a motion prompt. This makes V1 usable for product shots, illustrations, and photos, not only Midjourney generations.

### The Midjourney look, in motion

This is the real selling point. The painterly, stylized, slightly surreal aesthetic that defines Midjourney stills carries into video. If you've already built a consistent visual style with style references or [consistent characters](/tutorials/how-to-create-consistent-characters-in-midjourney-2024/), animating those images keeps the look intact in a way that's hard to match by pasting them into another tool.

## Output Quality

**Where it's strong:** stylized, illustrated, and atmospheric content. Motion is generally coherent and pleasant, textures hold together, and slow camera drifts look deliberate. Fantasy landscapes, character portraits with subtle movement, and animated concept art all play to its strengths.

**Where it's weak:**

- **Resolution.** Output is 480p at launch. That's fine for a phone feed and soft on anything larger. Serious use means running clips through an upscaler.
- **No audio.** Clips are silent.
- **Limited control.** No camera controls, no keyframes, no end-frame targeting, no lip sync.
- **Drift on extensions.** Faces and fine details wander the further you push past the first five seconds.
- **Text and hands.** Signs warp and fingers still misbehave under high motion.

Because every job returns four variations, you'll usually find one keeper. Expect to throw away the other three.

## Pros

- **Very low cost per clip** compared with most rivals.
- **No extra subscription.** It's part of the plan you may already have.
- **Preserves Midjourney's aesthetic**, including your own style and character work.
- **Simple workflow.** One button, two motion settings.
- **Four clips per job** gives you options without re-prompting.
- **Accepts outside images.**

## Cons and Limitations

- **480p only** at launch.
- **No sound.**
- **Image-to-video only.** No direct text-to-video.
- **Minimal direction tools** next to Runway or Kling.
- **Short clips.** About 21 seconds at most, with quality fading toward the end.
- **Website only** for now.
- **Public by default.** Your generations appear in Midjourney's public feed unless you have Stealth Mode, which requires a higher-tier plan.
- **No free tier** to test with.

## Pricing

**As of late June 2025 (approximate, and subject to change):**

| Plan | Monthly price | Video notes |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Basic | ~$10 | Video included, limited fast GPU time |
| Standard | ~$30 | More fast time |
| Pro | ~$60 | Stealth Mode, relaxed-mode video in testing |
| Mega | ~$120 | Most fast time, relaxed-mode video in testing |

Annual billing lowers the monthly rate.

Video draws from the same GPU time as images. A video job costs about eight times an image job and produces four five-second clips. Midjourney frames this as roughly one image's worth of cost per second of video.

As an illustration, a Basic plan used only for video covers somewhere around a couple dozen jobs a month. That's enough to experiment and not enough for a regular content schedule. Anyone planning real volume should look at Standard or above, and at the relaxed-mode video option Midjourney is testing on Pro and Mega.

## How It Compares

**Google Veo 3.** The quality leader right now, with native audio, dialogue, and proper text-to-video. Its fullest access sits in Google's roughly $250-per-month AI Ultra plan. Different league, different budget.

**Runway Gen-4.** Far more control, with references for consistent characters and locations and a full editing toolset around the model. Better for production work and more expensive per clip. Our [Sora vs Runway comparison](/compare/sora-vs-runway-2025-which-ai-video-generator-deserves-your-money/) covers its strengths.

**Kling.** Strong realism and physics at higher resolution. If you need believable human movement, it's a safer choice. See [Runway vs Kling](/compare/runway-vs-kling-2024-which-ai-video-generator-wins-right-now/) for background.

**Sora.** Text-to-video with storyboard tools, bundled into paid ChatGPT plans.

Midjourney wins on price per clip and on aesthetics when you start from a stylized still. It loses on resolution, sound, and control.

## The Legal Backdrop

The launch came a week after Disney and Universal filed a copyright lawsuit against Midjourney over its image generator. The case is unresolved and may take years.

For commercial users, the practical advice is unchanged but more pressing. Don't generate recognizable characters or franchises, keep records of your prompts and source images, and read the current terms. Midjourney requires larger companies, those above roughly $1 million in annual revenue, to be on a Pro or Mega plan.

## Who It's For

**A good fit for:**

- **Existing Midjourney subscribers.** You already have access. Try it before paying for anything else.
- **Illustrators and concept artists** who want motion tests of their own style.
- **Social media creators** making short, silent, stylized loops and adding sound in an editor.
- **Anyone curious about AI video on a small budget.**

**Not a fit for:**

- **Client or broadcast work** that needs HD output and precise direction.
- **Talking-head or dialogue content.** There's no audio or lip sync.
- **Photoreal product or human footage**, where Veo 3 and Kling are ahead.

## Verdict

Midjourney's V1 video model is a modest release on purpose. It doesn't try to beat Veo 3 on realism or Runway on control. It turns Midjourney images into short, attractive clips with one click, for a fraction of what competitors charge.

Inside those limits it works well. Stylized content looks great, the workflow is about as simple as AI video gets, and it costs existing subscribers nothing extra to try. The 480p ceiling, the silence, and the lack of direction tools keep it out of professional pipelines for now.

If you already pay for Midjourney, spend an afternoon with it. If you're choosing a primary video generator, treat this as a sketching tool and keep a more capable model for final shots.
