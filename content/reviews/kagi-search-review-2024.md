Most search engines are free because you aren't the customer. Kagi turns that around: you pay a monthly subscription, and in return you get search with no ads, no tracking-based profiling, and a lot of control over what shows up. In 2024, with AI answers appearing at the top of every search results page, Kagi has also added its own AI layer. The question is whether any of that is worth paying for when Google and Bing cost nothing.

We used Kagi as our default search engine for several weeks across work research, coding questions, shopping, and everyday lookups. Here's what we found.

## What is Kagi?

Kagi is an independent, subscription-funded search engine. It combines results from its own index with results from other search providers, then ranks and filters them without advertising pressure. The company also makes **Orion**, a WebKit-based browser for Mac and iPhone, and a set of AI tools built on top of search: Quick Answer, the Universal Summarizer, FastGPT, and Kagi Assistant.

The pitch is simple: because you pay, Kagi has no reason to fill the page with ads, sponsored listings, or low-quality pages optimized for clicks.

## Key Features

### Clean, ad-free results

The first thing you notice is what's missing. No sponsored results, no shopping carousels pushed ahead of real answers, and noticeably fewer content-farm pages. Kagi also groups "listicle"-style articles into a collapsed section, so they don't crowd out primary sources.

### Personalized rankings

You can **block**, **lower**, **raise**, or **pin** any domain. Block the recipe site with twelve pop-ups, raise the documentation site you trust, pin Wikipedia. These settings apply to every future search, and after a few weeks your results feel noticeably tuned to you. Kagi also publishes aggregate stats on which domains its users block most, which is an interesting look at what people consider junk.

### Lenses

Lenses narrow a search to a type of source. Built-in options include Forums, Programming, Academic, and PDFs, and you can create your own. For example, a "Docs" lens limited to official documentation sites. It's a practical feature that most free search engines don't offer.

### Quick Answer

End a query with a question mark and Kagi writes a short AI summary at the top of the results, with numbered citations to the pages it used. It's similar to what [Perplexity](/perplexity-ai-review-2024/) does, but it's optional and appears on top of normal results instead of replacing them. If you don't want AI in your search, you simply don't trigger it.

### Universal Summarizer

Paste a URL, a PDF, or a YouTube link and Kagi summarizes it, either as a paragraph or as key points. You can also summarize any search result straight from the results page. It handles long articles and video transcripts well and saves real time on "is this worth reading?" decisions.

### Kagi Assistant

On the top tier, **Kagi Assistant** gives you a chat interface with models from several providers, including OpenAI's GPT-4o and Anthropic's Claude 3.5 Sonnet, plus options from Google, Mistral, and Meta. The key difference from using those chatbots directly: the Assistant can ground its answers in Kagi search results, including your personal domain rankings and lenses. You can also set up custom assistants with their own instructions.

For people currently paying separately for ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro, this is the feature that changes the value calculation.

### Bangs

Kagi supports "bangs," shortcuts like `!w` for Wikipedia or `!yt` for YouTube that send your query straight to another site. Power users coming from DuckDuckGo will feel at home.

## Pros

- **No ads and noticeably less spam.** Results feel like they're working for you.
- **Real control.** Domain rankings and lenses are the best personalization tools we've seen in a search engine.
- **AI is opt-in.** Quick Answers and summaries are there when you ask, not forced on every query.
- **Kagi Assistant bundles multiple frontier models** into one subscription on the Ultimate plan.
- **Privacy-friendly business model.** Revenue comes from subscriptions, not selling attention.
- **Fast.** Search results load quickly and the interface is uncluttered.

## Cons and Limitations

- **It costs money.** This is the obvious hurdle. Free search is good enough for most casual users, and $10 a month for search is a hard sell.
- **Local search is weaker.** Maps, business listings, opening hours, and "near me" queries still trail Google.
- **Fresh news and niche images can lag.** Breaking news and image search are serviceable but not Kagi's strength.
- **The cheapest plan has a search cap.** Starter users need to watch their monthly count, which works against building a habit.
- **The best AI features sit on the top tier.** Kagi Assistant requires Ultimate.
- **You need an account.** Searches aren't used for ads, but they are tied to a paid login, which some privacy-focused users will dislike.
- **Small-company risk.** Kagi is a small, independent company competing with some of the largest companies in the world.

## Pricing (as of October 2024)

Prices are approximate and can change, so check Kagi's pricing page for current details.

- **Trial:** A limited number of free searches to test the product.
- **Starter:** About **$5/month** for a capped number of searches (around 300 per month).
- **Professional:** About **$10/month** for unlimited searches. This is the plan most regular users choose.
- **Ultimate:** About **$25/month** for unlimited searches plus Kagi Assistant, early access to features, and support for the company's development.
- **Family and team plans** are available, and annual billing brings a discount.

A useful way to think about Ultimate: if you already pay about $20 a month for a single AI chatbot, $25 gets you unlimited ad-free search plus access to several top models. If you don't use AI chat much, Professional is the sensible tier.

## Who it's for

**Kagi is a strong fit for:**

- Researchers, developers, and writers who search all day and are tired of SEO spam
- People who want AI answers with sources but still prefer a traditional results page
- Anyone paying for more than one AI chatbot who'd like to consolidate
- Privacy-minded users who would rather pay than be the product

**It's probably not for:**

- Casual searchers who mainly look up weather, sports scores, and local businesses
- Anyone who won't pay for search on principle
- People who want a full AI answer engine. Perplexity or [You.com](/reviews/you-com-review-2023/) are more conversational by design (see our [Perplexity vs Google comparison](/compare/perplexity-vs-google/)).

## How it compares

Against Google, Kagi wins on cleanliness and control and loses on local results and freshness. Against AI-first tools like Perplexity and You.com, Kagi is more conservative: it keeps the classic results page as the core and adds AI where you ask for it. If you want AI to do the searching for you, look at our [Perplexity vs You.com comparison](/compare/perplexity-vs-you-com-2024/). If you want better search results with AI available when needed, Kagi is the more natural fit.

## Verdict

Kagi is the best argument yet that paying for search can make sense. Results are cleaner, personalization actually changes what you see, and the AI features are useful without taking over the page. The Professional plan is worth trying for anyone who searches heavily for work, and Ultimate is a good deal if it replaces a separate AI chatbot subscription. Keep Google bookmarked for maps and local lookups, and use the trial to see whether a few weeks of ad-free results change how you search.
