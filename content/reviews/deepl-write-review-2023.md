DeepL built its reputation on one thing: translations that read like a human wrote them. Now the Cologne-based company wants to fix the text you wrote yourself. DeepL Write launched in beta on January 17, 2023, and we have spent a week running our own drafts, emails, and a couple of reader submissions through it.

The short version: it is the most natural-sounding rewriting tool we have used, it is currently free, and it is missing about half the features you would expect from a mature writing assistant.

## What is DeepL Write?

DeepL Write is a browser-based writing assistant. You paste text into the left panel, and the right panel shows a rewritten version with improved grammar, punctuation, word choice, and phrasing. Click on any word or sentence in the output and you get a menu of alternatives.

That is the whole product right now. There is no browser extension, no Word add-in, no tone detector, no plagiarism checker. It is one page at deepl.com/write, no sign-in required. Think of it as a "make this read better" button with a second opinion attached.

It currently supports English (US and UK) and German. Given that DeepL's translator handles around 30 languages, the narrow launch is a little surprising, but the company has said more are on the way.

## How it actually works

The workflow is fast:

1. Paste text up to the beta's character limit (a few paragraphs at a time).
2. Read the suggested version. Changes are subtly highlighted so you can see what moved.
3. Click any word for synonyms, or click a sentence for a different construction.
4. Copy the result.

Where DeepL Write differs from a checker like [Grammarly](/reviews/grammarly-review/) is that it does not flag errors one at a time. It rewrites the whole passage and lets you compare it against your original. If you are a confident writer, that can feel aggressive. If English is your second language, it feels like having an editor on call.

## Key features

**Full-passage rewriting.** DeepL Write does more than fix commas. It restructures awkward sentences, swaps overused words, and tightens phrasing. In our tests it turned a clumsy, translated-sounding paragraph from a German colleague into something we would have happily published, with no manual edits.

**Alternatives on demand.** The click-for-alternatives feature is the best part of the product. You are not stuck with the machine's first choice. Click "significant" and you get "considerable," "major," and "substantial," each with the sentence re-flowed to fit.

**Register preservation.** Unlike ChatGPT, which tends to inflate a simple email into a corporate memo when asked to "improve" it, DeepL Write mostly keeps your tone. Casual stays casual. Formal stays formal.

**German support.** This is a bigger deal than it sounds. Most AI writing tools treat German as an afterthought. DeepL's German output is, unsurprisingly, excellent, including sensible handling of formal "Sie" versus informal "du."

**No account, no cost.** For now.

## What we liked

- **Output quality.** The rewrites read naturally. DeepL Write rarely introduces the stilted, overly formal phrasing that plagues rule-based grammar tools.
- **Speed.** Rewrites appear in a second or two. No queue, no waitlist, no "at capacity" messages.
- **Control.** The alternatives menu means you stay the author. It suggests, you choose.
- **Restraint.** It fixes what needs fixing and mostly leaves good sentences alone, which is rarer than it should be in this category.
- **It is honest about being a beta.** The interface is minimal and does not pretend to do things it cannot.

## What we didn't like

- **Two languages.** If you write in Spanish, French, or Portuguese, DeepL Write is useless to you today.
- **No integrations.** No Chrome extension, no Gmail, no Google Docs, no Word. You copy and paste every time. Grammarly, [Wordtune](/reviews/21-wordtune-review/), and even DeepL's own translator all have extensions.
- **Character limit.** The beta caps input length. For a long report you will be pasting section by section.
- **No explanation of changes.** It rewrites, but it does not tell you why. Grammarly's "this is a comma splice" style feedback helps you learn. DeepL Write just hands you the fixed version.
- **Occasional overreach.** Once or twice it smoothed a deliberately short, punchy sentence into something flatter. Read the output; do not just accept it.
- **No tone or length controls.** You cannot ask for "shorter" or "more formal." You get one rewrite and a set of alternatives.
- **Privacy caveat.** DeepL's policy for free services allows text to be retained temporarily to improve the product. The stricter no-retention terms of DeepL Pro do not yet apply to Write, so keep confidential material out of it.

## Pricing (as of January 2023)

DeepL Write is free during the beta. There is no paid tier yet. DeepL has indicated that a premium version with higher limits and additional features will arrive later. Given that DeepL Pro for translation starts at under $10 per month, we would expect Write pricing to land in a similar range or be bundled into existing Pro plans.

If you come to rely on it, plan for the free version to become more limited when paid plans launch.

## Who it's for

**Non-native English and German writers.** This is the killer use case. If you write competent but slightly unnatural English, DeepL Write smooths it out better than anything else we have tried, including [QuillBot](/reviews/quillbot-review-2023/).

**Anyone who wants a quick second pass** on an important email or short document without installing anything or creating an account.

**Not for:** people who need in-line checking across apps, writers who want to understand their mistakes, long-form authors working on full manuscripts, or anyone writing outside English and German.

## How it compares

The obvious comparison is Grammarly, but they are different tools. Grammarly lives inside your apps and flags issues as you type. DeepL Write is a destination you visit with a finished draft.

Wordtune is the closest match in spirit, with its rewrite-this-sentence approach, but Wordtune works sentence by sentence while DeepL handles whole passages. QuillBot's paraphraser is more about changing text than improving it. For a fuller look at the rewriting category, see our [Wordtune vs QuillBot](/compare/wordtune-vs-quillbot-2023/) comparison.

ChatGPT can do all of this too, of course. But it requires prompting, tends to change more than you asked, and has been unreliable at peak hours. DeepL Write is a single-purpose tool that does one thing quickly and predictably.

## Verdict

DeepL Write is a very good first release of a very small product. The core rewriting quality is the best we have seen from a dedicated tool, and the alternatives menu is a genuinely smart piece of interface design. If you write in English or German and your drafts need polish, bookmark it while it is free.

But it is a beta in every sense. No integrations, two languages, and a character limit mean it will not replace a full writing assistant yet. Our advice: use it alongside your existing checker, and watch this space. If DeepL ships extensions and a few more languages, it becomes a serious Grammarly alternative. Until then, it is an excellent free tool with an obvious ceiling.

**Rating: 4/5** for what it does today. Expect that to move once the beta ends and pricing appears.
