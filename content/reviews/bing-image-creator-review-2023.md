AI image generation has a gatekeeping problem in 2023: Midjourney requires a paid plan and a Discord account, DALL-E 2 runs on limited credits, and Stable Diffusion rewards those willing to fiddle with settings. **Bing Image Creator** is Microsoft's answer — a completely free, browser-based image generator powered by OpenAI's DALL-E technology, wrapped in an interface your parents could use.

We've generated several hundred images with it over the past two months. Here's where it genuinely impresses and where the "free" shows.

## What is Bing Image Creator?

Bing Image Creator (at bing.com/create) is Microsoft's text-to-image tool, launched broadly in March 2023 as part of the company's sprint to put OpenAI's models everywhere. Type a description, wait a few seconds, and get four image options at 1024×1024. It's also built into Bing Chat, so you can ask for images mid-conversation, and into the Edge sidebar.

It runs on an updated version of OpenAI's DALL-E model — Microsoft is deliberately vague about the exact version, but the results are noticeably better than what the standard DALL-E 2 site produces with the same prompts.

All you need is a free Microsoft account. No Discord, no credit card, no waitlist.

## How the boost system works

Free products need throttles, and Microsoft's is the "boost." Each week you get a batch of boosts; while you have them, generations come back fast — typically 10–30 seconds. Run out, and you can keep generating, but you drop into a slow queue that can take a few minutes per batch.

Two wrinkles worth knowing:

- You can redeem **Microsoft Rewards points** (earned by searching with Bing) for additional boosts, which effectively makes heavy use free if you're in the Microsoft ecosystem anyway.
- Boost counts and refresh rates have shifted a couple of times since launch, so treat the exact numbers as fluid.

For casual use — a handful of images a day — you'll rarely feel the ceiling.

## Image quality: better than expected

The pleasant surprise: for a free tool, output quality is solid. Prompts describing concrete scenes — "a red fox sleeping in a snowy forest, morning light, photograph" — come back with believable lighting and composition. Simple illustrations, logo concepts, blog imagery, and social-post visuals are all comfortably within its range.

Where it lags behind [Midjourney](/reviews/01-midjourney-review/) is *polish*. Midjourney v5 images have an art-directed quality — dramatic lighting, coherent style, striking detail — that Bing's output rarely matches. Bing images tend toward a flatter, stock-photo-meets-render look, and fine details (hands, text in images, faces at a distance) still go wrong regularly, as they do for every generator this year.

Interestingly, Bing often *follows instructions more literally* than Midjourney, which reinterprets prompts artistically. If you ask for "three blue mugs on a wooden table," Bing is more likely to give you exactly three mugs. For functional images, literal is a feature.

## Key features

- **Four variations per prompt** at 1024×1024, downloadable individually.
- **Bing Chat integration** — describe and refine images conversationally, which is genuinely useful for iterating without prompt-crafting skills.
- **Edge sidebar access** for generating images while you browse or write.
- **Surprise Me button** that generates a random example prompt — better than it sounds for learning what phrasing works.
- **Content filtering** that is strict, sometimes to a fault: harmless prompts occasionally trip the "unsafe content" block, and named public figures are off-limits entirely.

## Pros

- **Genuinely free** — the best zero-cost image generator available right now.
- **Easiest onboarding in the category**: browser, Microsoft login, done.
- **Good prompt adherence** for literal, concrete scenes.
- **No Discord required** — a real accessibility win over Midjourney.
- **Rewards integration** lets heavy users earn more fast generations.

## Cons and limitations

- **Artistic ceiling.** For stylized illustration and concept art, Midjourney and a well-tuned [Stable Diffusion](/reviews/stable-diffusion-review/) setup are clearly ahead.
- **Commercial use is murky.** Microsoft's terms speak to personal, non-commercial use — a problem for freelancers and businesses.
- **No control surface.** No negative prompts, no style presets, no aspect ratios (square only), no upscaling, no variations on a chosen image. You get what you get and you re-roll.
- **Overzealous filter** with occasional false positives and no explanation of what tripped it.
- **Slow mode is slow** once boosts are gone — fine for patience, bad for iteration.

## Pricing

Free, as of August 2023, with no paid tier at all. The cost comparison against alternatives: Midjourney starts around $10/month, DALL-E 2 sells credit packs, and Stable Diffusion is free but wants technical setup or GPU rental. ## Who is it for?

- **Beginners** taking their first swing at AI image generation — this is the on-ramp we now recommend to non-technical friends.
- **Casual users** who need occasional images for presentations, posts, or fun.
- **Bing/Edge users** already collecting Rewards points.
- **Anyone evaluating** whether AI imagery fits their workflow before paying for Midjourney.

It's *not* for professional designers, brand work requiring clear licensing, or anyone who needs aspect-ratio control and fine-grained iteration.

## Verdict

Bing Image Creator is the best free AI image generator of 2023, and it isn't especially close. Microsoft is clearly using free DALL-E access to pull people into Bing and Edge — and it's working, because the trade is a good one for users. Quality lands well below Midjourney's ceiling but well above "toy," and the frictionless setup makes it the obvious first stop for the AI-curious.

**Rating: 4/5** for what it is: an accessible, capable, genuinely free tool with real limits on control and licensing. Start here; graduate to a paid generator when your ambitions outgrow it.
