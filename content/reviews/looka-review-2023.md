A logo is one of the first things a new business needs and one of the easiest things to overspend on. A freelance designer can charge anywhere from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. Logo contest sites sit somewhere in the middle. Looka promises something closer to the price of lunch: answer a few questions, pick from a page of generated options, adjust one, and download.

We made logos for three test businesses (a coffee cart, a bookkeeping practice, and a kids' coding camp) to see whether the output is good enough to put on a storefront or only good enough as a placeholder.

## What Looka is

Looka is a Toronto-based logo and branding platform. It started life as Logojoy and rebranded as Looka in 2019 as it expanded from logos into a broader brand kit.

It's worth being clear about what "AI" means here. Looka doesn't paint new images from a text prompt the way DALL-E 2 or Midjourney do. It assembles logos from a curated library of symbols, typefaces, colors, and layouts, and it uses your choices to decide which combinations to show you. That limits originality. It's also why the results are clean, legible, and available as vector files, which text-to-image generators can't give you.

## Key features

### The logo generator

You enter your company name and an optional slogan, choose your industry, then pick a handful of sample logos you like from a gallery. Next you choose color moods and, optionally, symbol keywords such as "coffee," "leaf," or "ledger." Looka then produces a scrolling page of options, and you can keep loading more.

The preference step matters more than it looks. When we picked minimalist samples for the bookkeeping practice, the results were clean wordmarks and simple monograms. When we picked playful samples for the coding camp, we got rounded fonts and bright icons. When we skipped the step, we got a messy mix of both.

### The editor

Click any option to open the editor, where you can change:

- **Symbol:** swap it for another icon from Looka's library
- **Typography:** font, weight, and letter spacing
- **Colors:** icon, text, and background
- **Layout:** icon above text, icon beside text, text only, or enclosed in a badge shape
- **Slogan:** placement and style

The editor is quick and hard to break. It's also limited. You can't draw, edit an icon's shape, or build something from scratch. If you want a different coffee cup instead of a different *version* of the coffee cup, you need a real design tool.

### Mockups and previews

Every logo can be previewed on business cards, shirts, signage, a phone screen, and social media profiles. This is one of Looka's best features. A logo that looks fine on a white background often falls apart at small sizes or on a dark shirt, and the mockups show you that before you pay.

### Brand Kit

The Brand Kit subscription turns one logo into a set of matching assets: business card designs, social media profile images and cover photos, email signatures, letterhead, and a simple brand guidelines document with your colors and fonts. A higher tier adds a basic website builder. For a new business owner, having all of that match on day one saves real time.

## How the results held up

- **Coffee cart:** Our best result. A simple cup icon beside a rounded serif font looked professional on the signage and cup mockups. We'd have used it.
- **Bookkeeping practice:** A text-only wordmark in a clean sans serif. It looked like a designer's reasonable first draft, which is a compliment at this price.
- **Kids' coding camp:** Our weakest result. The icons for "code" and "kids" were predictable: curly brackets, rockets, and lightbulbs. Many options looked like clip art.

The pattern was consistent. Looka does best when the logo is mostly typography and worst when you need a distinctive symbol in a crowded category.

## Pros

- **Fast.** We had a usable logo in under 30 minutes for two of three businesses.
- **Free to explore.** You only pay when you download, so you can try it with no risk.
- **Vector files.** The Premium package includes SVG, EPS, and PDF files that scale cleanly for print and signage.
- **Mockups catch problems early.** You see the logo in context before you commit.
- **Useful follow-on assets.** The Brand Kit handles the boring pieces, such as business cards and social headers, in matching style.
- **Beginner-proof editor.** It's hard to make something truly ugly.

## Cons and limitations

- **Limited originality.** Icons come from a shared library, so similar logos can exist elsewhere. That's a real concern if you plan to register a trademark.
- **Shallow editing.** You can swap and adjust elements, but you can't create new ones.
- **Generic results in crowded categories.** Tech, fitness, and real estate options often look alike.
- **The cheapest tier is a trap.** About $20 gets you one low-resolution PNG, which isn't enough for print, signage, or most merchandise.
- **Brand Kit is a subscription.** Before you commit, check what you keep if you cancel.
- **Upsells.** The checkout flow pushes you toward bigger packages.

## Pricing (as of March 2023)

Prices are approximate and change from time to time, so check Looka's pricing page before you buy.

- **Basic Logo:** about **$20** one-time for a single low-resolution PNG.
- **Premium Logo:** about **$65** one-time for high-resolution files, vector formats, color variations, and edits after purchase.
- **Brand Kit:** about **$96 per year** for the Premium logo files plus the brand asset templates.
- **Brand Kit Web:** about **$129 per year**, which adds the website builder.

For most businesses, **Premium** is the right choice. Skip Basic unless you only need a quick social media avatar. Compared with a freelance designer, even the Brand Kit is inexpensive. The difference is that a designer gives you something made for you, and Looka gives you a well-assembled combination of parts.

## Who it's for

Looka is a good fit for:

- Solo founders, side projects, and new small businesses that need a logo this week.
- Businesses whose logo will be mostly text anyway, such as consultancies, accountants, and law practices.
- Anyone who wants a solid placeholder now and plans to hire a designer once revenue justifies it.

It's a poor fit for:

- Consumer brands where the logo is a competitive asset, such as packaged food, apparel, or apps.
- Companies planning to trademark a distinctive symbol.
- Anyone who needs custom illustration or a mascot.

## Verdict

Looka is one of the best-value ways to get a clean, professional starter logo, as long as you understand what you're buying. It's a smart assembler of fonts, icons, and layouts, not a designer. For a coffee cart, a consultancy, or a new service business, the Premium package produces something you can put on a business card and a storefront without embarrassment.

Where it falls short is distinctiveness. If your logo needs to stand out in a crowded market or carry a trademark, treat Looka as a way to explore directions and then take your favorite to a human designer. For everyone else, it turns a stalled to-do item into a finished one for less than the cost of a nice dinner.
