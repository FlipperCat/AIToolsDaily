## Google Duet AI for Workspace Review (2023): Worth $30 a Seat?

Google spent most of this year promising that generative AI would show up inside the apps people already use rather than in a separate tab. At Cloud Next in late August, Duet AI for Workspace went generally available at $30 per user per month, and the promise became a line item.

I have been using it across Gmail, Docs, Slides and Sheets for several weeks on real work — client updates, a pitch deck, meeting summaries, and a fair amount of inbox triage. Here is what holds up and what does not.

## What It Is

Duet AI is not a chatbot bolted onto Workspace. It is a set of generative features embedded in each app, surfaced through contextual buttons: "Help me write" in Gmail and Docs, "Help me visualize" in Slides, "Help me organize" in Sheets, plus background generation and studio-quality video tweaks in Meet.

The strategic idea is that the AI sits where the work already lives. You do not copy a draft into another window, prompt it, and paste it back. You click a button in the compose box. That sounds like a small difference. In daily use it is not — the friction of the copy-paste round trip is exactly what stops most people from using [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) for routine email.

## Key Features

**Help me write (Gmail).** The single most-used feature. You describe what you want, get a draft, then refine with Formalize, Elaborate, Shorten, or "I'm feeling lucky." Replies are context-aware — it reads the thread. For a polite decline, a scheduling reply, or a status update, it produces something serviceable in about four seconds.

**Help me write (Docs).** Same idea with more room to run. The genuinely differentiating trick here is referencing your own Drive files: you can point it at an existing document and ask it to draft from that context. That is the thing a generic assistant cannot do, and it is where the enterprise pitch actually lands.

**Help me visualize (Slides).** Generates images from a text prompt directly into a slide, with style options. It is convenient and it kills the stock-photo hunt, but the output quality is below what dedicated image tools produce today. Fine for internal decks, thin for client-facing work.

**Help me organize (Sheets).** Describe a tracker and it generates a structured starter table with sensible column types. This is the most underrated feature — not because the output is brilliant, but because "blank spreadsheet" is a real productivity tax and this removes it.

**Meet enhancements.** Generated backgrounds and studio look. Pleasant, not a reason to buy.

## Pros

- **Zero context switching.** The features are where you already are. Adoption inside a team is dramatically higher than with a separate tool.
- **Drive grounding in Docs.** Drafting from your own files is a real advantage over standalone assistants.
- **Sensible refinement controls.** Shorten/Formalize/Elaborate is a better interface for non-technical users than teaching them prompt engineering.
- **Admin-friendly.** It arrives through your existing Workspace admin console with the governance and data handling you already agreed to, which shortens the security review considerably.
- **Consistent quality on short-form text.** Emails, summaries, and first drafts of routine documents come out clean.

## Cons and Limitations

**The writing is competent and completely generic.** Duet AI writes in a hedged, corporate, slightly over-polite register and pushes back toward that register even when you ask for something else. It is a first-draft engine, not a voice. Anything going in front of customers needs a real rewrite.

**It does not reason about your data.** Ask Sheets for structure and it will help. Ask it to interpret the numbers in a complex sheet and you will be disappointed. The analytical depth is not there yet.

**Rollout is uneven.** Features have appeared in some apps and accounts before others, and the side-panel experience Google demoed is not uniformly available. Buying today means buying partly on roadmap.

**Slides image generation lags the field.** Compared with dedicated generators, results are safe and a bit flat, and text rendering inside images is unreliable.

**$30 per seat is a serious number.** For a 40-person company that is $14,400 a year on top of existing Workspace fees. The value is real but diffuse — saved minutes across many people — which makes it genuinely hard to justify on a spreadsheet.

## Pricing (Approximate, as of September 2023)

$30 per user per month as an add-on to an existing Workspace subscription, announced as generally available at Cloud Next in late August 2023. Enterprise agreements and annual commitments will vary; talk to your reseller before paying list.

For comparison, Microsoft has announced 365 Copilot at the same $30 per user per month, though as of this writing it remains limited to a paid early-access program rather than broad availability. Notion's AI add-on sits around $10 per member per month, and a [ChatGPT Plus](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) seat is $20 per month for individuals. The $30 tier is clearly a deliberate enterprise price point rather than a competitive one.

Workspace Labs remains the practical way to evaluate before buying. It is free, waitlisted, and covers enough of the writing features to tell you whether your team will actually click the button.

## Who It's For

**Buy it if** you are already all-in on Workspace, you have a large volume of routine written communication, and your bottleneck is getting people to use AI at all. The embedded placement solves the adoption problem better than any standalone tool, and Drive grounding in Docs is a genuine capability others do not have.

**Skip it if** you are a small team where two or three people do most of the writing — individual assistant subscriptions will cost a fraction of per-seat licensing and give you stronger models. Also skip it if your primary need is research or synthesis rather than drafting; [Bard](/compare/bard-vs-chatgpt-2023/) and [Bing Chat](/reviews/bing-chat-review-2023/) both handle open web questions better than anything inside Workspace does today, and for structured knowledge work a tool like [Notion AI](/notion-ai-complete-guide-2023/) covers more ground per dollar.

## Verdict

Duet AI for Workspace is the most convincing argument yet that the winning form factor for workplace AI is a button, not a chat window. The features are unremarkable in isolation and quietly effective in aggregate, precisely because nobody has to change how they work to use them.

What it is not, yet, is a $30-per-seat productivity transformation. The writing needs editing, the analysis is shallow, and parts of the pitch are still shipping. If you can run a Labs pilot with a representative group first, do that. If you are a Workspace enterprise with a mandate to deploy AI this year and a security team that would rather approve one vendor than twelve, this is the least painful path available.

**Rating: 3.5/5** — excellent placement, average output, aggressive price. Pilot before you buy seats.
