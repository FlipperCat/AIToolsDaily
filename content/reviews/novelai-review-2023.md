Most AI writing tools in 2023 are chatbots wearing a writer's hat. You ask, they answer, and the result reads like a helpful assistant describing a story instead of telling one. NovelAI takes a different approach. It is a co-writing engine: you write a paragraph, it continues in the same voice, and you steer as you go.

We spent several weeks drafting short fiction, a fantasy serial, and a text adventure with it. Here's how it holds up.

## What NovelAI is

NovelAI is a subscription web app from Anlatan built around two things: a story-continuation text model and an anime-leaning image generator. The current flagship text model, **Kayra**, arrived this summer and is a clear step up from the older Clio and Krake models in coherence and prose quality.

The key difference from [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) is the interaction model. NovelAI doesn't take requests. It predicts what comes next in your document, so the quality of your opening lines and setup has a big effect on the output. If you've only used instruction-following chatbots, that takes a while to get used to. If you're a fiction writer, it can feel much more natural.

## Key features

### Story editor with continuation
You write, hit **Send**, and the AI adds a chunk of text. You can retry for alternatives, undo, edit anything inline, and branch the story to explore different directions. The retry-and-edit loop is where most of the work happens.

### Memory and Author's Note
**Memory** is persistent text that's always inserted into the AI's context: the premise, point of view, and tone. **Author's Note** sits closer to the end of the context and nudges style right now ("tense, short sentences, the storm is getting worse"). These two fields are the main steering tools, and learning to use them well is the biggest skill jump for new users.

### Lorebook
The Lorebook is a keyword-triggered encyclopedia for your world. Write an entry for a character, city, or magic system, attach trigger words, and NovelAI inserts that entry into context only when the words appear. For long projects this is the best feature in the app. It keeps a 40-chapter fantasy consistent without spending the whole context window on background.

### Presets, modules, and generation settings
You can adjust temperature, repetition penalty, and sampling order, or just pick a preset. Style modules push the model toward particular genres or text formats. Casual users can ignore all of it. Power users will spend hours tuning.

### Text Adventure mode
There's a dedicated mode for interactive fiction in the style of classic AI dungeon games: you type actions, the AI narrates the result. It works better with Kayra than it did with older models, though it still loses track of inventory and minor details over long sessions.

### Image generation
NovelAI's image generator is tuned heavily toward anime and illustration styles. It's good at character art and fan-style illustration, weaker at photorealism and general-purpose imagery than tools like [Leonardo.Ai](/reviews/leonardo-ai-review-2023/) or Midjourney. Image generation uses Anlas, a monthly credit allowance tied to your tier.

## Pros

- **Prose that sounds like fiction.** Continuations read like a novel, not like a summary of one.
- **The Lorebook is excellent** for world-building and long serials.
- **Privacy focus.** Stories are encrypted, and the company doesn't market itself on training from user content.
- **Few content restrictions.** Dark themes, violence, and mature romance don't trigger constant refusals.
- **Deep control** for people who want to tune generation behavior.
- **An active community** sharing presets, modules, and Lorebook templates.

## Cons and limitations

- **Steep learning curve.** Memory, Author's Note, Lorebook priorities, and sampling settings are powerful but not intuitive. Expect a few frustrating sessions first.
- **It doesn't follow instructions well.** You can't say "rewrite this scene from Mara's perspective" the way you would in a chatbot. You have to set up the text so that the next thing the model writes is what you want.
- **Limited context.** Even on the top tier, the model sees a fraction of a novel at once. Details drift unless you keep Memory and the Lorebook up to date.
- **Repetition and loops.** Without the right settings, the model can fall into repeated phrases or circular dialogue.
- **Image generator is style-specific.** Great for anime art, less useful for marketing images or realistic scenes.
- **No real non-fiction use case.** It's the wrong tool for blog posts, emails, or research.

## Pricing (approximate, as of November 2023)

NovelAI uses tiered monthly subscriptions. Prices and allowances change, so check the official pricing page.

| Tier | Approx. price/month | What changes |
|------|--------------------|--------------|
| Free trial | $0 | A limited number of text generations to test the model |
| Tablet | ~$10 | Entry tier with smaller context and a monthly Anlas allowance |
| Scroll | ~$15 | Larger context memory and more Anlas |
| Opus | ~$25 | Largest context, the most Anlas, and unlimited standard-size image generations |

For serious long-form writing, the context difference matters. Tablet is fine for short fiction and testing. Anyone writing serials or novels will probably want Scroll or Opus.

## Who NovelAI is for

- **Fiction writers** who want a drafting partner that matches their voice rather than an assistant that describes plot.
- **Serial and web-fiction authors** managing large casts and worlds through the Lorebook.
- **Interactive fiction fans** who enjoyed AI dungeon-style games and want better prose.
- **Writers in genres chatbots police heavily**, such as horror, crime, and adult romance.
- **Anime and illustration hobbyists** who want character art alongside their stories.

It's a poor fit if you mainly need marketing copy, research, or structured editing. For those, a general assistant like [Claude](/reviews/claude-review/) or ChatGPT is more flexible, and our [Claude 2 vs ChatGPT comparison](/compare/claude-2-vs-chatgpt-2023/) covers which one suits long documents better. If you want casual character chat rather than co-writing, [Character.AI](/reviews/character-ai-review-2023/) is the lighter, free option.

## How we'd use it

A workflow that worked well in our testing:

1. Outline the plot and characters in a general chatbot.
2. Move character sheets and setting notes into NovelAI's Lorebook, with specific trigger words.
3. Put the premise, POV, and tense in Memory. Keep it under a few hundred words.
4. Write the first 300 to 500 words of the chapter yourself to set the voice.
5. Generate in short bursts, retry freely, and edit hard. Treat outputs as raw material.
6. Update Author's Note at each scene change to steer pacing and mood.

The writers who get the most out of NovelAI treat it as a fast, sometimes brilliant, sometimes unreliable collaborator. They don't use it as a book generator.

## Verdict

NovelAI is the most writer-focused AI tool we've tested this year. Kayra produces fluid, voice-consistent prose, the Lorebook solves a real problem for long projects, and the privacy stance and light filtering make it a good home for fiction that mainstream chatbots won't touch.

The trade-off is effort. It won't take instructions, it forgets things outside its context window, and getting good output depends on learning its steering tools. If you write fiction regularly and are willing to spend that time, it's worth the subscription. If you want an assistant that does what you ask the first time, stick with a general chatbot.

**Rating: 4.1 / 5.** Best-in-class for co-writing fiction, and demanding to learn.
