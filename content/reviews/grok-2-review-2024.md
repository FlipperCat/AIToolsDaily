Earlier versions of Grok were mostly a novelty: a chatbot with an attitude, built into X, that lagged well behind ChatGPT and Claude on anything serious. Grok-2, which xAI released in beta in August 2024, is a different story. It's the first Grok I'd use for real work rather than as a toy.

I've used Grok-2 and Grok-2 mini daily for about a month, on writing, research, coding, and image generation. Here's what it does well, where it still falls short, and who should pay for it.

## What Grok-2 is

Grok-2 is xAI's second-generation large language model, offered in two sizes: **Grok-2** (the full model) and **Grok-2 mini** (smaller and faster). Both run inside X for paying subscribers. xAI has said that developer API access is coming, but for most people right now Grok means "the chatbot in the X app."

The headline changes from Grok-1.5 are much better reasoning and writing, plus built-in image generation. Grok-2 has also posted competitive results on public chatbot leaderboards, which matches my experience: for everyday tasks it's close to the leading models.

## Key features

### Real-time access to X

Grok's distinguishing feature is still its connection to X. Ask about something that happened an hour ago and Grok can pull recent posts and summarize what people are saying. For breaking news, sports, tech launches, and anything where the conversation on X is the source, that's useful.

The catch is that X posts aren't verified reporting. Grok sometimes summarizes speculation or jokes as if they were facts. Treat its "what's happening" answers as a quick read on the conversation, not as news.

### Image generation with FLUX.1

Grok-2 can generate images, powered by FLUX.1 from Black Forest Labs. Quality is strong, especially for photorealistic scenes, and it handles text in images better than many older generators.

The controversial part is the guardrails. Grok will produce images of real public figures and edgy content that tools like DALL-E 3 refuse. Some users see that as a feature. For businesses, it's a brand-safety risk worth thinking about before you use the output publicly.

### Fun mode and regular mode

Grok still offers a "fun" mode with a sarcastic, irreverent tone, alongside a regular mode that answers more plainly. Regular mode is the one to use for work. Fun mode is entertaining for a few minutes and then tiring.

### Grok-2 mini for speed

Mini answers quickly and is fine for simple lookups, rewrites, and short questions. I switched to full Grok-2 for anything that needed multiple steps of reasoning or more than a few paragraphs of output.

## How it performs

**Writing.** A clear improvement. Grok-2 produces clean, well-structured drafts for emails, summaries, and blog outlines. Its default voice is a little more casual and opinionated than ChatGPT's, which some people will prefer. For polished long-form prose, I still found [Claude](/reviews/claude-review/) more natural.

**Reasoning and analysis.** Good on multi-step problems and logic puzzles, with fewer obvious mistakes than earlier Grok versions. It still occasionally commits to a wrong answer confidently, like every model in this class.

**Coding.** Capable for common tasks: writing functions, explaining errors, translating between languages. It's not my first choice for larger refactors, but it's no longer embarrassing.

**Research.** The X integration helps with recency. Grok doesn't consistently cite sources the way a dedicated research tool does, though. If citations matter, [Perplexity](/perplexity-ai-review-2024/) is still the better choice.

## Pros

- A big quality jump; competitive with leading chatbots for everyday tasks.
- Real-time access to X conversations for breaking topics.
- Strong image generation built in at no extra cost.
- Fast responses, especially with Grok-2 mini.
- Included with an X subscription you might already pay for.

## Cons and limitations

- Only available inside X, so you need an X account and subscription.
- Real-time answers can repeat unverified claims from posts.
- Loose image guardrails create brand and ethical risks.
- Fewer productivity features than ChatGPT: limited file handling, no custom assistants, no mature voice mode.
- No public developer API yet, so you can't build on it today.
- Privacy questions about how X data is used for training are worth reading up on before you share sensitive information.

## Pricing

As of September 2024, approximately:

- **X Premium:** around $8/month, which includes access to Grok.
- **X Premium+:** around $16/month, with fewer ads and higher priority on X features.

Grok isn't sold separately, so the value depends on whether you already use X. If you're on X daily, Grok is a solid add-on. If you aren't, paying for a social media subscription to get a chatbot is a harder sell when ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro cost about $20/month and offer more complete products.

## Who it's for

- **Heavy X users** who want a capable assistant without another subscription.
- **Journalists, marketers, and social media managers** tracking what's trending in real time.
- **Creators** who want quick, high-quality image generation and accept the looser guardrails.

It's less suited to teams that need admin controls, document workflows, or an API, and to anyone who doesn't want their AI assistant tied to a social network.

## How it compares

Against ChatGPT, Grok-2 is now close on raw model quality but behind on product features. Our [ChatGPT review](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) covers why its ecosystem is still the default for most people. Against Microsoft's offering, see [Microsoft Copilot vs ChatGPT](/compare/microsoft-copilot-vs-chatgpt-2024/) for another bundled-assistant approach. Grok's unique advantage is the live X feed. Nobody else has that.

## Verdict

Grok-2 is the moment Grok became a serious chatbot. Writing, reasoning, and coding are now good enough for daily work, and image generation is a strong bonus. The X-only access, thin productivity features, and loose guardrails keep it from being a top pick for most people.

**Rating: 3.8/5.** Worth trying if you already pay for X Premium. Not a reason on its own to subscribe, unless real-time X data is central to your work.
