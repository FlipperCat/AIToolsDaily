Google spent the first half of 2023 watching Microsoft put a chatbot inside Bing. Its reply came at I/O in May: **Search Generative Experience (SGE)**, an opt-in experiment that puts AI-written summaries at the top of ordinary Google results.

We've had SGE turned on for daily searching for several weeks. Below is how it works, where it helps, where it gets in the way, and whether it's worth joining Search Labs.

## What Is Google SGE?

SGE is an experimental layer on top of Google Search. When you search for something that suits it, an **AI snapshot** appears above the usual ten blue links. The snapshot is a few paragraphs of generated text, and a row of source cards next to it links to the pages it drew from.

Google says SGE runs on several large language models, including PaLM 2 and its MUM model. Unlike [Bing Chat](/reviews/bing-chat-review-2023-search-with-an-opinion-problem/), it isn't trying to be a chat assistant you talk to. It's search first, with AI added where Google thinks it helps.

You get it through **Search Labs**, Google's new opt-in program for search experiments. It works in Chrome on desktop and in the Google app on Android and iOS. For now it only covers English searches in the US.

## Key Features

### AI snapshots

This is the main feature. Search "best way to clean cast iron after cooking eggs" and you get a short, organized answer before any links. Source cards sit beside the text so you can check where each claim came from.

For some searches the snapshot doesn't load automatically. You get a **Generate** button and decide yourself whether it's worth waiting for.

### Conversational follow-ups

Below the snapshot there's an **Ask a follow-up** box. It opens a conversation that keeps track of your earlier question, so "what about stainless steel?" works without repeating yourself. It's a lighter version of what Bing Chat and Bard do, and it stays tied to search results.

### Shopping snapshots

Product searches get a different layout. A query like "waterproof hiking boots for wide feet under $150" returns an overview of what to look for, a list of products with prices and reviews from Google's Shopping data, and links to retailers. For research-heavy purchases, this was the most useful part of SGE in our testing.

### Local and travel queries

Searches for restaurants, things to do, or trip planning pull in Maps data such as ratings, hours, and locations. The snapshots work best when the question has several parts, for example "kid-friendly brunch spot near downtown with outdoor seating."

### Ads stay in the picture

Search ads still run. They show up in their own labeled slots above or below the snapshot. Advertisers are watching that placement closely, and you should too: the line between "AI recommendation" and "paid placement" matters when you're about to buy something.

## What We Liked

- **It saves clicks on multi-part questions.** "Which is better for a family of four, Bryce Canyon or Arches, and when should we go?" gets a useful comparison in one place, where normal search means opening four or five tabs.
- **Sources are easy to find.** The source cards are more prominent than Bard's citations, which only appear sometimes, and roughly on par with Bing's numbered footnotes.
- **The rest of the page is unchanged.** Everything below the snapshot is normal Google. If the AI part is no help, you scroll past it.
- **Shopping research is much better.** Getting product considerations and a product list on the same page is a real improvement.
- **It's free and easy to undo.** Turning the experiment off takes one tap.

## Cons and Limitations

### It's slow

The snapshot takes a few seconds to generate, and on a quick lookup that's long enough to notice. If you just want a phone number or opening hours, the delay is annoying. We often started scrolling before the snapshot finished.

### Accuracy is uneven

Most snapshots were close. "Close" still isn't good enough for some searches. In our testing we saw:

- A product spec mixed up from two different model years.
- A summary that treated one forum post as if it were widely agreed.
- Outdated hours for a local business that the regular results below had right.

None of these was dramatic. All of them would have mattered if we hadn't checked. Treat a snapshot as a draft summary. For a habit worth building, see our guide to [using Bard for research](/tutorials/how-to-use-google-bard-for-research-2023-a-practical-workflow/), which applies to SGE too: check the sources, not just the summary.

### Pushes links down the page

The snapshot plus the source cards can take up the whole first screen on mobile. If you know which site you want, SGE gets in your way. That's also why publishers are worried about SGE: fewer people may click through to the pages it summarizes.

### Inconsistent coverage

SGE doesn't show up on every search. It holds back more on health and finance questions, and some searches just don't trigger it. That's a reasonable choice for sensitive subjects, but it means you can't predict when you'll get a snapshot.

### Access restrictions

You need a personal Google account. Work and school accounts weren't eligible when we signed up. Outside the US, you can't use it yet.

## How It Compares

| | Google SGE | Bing Chat | Google Bard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | Inside Google Search | Bing and Edge sidebar | Standalone chatbot |
| Best at | Quick overviews, shopping | Longer research chats | Drafting, brainstorming |
| Citations | Source cards beside answer | Numbered footnotes | Sometimes |
| Normal results on the page | Yes, below the snapshot | Separate tab | No |
| Cost (as of July 2023) | Free (opt-in) | Free | Free |

Bing Chat is still better for long research conversations, and Bard is better for writing tasks. For a privacy-minded alternative, [You.com](/reviews/you.com-review-2023-the-ai-search-engine-that-bundles-everything/) has been mixing chat and search for a while. SGE's advantage is that it lives where most people already search.

## Pricing

As of July 2023, SGE is **free**. You opt in through Search Labs, and there's no paid tier. Google hasn't said whether SGE will stay free, roll out to everyone, or change a lot before any official launch. Since it's labeled an experiment, the features we describe here could change.

## Who It's For

**Turn it on if you:**
- Do a lot of comparison research (products, travel, "X vs Y" questions).
- Like Bing Chat's summaries but don't want to leave Google.
- Want to see how search might change, especially if you work in SEO or publishing.

**Skip it if you:**
- Mostly do quick lookups and care about speed.
- Usually know which site you're trying to reach.
- Aren't willing to check sources. The snapshots aren't reliable enough to take on faith.

## Verdict

Google SGE is a careful but useful first step toward AI in Google Search. The snapshots save real time on complicated or multi-part questions, and the shopping layout is the best feature in the experiment. It's held back by slow generation, accuracy that's good but not reliable, and a layout that pushes regular results further down.

Since it's free and easy to switch off, most US searchers should try it for a week. Pay attention to which searches it actually helps with. For us, it was product research and trip planning, not everyday lookups. If you mostly want an AI assistant to have a longer conversation with, [Claude](/reviews/claude-ai-review-2026-the-best-ai-for-writing/) or Bing Chat will do more. If you want normal Google results with a summary on top, SGE is worth the opt-in.

**Rating: 3.5/5.** A promising experiment that needs to get faster and more reliable.
