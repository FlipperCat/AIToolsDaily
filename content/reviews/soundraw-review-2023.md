If you make videos, you have this problem: every track in the free music library has been used by nine thousand other creators, and the good stock libraries cost more per year than your editing software. Soundraw's pitch is that you skip the library entirely — describe the mood you want, and it generates an original instrumental you can cut to length.

I spent a couple of weeks using it as the sole music source for video projects. It's a legitimately useful tool with a narrow, honest scope, and a licensing model you need to read carefully.

## What Soundraw Is

Soundraw is an AI music generator built for content creators rather than musicians. You don't type a text prompt or upload a reference track. Instead you choose from structured options — mood, genre, theme, length, tempo — and it returns a batch of complete instrumental tracks that match. Everything is generated, so nothing you download is shared with the rest of the internet's YouTube channels.

The tool has been around since 2020 and comes from a Japanese team, but 2023 is when this category stopped being a novelty. Free stock music got worse as it got more crowded, and generated music got good enough that most viewers won't notice the difference in a background bed.

## Key Features

**Structured generation instead of prompts.** You pick "Happy," "Epic," or "Melancholic," add a genre like Hip Hop or Acoustic, set a length of 30 seconds to five minutes, and hit generate. It returns a page of options in seconds. The lack of a free-text prompt sounds limiting, and it is — but it also means you never fight the tool for a usable result. The floor is high even when the ceiling is low.

**The section editor.** This is the feature worth paying for. Every track is displayed as blocks along a timeline, and you can raise or lower the energy of each block independently — quiet intro, building middle, big finish, quiet outro. If your edit needs the music to drop out at 0:42 and swell at 1:10, you can actually make that happen instead of hunting for a track that coincidentally does it. For anyone cutting video, this solves the real problem, which was never "find music" but "find music that fits *this* edit."

**Length and tempo control.** Set the exact duration you need and the arrangement is generated to fit it, with a proper ending rather than a fade-out. Anyone who has spent twenty minutes crossfading a stock track to end cleanly at 1:47 will appreciate this immediately.

**Instrument and pro editing.** Higher-level editing lets you adjust which instruments are prominent and tweak the arrangement. It's shallower than a DAW, deliberately — this is not a production environment, it's a fitting room.

**Straightforward downloads.** Export as MP3 or WAV. No stems, which is a real limitation if you wanted to remix.

## Pros

- **Genuinely fast.** From opening the site to a downloaded, correctly-timed track is a few minutes.
- **Nothing sounds overused,** because nothing is shared. That alone justifies it for channels with an established audience.
- **The section editor is unmatched** by any stock library, which by definition can't restructure a recording for you.
- **Reliable licensing story** for commercial video, ads, and client work, without per-project fees.
- **No music theory required.** The interface is built for editors, not composers.

## Cons and Limitations

- **Instrumental only.** No vocals, no lyrics. For a large amount of content this is fine; if you wanted a song, this isn't it.
- **The output is competent, not memorable.** It's background music, and it sounds like background music — pleasant, functional, faintly generic. It will never be the reason someone watches your video. Genres with a lot of human feel, particularly jazz and anything acoustic and intimate, come out weakest.
- **Recognizable house sound.** Generate enough tracks in one mood and you start hearing the same chord movements and drum patterns recur. Heavy users will notice.
- **The license depends on your subscription.** Broadly, tracks downloaded while subscribed remain usable, but the details matter and the terms are the kind of thing that gets revised. Read the current license page before you build a whole channel on it — that advice applies to any AI tool right now, not just this one.
- **No stems and no MIDI export,** so you can't take a generated idea into a DAW and finish it properly.

## Pricing

As of March 2023, pricing is simple, and these figures are approximate:

- **Free:** unlimited generation and preview, no downloads. Useful for evaluating whether the sound suits you.
- **Creator/Personal:** roughly $17–20 per month depending on whether you pay annually or monthly, with unlimited downloads and a license covering monetized content.
- **Higher tiers and enterprise:** broader licensing for agencies, larger teams, and use in commercials or apps.

The comparison worth making is against a stock music subscription. Those typically run in a similar range but give you professionally recorded tracks that other creators also use. Soundraw trades a little polish for exclusivity and editability. Which side of that trade you want depends entirely on how tight your edits are.

## Who It's For

**Good fit:** YouTubers and short-form creators publishing on a schedule, video editors doing client work, podcasters who need intros and transitions, indie game developers needing loopable background beds, and social media managers producing volume.

**Poor fit:** musicians looking for a composition tool — there's no stem export and no real production depth. Also a poor fit for anyone whose project needs music with a distinct point of view. A generated bed can support a scene; it won't carry one. And if you need vocals, this simply isn't the category.

## Verdict

Soundraw does one job and does it well: producing unlimited, unique, correctly-timed instrumental music for people whose actual job is something else. The section editor is the standout — it's the only feature here that a stock library fundamentally can't replicate.

Set your expectations at "reliable background music," not "great music," and it's an easy recommendation for anyone publishing video regularly. If you're only making a few videos a year, the free tier plus a careful read of what stock libraries offer will serve you better than a subscription.
