Recording a podcast episode is the fun part. What comes after is a slog: transcripts, show notes, timestamps, a title that'll get clicks, three LinkedIn posts, a newsletter blurb, and a pull quote for Instagram. For a weekly show, that post-production writing can easily take longer than the recording itself.

**Castmagic** exists to remove that slog. You upload an episode, and a few minutes later you have a transcript and a stack of ready-to-edit content. We ran a dozen episodes through it, including interviews, solo episodes, and a recorded webinar, to see whether it really earns a place in a creator's stack.

## What it is

Castmagic is an AI content repurposing tool for spoken-word media. You upload audio or video, or paste a link, and it does two things:

1. **Transcribes** the recording with speaker labels.
2. **Generates content assets** from the transcript using large language models.

It is not an audio editor. If you need to cut, clean up, or level your audio, you still need something like [Descript](/reviews/04-descript-review/), [Podcastle](/reviews/podcastle-review-2023/), or [Adobe Podcast Enhance](/reviews/adobe-podcast-enhance-review-2023/). Castmagic picks up after the episode is done.

## Key features

**Automatic asset generation.** Every upload produces a default bundle: show notes, episode descriptions, title suggestions, timestamps or chapters, key takeaways, notable quotes, social posts for different platforms, a newsletter draft, and a blog-style summary. You don't have to prompt for any of it.

**Transcripts with speaker detection.** Transcripts come back with speakers separated and are exportable. On clean, two-person interview audio, accuracy was good enough that we only fixed names and some jargon.

**Custom prompts.** You can write your own prompts and save them as reusable templates. This is the feature that makes Castmagic useful long-term. Once you've built a prompt that writes show notes in your format and your voice, it runs automatically on every future upload.

**Magic Chat.** A chat interface that lets you ask questions about an episode or pull specific material: "What did the guest say about pricing?" or "Write a thread from the section on hiring." It's handy for digging out specific moments without scrubbing through audio.

**Library and organization.** Every episode's transcript and assets are stored together, so your back catalog becomes a searchable archive of content you can repurpose later.

**Import and integrations.** You can upload files directly or import from cloud links, and automation integrations let you connect it to the rest of your publishing workflow.

## Pros

- **Huge time savings on routine writing.** Show notes, timestamps, and descriptions that used to take an hour or more took us about 15 minutes of editing.
- **Good default output.** Takeaways and quote extraction were consistently useful, often surfacing lines we'd have missed.
- **Custom prompts make it scale.** A weekly show can lock in a consistent format and voice without rewriting prompts each time.
- **Works beyond podcasts.** Webinars, YouTube interviews, coaching calls, and internal talks all repurposed well.
- **Easy to use.** Upload, wait, edit. There's essentially no learning curve for the defaults.

## Cons and limitations

- **Transcription struggles with messy audio.** Crosstalk, heavy accents, and poor mics led to more errors, and those errors carry into every generated asset. Clean audio first.
- **Social posts need a voice pass.** The posts are structurally fine but often generic. Expect to rewrite hooks and add your own personality, especially for LinkedIn and X.
- **Hour-based pricing punishes long shows.** If your episodes run 90+ minutes or you publish several times a week, you'll hit plan limits faster than you expect.
- **Occasional factual drift.** Summaries sometimes overstate what a guest said or merge two points. Spot-check takeaways and quotes against the transcript before publishing.
- **Overlap with tools you may already pay for.** If you already transcribe in Descript or [Otter](/reviews/16-otter-ai-review/) and are comfortable with ChatGPT, you can rebuild much of this workflow yourself.

## Pricing

Pricing is approximate as of January 2024 and has changed several times, so confirm on Castmagic's site.

- **Entry plans**: roughly the low-to-mid $20s per month, covering a limited number of upload hours each month. Suitable for a single weekly show of normal length.
- **Mid tiers**: higher monthly hour allowances and more features, aimed at creators with multiple shows or longer episodes.
- **Business and agency tiers**: priced substantially higher, with more hours, team seats, and support for producing content across several clients.

Annual billing brings the effective monthly cost down. A free trial is typically available, and it's worth running your three most representative episodes through it before choosing a tier.

How to judge the value: estimate how many hours a month you or a freelancer currently spend writing post-production content. If Castmagic saves even three or four of those hours, the entry tier pays for itself quickly. If you only publish once a month, the math is weaker.

## Castmagic vs doing it yourself with ChatGPT

This is the real competitor. You can export a transcript from almost any tool, paste it into ChatGPT, and ask for show notes and social posts. With good prompts, the results can be comparable.

The differences:

- **Length limits.** Long transcripts often have to be split up for ChatGPT, which takes time and can lose context. Castmagic handles full episodes in one go.
- **Automation.** Castmagic generates everything on upload. DIY means repeating the same prompts every week.
- **Organization.** Castmagic keeps each episode's assets together. DIY means managing documents and chat histories yourself.

If you publish occasionally and enjoy prompting, DIY is fine and cheaper. If you publish weekly or manage multiple shows, the automation is worth paying for. ## Who it's for

**Good fit:**

- Weekly or more frequent podcasters who handle their own marketing
- Podcast production agencies managing several client shows
- Coaches, consultants, and educators who record webinars or long-form talks
- Marketing teams repurposing customer interviews and video content
- YouTubers with interview or talking-head formats

**Probably not worth it:**

- Monthly publishers with low content volume
- Shows with very long episodes on a tight budget
- Creators who need audio editing, not writing (get an editor first)
- People happy with a transcript plus ChatGPT and a few saved prompts

## Verdict

Castmagic does one job and does it well: it turns finished recordings into a full set of written content fast enough to change how much repurposing you actually do. The default outputs are a solid starting point, and custom prompts are what make it stick for regular publishers.

It doesn't replace editing. You still need to check transcripts, add personality to social posts, and verify that summaries reflect what was really said. And for low-volume creators, a transcript and ChatGPT will get most of the way for less money.

But if post-production writing is the reason your episodes get published late, or your back catalog sits untouched because repurposing feels like a second job, Castmagic is one of the most practical AI tools a podcaster can add right now. Start with the trial, run your real episodes through it, and measure the hours saved.
