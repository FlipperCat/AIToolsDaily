## Superhuman Review (2025): Is a $30 Email Client Still Worth It?

Superhuman has been asking people to pay premium subscription money for email since 2017, which by 2025 makes it either a remarkable business or a remarkable act of stubbornness. The pitch has shifted over the years — first it was raw speed, then keyboard mastery, and now it is AI. I have used it as my primary inbox for about four months on a Gmail account that receives somewhere north of 150 messages a day.

The verdict is not complicated, but it is conditional, and most reviews skip the condition.

## What Is Superhuman?

Superhuman is a paid email client that sits on top of Gmail or Outlook. Your mail still lives with your provider; Superhuman is a faster, keyboard-driven interface with an AI layer bolted on top of your own archive.

Everything in it is built around one premise: touching the mouse is the enemy. Every action has a shortcut, the command bar (Cmd+K) reaches everything, and the interface is engineered so nothing ever makes you wait. That last part matters more than it sounds — Gmail's small hesitations are individually trivial and collectively exhausting.

## Key Features

**Speed as a design principle**
Superhuman feels instant. Navigating, opening, archiving, searching — there is no perceptible delay anywhere. After a few weeks in it, going back to Gmail in a browser tab feels like wading. This is still the single strongest thing about the product and the hardest to convey in a feature list.

**Split Inbox**
You define rules that split incoming mail into separate lanes — Important, Team, Calendar, News, whatever you build. Instead of one undifferentiated pile you get several small piles you can clear at different times. It is the most useful organizational idea in the app and, notably, one you can partially replicate with Gmail's multiple inboxes if you are willing to fiddle.

**Ask AI**
Natural-language search across your mail: "what did the Northwind team say about the launch date?" It answers with a synthesized response and links to the underlying messages. This works well and is genuinely hard to do without an AI layer — Gmail's search requires you to already know the keyword. Occasionally it misses a thread that a keyword search finds, so it supplements rather than replaces normal search.

**AI drafting and Instant Reply**
Superhuman drafts replies in your voice, having learned from your sent mail. On short transactional messages — scheduling, acknowledgments, quick answers — the drafts are usable with minor edits. On anything with nuance, they are a starting point. The voice matching is better than generic tools like [using ChatGPT for email](/tutorials/how-to-use-chatgpt-email-writing/), because it has context you would otherwise have to paste in.

**Auto Labels**
AI-driven categorization that sorts mail into your Split Inbox lanes without hand-written rules. Accuracy was good after a week or two of corrections. This is the feature that made the Split Inbox actually stick for me, since maintaining manual rules is where most people abandon inbox systems.

**Snippets, reminders, and follow-ups**
Text expansion for phrases you send constantly, plus "remind me if no reply in 3 days," which surfaces the thread back at the top of the inbox. Both are old features and both remain among the most practically useful things in the app.

**Read statuses**
You can see whether and when recipients opened your email. Useful for sales, mildly invasive as a default, and easy to disable. Worth knowing it exists before you have opinions about it.

## Pros

- **The speed is real** and it compounds across hundreds of daily interactions.
- **Ask AI genuinely finds things** you could not have found by keyword.
- **Voice-matched drafts** beat generic AI writing tools for routine replies.
- **Split Inbox plus Auto Labels** is the first inbox-organization system I have stuck with past month two.
- **Excellent onboarding.** The guided setup session is unusual and effective — you actually learn the shortcuts instead of ignoring them.
- **Mobile apps are fast**, which is rare in this category.

## Cons and Limitations

- **The price.** Roughly $30 per user per month is a lot for a client wrapped around free email, and no feature here is individually irreplaceable.
- **Gmail and Outlook only.** No IMAP, no Fastmail, no Proton.
- **Sparse view options.** Superhuman's opinionated design is mostly a strength, but if you want a preview pane, dense list, or heavy customization, you will chafe.
- **AI drafts flatten your voice on longer messages.** Fine for "sounds good, Thursday works," noticeably generic for anything you actually care about.
- **The value curve is steep.** At low volume, most of what you are paying for never activates.
- **Search occasionally trails Gmail's** on exact-phrase lookups, oddly enough.

## Pricing

As of April 2025, Superhuman runs roughly $25–$35 per user per month depending on tier and whether you pay annually, with business and enterprise tiers adding team features and admin controls. There is a trial, and the guided onboarding happens during it.

Treat those figures as approximate — Superhuman has restructured pricing more than once and the tier names have moved around. Check the current page before committing a team.

## Who It's For

**Worth it if:** you receive 100+ emails a day; email is a primary channel for your actual job (sales, recruiting, founders, client services, executive assistants); you already use keyboard shortcuts everywhere else and resent tools that make you reach for the mouse; your time is billed at a rate where twenty minutes a day pays for it several times over.

**Skip it if:** your inbox is quiet; you are on a personal budget where $360 a year is a real decision; you use a provider other than Gmail or Outlook; you have never once been frustrated by Gmail's speed. That last one is a genuinely good test — if Gmail has never annoyed you, Superhuman is solving a problem you do not have.

## Verdict

Superhuman remains the best email client I have used, and it remains hard to recommend universally. Nothing in it is magic. What it does is remove friction from an activity you perform hundreds of times a day, and friction removed at that frequency turns into real time.

The AI additions have made the value proposition more defensible than it was when the pitch was pure speed. Ask AI and Auto Labels do things that are genuinely difficult to replicate with free tools and an afternoon of configuration.

But the math only works above a volume threshold. Below it, spend the afternoon on Gmail shortcuts and filters instead — our guide to [managing email with AI](/ai-for-email-management/) covers the free approach — and put the $360 somewhere it does more work. Above it, take the trial seriously and see whether the speed changes how you feel about your inbox. For a lot of heavy email users, it does.
