Speechify is built around one simple promise: anything you can read, you can listen to instead. Paste an article, import a PDF, snap a photo of a page, or open a web page, and Speechify turns it into spoken audio with AI voices you can speed up well past normal talking pace. It's marketed heavily at students, busy professionals, and people with dyslexia or other reading challenges.

I spent a couple of weeks pushing articles, work documents, and a few book chapters through it. Here's where Speechify genuinely helps, where it falls short, and whether it's worth paying for as of mid-2023.

## What Speechify Is

Speechify is a **text-to-speech (TTS) reader** available as a browser extension, an iOS and Android app, and a web app. You feed it text from almost any source and it reads aloud using either basic voices (free) or more natural-sounding AI voices (premium).

The core use case is consumption. Instead of reading a long report at your desk, you listen to it on a walk or commute. Instead of skimming a dense PDF, you let it play at 1.5x while you follow along. For people who absorb information better by ear, or who simply have eye strain, that shift is the whole point.

There's also a creator-facing side — a voice-over studio for generating spoken audio from scripts — but the heart of Speechify is the personal reading assistant.

## Key Features

- **Read almost anything.** Web pages, Google Docs, PDFs, emails, and pasted text all work. The browser extension reads the page you're on with a click.
- **Document and photo scanning.** Import documents or use OCR to scan printed pages and physical books into listenable audio.
- **Adjustable speed.** You can crank playback speed far beyond normal speech once your ears adjust, which is how power users get through huge reading loads fast.
- **Premium AI voices.** A library of more natural voices across multiple languages, a clear step up from the default robotic options.
- **Multi-language support.** Reads text in a range of languages, useful for learners and multilingual readers.
- **Cross-device sync.** Start on your laptop, continue on your phone. Your library and progress follow you.

## Pros

**It removes friction from reading.** The biggest win is behavioral. When listening is one click away, you actually get through the articles and documents that would otherwise pile up unread. That alone changes how much you consume.

**The premium voices are easy to live with.** They're natural enough that you forget you're listening to TTS within a minute or two. For long sessions — a full article or a book chapter — that comfort matters more than perfection on any single sentence.

**Speed listening is a genuine productivity unlock.** After a few days your brain adapts to faster playback, and you can move through reading material at a pace that would be impossible by eye. Students with heavy reading loads benefit the most.

**Accessibility is real, not a marketing line.** For people with dyslexia, ADHD, or visual fatigue, having text read aloud with synchronized highlighting is a meaningful accommodation, not a gimmick.

**It handles messy inputs.** PDFs and scanned pages — usually the hardest formats — are imported and read without much fuss.

## Cons and Limitations

**The best parts are paywalled.** The free tier's voices are noticeably more robotic, and the features most people actually want are Premium-only. Judge Speechify by the paid experience, because that's what you'll end up using.

**Voices still stumble on edge cases.** Unusual names, acronyms, code, and heavy punctuation can produce awkward pronunciation or pacing. For straightforward prose it's great; for technical text it's hit or miss.

**It's a consumption tool, not a creation powerhouse.** If your goal is producing polished voiceovers for videos or podcasts, dedicated voice-generation tools like [ElevenLabs](/elevenlabs-review-ai-voice-generation/) and [Murf](/compare/elevenlabs-vs-murf/) are built more specifically for that and give you finer control.

**Pricing is annual-first.** The headline value comes from the yearly plan, which is a real commitment if you're not sure you'll keep the habit.

**Highlighting and navigation can lag on huge files.** Very long documents sometimes feel sluggish to scrub through compared with short articles.

## Pricing

Pricing is approximate and changes over time, so check the current plans, but **as of mid-2023** the structure looks like:

- **Free:** basic voices, limited features, fine for trying it out.
- **Premium:** roughly **$139/year** (about **$11.58/month** when paid annually), unlocking the natural AI voices, higher speeds, scanning, and unlimited use.

There are occasional discounts, especially for students, so it's worth checking for an education rate. The free tier is enough to decide whether the workflow fits you before you pay.

## Who It's For

Speechify makes the most sense if you:

- Have a heavy reading load — students, researchers, lawyers, or anyone drowning in documents.
- Prefer to consume content while moving, commuting, or resting your eyes.
- Have dyslexia, ADHD, or visual fatigue and benefit from audio plus highlighting.
- Want to clear a backlog of saved articles you never get around to reading.

It's a weaker fit if you mainly need to *create* audio content rather than consume it, or if you only occasionally read long-form text and won't build a daily habit. ## Verdict

Speechify does the unglamorous thing well: it gets text into your ears with minimal friction and voices that are pleasant enough to listen to for hours. For students, heavy readers, and anyone with accessibility needs, it can quietly change how much you actually get through in a week.

The catch is that the experience worth paying for is the Premium one, and the value depends entirely on whether you build the listening habit. Try the free tier first, push a few real documents and a long article through it, and see if you naturally reach for it on your next commute. If you do, the annual plan pays for itself in reclaimed time. If listening just isn't how your brain works, no amount of voice quality will change that — and that's worth finding out before you subscribe for a year.
