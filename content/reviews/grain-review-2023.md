Meeting recorders all promise the same thing: never take notes again. Grain makes a slightly different promise. It wants to turn the best 40 seconds of an hour-long customer call into something your whole team will actually watch.

That's a narrower goal than "transcribe everything," and it shapes the whole product. Grain records your meetings, generates a transcript and AI notes, and then makes it very easy to cut highlights, stitch them together, and share them in Slack, Notion, or your CRM.

We used Grain for about a month across sales calls, user interviews, and internal syncs. Here's what works, what doesn't, and who should pay for it.

## What Grain Is

Grain is a meeting recorder with a web app attached. You connect your calendar and video platform, and Grain's notetaker joins your scheduled Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams calls. When the meeting ends, you get:

- A video recording with a synced, speaker-labeled transcript
- AI-generated meeting notes based on a template you choose
- A workspace where you can search, clip, and share moments

Grain started as a Zoom-focused tool for clipping and sharing video. The AI layer, which uses large language models to write summaries and structured notes, arrived more recently and is still maturing. That history shows: the clipping tools feel polished, while the AI notes are useful but not yet best-in-class.

## Key Features

### Highlight-to-clip

This is Grain's signature feature. You select text in the transcript, and Grain creates a video clip of exactly that moment. You can trim it, title it, and share a link. Viewers don't need a Grain account to watch shared clips (depending on your workspace settings).

For product and research teams, this changes how feedback travels. Instead of writing "customers find onboarding confusing" in a doc, you post three 30-second clips of customers saying it themselves. That's far more persuasive.

### Stories (highlight reels)

Grain lets you combine clips from different meetings into a single reel. It's a lightweight way to build a "voice of the customer" compilation for a quarterly review or a sales enablement library. The editing is basic, with no transitions or fancy effects, but it's fast.

### AI meeting notes

After each call, Grain produces notes from a template. Templates cover common call types like sales discovery, user interviews, and general meetings. You can customize the sections you want filled in, such as pain points, next steps, objections, or decisions.

In our testing, the notes were accurate on the big picture and occasionally fuzzy on specifics like numbers, names, and who agreed to what. They're a strong first draft. Read them before you send them to a client.

### Integrations

Grain connects to Slack, Notion, HubSpot, Salesforce, and several product and research tools. The CRM integrations are the main reason sales teams pick it: notes and clips can land on the right contact or deal record without a rep doing manual data entry. Most of these integrations sit on the higher-priced tier.

### Search and libraries

You can search across transcripts and organize recordings and clips into collections. Search works well for keywords. It's not as strong as dedicated conversation-intelligence platforms at surfacing trends across hundreds of calls.

## Pros

- **Clipping is genuinely fast.** Highlighting transcript text to create a clip is the best implementation of this idea we've used.
- **Built for sharing.** Clips and reels are easy to drop into Slack or a doc, which gets customer evidence in front of people who never attend calls.
- **Useful note templates.** Sales and research templates give you structured notes instead of a generic paragraph summary.
- **CRM sync on the business tier.** Removes a real chunk of post-call admin for sales reps.
- **Clean, simple interface.** New team members get the hang of it in one meeting.

## Cons and Limitations

- **Bot in the room.** Like most recorders, Grain joins as a participant. Some clients find that off-putting, and some IT teams block it. If that's an issue, a browser-extension approach like [Tactiq](/reviews/tactiq-review-2023-meeting-transcripts-without-a-bot-in-the-room/) avoids the visible bot.
- **AI notes still need review.** Summaries sometimes miss nuance or misattribute action items. They save time, but they aren't hands-off.
- **Transcript accuracy varies.** Clear audio with one speaker at a time is fine. Crosstalk, heavy accents, and industry jargon cause more errors, as with every tool in this category.
- **Key features are on the pricier plan.** CRM integrations and some admin controls require the business tier, which adds up across a sales team.
- **Not a full conversation-intelligence platform.** If you need deal-risk scoring, talk-ratio analytics across a whole sales org, or coaching scorecards, enterprise tools like Gong are built for that. Grain is lighter and cheaper.

## Pricing (approximate, as of July 2023)

Grain's pricing has a free tier plus paid per-seat plans. As of July 2023, it looked roughly like this:

| Plan | Approx. price | Best for |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Free | $0 | Trying Grain on a limited number of meetings |
| Starter | ~$15–20 per seat/month | Individuals and small teams who want unlimited recording and clipping |
| Business | ~$30–40 per seat/month | Sales and CS teams that need CRM sync and admin controls |

Annual billing usually lowers the per-seat cost. Prices and limits change often in this category, so check Grain's pricing page before you commit.

## How It Compares

The meeting-assistant market got crowded fast in 2023. A quick orientation:

- **Otter.ai** is the default choice for searchable transcripts and live notes. It's stronger as an archive and less focused on clips. Our [Otter vs Rev comparison](/compare/otter-vs-rev-2023/) covers where Otter fits.
- **[Supernormal](/reviews/supernormal-review-2023/)** focuses on AI-written notes and templates with a cleaner, notes-first output. It doesn't match Grain's clipping workflow.
- **Fireflies.ai** leans into transcription plus a wide integration list, and it's a good pick if you want everything logged automatically.
- **Gong and Chorus** are the enterprise end: deep analytics and coaching, priced for large sales orgs.

## Who Grain Is For

**Good fit:**

- **Product managers and UX researchers** who want to share customer quotes as video, not paraphrase
- **Sales teams** that want call notes pushed into HubSpot or Salesforce automatically
- **Customer success teams** building a library of feedback and objection-handling examples
- **Founders** doing lots of discovery calls who need to show investors or teammates what customers actually said

**Not a great fit:**

- People who mostly want a cheap, searchable transcript archive
- Teams whose clients won't accept a recording bot on calls
- Large sales orgs that need full conversation analytics and coaching workflows

## Tips If You Try It

1. **Start with one call type.** Set up a template for discovery calls or user interviews first, then expand.
2. **Clip during the review, not the call.** Stay present in the meeting. Spend five minutes afterward highlighting the best moments.
3. **Create a shared clips channel.** A Slack channel where the team posts customer clips is where Grain pays for itself.
4. **Announce the recorder.** A quick "I'm recording so I can focus on the conversation" at the start avoids awkwardness.
5. **Edit AI notes before they hit the CRM.** A 60-second review prevents wrong next steps from becoming the official record.

## Verdict

Grain is the best tool we've tried for turning meetings into shareable evidence. The highlight-to-clip workflow is fast, the sharing is frictionless, and the CRM integrations on the business plan save sales reps real time.

It's less compelling as a general-purpose note taker. The AI notes are solid but not standout, and if you just want a transcript of every meeting, cheaper or more archive-focused tools will serve you better.

**Our take:** if your team's problem is "nobody outside the call hears what customers actually say," Grain solves it well and is worth the subscription. If your problem is "I hate typing notes," start with a notes-first tool and keep Grain in mind for later.
