Craiyon is the tool a lot of people first used to make AI images. In mid-2022 it was still called **DALL·E mini**, and its blurry, slightly cursed 3x3 grids were all over social media. A year later the field looks very different. [Midjourney v5](/reviews/midjourney-v5-review-2023/) produces near-photographic images, [DALL·E 2](/reviews/dall-e-2-review-2023/) is a polished paid product, and Stable Diffusion runs on home PCs.

So is there still a reason to open Craiyon in June 2023? We used it alongside the bigger names for a few weeks to find out.

## What Is Craiyon?

Craiyon is a free, browser-based text-to-image generator. You type a prompt, wait, and get a grid of image options. Click one to see it larger, then download, share, or upscale it.

The project started as an open-source research effort inspired by OpenAI's original DALL·E paper. It was renamed Craiyon partly to make clear it has nothing to do with OpenAI. Since then the team has updated the model and added a commercial layer on top: ads on the free tier and optional paid plans.

The pitch is simple. You don't need an account, a credit card, a Discord server, or a GPU. You only need a browser.

## Key Features

### Free, no-login generation

This is still Craiyon's biggest advantage. Most competitors now ask for something first: Midjourney needs a paid subscription and Discord, DALL·E 2 uses paid credits, and DreamStudio needs an account and credits. With Craiyon you type a prompt and press Draw.

### Multiple images per prompt

Each prompt returns a grid of variations rather than one image. That suits brainstorming: you can scan the batch for one usable composition and ignore the rest.

### Style picker and negative words

Recent updates added a simple style selector (art, drawing, photo, or none) and a field for words you want the model to avoid. Neither is as fine-grained as Stable Diffusion's negative prompts, but both help. Picking "drawing" for a concept sketch gives noticeably cleaner results than leaving it on default.

### Upscaling

Raw Craiyon images are small. There is a built-in upscale option, and it helps for screen use, but don't expect print-quality detail from it.

### Prompt suggestions and community gallery

Craiyon shows example prompts and a public stream of recent generations. Beginners get a feel for what the model is good at without reading a prompt guide.

## Image Quality: Where It Lands in 2023

Here's where expectations need adjusting. Next to Midjourney v5 or DALL·E 2, Craiyon looks a generation behind, because it is.

**What it does reasonably well:**
- Painterly and illustrated styles (watercolor, oil painting, pencil sketch)
- Landscapes, skies, and moody environments
- Simple objects and food
- Loose concept art where "roughly right" is the goal
- Meme-style and absurd prompts, which is how it got famous

**What it still struggles with:**
- Faces: often smeared or asymmetrical, especially in "photo" mode
- Hands and fingers: frequently wrong
- Text inside images: essentially unusable
- Complex scenes with several subjects interacting
- Fine detail at anything above thumbnail size

A prompt like "a lighthouse on a cliff at sunset, oil painting" gives you several usable options. "A business team shaking hands in a modern office, photo" gives you an uncanny-valley meeting you won't want on your website.

## Pros

- **Free with no account required.** That's the lowest barrier to entry of any image generator we've tested.
- **Fast to start.** It works on any device with a browser, including school or work machines where you can't install anything.
- **Good for ideation.** A grid of rough options is useful for mood boards and early creative direction.
- **Simple interface.** There are no parameters to learn and no Discord commands.
- **Paid plans are modest.** If you use it often, the ad-free tier costs less than most competitors.

## Cons and Limitations

- **Quality gap.** It's well behind Midjourney, DALL·E 2, and a tuned Stable Diffusion setup.
- **Ads and waits on the free tier.** Generation can take a minute or more at busy times, with ads on the page while you wait.
- **Watermark on free images.** It's small, but you'll want to crop or pay before using images publicly.
- **Low native resolution.** Upscaling helps, but output still isn't suitable for print or large hero images.
- **Weak prompt control.** You get none of the fine control that Stable Diffusion users rely on, such as seeds, aspect ratios, inpainting, or ControlNet-style guidance.
- **Privacy.** Free generations can appear in public galleries. Don't type anything confidential into a prompt.

## Pricing (approximate, as of June 2023)

- **Free:** Unlimited use with ads, slower queue, and watermarked images.
- **Entry paid tier:** Roughly $5–6 per month. Removes ads and watermarks, speeds up generation, and makes images private.
- **Professional tier:** Roughly $20 per month, aimed at heavier users who want faster generation and more images.

Discounts usually apply if you pay annually. Prices and plan names for AI tools change often, so check Craiyon's pricing page before you subscribe.

To compare: Midjourney's cheapest plan costs more and needs Discord, and DALL·E 2 charges per batch of credits. If cost is your main constraint, Craiyon's free tier and Microsoft's Bing Image Creator are the two options to try first.

## Who It's For

**Good fit:**
- Students, teachers, and hobbyists who want to try AI images at zero cost
- Writers and game masters who need quick visual references for characters or settings
- Anyone making memes, joke images, or rough mood boards
- People on locked-down computers or Chromebooks who can't install software

**Not a good fit:**
- Marketers or designers who need images ready to publish
- Anyone who needs realistic people, readable text, or product shots
- Users who want precise control over composition and style

## Verdict

Craiyon is no longer a serious competitor on image quality, and it doesn't try to be. Its value in 2023 is access: it's the easiest way to go from an idea to a rough picture without paying or signing up.

We'd use it for brainstorming, classroom demos, and fun, and switch to something stronger once an image actually needs to look good. If you're curious about AI art and don't want to commit money or set anything up, start here. If you already know you need polished images, skip Craiyon and go straight to Midjourney, DALL·E 2, or Stable Diffusion.

**Rating: 3/5.** Free and easy to use, but its image quality is a generation behind the leaders.
