For six months, using ChatGPT on a phone meant keeping a browser tab open. The alternative was worse: one of the dozens of lookalike apps in the App Store, many charging weekly subscriptions for access to someone else's model.

On May 18, OpenAI shipped an official ChatGPT app for iPhone. It's free, it syncs with your account, and it shot up the App Store charts almost immediately.

A few weeks in, the picture is clear. This is the best way to use ChatGPT on a phone. It is also a deliberately trimmed-down version of what you get on the web, and whether that matters depends on how you use the tool.

## What It Is

The ChatGPT app is the official iOS client for OpenAI's chatbot. You sign in with the same account you use on the web, and your conversations sync in both directions.

The basics:

- **Free to download, no ads.**
- **Requires iOS 16.1 or later.**
- **Free accounts get GPT-3.5.** ChatGPT Plus subscribers can switch to GPT-4.
- **Launched in the US first**, then expanded to dozens more countries over the following weeks.
- **A recent update added a proper iPad layout** along with Siri and Shortcuts integration.

If you're still deciding whether the chatbot itself is worth your time, our comparison of [ChatGPT Plus vs Free](/compare/chatgpt-plus-vs-free-2023-is-the-20-upgrade-worth-it-yet/) covers the underlying product. This review focuses on the app.

## Key Features

### Voice input powered by Whisper

This is the standout feature. Tap the waveform icon, talk, and the app transcribes your speech using Whisper, OpenAI's open-source speech recognition model.

It is noticeably better than standard iOS dictation. It copes with technical terms, names, accents, false starts, and long rambling thoughts, and it handles many languages. Dictating a messy two-minute brain dump and asking ChatGPT to turn it into a tidy email is the single best use of the app.

One limit to know: voice is input only. The app transcribes what you say, but it doesn't talk back.

If you want the same transcription quality for recordings and interviews, see our guide to [transcribing audio with OpenAI Whisper](/tutorials/how-to-transcribe-audio-for-free-with-openai-whisper-2023-a-step-by-step-guide/).

### History sync

Start a conversation on your laptop and continue it on your phone. Everything appears in the same history list. It sounds basic, but it's the main thing third-party wrappers can't offer, and it makes the phone feel like an extension of the desktop tool.

### GPT-4 for Plus subscribers

Plus users get a model switcher at the top of each new chat. GPT-4 is slower than GPT-3.5 but much stronger at reasoning, careful writing, and following detailed instructions. The usual cap applies: 25 GPT-4 messages every three hours at the time of writing. Our [GPT-4 vs GPT-3.5 comparison](/compare/gpt-4-vs-gpt-3.5-2023-is-chatgpt-plus-worth-20-for-the-new-model/) explains where the difference shows.

### A native feel

The app is fast and uncluttered. Responses stream in with subtle haptic feedback, long-pressing a message lets you copy it, and the interface is a single chat window with a history drawer. There's almost nothing to learn.

The recent Shortcuts support is more useful than it sounds. You can build a shortcut that opens a new chat with a preset prompt, then trigger it from the Home Screen or with Siri.

### Privacy controls

Under Settings you'll find Data Controls, where you can turn off chat history and training. With it off, new conversations aren't used to train OpenAI's models and are deleted from its systems after 30 days. You can also export your data or delete your account from inside the app.

## Pros

- **Free, official, and ad-free.** No more guessing which "AI Chat" app is legitimate.
- **Excellent voice input.** Whisper transcription is accurate enough to change how you use the tool.
- **Seamless sync** with the web version.
- **Fast and clean.** It feels like a messaging app, not a web page in a wrapper.
- **GPT-4 in your pocket** if you already pay for Plus.
- **Real data controls**, including the option to keep chats out of training.

## Cons and Limitations

- **No plugins or web browsing.** Plus subscribers have had both as beta features on the web since May. Neither is in the app. That means the app's knowledge still stops in September 2021, with no way to look anything up.
- **No Android version.** OpenAI says Android is next. There's no date.
- **Voice is one-way.** You can speak to it, but it won't read answers aloud.
- **Text only.** You can't attach photos, PDFs, or other files.
- **The GPT-4 cap follows you.** Messages sent from your phone count against the same limit as the web.
- **Thin organization.** There are no folders or pinned chats, so a long history becomes a long scroll.
- **Same model, same flaws.** It still states wrong facts with complete confidence. A nicer interface doesn't change that, and a phone screen makes it more tempting to skip verification.

## Pricing

**As of June 2023:**

- **App:** Free.
- **ChatGPT Plus:** About $19.99 per month as an in-app purchase in the US, versus $20 per month on the web. It's the same subscription either way. If you already subscribe on the web, sign in and your Plus features appear automatically.

One practical note: if you subscribe through the App Store, you manage and cancel through your Apple ID, not through OpenAI's website. Prices and availability vary by country and change often, so check the current listing.

## How It Compares to Other Mobile Options

**Bing app.** Microsoft's app offers GPT-4-powered chat for free with a Microsoft account, plus live web results and citations. It's the better choice for current events and anything that needs sources. The trade-off is a busier interface and limits on conversation length. See our [Bing Chat review](/reviews/bing-chat-review-2023-search-with-an-opinion-problem/).

**Poe.** Quora's app puts several chatbots in one place, including Claude and limited GPT-4 access. Good if you like comparing answers across models.

**Perplexity.** A search-first assistant that answers with linked sources. Stronger for research, weaker for long writing tasks.

**Google Bard.** No dedicated app yet. You use it in a mobile browser.

**Third-party "ChatGPT" apps.** Avoid them. Most are wrappers around OpenAI's API with a subscription on top, and several charge more than Plus for less.

## Who It's For

**Install it if you:**

- Already use ChatGPT on the web and own an iPhone. There's no reason not to.
- Think out loud. Dictation followed by "clean this up" is quicker than typing on glass.
- Pay for Plus and want GPT-4 away from your desk.

**Look elsewhere if you:**

- Use Android. You'll need the mobile site or a competitor for now.
- Need current information. Bing or Perplexity will serve you better.
- Rely on plugins. Those remain web-only.

## Tips for Getting More From It

- **Dictate first, structure second.** Talk through your thoughts, then ask for an outline, an email, or a to-do list.
- **Create a Shortcut for your most common prompt**, such as "summarize the text on my clipboard."
- **Turn off chat history before sensitive conversations**, and turn it back on afterward if you want your history saved.
- **Verify the developer.** The real app is published by OpenAI.

## Verdict

The ChatGPT iOS app does a small number of things and does them well. Voice input is outstanding, sync works as it should, and the whole experience is quicker and more pleasant than the mobile website.

What it lacks is everything OpenAI has added to the web version recently. With no browsing, no plugins, and no file or image input, it's a companion to the desktop experience and not a replacement for it.

For most iPhone owners who use ChatGPT, it's an easy install. Go in knowing that the most capable version of ChatGPT still lives in a browser.
