Ten months ago OpenAI showed a handful of Sora clips, including a woman walking through neon Tokyo and woolly mammoths in the snow, and then went quiet. In the meantime, Runway, Luma, Kling, and Pika all shipped products you could actually use. On December 9, Sora finally launched to the public as part of ChatGPT Plus and Pro.

We've spent the week since generating clips on both tiers. Sora is a good tool with some strong ideas in its editor, but it isn't the leap over the competition that February's demos suggested, and what you get depends heavily on which plan you pay for. If you want the backstory, see our [reaction to the original announcement](/sora-announcement-reaction-2024/).

## What it is

Sora is OpenAI's text-to-video model, released as a standalone web app at sora.com rather than inside the ChatGPT chat window. You sign in with your ChatGPT account. The launch version runs a faster model OpenAI calls Sora Turbo.

You can generate video from a text prompt, from an uploaded image, or by extending and modifying existing video. Output comes in widescreen, vertical, or square formats. There's no audio: clips are silent, so music and sound are your job.

The app has two halves. One is a library and editor for your own work. The other is a feed of community videos, where you can see the exact prompt behind each clip and reuse it.

## Key features

**Storyboard.** This is Sora's most distinctive tool. Instead of one prompt for the whole clip, you place cards along a timeline, each describing what should happen at that moment, and Sora fills in the motion between them. It's the closest thing we've used to directing a shot rather than hoping for one.

**Remix.** Describe a change in plain language, such as "replace the car with a horse-drawn carriage," and Sora regenerates the clip with that edit. You pick how strong the change should be.

**Re-cut.** Trim a generated clip to its best section and have Sora extend it in either direction from there.

**Loop.** Produces a seamless repeating version of a clip, handy for backgrounds and social posts.

**Blend.** Merges two videos into one that transitions between them. Results are unpredictable, and occasionally great.

**Style presets.** Saved looks, like film noir or cardboard-and-papercraft, that you can apply to a prompt. You can also create your own.

**Image-to-video.** Upload a still and animate it. At launch, uploads that contain people are restricted for most users, a safety measure that limits one of the most popular use cases.

## Video quality

At its best, Sora produces the most cinematic-looking footage we've seen from a consumer tool. Lighting, depth of field, and textures are consistently strong. Landscapes, aerial shots, animals, slow camera moves, and stylized animation look excellent, and Sora is good at interpreting loosely written prompts in an appealing way.

The problems show up with motion and physics. People walking are usually fine. People doing anything complicated, like gymnastics, dancing, handling objects, or eating, often aren't. Limbs merge, objects appear and vanish, and cause and effect breaks down. Text inside videos is unreliable. Hands are still hit and miss.

Consistency is the other limit. Sora has no built-in way to keep a character identical across separate generations, so building a multi-shot story with the same person takes a lot of rerolling and luck.

In our side-by-side prompts, Sora generally won on overall look and prompt interpretation. [Runway Gen-3 and Kling](/compare/runway-vs-kling-2024/) were often as good or better at realistic human motion and gave more predictable results from an input image. None of them gets it right on the first try most of the time. Plan on generating several takes per usable clip.

## Pros

- **Storyboard gives real control** over the sequence of events within a clip.
- **Strong visual quality** in lighting, texture, and composition.
- **Included with ChatGPT Plus**, so existing subscribers pay nothing extra to try it.
- **The community feed shows prompts**, which shortens the learning curve considerably.
- **A clean, well-designed editor** that's easier to learn than most rivals.

## Cons and limitations

- **Physics and complex motion** fail often enough that you can't rely on a single take.
- **The Plus tier is tight.** Short clips, 720p at most, a watermark, and a credit allowance you can burn through in an afternoon.
- **The good stuff costs $200 a month.** 1080p, 20-second clips, and watermark-free downloads need Pro.
- **No audio** of any kind.
- **Not available in the UK or EU** at launch.
- **Restrictions on uploading images of people** rule out many image-to-video ideas.
- **Launch-week strain.** Sign-ups were paused at times and queues were long. This should ease, but expect slow generations at busy hours.
- **Strict content moderation.** Prompts involving real people, brands, or anything borderline are often refused, sometimes without a clear reason.

## Pricing

These are approximate figures as of December 2024. OpenAI adjusts limits often, so confirm before subscribing.

- **ChatGPT Plus, about $20/month:** a monthly credit allowance worth up to about 50 videos at 480p (fewer at 720p), clips of around 5 seconds, with a watermark on downloads.
- **ChatGPT Pro, about $200/month:** roughly ten times the priority credits, unlimited generations in a slower queue, up to 1080p and 20 seconds, several generations running at once, and downloads without the watermark.

There's no free tier and no way to buy extra credits on top of Plus at launch. Higher resolution and longer duration both cost more credits per clip, so a 20-second 1080p video uses many times what a 5-second 480p draft does.

Whether Pro is worth it for Sora alone is a hard sell. For comparison, the standard paid plans from Runway, Luma, and Kling cost a small fraction of that and include 1080p output. Pro makes sense mainly if you also want its other perks, which we cover in our [ChatGPT Pro review](/reviews/chatgpt-pro-review-2024/).

## How it compares

- **Runway Gen-3 Alpha** has the most mature toolset for professionals, including camera controls and video-to-video. It's better for precise, repeatable work. See our [Luma vs Runway comparison](/compare/luma-dream-machine-vs-runway-gen-3-2024/) for more.
- **Kling** produces some of the most convincing human motion and offers longer clips, though its queue times on cheaper plans can be long.
- **Luma Dream Machine** is fast and good at image-to-video, and has a usable free tier.
- **Pika** leans toward playful effects and social content, and its latest release lets you drop your own characters and objects into scenes.
- **Google Veo 2** was announced this week with impressive demos, but access is still limited to a waitlist, so we can't compare it fairly yet.

## Who it's for

**Good fit:** existing ChatGPT Plus subscribers who want to experiment at no extra cost, creators making short stylized or atmospheric clips, marketers producing concept visuals and mood pieces, and anyone who values the Storyboard approach to sequencing a shot.

**Poor fit:** anyone in the UK or EU for now, filmmakers who need consistent characters across shots, teams that need realistic footage of people doing specific things, and budget-conscious creators who need 1080p without a watermark.

## Verdict

Sora is a strong first release that arrived in a much more crowded market than the one it was announced into. Its visual quality is excellent, and Storyboard and Remix are good ideas that competitors will likely copy. But it doesn't solve the hard problems of AI video. Physics still breaks, characters still drift, and you'll still throw away more clips than you keep.

If you already pay for ChatGPT Plus, Sora is a worthwhile bonus, and you should try it. If you're choosing a video tool from scratch, the Plus limits are restrictive and the Pro price is hard to justify for video alone. Runway or Kling give you more usable output per dollar today.

**Rating: 3.5 out of 5.** Beautiful output and a smart editor, held back by tight limits on the affordable plan and the same motion problems as everyone else.
