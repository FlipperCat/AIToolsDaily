The pitch behind Limitless is seductive: what if you never forgot a conversation again? Not the action items from Tuesday's call, not the name of the person you met at a conference, not the promise a vendor made on the phone.

Limitless — the company that grew out of Rewind — attacks this with two pieces: a lightweight wearable called the **Pendant** that records audio you're allowed to capture, and an app that turns everything into a searchable, chat-queryable memory. It's one of the few AI wearables that survived the category's rough first wave with its reputation intact.

We've tested a Pendant through several weeks of meetings, calls, and one conference. Here's where it earns its keep and where it doesn't.

## What it is

The Pendant is a small, clip-on/magnetic disc that records audio throughout your day. Recordings sync to the Limitless app, where they're transcribed, diarized (split by speaker), summarized, and indexed. You can then ask natural-language questions — "What did Sam say about the Q3 budget?" "What was that book Ana recommended?" — and get answers with links back to the source moment.

The app also captures scheduled meetings directly on your computer, no hardware required, which puts it in partial competition with tools like [Fathom](/reviews/fathom-ai-review-2026/) and [Granola](/granola-ai-meeting-notes-review-2026/).

## Key features

**All-day capture.** Multi-day battery life and passive operation mean the Pendant's job is to be forgotten. Ours mostly was — which is exactly the point, and exactly what makes the consent question so important (more below).

**Ask-your-memory chat.** The killer feature. Querying weeks of conversations like a database is genuinely novel; it's the difference between having recordings and having *memory*. Answers cite the underlying moments so you can verify.

**Automatic summaries and action items.** Each day and each detected conversation gets a digest. Quality is good for structured meetings, weaker for ambient chatter, which often produces summaries of noise.

**Speaker identification.** After some training, it labels who said what with reasonable accuracy in small groups. Crowded rooms remain hard.

**Consent-aware design.** Limitless has been more thoughtful than most in this category: features designed around capturing what you're permitted to capture, and controls for pausing and deleting. The tool still ultimately trusts you to behave.

**Integrations.** Notes can flow into your existing stack, so summaries end up where work happens rather than trapped in another silo.

## Pros

- **Recall you didn't know you needed.** Retrieving an exact commitment from a two-week-old hallway conversation feels like a superpower and occasionally settles disputes.
- **Hardware you actually forget.** Light, decent battery, unobtrusive. The best wearable is the one you stop noticing.
- **Works without hardware.** The software-only meeting capture is a real product on its own and a free-ish way to evaluate the experience.
- **Strong search.** Retrieval across a large personal archive is fast and usually accurate, with sources you can check.

## Cons and limitations

- **The social cost is real.** Wearing a recorder changes rooms. Some colleagues won't care; some will, visibly. You'll have the "should I mention the pendant?" conversation many times, and in two-party-consent jurisdictions you *must* have it.
- **Transcription quality is environment-dependent.** Quiet meetings: excellent. Restaurants, conferences, wind: expect gaps and garbled speaker attribution.
- **Summaries inherit transcription errors.** A misheard number in a noisy room can surface confidently in a digest. Verify anything consequential against the audio.
- **Subscription required for the good parts.** The hardware is only half the price; the AI features that make it worthwhile sit behind a monthly plan.
- **Privacy surface area.** Your most sensitive conversations living in a cloud service is a threat model, full stop. Limitless makes meaningful commitments here, but "trust us" is doing load-bearing work for any product in this category.

## Pricing

As of this writing (July 2026): the Pendant is a one-time hardware purchase in the low hundreds of dollars, with a free tier covering basic use and a paid subscription (roughly comparable to other AI productivity subscriptions per month) unlocking unlimited capture and the full AI feature set. Exact numbers have shifted since launch and promotions are common — check the current pricing page before buying, and remember the true cost is hardware plus a year of subscription, not the sticker price.

## Who it's for

- **People whose job is conversations** — founders, salespeople, journalists, consultants — where a forgotten detail has a dollar cost
- **Conference-heavy networkers** who meet forty people in two days and want to remember any of them
- **Anyone with memory concerns**, for whom this category can be quietly life-changing
- **Meeting-notes users who've outgrown call capture** and want the same power in the physical world

Skip it if your important conversations happen in scheduled video calls — a dedicated meeting assistant is cheaper and socially simpler; our [Granola vs Otter comparison](/compare/granola-vs-otter-2026/) covers the strongest options. And skip it if you work somewhere recording would be legally or culturally radioactive.

## Verdict

Limitless is the most convincing case yet for AI wearables, because it picked a problem — imperfect human memory — that software alone can't fully reach. The retrieval experience is genuinely excellent, the hardware disappears, and the company has taken consent more seriously than the category's reputation suggests.

But you're not just buying a gadget; you're adopting a practice of recording your life, with social friction, legal homework, and privacy exposure as recurring costs alongside the subscription. For conversation-heavy professionals who'll do the disclosure work, it's worth it. For everyone else, the meeting-app version of this idea delivers most of the value with a fraction of the complications.

**Rating: 4/5** — a real glimpse of augmented memory, priced in dollars and social capital alike.
