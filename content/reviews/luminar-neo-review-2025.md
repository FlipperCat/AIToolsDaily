Luminar Neo makes a simple promise: edits that used to take an hour in Photoshop should take a slider and a few seconds. Replace a dull sky, relight a backlit subject, remove power lines, or upscale a cropped shot, all with AI that does the masking for you.

Skylum has been building toward that promise for years, and Neo is the most complete version so far. It's also a product with real weaknesses in speed, cataloging, and licensing complexity. We used Luminar Neo as both a standalone editor and a Lightroom Classic plugin across landscape, portrait, and real estate work. Here's what we found.

## What Luminar Neo is

Luminar Neo is a desktop photo editor for Windows and macOS from Skylum. It works on its own with a basic photo library, or as a plugin for Lightroom Classic and Photoshop. Its identity is AI-first editing: most tools detect the sky, people, faces, buildings, or background automatically, so you adjust sliders instead of painting masks.

Over time, Skylum has added generative tools that rebuild image content rather than just adjusting it. That puts Neo partly in the same territory as Photoshop's generative features and standalone AI editors.

## Key features

### Sky AI and atmosphere tools

Sky replacement is still Luminar's best-known feature, and it's very good. Neo finds the sky, swaps it, and relights the foreground to match the new sky's color temperature. On clean horizons, results look natural with a little adjustment. It struggles with fine detail like tree branches against the sky or hair at the edge of the frame, where you may see halos.

Atmosphere tools add fog, mist, or haze with depth awareness. Used lightly, they add mood. Pushed too far, they look like a filter.

### Relight AI

Relight AI estimates depth in the scene and lets you brighten the foreground or background separately. It works especially well on backlit portraits and interiors. It isn't perfect at depth estimation, but it's often faster than manual dodging and burning.

### Portrait tools

Face AI, Skin AI, and Body AI handle common retouching: smoothing skin, brightening eyes, whitening teeth, and slimming. The defaults are heavy-handed. Keep them around a quarter of the slider range for natural results. Portrait Bokeh AI adds background blur and works well on clean subject edges, less well on frizzy hair or glasses.

### Generative tools

- **GenErase** removes objects and fills the space. It's strong on power lines, tourists, and small distractions, and less reliable on large objects against complex backgrounds.
- **GenSwap** replaces a selected area with content from a text prompt. It's useful occasionally, but results vary. It's better for small elements than for anything central to the image.
- **GenExpand** extends the canvas beyond the original frame. It's handy for reframing to a different aspect ratio, and it struggles with repeating patterns and architecture.

These tools run in the cloud, so they need an internet connection, and each generation can take noticeably longer than a local edit.

### Enhancement extensions

Neo includes or sells several technical tools: noise reduction, upscaling, sharpening, HDR merge, focus stacking, and background removal. Noise reduction and upscaling are solid for most hobby work. Dedicated specialists like [Topaz Photo AI](/reviews/topaz-photo-ai-review-2024-is-the-one-time-price-worth-it/) still pull out more detail on difficult files, especially high-ISO wildlife shots.

## Pros

- **Fast results on difficult edits.** Sky swaps, relighting, and object removal that take skill in Photoshop take seconds here.
- **Automatic masking works well.** Neo's subject and sky detection covers most scenes without manual brushwork.
- **Works as a plugin.** You can keep Lightroom for organization and send images to Neo for the AI-heavy edits.
- **Approachable interface.** It's easier to learn than Photoshop, with presets that work as starting points.
- **One-time purchase available,** which is rare among AI editors.

## Cons and limitations

- **Performance.** Neo can feel sluggish, particularly with many AI tools stacked on a large RAW file. Slider lag on older hardware is common.
- **Weak catalog.** The library has basic folders and albums, without the keywording, smart collections, and metadata tools that Lightroom users depend on.
- **Licensing complexity.** Subscriptions, lifetime licenses, extension packs, and promotional bundles change often. Working out what you're buying takes effort.
- **Generative tools need the cloud.** That means waiting, an internet requirement, and less predictability than local tools.
- **Easy to overdo.** The AI makes dramatic edits easy, and dramatic edits are easy to spot. Restraint is up to you.

## Pricing (approximate, as of December 2025)

Skylum sells Luminar Neo through several channels, and prices shift with frequent promotions. Expect roughly:

- **Subscription plans** billed monthly or annually, with the higher tiers including extensions and the latest AI features. Annual billing typically works out to around $10 or less per month during promotions.
- **One-time license** for a perpetual copy, often with a lower "Explore" option and a fuller "Pro" option. Future major features may not be included.

Skylum runs discounts often, especially around holidays. If you're not in a hurry, wait for a sale. Always check what each plan includes, particularly whether GenErase, upscaling, and noise reduction are part of it.

## Who it's for

**Good fit:**

- Hobbyist photographers who want strong results without learning advanced Photoshop masking.
- Real estate and travel photographers who replace skies and remove distractions often.
- Lightroom users who want an AI-heavy plugin for specific edits.

**Less suitable:**

- Professionals who need a fast, reliable catalog for thousands of images per shoot.
- Anyone on older hardware who can't tolerate slow previews.
- Photographers who mainly need generative image creation rather than editing. A tool built for that, like [FLUX.1 Kontext](/reviews/flux.1-kontext-review-2025-black-forest-labs-image-editor-put-to-work/), may suit you better.

## Tips for using it well

1. **Do global edits first,** then AI tools. Getting exposure and white balance right gives the AI masks cleaner input.
2. **Zoom to 100% on edges** after sky replacement. Halos around trees and hair are the most common giveaway.
3. **Use GenErase in small passes.** Removing three small objects separately usually beats removing them in one big selection.
4. **Save your own presets.** Skylum's presets are dramatic. A few subtle presets of your own will save more time.
5. **For quick object removal on a single image,** a browser tool like [Cleanup.pictures](/reviews/cleanup.pictures-review-2023-the-fastest-way-to-erase-things-from-photos/) can be faster than opening Neo.

## Verdict

Luminar Neo is one of the most capable AI-assisted photo editors for non-specialists. It turns sky replacement, relighting, and object removal into routine tasks. Its weaknesses are real: slower performance than we'd like, a basic catalog, and a confusing set of licensing options.

If you organize in Lightroom and want faster AI edits, Neo is a strong plugin. If you want a full Lightroom replacement for professional volume, it isn't there yet.

**Rating: 4.0 / 5.** Excellent AI editing, held back by speed and catalog tools.
