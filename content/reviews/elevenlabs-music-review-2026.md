ElevenLabs built its reputation on voice — arguably the best AI voiceover on the market. Music is a different beast. Melody, arrangement, vocal performance, and mixing all have to hold together for three minutes, and listeners are far less forgiving of "almost right" in a song than in a narration.

So does ElevenLabs Music earn a place next to [Suno](/reviews/suno-ai-review-2024/) and Udio? After a couple of weeks generating tracks for real projects — podcast intros, background beds for YouTube videos, and a few full songs for fun — here's the honest picture.

## What it is

ElevenLabs Music is a text-to-music generator inside the main ElevenLabs platform. You describe a track — genre, mood, tempo, instrumentation, whether you want vocals — and it produces a complete piece of music, from short cues up to full-length songs with sung lyrics.

Two things separate it from the field on paper. First, licensing: ElevenLabs has leaned hard into rights-cleared training and commercial-use terms, positioning the tool for professional use rather than novelty. Second, integration: music sits alongside the company's voice, dubbing, and [Scribe transcription](/reviews/elevenlabs-scribe-review-2026/) products, all sharing one account, one credit pool, and one API.

## Key features

- **Prompt-based generation** — natural-language descriptions work, and a structured mode lets you specify sections (intro, verse, chorus, drop) rather than hoping the model guesses your arrangement.
- **Vocals or instrumental** — you can supply your own lyrics or let the model write them. Instrumental-only mode is where I got the most consistently usable results.
- **Section editing** — regenerate a verse or extend an outro without redoing the whole track. This is a genuine workflow improvement over regenerate-and-pray.
- **Multi-language vocals** — useful if your audience isn't English-speaking; quality varies noticeably by language.
- **API access** — you can generate music programmatically, which none of the consumer-first competitors handle as cleanly.
- **Stems on export** (plan-dependent) — separated tracks make it possible to actually mix the output rather than treating it as a finished file.

## Pros

**Licensing you can explain to a client.** This is the big one. If you make content for brands, "the terms say commercial use is covered on my plan" is a sentence you can say with a straight face. The legal gray zone around AI music hasn't fully resolved, but ElevenLabs has done more than most to de-risk it.

**Excellent for functional music.** Background beds, podcast intros, 30-second stings, ambient loops — the model nails "music that supports something else" reliably. For this use case it's already better value than most stock music subscriptions.

**Real editing controls.** Section regeneration and stems mean you can fix the one bad chorus instead of discarding a track that's 80% right. Suno has moved this direction too, but ElevenLabs' implementation feels built for people who will actually open the file in a DAW afterward.

**One subscription, one API.** If you already pay for ElevenLabs voice — and a lot of content teams do — music is effectively an added capability, not another line item.

## Cons and limitations

**Vocals still trail Suno.** Sung vocals are competent but often carry a slightly processed, compressed character, and emotional delivery is flatter. When I generated the same pop brief in both tools, Suno's version was the one I'd play for someone without a disclaimer. For instrumental work the gap mostly disappears.

**Credit burn is real.** Full-length songs with vocals eat credits fast. If you're generating dozens of candidates to find one keeper — which is still how AI music works — costs add up quicker than the plan pricing suggests.

**Genre coverage is uneven.** Electronic, ambient, cinematic, and lo-fi styles are strong. Anything that depends on loose human timing — jazz, folk, live-feeling rock — comes out stiff more often than not. That's an industry-wide weakness, but Suno and Udio handle some of these edge genres more gracefully.

**No real community layer.** Suno's feed of public creations is a discovery engine and a prompt-learning resource. ElevenLabs Music is a tool, not a scene. Fine for professionals, less fun for hobbyists.

## Pricing

Music shares the standard ElevenLabs plan structure — as of August 2026, roughly: a limited free tier, a starter plan around $5/month, a creator tier around $22/month, and professional tiers from about $99/month upward. Music generation consumes plan credits, and full songs are among the most expensive things you can spend them on.

Treat these numbers as approximate — AI tool pricing shifts constantly. The practical takeaway: light users fit in the creator tier; anyone producing music daily should price out the higher tiers first, and compare against what you'd pay for [Suno](/compare/suno-vs-elevenlabs-music-2026/) as a dedicated subscription.

## Who it's for

- **Content teams and agencies** that need commercially safe background music at volume, especially if they already use ElevenLabs for voice.
- **Developers** building products that generate music programmatically — the API story here is the best in the category.
- **Podcasters and YouTubers** who want custom intros and beds without stock-music sameness.

Who it's *not* for: musicians chasing the best possible AI-generated *song*, or hobbyists who enjoy the social side of tools like Suno. For a head-to-head on exactly that question, see our [Suno vs ElevenLabs Music comparison](/compare/suno-vs-elevenlabs-music-2026/), and if you're weighing the older generation of tools, [Suno vs Udio](/compare/suno-vs-udio/) still covers the fundamentals.

## Verdict

ElevenLabs Music is the most *professional* AI music tool available — clear licensing, real editing controls, stems, and an API — wrapped around a model that is very good at functional music and merely decent at songs. If your question is "can I stop paying for stock music," the answer is probably yes. If your question is "can this write my next single," Suno remains the safer bet, for now.

Score: strong recommendation for commercial content work; qualified recommendation for everything else. Given how fast ElevenLabs ships — voice, dubbing, transcription have all improved on aggressive schedules — the vocal-quality gap is the kind of problem I'd expect them to close rather than live with.
