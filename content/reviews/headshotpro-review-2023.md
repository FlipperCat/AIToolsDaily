Every few months an AI trend escapes tech Twitter and lands in normal people's group chats. This summer it's AI headshots. Suddenly half of LinkedIn seems to have suspiciously well-lit profile photos, and HeadshotPro is one of the main reasons why.

The pitch is simple: skip the photographer, upload some selfies, and get a folder of professional-looking headshots for less than the cost of a nice dinner. We ran two team members through the full process to see whether the results are actually usable — or whether you'll end up with a portrait of your slightly-off digital twin.

## What it is

HeadshotPro is a web service that fine-tunes an image-generation model on your face. You upload a batch of photos — the more varied the angles and lighting, the better — pick backdrops and outfits, and wait a couple of hours while it trains and renders. Depending on the package, you get back somewhere between 40 and 120+ headshots in different styles: office backgrounds, neutral studio grays, outdoor bokeh, the works.

Under the hood this is the same fine-tuning idea that powers a lot of 2023's personalized-avatar apps, pointed at a corporate use case. Unlike general tools such as [Midjourney](/reviews/01-midjourney-review/) or [Leonardo AI](/reviews/leonardo-ai-review-2023/), you're not writing prompts — the entire experience is upload, select, wait, download.

## Key features

- **Guided photo upload** — the site coaches you on what to submit (varied angles, no sunglasses, no heavy filters). Following this advice matters more than anything else you do.
- **Style and backdrop selection** — choose outfits (suits, business casual, scrubs for medical professionals) and settings before generation.
- **Batch output** — dozens of images per order, which is the right design: AI headshots are a numbers game.
- **Team plans** — a company can run its whole staff through one dashboard for consistent-looking team pages. This is genuinely clever and probably the strongest business case.
- **Turnaround in hours** — our orders came back in about two hours, well within the promised window.

## Pros

**The hit rate is better than expected.** Out of one 40-image batch, we counted 19 that genuinely looked like the person, and about 8 we'd be comfortable using professionally. That's a real result. A year ago this technology produced fairground caricatures; now it produces photos your mom might not question.

**It's fast and cheap compared to the alternative.** No scheduling, no commute, no awkward small talk with a photographer. For anyone who has been putting off updating a decade-old profile photo — which, let's be honest, is most of LinkedIn — the convenience is the product.

**Team consistency.** Getting ten remote employees photographed in a matching style is a logistics headache. Getting them all to upload selfies is an email. For company about-pages, this beats the usual mismatched collection of vacation crops.

**No prompt skills required.** Compared with wrangling [image models yourself](/reviews/playground-ai-review-2023/), the zero-knowledge workflow is a legitimate advantage for the target audience.

## Cons and limitations

**Resemblance is a coin flip per image.** The AI produces a *plausible professional person* who shares most of your features. Sometimes it's you. Sometimes it's your cousin who works in insurance. One of our testers had a distinctive nose that the model quietly "fixed" in about a third of the images — flattering, maybe, but not accurate.

**Artifacts cluster in predictable places.** Teeth, earrings, shirt collars, eyeglass frames, and hair edges are where the illusion breaks. Anything intricate gets smoothed or scrambled. If you wear glasses, expect a lower usable-image rate.

**Skin texture runs airbrushed.** Nearly every output has that lightly retouched, poreless quality. At LinkedIn thumbnail size it reads as "good lighting." At full size it reads as AI.

**No do-overs without paying.** If your batch comes back weak — usually because the input photos were too similar — there's no free regeneration. Read the upload guidance twice; it's the difference between a good and bad order.

**The ethics are your call.** A headshot is implicitly a claim about what you look like. A mild idealization is normal — photographers retouch too — but if your AI headshot is notably better-looking than you, the first video call becomes a small credibility tax.

## Pricing

As of August 2023, packages run roughly $29 to $59 per person depending on how many photos and styles you want, with team pricing scaled per seat. Treat those numbers as approximate — pricing in this category has been moving around all year as competitors like Try it on AI, Secta Labs, and Aragon fight for the same customers.

Against the $150–$400 cost of a typical professional session, the value math is obvious, *if* you get usable images — which most people will, but not all.

## Who it's for

- **Job seekers and LinkedIn updaters** who need a decent headshot this week, not next month.
- **Remote teams** standardizing their about-page or sales-deck photos.
- **Founders and freelancers** who need press-kit photos before they can justify a real shoot.

Skip it if you're a public-facing professional whose photo does heavy lifting — speakers, on-camera talent, executives with press exposure. At that level the artifacts and the resemblance lottery aren't worth it; hire the photographer.

## Verdict

HeadshotPro is the rare 2023 AI product that solves a boring, universal problem competently. It won't replace professional photography at the top end, and roughly half of what it generates belongs in the recycle bin — but the half that works, works. For $30-something and an hour of effort, most people will walk away with a LinkedIn photo meaningfully better than the one they have now.
