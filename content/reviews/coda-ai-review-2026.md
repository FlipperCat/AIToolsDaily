Coda has spent years pitching itself as the doc that replaces a dozen apps: notes, spreadsheets, project trackers, and lightweight internal tools all living in one surface. The AI layer, folded directly into that surface, is what most people are actually evaluating in 2026. This review looks at what Coda AI does well, where it still frustrates, and whether it earns a seat in your stack.

## What Coda Is

Coda is a document platform built around "building blocks." A Coda doc can hold ordinary text, but its real power comes from tables that behave like databases, buttons that run actions, and a formula language that ties everything together. On top of that sits **Coda AI**, an assistant you can call inline to write, summarize, restructure, or reason over the content already in your doc.

Since being acquired by Grammarly in late 2024, Coda has kept investing in the AI side, and the assistant now feels less like a bolt-on and more like a native part of the editor. You can drop an AI block into a page, generate a first draft, and then keep editing it like any other content. Inside tables, AI can fill entire columns — categorizing feedback, extracting fields from messy notes, or drafting per-row summaries.

For teams, **Coda Brain** extends the assistant beyond a single doc so it can answer questions across your workspace and connected tools. That's the feature Coda leans on to compete with dedicated enterprise search products.

## Key Features

- **Inline AI writing and editing.** Generate drafts, rewrite for tone, summarize long pages, or turn bullet points into prose without leaving the doc.
- **AI in tables (column fill).** Point the assistant at a column and have it process every row — sentiment tags, extracted entities, short summaries, or translated text. This is where Coda AI genuinely saves time versus a chat window.
- **Chat over your doc.** Ask questions about the content in the current doc and get answers grounded in what's actually there, which is handy for long meeting logs or research collections.
- **Coda Brain and Packs.** Connect Slack, Jira, Google Drive, and dozens of other tools so the assistant can reference live data instead of stale copies.
- **Automations and buttons.** Combine AI outputs with Coda's native automation so a generated summary can trigger a notification or update another table automatically.

## Pros

The biggest win is **context**. Because the assistant lives where your data lives, you spend far less time copying content into a separate chatbot and pasting results back. Column-fill AI in particular turns tasks that used to be tedious — tagging hundreds of survey responses, for example — into a single action.

Coda's **formula and table engine** remains best-in-class for people who want to build actual tools rather than just take notes. Pairing that with AI means you can prototype a small internal app, then have the assistant populate or explain parts of it.

The **learning materials and templates** are strong, so a motivated user can get from blank doc to working tracker quickly. And for teams already living in Coda, the AI adds capability without introducing yet another subscription to manage.

## Cons and Limitations

Coda's flexibility is also its steepest tax. The **learning curve is real** — buttons, formulas, and Packs reward power users but overwhelm people who just want a clean page to write on. If your team mostly needs docs and wikis, a lot of Coda's surface area goes unused.

AI quality is **good, not exceptional**. For open-ended writing, a dedicated assistant like [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) or [Claude](/reviews/claude-review/) will often produce stronger long-form output. Coda AI shines on structured, in-doc tasks more than on being your best general-purpose writer.

**Performance** can lag in large, formula-heavy docs, and AI actions on big tables aren't instant. Coda Brain's usefulness is also only as good as your connected data and structure; teams with messy workspaces get vaguer answers.

Finally, **pricing complexity** trips people up. Coda bills by "maker" seats (people who build docs) versus editors and viewers, which is fair but takes a minute to reason about when budgeting.

## Pricing (as of July 2026)

Coda's tiers are approximate and change over time, so verify before committing:

- **Free** — generous for individuals, with a capped number of AI actions per month and limits on doc size. Fine for testing.
- **Pro** — roughly **$10 per maker/month**, unlocking larger docs, more automations, and expanded AI usage.
- **Team** — roughly **$30 per maker/month**, adding collaboration controls and higher limits, and where most growing teams land.
- **Enterprise** — custom pricing with advanced admin, security, and Coda Brain at scale.

The key nuance: only *makers* need a paid seat. People who merely edit or view shared docs are cheaper or free, which keeps costs reasonable for read-heavy teams. Notably, AI is largely folded into these plans now rather than sold separately, which is a welcome simplification.

## Who Coda AI Is For

Coda AI is a strong fit if you:

- Build **interactive trackers, dashboards, or lightweight internal tools**, not just static docs.
- Regularly process **structured data** — feedback, tasks, CRM-style records — where column-fill AI pays off.
- Already run your team's workflows in Coda and want AI without adding another app.
- Enjoy tinkering with formulas and automations to get exactly the workflow you want.

It's a weaker fit if you want a simple, fast note-taking or wiki experience, in which case [Notion](/reviews/notion-review-2026/) is the friendlier choice — and our [Notion vs Coda](/compare/notion-vs-coda/) breakdown covers that tradeoff in detail. Writers who mainly need long-form drafting will also get more from a dedicated assistant. ## Verdict

Coda AI is one of the better examples of AI embedded *where the work happens* rather than in a separate tab. For teams that already treat Coda as their build-anything workspace, the assistant meaningfully speeds up structured, repetitive tasks and makes long docs searchable in plain language. Column-fill alone can justify the upgrade for data-heavy workflows.

But Coda asks a lot up front. The power comes bundled with complexity, and if you don't need tables, buttons, and formulas, you're paying for capability you won't touch — and getting AI writing that's merely good rather than best-in-class. Try the free tier on a real project, lean on the column-fill features, and you'll know within a week whether Coda's particular blend of doc, spreadsheet, and app is the one you want to build on.

*Bottom line: an excellent AI layer for people who genuinely use Coda's power features, and overkill for everyone who just wants a clean place to write.*
