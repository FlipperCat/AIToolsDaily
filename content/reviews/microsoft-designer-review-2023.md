Microsoft announced Designer at its Surface event last October, positioning it as a Canva-style tool for people who need a flyer or Instagram post but have no design training. The twist is that it has OpenAI's DALL-E 2 built into it, so you can generate the artwork as well as the layout. We have been using the preview for a few weeks. Here is what it actually does.

## What is Microsoft Designer?

Designer is a free web app at designer.microsoft.com, currently behind a waitlist. You describe what you want to make in a sentence, and the app generates a set of finished-looking design options: headline, supporting text, imagery, colors, and layout. Pick one, tweak it in a lightweight editor, and download it.

Under the hood it combines two things Microsoft already had. The layout intelligence comes from the same "Design Ideas" technology that has been suggesting slide layouts in PowerPoint for years. The image generation comes from DALL-E 2, via Microsoft's partnership with OpenAI. Designer is essentially those two systems wrapped in a much simpler interface.

It is aimed squarely at [Canva](/reviews/20-canva-ai-review/) and, to a lesser degree, Adobe Express.

## How it works

The workflow is short enough to describe in full:

1. **Describe the design.** Something like "Instagram post announcing a spring sale at a small bakery." You can also start from a blank size instead.
2. **Add images, or generate them.** Upload your own, pick from Microsoft's stock library, or type a prompt and let DALL-E produce options.
3. **Choose a layout.** Designer shows several generated compositions in the right-hand "Ideas" panel.
4. **Edit.** Change text, swap fonts, adjust colors, move elements. Every edit triggers fresh suggestions in the Ideas panel.
5. **Resize and export.** Switch between Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, story, and print sizes, then download as PNG, JPG, or PDF.

From description to a usable social post took us well under a minute on most attempts. That is the headline feature and it is genuinely impressive.

## Key features

**Text-to-design.** The description-first approach is the main difference from Canva, where you start by browsing templates. Designer starts by asking what you want and builds around your answer.

**DALL-E 2 image generation.** Generate images without leaving the editor. In our tests the results were noticeably more usable for product-style photos and clean illustrations than Canva's own text-to-image feature. For a broader look at how DALL-E stacks up, see our [DALL-E 2 vs Stable Diffusion](/compare/dalle-2-vs-stable-diffusion-2023/) comparison.

**Live Ideas panel.** The suggestions refresh as you edit. Change the headline and Designer proposes new layouts that fit the new text length. It is the feature that makes the app feel "AI-powered" rather than just template-driven.

**Stock library.** Microsoft's own collection of photos, icons, and illustrations is included at no cost.

**Multi-format resizing.** One design, several output sizes, with layouts re-flowed automatically.

**Planned integrations.** Microsoft has said Designer will show up inside Edge and connect with Microsoft 365 apps. Neither is available in the preview yet.

## What we liked

- **Speed to first draft.** Nothing else we have tried gets from a sentence to a presentable post this fast.
- **Built-in DALL-E.** Having a capable image generator inside the design tool removes the usual generate-download-upload loop. Generation has been free and unlimited in the preview.
- **Clean interface.** There is very little chrome. First-time users will not get lost.
- **Suggestions that respond to your edits.** The Ideas panel makes iteration feel collaborative instead of manual.
- **Free.** No credits, no watermarks, no upsell prompts, at least for now.

## What we didn't like

- **It is a preview, and it shows.** Features appear and disappear between sessions. Some generated layouts have overlapping text or off-canvas elements.
- **Small template library.** Canva has hundreds of thousands of templates. Designer has a modest set of styles that its AI recombines. After a couple of dozen designs you start to recognize the patterns.
- **Basic editor.** No layers panel, no masking, limited effects, no animation, no video. If you need to do real editing you will export and finish elsewhere.
- **DALL-E's known limits.** Text inside generated images comes out garbled. Hands and faces are unreliable. Square output only.
- **No brand kit or team features.** You cannot save brand colors and fonts, and there is no sharing or collaboration. For a small business posting daily, that is a real gap.
- **Web only.** No mobile app yet, and the site is clumsy on a phone browser.
- **Generic copy.** The AI-generated headlines are serviceable but bland. Expect to rewrite them.
- **Waitlist.** You may read this review and still be unable to try it for weeks.

## Pricing (as of February 2023)

Designer is free in preview, and Microsoft has said the base product will remain free at launch. Some premium features will be gated behind Microsoft 365 Personal (about $6.99 per month or $69.99 per year) or Family (about $99.99 per year). Microsoft has not said which features.

For comparison, Canva Pro is roughly $12.99 per month, and Adobe Express Premium is about $9.99 per month. If Designer's premium tier ends up bundled with a Microsoft 365 subscription many people already pay for, that is a meaningful price advantage. See our [Canva vs Adobe Express](/compare/canva-vs-adobe-express/) breakdown for where those two currently stand.

## Who it's for

**Small business owners and solo marketers** who need a steady stream of social posts and do not have time to learn a design tool. This is the target audience and Designer serves it well.

**Microsoft 365 subscribers** who would rather not add another subscription. If the promised integrations land, Designer becomes an obvious default.

**Anyone curious about DALL-E** who does not want to pay OpenAI for credits. Designer is currently one of the cheapest ways to use it.

**Not for:** professional designers, teams that need brand consistency and approvals, anyone producing video or animated content, or people who need a large template library today.

## How it fits Microsoft's AI push

Designer arrived a few months before the [new Bing](/reviews/bing-chat-review-2023/) launched with its chat assistant, and both come from the same OpenAI partnership. Microsoft is clearly working toward putting generative AI in front of ordinary consumers rather than only developers. Designer is the friendliest example of that strategy so far, and the cheapest.

## Verdict

Microsoft Designer is a promising preview of a product that does not fully exist yet. The core idea, describe what you want and let AI build the first draft with generated imagery, works, and it works fast. For quick social posts it is already the least effortful tool we have used.

But the gaps are large. The editor is thin, the template variety is limited, and there is nothing for teams. Canva remains the safer choice for anyone who designs regularly, and it will stay that way until Designer leaves preview and Microsoft clarifies what the free tier includes.

If you can get an invitation, try it. Just do not cancel your Canva subscription yet.

**Rating: 3.5/5** for the preview. The ceiling is much higher.
