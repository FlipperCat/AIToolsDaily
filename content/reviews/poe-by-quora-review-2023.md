There are a lot of AI chatbots in 2023, and keeping a tab open for each one gets old fast. Poe, built by Quora, takes a different approach: put all the major assistants behind a single, clean interface so you can switch between them in two taps. It's one of the more practical ideas to come out of this year's chatbot rush.

I've been using Poe across desktop and mobile for a while now. Here's what it does, where it shines, and where it still feels like an early product.

## What Poe is

Poe — short for "Platform for Open Exploration" — is an aggregator. Instead of building its own foundation model, Quora wired up the popular ones and wrapped them in a chat app. From a single screen you can message a bot powered by GPT-4, jump to one based on GPT-3.5 for quick answers, switch to a Claude bot for long-document work, or try a PaLM-based assistant — all without juggling separate logins.

If you've used [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) and [Claude](/reviews/claude-review/) separately and wished you could compare them side by side, Poe is essentially that wish turned into a product.

## Key features

**Multiple models, one interface.** This is the core pitch. Poe ships with bots wired to the leading models of 2023. Each has different strengths — some are faster, some handle longer context, some write more carefully — and Poe makes flipping between them effortless. For anyone who likes to sanity-check an answer against a second model, it's a genuine time-saver.

**Custom bots.** Poe lets you create your own bot by writing a base prompt and picking an underlying model. Want a bot that always replies as a concise editor, or one that drafts cold emails in your voice? You set it up once and reuse it. It's the same idea as a saved system prompt, but packaged so non-technical users can do it in a minute.

**Fast, clean mobile experience.** Poe launched mobile-first, and it shows. The iOS and Android apps are quick and uncluttered, conversations sync to the web, and the whole thing feels lighter than loading several chatbot sites in a browser.

**Shareable conversations.** You can share a chat via a link, which is handy for showing a colleague how a bot answered or saving a useful exchange.

## Pros

- **One subscription mindset, many models.** Rather than weighing separate paid plans for each assistant, you get access to the premium ones through a single Poe subscription. For people who want breadth over depth, that's a clean deal.
- **Effortless model comparison.** Asking the same question to two different bots and comparing the answers is the kind of thing Poe makes trivial. It's a great way to learn each model's personality.
- **Custom bots lower the barrier.** Saving a tuned prompt as a reusable bot is genuinely useful and doesn't require any technical skill.
- **Polished and fast.** It feels like a finished consumer app, not a developer demo.

## Cons and limitations

- **Message limits on the best models.** Access to the high-end bots comes with caps. Heavy users can bump into limits on the most capable models faster than they'd like, even on the paid plan.
- **No live web access.** As of mid-2023, the bots answer from their training knowledge rather than browsing the internet. For current events or fresh facts, you'll still want a search-connected tool. If real-time answers matter most to you, a dedicated answer engine like [Perplexity](/reviews/perplexity-review/) is a better fit for that job.
- **You're a layer removed.** Because Poe sits on top of other companies' models, you don't always get the very newest features the moment a model provider ships them. There's a lag between a model gaining a capability and Poe surfacing it.
- **The subscription isn't cheap.** Around twenty dollars a month is reasonable if you'd otherwise pay for multiple assistants, but steep if you mostly use one.
- **Custom bots are shallow.** They're great for prompt presets, but they aren't connected to your files or tools, so don't expect a deeply customized agent.

## Pricing

As of August 2023, Poe offers a free tier that covers the faster, lighter models with generous daily limits — enough for casual users to get real value without paying. The subscription, at roughly twenty dollars per month (with a discounted annual option), unlocks the premium bots based on the most capable models along with larger message allowances.

As always, treat these numbers as approximate and check the current plan before subscribing — pricing and model availability in this space change month to month. The free tier is good enough to decide whether Poe's all-in-one approach clicks for you before you pay.

## Who it's for

Poe is a strong pick if you:

- Want to try several AI assistants without managing multiple accounts and subscriptions.
- Like comparing how different models answer the same prompt — see our broader take in [ChatGPT vs Claude](/compare/chatgpt-vs-claude/).
- Prefer a fast, simple mobile app over a stack of browser tabs.
- Want to build quick reusable prompt-based bots without code.

It's a weaker fit if you live inside one specific assistant's ecosystem, need live web browsing, or want the absolute latest features the instant they ship.

## Verdict

Poe is one of the smartest packaging ideas in the 2023 AI landscape. It doesn't try to out-build OpenAI or Anthropic; it just makes their models, and others, pleasant to use side by side. For curious users and generalists who want breadth, that convenience is worth a lot.

The honest caveats are the message caps on top models, the lack of web access, and the reality that you're always one step behind whatever the underlying providers ship directly. If you already know you'll only ever use one assistant, paying that provider directly may serve you better.

But if you want a single, polished home for the whole 2023 chatbot field — and the ability to spin up custom bots in seconds — Poe earns its keep. Start on the free tier, lean on the model-switching, and you'll quickly know whether the subscription is worth it for you.
