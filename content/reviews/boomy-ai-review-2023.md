Most AI music tools in early 2023 are demos: impressive for ten seconds, then you close the tab. Boomy is different in one important way. It doesn't stop at generating a track. It takes you all the way to a released song on streaming services, with a share of the revenue coming back to you.

That pitch has made Boomy one of the most talked-about music generators this year. The company says its users have created millions of songs. I spent two weeks making tracks across every style it offers, editing them, adding vocals, and walking through the release flow to see what the tool is actually good for.

## What Boomy Is

Boomy is a browser-based music generator for people who don't make music. You don't write a prompt, play an instrument, or touch a piano roll. You choose a style, Boomy generates a complete instrumental arrangement, and you decide whether to keep it, tweak it, or throw it away and roll again.

The founders frame it as a way to let anyone create original music. In practice it sits between a toy and a tool. It's fast and fun, the output is usable more often than you'd expect, and the release pipeline gives it a purpose most generators lack.

## Key Features

**One-click song generation.** Pick a broad style, such as electronic dance, lo-fi, rap beats, global groove, or relaxing meditation, then choose a sub-style if offered. Boomy builds a full track, usually in under 30 seconds. Most songs land in the two-to-three minute range with a recognizable intro, development, and ending.

**Editing tools.** Once you have a track, you can regenerate parts of it, rearrange sections, adjust the mix, and change the overall feel. It's coarse editing. You're nudging the AI in a direction, not sculpting individual notes. That's fine for the audience, but producers will hit the ceiling within minutes.

**Vocals from your own mic.** Boomy doesn't sing. You can record vocals in the browser over your track, and Boomy applies processing to help them sit in the mix. It's a nice touch for casual songwriting. If you want polished vocals, though, you'll get better results recording in a proper app and mixing elsewhere.

**Built-in release and distribution.** This is the headline feature. From inside Boomy you can submit a finished song for release on major streaming platforms. You add a title and cover art, and the song goes through a review. If it's approved, it shows up under your artist name. Streaming revenue is shared between you and Boomy according to your plan's terms.

**Community and discovery.** Boomy has a social layer where you can browse what others have made. It's mostly a curiosity, but it gives you a sense of what the tool can do in each style before you spend your own time on it.

## What the Music Actually Sounds Like

Honest answer: competent, pleasant, and generic. Electronic and lo-fi styles are the strongest. These genres are built on loops, textures, and repetition, which suits how the generator works. The beats are clean, the mixes are balanced, and a lo-fi track from Boomy would not sound out of place on a study-music playlist.

Styles that depend on performance and feel are weaker. Anything that should swing, build tension over a long arc, or surprise you tends to come out flat. Melodies wander without going anywhere. The arrangements are structurally fine, but you rarely get a hook you remember an hour later.

Expect to generate several versions to get one keeper. Because generation takes seconds, that isn't painful. A typical session for me was ten generations, two keepers, and some editing on the best one.

If you want to hear where AI music is headed technically, [Riffusion](/reviews/riffusion-review-2023-music-from-pictures-of-sound/) is a more experimental take. Boomy is the more finished product.

## Pros

- **Very fast.** A complete, mixed instrumental in under a minute, with no learning curve.
- **No skill floor.** Anyone can make something that sounds like a real track on the first try.
- **Release pipeline included.** Getting a song onto streaming services is normally a separate service and a separate fee. Boomy folds it in.
- **Cheap to try.** The free tier is enough to judge the output quality before you pay anything.
- **Good for loop-based genres.** Lo-fi, ambient, and electronic output is consistently usable.

## Cons and Limitations

- **Generic results.** The output rarely has a distinct identity. If you want music that sounds like *you*, this isn't the tool.
- **Shallow control.** No note-level editing, no text prompts, and limited say over arrangement details. You steer; you don't drive.
- **No AI vocals.** Vocals are your own recordings, processed lightly. That's a feature for some people and a dealbreaker for others.
- **Rights are complicated.** Boomy keeps meaningful rights on lower tiers and takes a share of revenue. That's a reasonable trade for free distribution, but it's not the same as owning a song outright.
- **Streaming income is tiny.** Per-stream payouts are fractions of a cent. Unless a track finds a real audience, expect pocket change, not a side income.
- **Platform rules can change.** Streaming services set their own policies on what they accept. A business plan that depends on uploading large volumes of generated music sits on rules you don't control.

## Pricing (approximate, as of April 2023)

Boomy runs a freemium model:

- **Free:** create and save songs, with limits on how many you can release and on what you can download.
- **Entry paid tier:** a few dollars a month. It raises release and download limits.
- **Pro tier:** roughly $10/month. It gives the highest limits and a better position on rights and revenue.

Plans and limits have changed before and will change again, so check Boomy's current pricing page before you commit. The free tier is genuinely useful for testing whether the output suits you.

## Who It's For

**Good fit:**
- Hobbyists and curious non-musicians who want to make a song and hear it on a streaming service.
- Teachers who want a hands-on way to show students how arrangement and genre work.
- Creators who want quick, original loop-style tracks and are comfortable reading the license terms.

**Poor fit:**
- Producers and musicians who need stems, MIDI, or precise control. A DAW is the right tool, with utilities like [LALAL.AI](/reviews/lalal-ai-review-2023/) for stem separation.
- Video editors whose only goal is background music with a clear commercial license. [Soundraw](/reviews/soundraw-review-2023-royalty-free-ai-music-for-creators/) is built around exactly that and gives you more control over timing.
- Anyone expecting meaningful streaming income from generated tracks.

## Tips If You Try It

1. **Start with lo-fi or electronic.** These are the styles where Boomy is most consistent, so you'll see its best output first.
2. **Generate in batches.** Make five to ten versions before you edit anything. Pick the strongest one and refine only that.
3. **Write a real title and make real cover art.** If you're releasing, presentation matters, and generic titles disappear on streaming platforms.
4. **Read the terms before releasing.** Know who owns what and how revenue is split on your plan.

## Verdict

Boomy is the most complete "anyone can make a song" product available right now. Generation is fast, the output is consistently listenable in loop-based styles, and the built-in release pipeline gives it a purpose that pure generators don't have.

Its limits are just as clear. The music is generic, the editing is shallow, and the economics of releasing AI songs to streaming services are thin. Treat Boomy as a creative toy that occasionally produces something worth sharing, and you'll enjoy it. Treat it as a music career or a reliable income stream, and you'll be disappointed.

**Rating: 3.5/5.** It's great fun and a smart product, but serious musicians and video creators have better-fitting options.
