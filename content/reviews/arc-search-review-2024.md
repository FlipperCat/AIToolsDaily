Most "AI search" products so far have been chatbots with a search box bolted on. Arc Search, the iPhone app The Browser Company released in late January 2024, tries something different: it's a mobile browser whose main trick is reading the web for you.

Type a question, tap **Browse for Me**, and instead of ten blue links you get a freshly built page, a mini-article assembled from several sources. It's a bold idea and a real challenge to how search has worked on phones for 15 years. We used Arc Search as our main mobile browser for about a month to see if it holds up.

## What Arc Search is

Arc Search is a free iOS browser built around three ideas:

1. **Search should answer, not just list.** Browse for Me summarizes multiple pages into one.
2. **Browsing should be clean.** Ads, trackers, cookie banners, and GDPR popups are blocked by default.
3. **Tabs shouldn't pile up.** Tabs archive themselves after a set period, so you don't end up with 400 open.

It's related to the company's Arc desktop browser but deliberately simpler. There are no Spaces, sidebars, or split views. The app opens straight to a search bar.

## Key features

**Browse for Me.** The core feature. Arc reads a handful of pages (typically around six) and builds a page with sections like overview, key details, and related links, tailored to your question. Sources are linked so you can dig in.

**Default search engine choice.** For regular searches, you pick a traditional engine such as Google, Bing, or DuckDuckGo. You can do a normal search anytime and use Browse for Me only when you want a summary.

**Built-in blocking.** Ads, trackers, and many cookie and consent popups are suppressed without any setup. On news and recipe sites this makes a big difference.

**Reader mode.** Pages open in a clean reading view by default, stripping clutter.

**Auto-archiving tabs.** Tabs you haven't touched in a while get archived automatically. You can still find them, but they don't clutter the view.

**Pinch to summarize.** A gesture that summarizes the page you're on. It's handy on long articles, though a bit hidden.

## What it's like to use

**For "explain this" questions, Browse for Me is great.** Questions like "how does a heat pump work in cold climates," "what's the difference between a Roth and traditional IRA," or "best way to store fresh herbs" all produced well-organized pages that were faster to read than clicking through three blog posts. Ad blocking helps here too: no cookie banners, autoplay video, or "subscribe" modals.

**For local and time-sensitive queries, it's weaker.** Restaurant recommendations, store hours, and "what happened today" questions were hit-or-miss. Sometimes the summary blended information from different locations or older articles. For these, a regular search engine or a maps app still works better.

**For shopping and product research, it's mixed.** It's good at pulling together general criteria for things like what to look for in running shoes. It's less reliable on specific current prices or models, where source quality varies and SEO-driven pages leak into the summary.

**Speed is the main tradeoff.** Browse for Me takes several seconds to build a page. That's fine for a considered question and annoying for a quick fact. We fell into a rhythm of normal search for quick lookups and Browse for Me for "I want to understand this."

## How it compares

Arc Search sits between a browser and an answer engine.

- Compared with [Perplexity](/posts/perplexity-ai-review-2024/), Arc gives you less control. There are no follow-up threads or focus modes, and citations are less granular. But it's a full browser with blocking built in, so it can replace Safari on your phone.
- Compared with [Bing Chat / Copilot](/reviews/bing-chat-review-2023/), Arc's output is more like a readable page than a chat reply, and it doesn't push you toward a conversation.
- Compared with using [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt-review/) for research, Arc starts from live web pages every time, which helps with freshness but means the summary is only as good as whichever pages it happened to read.

If you mostly want deep, multi-step research, Perplexity is still the stronger tool. If you want a better everyday mobile browser that sometimes does the reading for you, Arc Search is more appealing.

## Pros

- **Browse for Me is useful** for explainer-style questions and often saves several clicks.
- **Excellent default blocking.** The web feels noticeably cleaner and faster.
- **Thoughtful design.** Fast, calm, and minimal.
- **Free**, with no subscription required.
- **Easy to fall back** to a traditional search engine when AI isn't the right tool.

## Cons and limitations

- **Accuracy isn't guaranteed.** Summaries can blend sources, miss caveats, or present stale info. You still need to check sources for anything important.
- **Slower than a normal search.** Several seconds per Browse for Me query adds up.
- **iOS-first.** As of this review there's no Android version, which rules it out for a large share of phone users.
- **Weak on local and breaking news.**
- **Relationship with publishers is uneasy.** Summarizing pages and blocking ads means sites get fewer visits and less revenue. That's good for you in the moment and a real open question for the web long term.
- **Business model is unclear.** A free, ad-blocking browser with AI summaries is expensive to run. How it will make money, and whether features will stay free, remains to be seen.

## Pricing

As of March 2024, Arc Search is **free** on the App Store, with no in-app subscription. The Browser Company has said it intends to charge businesses for its products in the future. Treat that as uncertain, and assume features and pricing may change as the product matures.

## Who it's for

**Good fit:**
- iPhone users who want a cleaner, ad-free browsing experience
- People who ask many "explain this to me" questions on the go
- Arc desktop users who want a companion app

**Not a great fit:**
- Android users (for now)
- Researchers who need fine-grained citations and follow-up questions
- Anyone whose searches are mostly local, shopping, or breaking news

## Verdict

Arc Search is the most interesting mobile browser in years. Browse for Me isn't perfect: it's slower than search, sometimes wrong, and raises real questions about how the web gets paid for. But for the right kind of question, it's much better than wading through ad-heavy pages, and ad blocking alone makes it a nicer everyday browser than the default.

We'd suggest installing it, using it as your main mobile browser for a week, and using Browse for Me for questions where you want understanding rather than a quick fact. Keep a traditional search engine one tap away and always check the sources on anything important.

**Rating: 4.0 / 5.** A bold rethink of mobile search that's useful now, with rough edges that should improve.
