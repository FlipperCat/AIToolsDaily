Notion spent years absorbing docs, wikis, projects, and calendars into its workspace. This spring it came for the inbox. **Notion Mail** launched in April as a standalone email client that connects to your Gmail account and reskins it with Notion's editor, Notion-style database views, and AI that organizes and drafts for you.

Two months in, we've been daily-driving it. The short version: it's the most interesting rethink of the inbox since Superhuman, it's genuinely pleasant if you live in Notion already — and it has a list of limitations long enough that plenty of people should wait.

## What it is

Notion Mail is a front-end for Gmail. Your mail stays in Google's infrastructure; Notion Mail connects to your account and presents it through its own web app, Mac app, and iOS app. That framing matters for two reasons. First, it means trying it is low-risk — nothing migrates, and your Gmail keeps working everywhere else. Second, it means Notion Mail inherits Gmail's model (labels, archive, search operators) rather than inventing its own.

The pitch is that your inbox should work like a Notion database: filterable, viewable from multiple angles, and organized by AI instead of by you.

## Key features

**Custom views.** This is the headline idea. Instead of one chronological pile, you create views — filtered slices of your inbox that sit as tabs. A view for emails from your team, one for calendar invites, one for receipts, one for newsletters. Views can filter by sender, label, read state, and more, and they update live. It genuinely feels like applying Notion's database mindset to email, and once you've configured a few, the default single-stream inbox feels primitive.

**AI auto-labeling.** Describe a category in plain language — "emails about invoices or billing," "outreach from recruiters" — and Notion Mail's AI labels matching messages as they arrive. Combined with views, this is the engine of the whole product: the AI sorts, the views display. Accuracy in our testing was good-not-perfect; it occasionally misses edge cases, and you'll want a fallback view for unlabeled mail so things don't slip past silently.

**The Notion editor for composing.** Slash commands, markdown shortcuts, clean formatting. Writing email in it feels like writing in Notion, which is a compliment. Snippets (reusable templates with placeholders) are built in and work well for repetitive replies.

**AI drafting.** Select a thread, ask for a reply in a given direction, get a draft. It's competent at matching tone and pulling context from the thread — comparable to what we've seen from the AI drafting in [Superhuman](/reviews/superhuman-ai-review-2025/), and like all AI email drafts, best treated as a first pass you edit rather than something you send untouched.

**Scheduling via Notion Calendar.** Type a command to insert your availability, and recipients can book from the times you share. If you already use Notion Calendar, this is smooth; if you don't, it's an extra app to adopt.

**Keyboard-first design.** Nearly everything has a shortcut, and a command menu covers the rest. Triage speed after a week of muscle memory is legitimately fast — in the same conversation as Superhuman, which until recently owned this niche.

## Pros

- The views-plus-auto-labeling combination is a real innovation, not a gimmick — it changes how you process mail.
- Free core product; trying it costs nothing but setup time.
- Excellent compose experience and snippets.
- Fast, minimal, keyboard-driven interface with little visual clutter.
- Low-commitment: it's a layer over Gmail, so switching away is instant.

## Cons and limitations

- **Gmail only.** No Outlook, no IMAP. For many companies this is disqualifying on day one.
- **Platform gaps.** Web, Mac, and iPhone are covered; Windows and Android users are effectively out of luck as of June 2025.
- **AI costs are entangled with Notion's plans.** The features that make Notion Mail special — auto-labels, drafting — sit behind Notion AI, which as of this spring is bundled into Notion's pricier business tiers rather than sold as a cheap standalone add-on. If you're not otherwise paying for Notion, the effective price of "the good parts" is higher than it first appears.
- **No unified calendar pane.** Scheduling leans on the separate Notion Calendar app rather than an in-client calendar view.
- **Missing power-user staples.** Depending on your workflow you may hit gaps (advanced rules, some integrations, team features) that mature clients and raw Gmail cover. It's a young product and it shows at the edges.
- **AI processing your email** is inherent to the pitch; if that's a compliance or comfort problem, this isn't your client.

## Pricing

As of June 2025, treat all of this as approximate: the client itself is **free** with a Gmail account. AI features run on Notion AI — Notion now bundles AI into its **Business tier (roughly $20–25 per member per month)** rather than the older ~$10 add-on model, with trial usage so you can evaluate before paying. If you're already on a Notion plan with AI, Mail's AI features come along at no extra cost — the best-case scenario. Compare that with Superhuman at roughly $30/month and [Shortwave](/reviews/shortwave-ai-email-review-2025/)'s paid tiers, and Notion Mail is the cheapest path to an AI-organized inbox *if* you're in the Notion ecosystem, and a murkier value if you're not.

## Who it's for

- **Notion-first individuals and small teams on Gmail** — this is the bullseye. You already pay for (or trial) Notion AI, you know the editor, and Mail slots in naturally.
- **People curious about AI email but unwilling to pay Superhuman prices** — the free tier plus trial AI is a genuine test drive.
- **Not for**: Outlook shops, Android/Windows users, anyone needing a battle-tested client with years of edge cases sanded down, or anyone whose email can't touch AI processing. If you want a more mature AI email client and are willing to pay, see our [Superhuman vs Shortwave comparison](/compare/superhuman-vs-shortwave-2025/) — both are further along on polish.

## Verdict

Notion Mail is the most conceptually interesting email client of 2025. Views plus AI auto-labeling is a better organizing principle than folders, flags, or even Superhuman's split inbox, and the compose experience is best-in-class. But it's also a v1 with a narrow footprint — one email provider, two-and-a-half platforms, AI locked to Notion's plan structure — and it will fit your life in direct proportion to how much of your life already runs on Notion.

**Score: 7.5/10.** Notion users on Gmail should try it today; it's free and takes twenty minutes to set up meaningfully. Everyone else should bookmark it and check back in a year — if Notion ships Outlook support and broader platforms, this becomes a much bigger deal.
