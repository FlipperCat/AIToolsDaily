The AI meeting-notes space is crowded in 2025, and most of the tools do the same core thing: join your call, transcribe it, and hand you a summary. tl;dv (as in "too long; didn't view") stands out by leaning hard into two audiences — sales teams who need call intelligence wired into their CRM, and researchers who run stacks of user interviews and need to find patterns across them.

I've run tl;dv across weeks of real meetings — internal syncs, customer calls, a batch of user-research interviews — to see whether it earns its spot over the usual suspects. Here's the honest assessment.

## What tl;dv Is

tl;dv is an AI meeting recorder and note-taker for Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams. It joins your call (as a bot or via a desktop app), records the video and audio, transcribes everything with speaker labels, and generates AI notes and summaries. From there you can create timestamped clips, search across meetings, and — on paid plans — push the output into your CRM and other tools.

The tagline captures the pitch: you didn't have to sit through (or rewatch) the whole thing, because tl;dv turned it into something searchable and skimmable.

## Key Features

**Recording and transcription.** tl;dv captures Zoom, Meet, and Teams calls and transcribes them in a large number of languages with speaker detection. Transcripts are timestamped and clickable — jump to any moment by clicking the text.

**AI notes and summaries.** After each call you get an automatic summary and structured notes. You can also ask it to generate notes against a template — useful for consistent sales-call or interview write-ups.

**Timestamped highlights and clips.** You can mark moments during or after a call and turn them into short clips to share. For sales, that means sending a prospect's exact objection to your team; for research, it means cutting a montage of users hitting the same snag.

**Multi-meeting AI reports.** This is the standout. tl;dv can analyze across many meetings — surfacing recurring themes, objections, or feature requests from dozens of calls at once. For anyone doing customer discovery or user research at volume, this is the feature that saves real hours.

**CRM and workflow integrations.** On paid tiers, tl;dv syncs notes and insights to Salesforce and HubSpot and connects to Slack, Notion, and others, so meeting output lands where your team already works.

**Search library.** Every recording goes into a searchable library. Search a keyword and it finds the moment across all your calls, not just one transcript.

## Pros

- **Genuinely useful free plan.** Unlimited recordings and transcriptions for the three major platforms, at no cost, is more generous than most rivals. Individuals can run on the free tier indefinitely.
- **Cross-meeting analysis is a differentiator.** The ability to mine patterns across many calls is where tl;dv pulls ahead of tools that treat each meeting in isolation.
- **Strong for sales and research specifically.** The CRM sync and clip-sharing map neatly onto how revenue and product teams actually work.
- **Clean, fast interface.** Clicking a transcript to jump to that video moment is smooth, and the meeting library is well organized.
- **Broad language support.** Transcription covers a wide set of languages, which matters for international teams.

## Cons and Limitations

- **The best features are paywalled.** Multi-meeting reports, CRM integrations, templated notes, and downloads live on paid plans. The free tier is great for capture, but the intelligence that makes tl;dv special costs money.
- **Bot-in-the-meeting friction.** Like most recorders, tl;dv often joins as a visible participant. Some clients and interviewees are uneasy seeing a recording bot in the room, and consent handling is on you.
- **Transcription isn't magic.** Accuracy is good on clean audio but degrades with strong accents, crosstalk, and bad microphones — the same limitation every tool in this category shares. Always skim before trusting a critical transcript.
- **Summaries can flatten nuance.** AI summaries occasionally miss the one offhand comment that actually mattered. They're a strong first pass, not a replacement for reviewing important calls.
- **It's meeting-centric.** If your goal is lightweight, in-the-moment notes rather than full recordings, a bot-free tool that just listens locally may fit your habits better.

## Pricing

tl;dv uses a freemium model. As of early 2025 (and pricing shifts, so verify current numbers):

- **Free:** Unlimited recording and transcription across Zoom, Meet, and Teams, with basic AI notes. Surprisingly capable on its own.
- **Paid tiers (roughly $18–$30 per user/month, approximate):** Unlock AI templates, multi-meeting reports, CRM integrations, downloads, and more advanced controls, scaling up with team and admin features.

For an individual who just needs recordings and transcripts, free may be all you need. For a sales or research team that wants the call intelligence and CRM sync, the paid tiers are where the value concentrates — and that's the fair way to judge the cost.

## Who It's For

tl;dv is a strong fit for:

- **Sales teams** who want call notes and insights flowing into Salesforce or HubSpot automatically.
- **Product and UX researchers** running many interviews who need to spot patterns across all of them.
- **Distributed teams** who need a searchable, shareable record of decisions.
- **Individuals** who want a free, no-limits recorder and transcriber.

It's a weaker fit if you want quiet, bot-free note-taking, if your meetings involve sensitive material where a recording bot is a non-starter, or if you never revisit calls and just want a one-line summary.

## Verdict

tl;dv is one of the better meeting tools of 2025, and it knows exactly who it's for. If you're an individual who just wants free, unlimited recordings and clean transcripts, it's an easy yes. If you're a sales or research team, its cross-meeting reports and CRM sync are the features that justify a paid seat and genuinely change how you work with call data.

Where it's less compelling is anywhere a recording bot is awkward or where you want minimal, in-the-moment notes rather than a full archive. But for teams whose real value hides inside dozens of conversations they never have time to rewatch, tl;dv does the thing you actually wanted: it makes those hours searchable, shareable, and finally useful. Recommended, with the caveat that the magic mostly lives behind the paywall.
