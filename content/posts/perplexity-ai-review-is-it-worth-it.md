# Perplexity AI Review: I Replaced Google With It For 30 Days

I kept hearing that Perplexity was replacing Google for people. That sounded like hype. So I tried it.

For 30 days, Perplexity was my default search. Here's what actually happened.

## What Perplexity Actually Is

It's a search engine that gives you answers instead of links.

Ask Google: "What's the best laptop for programming?" → 10 links to SEO-optimized articles you have to read.

Ask Perplexity: Same question → A synthesized answer with recommendations, specs, and pros/cons, with sources cited.

The difference is massive when you actually need to understand something.

## Where Perplexity Completely Won

### Deep Research

This is where it's legitimately better than Google.

I was researching market trends for a client. In Google, this meant opening 15 tabs, reading through fluff content, piecing together information myself.

In Perplexity, I asked questions and got synthesized answers with citations. What would've been 2 hours became 30 minutes.

For any "I need to understand this topic" search, Perplexity wins.

### Current Events and Facts

ChatGPT's knowledge cutoff means it can't answer questions about recent things. Perplexity searches live.

"What happened at [recent event]?" → Actual answer with sources from today.

I stopped having to think about whether something happened before or after ChatGPT's cutoff date.

### Explaining Complex Things

"Explain how [technical thing] works" in Google gives you documentation written for experts.

Perplexity gives you an explanation at your level, then links to deeper resources if you need them.

I used it constantly for understanding things outside my expertise.

### Comparison Research

"What's the difference between [thing A] and [thing B]?" → Perplexity gives you a comparison table and explanation.

Google gives you 10 articles you have to synthesize yourself.

## Where Google Still Won

### Navigation

"Facebook login" → I just want the link. Perplexity gives me an explanation of Facebook. Google gives me the link.

For getting somewhere, not learning something, Google is faster.

### Shopping

"Nike Air Max 90" → I want to see products and prices. Perplexity gives me information about the shoe. Google shows me shopping results.

For buying things, Google (especially Google Shopping) is better.

### Local Stuff

"Coffee shop near me" → Google knows where I am and shows ratings, hours, maps. Perplexity gives me general information about finding coffee shops.

Google's integration with Maps makes local search much better.

### Quick Lookups

"Weather tomorrow" or "USD to EUR" or "Taylor Swift age" → Google answers these instantly in the search results. Perplexity works, but it's overkill.

## The Hybrid Workflow I Landed On

By day 15, I stopped forcing Perplexity for everything. Here's how I use them now:

**Use Perplexity when:**
- I need to understand something
- I'm researching a topic
- I want information synthesized, not links
- I need current information that ChatGPT wouldn't have
- I want sources I can verify

**Use Google when:**
- I want to go somewhere (navigation)
- I'm shopping
- I need local information
- It's a quick lookup I've done before
- I want images or videos

This isn't one replacing the other. They're different tools.

## Free vs. Pro - Is Pro Worth It?

**Free tier:**
- Limited searches per day (I think around 5-10 "Pro" searches)
- Uses a basic AI model
- Good enough for casual use

**Pro ($20/month):**
- Unlimited searches
- Better AI models (GPT-4 and Claude)
- Better at complex questions
- Faster

I paid for Pro after about a week. If you're doing research regularly, it's worth it. If you search a few times a day casually, free is fine.

## What's Still Annoying

### It's slower than Google for simple things
When I know exactly what I want, Perplexity's synthesis process is overkill. Google's instant answers are faster.

### Sometimes too thorough
I ask a simple question, I get a 400-word explanation with 8 sources. Sometimes I just wanted a quick answer.

### Sources aren't always good
It cites sources, but sometimes the sources aren't great. A random blog post isn't necessarily better than no source.

### Mobile app is just okay
It works, but the mobile experience isn't as polished as Google's.

## The Bottom Line

Perplexity is the best tool I've found for research and understanding things. It's genuinely transformative for that use case.

It's not a Google replacement for everything. It's a Google complement for specific things.

If you do any kind of research—writing, learning, decision-making—try Perplexity for a week. The free tier is enough to see if it fits your workflow.

If all you do is navigate to websites and check the weather, you don't need it.

**My verdict:** Pro subscription is worth it for me. Saves enough research time that $20/month is nothing. But I still use Google probably 40% of the time for quick stuff.
